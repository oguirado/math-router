## 1. Abstract and result contract

> We prove that both summands of every specified holomorphic splitting of the tangent bundle of a smooth rationally connected projective complex manifold into two positive-rank subbundles are integrable. This proves Höring's conjecture on rationally connected projective manifolds. Höring's product theorem then gives a product decomposition compatible with the specified splitting.

- **C1** (theorem, [source](#stmt-thm-main)): Let $X$ be a smooth connected projective complex manifold of dimension at least two. Suppose that two general points of $X$ lie on the image of a morphism $\mathbb P^1\to X$. For every specified holomorphic decomposition $$T_X=E_1\oplus E_2$$ into subbundles of positive rank, both $E_1$ and $E_2$ are integrable.
  - Explanation: Rational connectedness forces integrability of both specified embedded tangent summands.
- **C2** (conditional_result, [source](#stmt-cor-product)): Under the hypotheses of <a href="#thm:main" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:main">1</a>, there are smooth projective manifolds $X_1,X_2$ and an isomorphism $X\simeq X_1\times X_2$ under which $$E_i=\operatorname{pr}_i^*T_{X_i}\qquad (i=1,2).$$
  - Explanation: The resulting product identifies the specified summands with the factor tangent bundles.
- **C3** (construction, [source](#stmt-prop-box)): Every holomorphic map $g:C\to X$ admits a holomorphic map $F:S\to X$ such that $$\label{eq:box} F\|_\Delta=g,\qquad dF(T^{(1)})\subset F^*E_1,\qquad dF(T^{(2)})\subset F^*E_2.$$ Neither summand is assumed integrable.
  - Explanation: A tangent box extends a parameterized rational curve to the product while preserving the complementary tangencies without assuming integrability.
- **C4** (construction, [source](#stmt-lem-formal)): There is a unique formal holomorphic map $\widehat F$ along $\Delta$ with diagonal value $g$ and the two tangency properties in <a href="#eq:box" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:box">[eq:box]</a>, interpreted formally.
  - Explanation: The projectors determine a unique formal extension with the required tangencies.
- **C5** (construction, [source](#stmt-lem-rational)): Let $Y\subset\mathbb P^N$ be a closed complex submanifold and let $\widehat G$ be a formal holomorphic map from the formal neighborhood of $\Delta\subset S$ to $Y$. There is a rational map $R:S\dashrightarrow\mathbb P^N$ that is holomorphic on a neighborhood of a nonempty open subset of $\Delta$, where its formal expansion agrees with $\widehat G$. On that neighborhood it takes values in $Y$.
  - Explanation: Formal sections and cancellation recover a rational map agreeing with the formal map near a diagonal patch.
- **C6** (conditional_result, [source](#stmt-lem-removal)): Let $R:S\dashrightarrow X$ be a rational map such that, on its domain of definition, $$dR(T^{(1)})\subset R^*E_1,\qquad dR(T^{(2)})\subset R^*E_2.$$ Then $R$ extends to a holomorphic map $S\to X$.
  - Explanation: Complementary tangency contracts exceptional components and yields a global holomorphic extension.
- **C7** (construction, [source](#stmt-prop-transport)): Let $F:S\to X$ be a tangent box of $g:C\to X$. Transport along the fibers of $\operatorname{pr}_i$ gives holomorphic bundle isomorphisms $$\label{eq:transport} \Theta_i:\operatorname{pr}_i^*(g^*E_i)\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }F^*E_i, \qquad i=1,2,$$ whose restrictions to the diagonal are the identity.
  - Explanation: Relative transport identifies each pulled-back summand with its corresponding factor pullback and fixes the diagonal.
- **C8** (construction, [source](#stmt-lem-variation)): Fix distinct $a,b\in C$. There are a nonempty complex manifold $T$ and a holomorphic map $G:C\times T\to X$ such that $$(\operatorname{ev}_a,\operatorname{ev}_b):T\longrightarrow X\times X,\qquad \tau\longmapsto (G(a,\tau),G(b,\tau))$$ is a submersion. In particular, for $g=G(\,\cdot\,,\tau)$, the global sections of $g^*E_i$ vanishing at $a$ have values spanning $E_i$ at $g(b)$.
  - Explanation: A submersive family produces sections vanishing at a marked point and spanning the summand at the other.
- **C9** (classification, [source](#stmt-cor-rational-quotient)): Let $X$ be a connected uniruled compact Kähler manifold with a holomorphic splitting $T_X=V_1\oplus V_2$. Let $Z$ be a general fiber of its rationally connected quotient and set $W_i=T_Z\cap V_i\|_Z$. For each $i=1,2$, there is an almost-holomorphic fibration $\phi_i:X\dashrightarrow Y_i$ whose general fiber $F_i$ is rationally connected and, for compatible choices $F_i\subset Z$, satisfies $$T_{F_i}=W_i\|_{F_i}\subset V_i\|_{F_i}.$$ If $W_i=0$, one may take $\phi_i=\operatorname{id}_X$, with point fibers.* *If $\operatorname{rank}V_1=2$, exactly one of the following holds:* 1. *$W_1=V_1\|_Z$, and $X$ admits an analytic fiber bundle $X\to Y$ with rationally connected general fiber and $T_{X/Y}=V_1$.* 2. *$W_1$ is a line bundle, and there is an equidimensional holomorphic map $X\to Y$ whose general fiber $F$ is a rational curve with $T_F\subset V_1\|_F$.* 3. *$W_1=0$, equivalently $T_Z\subset V_2\|_Z$.
  - Explanation: General quotient fibers supply integrability for the stated fibrations and the conditional rank trichotomy.
- **C10** (construction, [source](#stmt-lem-surface)): A rational map $R:\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1\dashrightarrow\mathbb P^N$ is holomorphic outside a finite set $B$. A finite sequence of point blowups over $B$ gives a smooth projective surface $\mu:S'\to\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1$ on which $R\circ\mu$ extends holomorphically. The fibers of $\mu$ are connected; its exceptional components are smooth curves.
  - Explanation: Point blowups resolve the surface rational map with connected fibers and smooth exceptional components.

## 2. Definitions, validity and bounds

- **General points** ([source](#pm-h-2)): Here and below, “two general points” means all pairs in some nonempty Zariski open subset of $X\times X$.
- **Rational connectedness** ([source](#pm-h-2)): A smooth connected projective complex manifold is rationally connected if two general points lie on the image of a morphism $\mathbb P^1\to X$.
- **Integrability** ([source](#pm-h-2)): A necessary local condition is *integrability*: the holomorphic local sections of each $E_i$ must be closed under the Lie bracket.
- **Holomorphic projectors** ([source](#sec:boxes)): Let $P_i:T_X\to E_i\subset T_X$ be the corresponding holomorphic projectors.
- **Product surface and diagonal** ([source](#sec:boxes)): Put $$C=\mathbb P^1,\qquad S=C\times C,\qquad \Delta=\{(s,s):s\in C\},$$ and write $T^{(i)}=\operatorname{pr}_i^*T_C\subset T_S$.
- **Analytic completion** ([source](#pm-h-7)): On the topological space $\Delta$, set $$\widehat{\mathcal O}_{S,\Delta} =\varprojlim_{n\ge0}\iota^{-1}(\mathcal O_S/\mathcal I^{n+1}), \qquad \widehat{\mathcal I}=\ker(\widehat{\mathcal O}_{S,\Delta}\longrightarrow\mathcal O_\Delta).$$
- **Formal holomorphic map** ([source](#pm-h-7)): A formal holomorphic map $\widehat G:\widehat S_\Delta\to Y$ to a complex manifold $Y$ consists of a holomorphic diagonal map $g_0:\Delta\to Y$ and a morphism of sheaves of $\mathbb C$-algebras $$\widehat G^\#:g_0^{-1}\mathcal O_Y\longrightarrow\widehat{\mathcal O}_{S,\Delta}$$ reducing to $g_0^\#$ modulo $\widehat{\mathcal I}$.
- **Projected-bracket partial connection** ([source](#eq:partial)): For $i\ne j$, the splitting defines a projected-bracket partial connection on $E_i$ in directions of $E_j$: $$\label{eq:partial} \nabla^i_\xi e=P_i[\xi,e],$$ where $\xi$ and $e$ are local holomorphic sections of $E_j$ and $E_i$, respectively.
- **Bracket obstruction tensor** ([source](#sec:vanishing)): For $i\ne j$, define $$\mathcal B_i:\bigwedge\nolimits^2E_i\longrightarrow E_j,\qquad \mathcal B_i(e,e')=P_j[e,e'].$$

## 3. Proof roadmap

- **R1** [claim](#stmt-lem-formal) / [proof](#proof-stmt-lem-formal): Recursive formal Cauchy solution and coordinate-change uniqueness. -> A unique formal tangent box.
- **R2** [claim](#stmt-lem-rational) / [proof](#proof-stmt-lem-rational): Formal-section dimension bound, polynomial kernel pairs, and local cancellation. -> A rational map agreeing near a diagonal patch.

## 4. Engineering Triptych

### Panel 1 - epistemic contract

- **I-integrability**: Rational connectedness makes both specified positive-rank holomorphic tangent summands integrable.
- **I-section-count**: The formal-section bound has the explicit sum $(m+q+1)^2$ for $d=2q$ and $(m+q+1)(m+q+2)$ for $d=2q+1$.
- **I-box**: A tangent box has the prescribed diagonal value and complementary factor tangencies without an integrability assumption.

### Panel 2 - negative knowledge

- **A-abstract-split**: Infer compatible product directions from an abstract bundle decomposition. -> The embedded summands are lost, so compatibility with their factor directions is not established.
- **A-singular**: Apply automatic integrability to singular rationally connected spaces. -> The source reports a singular rationally connected example with a nonintegrable summand.
- **A-software**: Treat a software partition as a holomorphic tangent splitting. -> A partition supplies no complex manifold, rational connectedness, or holomorphic tangent subbundles.
- **A-arithmetic-scope**: Use agreement of the section-count arithmetic to certify the geometric proof. -> The finite sum does not establish formal-map gluing, rational reconstruction, or integrability.

### Panel 3 - reference implementation and oracle

Applicability: `constructive`. The explicit formal-section sum is a finite constructive arithmetic result. This implementation checks that sum only; it does not construct a tangent box or certify integrability.

# INPUTS: JSON list of pairs [d,m] on stdin, at most 64 pairs.
# PRECONDITIONS: d,m are integers in [0,64]; bool is excluded.
# OUTPUTS: JSON list of exact formal-section sums in input order.
# TIME: O(n) exact integer operations for n input pairs.
# SPACE: O(n) exact integers.
# ARITHMETIC: python-flint fmpz; no floating point.
# FAILURE: malformed or out-of-contract input raises and exits nonzero.
import json, sys
from flint import fmpz
pairs = json.loads(sys.stdin.read())
assert isinstance(pairs, list) and 1 <= len(pairs) <= 64
answers = []

    assert isinstance(pair, list) and len(pair) == 2
    assert all(type(x) is int and 0 <= x <= 64 for x in pair)
    d, m = pair
    q = fmpz(d // 2)
    a = fmpz(m) + q + 1
    if d % 2 == 0:
        value = a * a
    else:
        value = a * (a + 1)
    answers.append(int(value))
print(json.dumps(answers))

## 6. Applicability limits and search

- **L-SCOPE** (hypothesis): Automatic integrability requires a smooth connected projective complex manifold, rational connectedness, and a specified holomorphic splitting into positive-rank subbundles. Blocked use: ['Applying automatic integrability without establishing these geometric hypotheses.']
- **L-SPECIFIED** (hypothesis): Compatibility concerns the embedded summands of the specified splitting, rather than only their abstract bundle types. Blocked use: ['Replacing the specified embedded subbundles by abstractly isomorphic bundles.']
- **L-SINGULAR** (counterexample_scope): Smoothness cannot be dropped: the context records a singular rationally connected example with a nonintegrable summand. Blocked use: ['Extending the smooth theorem to arbitrary singular rationally connected varieties.']
- **L-ABSTRACT-PRODUCT** (counterexample_scope): An abstract product need not make a specified summand integrable. Blocked use: ['Inferring integrability of a given summand from the existence of an abstract product.']
- **L-BOX-CRITICAL** (edge_case): A tangent box may have critical points and need not supply local coordinates. Blocked use: ['Treating a tangent box as a local coordinate chart or assuming its differential is invertible.']
- **L-FORMAL** (formal_scope): Formal holomorphic series are not assumed convergent in the normal variable; passage to an ordinary map requires the rational reconstruction and cancellation argument. Blocked use: ['Reading a formal solution as an already convergent holomorphic solution.']
- **L-ASYMPTOTIC** (approximation): The formal-section asymptotic bound holds with the diagonal degree fixed; it is not an equality for the section-space dimension. Blocked use: ['Using the asymptotic bound as an exact dimension formula or allowing the degree to vary without a new bound.']
- **L-EXISTENCE** (nonconstructive): The tangent-box existence conclusion does not by itself provide an executable construction for an arbitrary presented manifold and curve. Blocked use: ['Treating geometric existence as an implemented routine with certified input representation and runtime.']
- **L-GENERAL-FIBER** (hypothesis): The quotient consequence proves integrability of the induced splitting on the general fiber; it does not assert ambient integrability for every summand on an arbitrary uniruled compact Kähler manifold. Blocked use: ['Transferring the general-fiber conclusion to ambient summands without further hypotheses.']
- **L-NO-SOFTWARE** (unknown): ABSENT: a correspondence between holomorphic tangent subbundles and arbitrary software partitions or interfaces. Blocked use: ['Using integrability or the compatible product conclusion to certify independence of software modules.']

> ## Scope
>
> The splitting question asks whether a holomorphic decomposition of the tangent bundle comes from a product decomposition of the universal cover. The formalized result gives an affirmative answer for a compact connected Kähler manifold whose tangent bundle splits into two positive-rank, integrable holomorphic subbundles. The universal cover is a product of connected simply connected complex manifolds of the prescribed dimensions, and the differential identifies the two tangent factors with the original summands.
>
> Both integrability assumptions are required. Automatic integrability and the paper's additional corollaries are not included.
>
> The formalization proves integrability of both summands in a holomorphic splitting of the tangent bundle of a rationally connected projective manifold. More precisely, for a compact connected complex manifold of dimension at least two with a projective embedding witnessing rational connectedness, each positive-rank summand of a holomorphic tangent-bundle splitting is integrable. The paper's compatible product decomposition is a separate consequence, outside this selected statement.

namespace OAI

structure ComplexManifold where
  dim : ℕ
  Carrier : Type u
  [topology : TopologicalSpace Carrier]
  [charts : ChartedSpace (Fin dim → ℂ) Carrier]
  [holomorphicAtlas : IsManifold 𝓘(ℂ, Fin dim → ℂ) ω Carrier]
  [hausdorff : T2Space Carrier]
  [secondCountable : SecondCountableTopology Carrier]

attribute [instance] ComplexManifold.topology ComplexManifold.charts
  ComplexManifold.holomorphicAtlas ComplexManifold.hausdorff
  ComplexManifold.secondCountable

abbrev Model (X : ComplexManifold.{u}) := Fin X.dim → ℂ
abbrev ModelWithCorners (X : ComplexManifold.{u}) := 𝓘(ℂ, Model X)
abbrev Tangent (X : ComplexManifold.{u}) (x : X.Carrier) :=
  TangentSpace (ModelWithCorners X) x

abbrev ProjectiveSpace (N : ℕ) := ℙ ℂ (Fin (N + 1) → ℂ)

 

def projectiveCoordinates {N : ℕ} (i : Fin (N + 1))
    (p : ProjectiveSpace N) : Fin (N + 1) → ℂ :=
  fun j => (p.rep i)⁻¹ * p.rep j

 

structure ProjectiveEmbedding (X : ComplexManifold.{u}) where
  N : ℕ
  map : X.Carrier → ProjectiveSpace N
  injective : Function.Injective map
  open_patch : ∀ i, IsOpen {x | (map x).rep i ≠ 0}
  holomorphic : ∀ i, ContMDiffOn (ModelWithCorners X) 𝓘(ℂ, Fin (N + 1) → ℂ) ω
    (fun x => projectiveCoordinates i (map x)) {x | (map x).rep i ≠ 0}
  immersion : ∀ x i, (map x).rep i ≠ 0 → Function.Injective
    (mfderiv (ModelWithCorners X) 𝓘(ℂ, Fin (N + 1) → ℂ)
      (fun y => projectiveCoordinates i (map y)) x)

 
structure RationalCurve (X : ComplexManifold.{u}) where
  finite : ℂ → X.Carrier
  infinity : ℂ → X.Carrier
  finite_holomorphic : ContMDiff 𝓘(ℂ, ℂ) (ModelWithCorners X) ω finite
  infinity_holomorphic : ContMDiff 𝓘(ℂ, ℂ) (ModelWithCorners X) ω infinity
  overlap : ∀ z : ℂ, z ≠ 0 → finite z = infinity z⁻¹

def RationalCurve.value {X : ComplexManifold.{u}} (g : RationalCurve X)
    (z : OnePoint ℂ) : X.Carrier :=
  z.elim (g.infinity 0) g.finite

abbrev PairPolynomial (N : ℕ) := MvPolynomial (Fin 2 × Fin (N + 1)) ℂ

 
def Bihomogeneous {N : ℕ} (p : PairPolynomial N) : Prop :=
  ∃ a b : ℕ, ∀ m ∈ p.support,
    (∑ i : Fin (N + 1), m (0, i)) = a ∧
    (∑ i : Fin (N + 1), m (1, i)) = b

def pairEval {X : ComplexManifold.{u}} (e : ProjectiveEmbedding X)
    (p : PairPolynomial e.N) (xy : X.Carrier × X.Carrier) : ℂ :=
  MvPolynomial.eval
    (fun k : Fin 2 × Fin (e.N + 1) =>
      if k.1 = 0 then (e.map xy.1).rep k.2 else (e.map xy.2).rep k.2) p

 

def IsZariskiOpenPair {X : ComplexManifold.{u}} (e : ProjectiveEmbedding X)
    (U : Set (X.Carrier × X.Carrier)) : Prop :=
  ∃ equations : Set (PairPolynomial e.N),
    (∀ p ∈ equations, Bihomogeneous p) ∧
    U = {xy | ∃ p ∈ equations, pairEval e p xy ≠ 0}

 

def RationallyConnected {X : ComplexManifold.{u}} (e : ProjectiveEmbedding X) : Prop :=
  ∃ U : Set (X.Carrier × X.Carrier), IsZariskiOpenPair e U ∧ U.Nonempty ∧ Dense U ∧
    ∀ xy ∈ U, ∃ g : RationalCurve X, ∃ a b : OnePoint ℂ,
      g.value a = xy.1 ∧ g.value b = xy.2

 

structure TangentSplitting (X : ComplexManifold.{u}) where
  projector : ∀ x : X.Carrier, Tangent X x →L[ℂ] Tangent X x
  idempotent : ∀ x v, projector x (projector x v) = projector x v
  holomorphic : ContMDiff
    ((ModelWithCorners X).prod 𝓘(ℂ, Model X))
    ((ModelWithCorners X).prod 𝓘(ℂ, Model X)) ω
    (fun v : TangentBundle (ModelWithCorners X) X.Carrier =>
      (⟨v.proj, projector v.proj v.2⟩ : TangentBundle (ModelWithCorners X) X.Carrier))
  first_positive : ∀ x, 0 < Module.finrank ℂ (LinearMap.range (projector x).toLinearMap)
  second_positive : ∀ x, 0 < Module.finrank ℂ (LinearMap.ker (projector x).toLinearMap)

def TangentSplitting.first {X : ComplexManifold.{u}} (S : TangentSplitting X) (x : X.Carrier) :
    Submodule ℂ (Tangent X x) := LinearMap.range (S.projector x).toLinearMap

def TangentSplitting.second {X : ComplexManifold.{u}} (S : TangentSplitting X) (x : X.Carrier) :
    Submodule ℂ (Tangent X x) := LinearMap.ker (S.projector x).toLinearMap

 

def HolomorphicSectionOn (X : ComplexManifold.{u})
    (V : ∀ x : X.Carrier, Tangent X x) (U : Set X.Carrier) : Prop :=
  ContMDiffOn (ModelWithCorners X) ((ModelWithCorners X).prod 𝓘(ℂ, Model X)) ω
    (fun x => (⟨x, V x⟩ : TangentBundle (ModelWithCorners X) X.Carrier)) U

 
def Integrable (X : ComplexManifold.{u}) (D : ∀ x : X.Carrier, Submodule ℂ (Tangent X x)) : Prop :=
  ∀ U : Set X.Carrier, IsOpen U → ∀ V W : ∀ x : X.Carrier, Tangent X x,
    HolomorphicSectionOn X V U → HolomorphicSectionOn X W U →
    (∀ x ∈ U, V x ∈ D x) → (∀ x ∈ U, W x ∈ D x) →
    ∀ x ∈ U, VectorField.mlieBracketWithin (ModelWithCorners X) V W U x ∈ D x

 

def IntegrabilityStatement : Prop :=
  ∀ (X : ComplexManifold.{u}), ConnectedSpace X.Carrier → CompactSpace X.Carrier →
    2 ≤ X.dim → ∀ e : ProjectiveEmbedding X, RationallyConnected e →
    ∀ S : TangentSplitting X, Integrable X S.first ∧ Integrable X S.second

## 7. Complete mathematical paper


We prove that both summands of every specified holomorphic splitting of the tangent bundle of a smooth rationally connected projective complex manifold into two positive-rank subbundles are integrable. This proves Höring's conjecture on rationally connected projective manifolds. Höring's product theorem then gives a product decomposition compatible with the specified splitting.

### Introduction

A product of complex manifolds has a tangent bundle split into the pullbacks of the tangent bundles of its factors. A converse must keep track of more than the abstract bundles. For a specified decomposition $$T_X=E_1\oplus E_2,$$ the embeddings of $E_1$ and $E_2$ in $T_X$ are part of the data, and a compatible product must identify these subbundles with its factor directions. A necessary local condition is *integrability*: the holomorphic local sections of each $E_i$ must be closed under the Lie bracket.

We prove that rational connectedness forces this local condition. Here and below, “two general points” means all pairs in some nonempty Zariski open subset of $X\times X$. A smooth connected projective complex manifold is rationally connected if two general points lie on the image of a morphism $\mathbb P^1\to X$.

<a id="stmt-thm-main"></a>
<div id="thm:main" class="theorem">

**Theorem 1** (Automatic integrability). *Let $X$ be a smooth connected projective complex manifold of dimension at least two. Suppose that two general points of $X$ lie on the image of a morphism $\mathbb P^1\to X$. For every specified holomorphic decomposition $$T_X=E_1\oplus E_2$$ into subbundles of positive rank, both $E_1$ and $E_2$ are integrable.*

</div>

The theorem has no further rank or positivity hypothesis. It proves the smooth rationally connected assertion formulated in <a id="pm-cite-1"></a>[[Hor26](#ref-Hor26), Conjecture 1.5]. Höring’s earlier product theorem then answers the global question for the same specified splitting.

<a id="stmt-cor-product"></a>
<div id="cor:product" class="corollary">

**Corollary 2** (Compatible product). *Under the hypotheses of <a href="#thm:main" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:main">1</a>, there are smooth projective manifolds $X_1,X_2$ and an isomorphism $X\simeq X_1\times X_2$ under which $$E_i=\operatorname{pr}_i^*T_{X_i}\qquad (i=1,2).$$*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-cor-product"><summary>Proof of stmt-cor-product</summary>

*Proof.* Höring’s applies to a smooth connected rationally connected projective complex manifold with a specified splitting of its tangent bundle and at least one integrable summand. By <a href="#thm:main" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:main">1</a>, these hypotheses hold. His theorem gives an isomorphism with a product that identifies the specified summands with the factor tangent bundles. Each factor is smooth. It is projective because its realization as a slice of the product is a closed complex submanifold of $X$, and a projective embedding of $X$ restricts to an embedding of that slice. ◻

</details>

#### Context and scope

Beauville studied how a tangent-bundle splitting on a compact Kähler manifold can lead to a product of its universal cover <a id="pm-cite-2"></a>[[Bea00](#ref-Bea00)]. An abstract product need not make a given summand integrable: Höring’s gives a nonintegrable summand on a product of an abelian surface with $\mathbb P^1$. Rational connectedness rules out that example and supplies the abundance of rational curves used in our proof.

Several earlier results establish compatible products under positivity or rank assumptions. Campana and Peternell treated Fano manifolds with a two-summand splitting when one summand has rank one or two, and hence every two-summand splitting on a Fano manifold of dimension at most five <a id="pm-cite-3"></a>[[CP02](#ref-CP02), Theorems 3.5–3.6 and Corollary 3.7]. Höring’s showed that, for a rationally connected projective manifold, integrability of one summand already gives a compatible product. He then isolated the remaining question by conjecturing that at least one summand is integrable <a id="pm-cite-4"></a>[[Hor08](#ref-Hor08), Conjecture 1.2]. His later low-rank result applies to a uniruled projective manifold whose tangent bundle splits into summands all of rank at most two, and proves that at least one of them is integrable <a id="pm-cite-5"></a>[[Hor08](#ref-Hor08), Lemma 4.21].

The recent Fano-type theorem <a id="pm-cite-6"></a>[[Hor26](#ref-Hor26), Theorem 1.1] establishes integrability with algebraic leaves for split tangent sheaves on normal $\mathbb Q$-factorial projective klt varieties of Fano type. In the smooth Fano case it yields an actual compatible product <a id="pm-cite-7"></a>[[Hor26](#ref-Hor26), Corollary 1.3]. The present theorem removes this positivity condition for smooth rationally connected manifolds. Smoothness matters: <a id="pm-cite-8"></a>[[Hor26](#ref-Hor26), Example 4.3] gives a singular rationally connected example with a nonintegrable summand. Section <a href="#sec:consequences" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:consequences">5</a> applies the smooth theorem to general fibers of the rationally connected quotient, supplying the integrability premise in Höring’s structure theorems for uniruled compact Kähler manifolds.

<a id="pm-h-4"></a>
#### Proof strategy

The construction that drives the proof does not require rational connectedness. Given any holomorphic map $g:\mathbb P^1\to X$ into a smooth projective manifold with a split tangent bundle, we construct $$F:\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1\longrightarrow X,\qquad F(s,s)=g(s),$$ with the first factor direction tangent to $E_1$ and the second tangent to $E_2$. We call $F$ a *tangent box*. The name records these tangencies; the map may have critical points and need not give local coordinates.

Write $P_i:T_X\to E_i\subset T_X$ for the holomorphic projectors defined by the splitting. The diagonal first carries a formal solution. In coordinates $u=s$, $v=t-s$, the equation $\partial_v\widehat F=P_2(\widehat F)\partial_u\widehat F$ recursively separates the derivative of $g$ into its two summands. The positive normal bundle of the diagonal then bounds the number of global formal sections of a twisted line bundle. Comparing that bound with pairs of polynomial sections forces the formal projective coordinate ratios to be rational. A local cancellation makes these ratios holomorphic near a diagonal patch. Finally, on a resolution of the rational map, the two tangency identities force every exceptional curve to be contracted. The resulting map is holomorphic on the entire product. The section-count argument is given directly for holomorphic formal series; the algebraic formal-function literature provides broader context <a id="pm-cite-9"></a>[[HM68](#ref-HM68), [Bad96](#ref-Bad96)].

The splitting also provides the projected-bracket partial connection $\nabla^i_\xi e=P_i[\xi,e]$ on $E_i$ in directions of the complementary summand. On a tangent box it gives transport along the fibers of $\operatorname{pr}_i$, which vary the complementary factor, and hence $$F^*E_i\simeq\operatorname{pr}_i^*(g^*E_i),$$ normalized to be the identity on the diagonal. Thus, on a factor curve, the corresponding summand retains the bundle $g^*E_i$, while the complementary summand is trivial.

Rational connectedness enters only in the last step. A two-point family of rational curves supplies sections of $g^*E_i$ that vanish at one marked point and span the summand at another. Applying the bracket tensor $\bigwedge^2E_i\to E_j$ to two such sections on a factor curve gives a section of a trivial bundle on $\mathbb P^1$. It is constant and vanishes at the first mark, so it vanishes at the second. The spanning property then kills the bracket tensor on an open set of $X$, and hence everywhere. The use of rational curves to annihilate this tensor has a direct antecedent in <a id="pm-cite-10"></a>[[Hor07](#ref-Hor07), Section 2.B and Lemma 3.18]; the tangent box supplies the trivial complementary target in arbitrary rank.

### Tangent boxes

Throughout this section, $X$ is a smooth projective complex manifold with a fixed splitting $T_X=E_1\oplus E_2$. Let $P_i:T_X\to E_i\subset T_X$ be the corresponding holomorphic projectors. Put $$C=\mathbb P^1,\qquad S=C\times C,\qquad
\Delta=\{(s,s):s\in C\},$$ and write $T^{(i)}=\operatorname{pr}_i^*T_C\subset T_S$.

<a id="stmt-prop-box"></a>
<div id="prop:box" class="proposition">

<a id="eq:box"></a>
**Proposition 3** (Tangent box). *Every holomorphic map $g:C\to X$ admits a holomorphic map $F:S\to X$ such that $$\label{eq:box}
F|_\Delta=g,\qquad
dF(T^{(1)})\subset F^*E_1,\qquad
dF(T^{(2)})\subset F^*E_2.$$ Neither summand is assumed integrable.*

</div>

The proof has three stages. The projectors first determine a formal map along $\Delta$. A count of formal sections then produces a rational map with that expansion near part of the diagonal. Finally, the two tangency conditions force every exceptional curve in a resolution to be contracted, so the rational map is holomorphic on all of $S$.

<a id="pm-h-7"></a>
#### The formal Cauchy problem

We specify the analytic formal neighborhood in which the first stage takes place. Let $\mathcal I\subset\mathcal O_S$ be the analytic ideal sheaf of $\Delta$, and let $\iota:\Delta\hookrightarrow S$ be the inclusion. On the topological space $\Delta$, set $$\widehat{\mathcal O}_{S,\Delta}
 =\varprojlim_{n\ge0}\iota^{-1}(\mathcal O_S/\mathcal I^{n+1}),
\qquad
\widehat{\mathcal I}=\ker(\widehat{\mathcal O}_{S,\Delta}\longrightarrow\mathcal O_\Delta).$$ Thus the analytic completion is the ringed space $\widehat S_\Delta=(\Delta,\widehat{\mathcal O}_{S,\Delta})$. On a diagonal coordinate disk with coordinates $u=s$, $v=t-s$, its structure sheaf is $\mathcal O_\Delta[[v]]$, and $\widehat{\mathcal I}=(v)$. The coefficients are holomorphic along the disk; convergence in the normal variable $v$ is not required.

A formal holomorphic map $\widehat G:\widehat S_\Delta\to Y$ to a complex manifold $Y$ consists of a holomorphic diagonal map $g_0:\Delta\to Y$ and a morphism of sheaves of $\mathbb C$-algebras $$\widehat G^\#:g_0^{-1}\mathcal O_Y\longrightarrow\widehat{\mathcal O}_{S,\Delta}$$ reducing to $g_0^\#$ modulo $\widehat{\mathcal I}$. We require this map to be given locally by holomorphic Taylor substitution, as follows. On a source disk with the above $\mathcal O_\Delta[[v]]$ description, view functions of $u$ as constant series in $v$. In target coordinates $z=(z^1,\ldots,z^q)$, put $f=(\widehat G^\#z^1,\ldots,\widehat G^\#z^q)$ and $\eta=f-z\circ g_0\in\widehat{\mathcal I}^{\oplus q}$. For a holomorphic function $h$ on the target chart, substitution at order $n$ is $$\widehat G^\#h\bmod\widehat{\mathcal I}^{n+1}
 =\sum_{|\alpha|\le n}
   \frac{(\partial^\alpha h)(z\circ g_0)}{\alpha!}\,
   \eta^\alpha\bmod\widehat{\mathcal I}^{n+1}.$$ Every monomial in $\eta$ of total degree $n+1$ is zero at this order, so the sum is finite. These substitutions are compatible as $n$ varies and preserve products and holomorphic coordinate changes. Consequently, one can specify a formal map by coordinate series with the stated constant terms, provided that on overlaps they agree by these substitutions. The same rule then determines the pullback of every holomorphic function, not only of the coordinate functions.

A source holomorphic vector field $D$ satisfies $D(\mathcal I^{k+1})\subset\mathcal I^k$ for $k\ge0$, and hence acts as a continuous derivation on the completion. Locally, $\partial_u$ differentiates the coefficients of a series and $\partial_v$ differentiates its powers of $v$. A derivation can lower the normal order, so it need not define a derivation on any one fixed-order thickening. Formal differential identities below are equalities in the completion. In target coordinates they are equalities of vectors of formal series; the formal chain rule makes them independent of the target coordinates.

<a id="stmt-lem-formal"></a>
<div id="lem:formal" class="lemma">

**Lemma 4**. *There is a unique formal holomorphic map $\widehat F$ along $\Delta$ with diagonal value $g$ and the two tangency properties in <a href="#eq:box" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:box">[eq:box]</a>, interpreted formally.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-formal"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-formal</summary>

<a id="eq:cauchy"></a>
*Proof.* Use the same coordinate on a source chart in each factor, denoting the two coordinates by $s,t$. Set $$u=s,\qquad v=t-s.$$ After restricting the diagonal disk so that $g$ takes values in one target coordinate chart, impose $$\label{eq:cauchy}
\widehat F(u,0)=g(u),\qquad
\partial_v\widehat F=P_2(\widehat F)\partial_u\widehat F.$$ Here the entries of the holomorphic matrix $P_2$ are substituted by the Taylor rule above. That rule preserves $P_2^2=P_2$ and $P_1+P_2=\operatorname{id}$.

Because $\partial_u=\partial_s+\partial_t$ and $\partial_v=\partial_t$, <a href="#eq:cauchy" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:cauchy">[eq:cauchy]</a> is equivalent to $$P_1(\widehat F)\partial_t\widehat F=0,\qquad
P_2(\widehat F)\partial_s\widehat F=0.$$ Indeed, the equation puts $\partial_v\widehat F$ in the image of $P_2(\widehat F)$, while applying $P_2(\widehat F)$ to $\partial_u\widehat F-\partial_v\widehat F$ gives zero. Conversely, these two statements recover <a href="#eq:cauchy" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:cauchy">[eq:cauchy]</a>. They are exactly the two formal tangency conditions.

Write $\widehat F=\sum_{k\ge0}f_k(u)v^k$, with $f_0=g$ in the chosen coordinates. At order zero, <a href="#eq:cauchy" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:cauchy">[eq:cauchy]</a> gives $$f_1=P_2(g)g'.$$ Thus along the diagonal the second-factor derivative is $P_2(g)g'$, and the first-factor derivative is its complement $g'-f_1=P_1(g)g'$. The higher coefficients extend this forced first-order decomposition. The coefficient of $v^k$ on the right side of <a href="#eq:cauchy" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:cauchy">[eq:cauchy]</a> involves only $f_0,\ldots,f_k$ and their $u$-derivatives. It determines $(k+1)f_{k+1}$, so induction gives a unique solution whose coefficients are holomorphic on the diagonal disk.

We verify that these local solutions define a formal map globally. Under a target-coordinate change $z'=\psi(z)$, Taylor substitution gives $\widehat F'=\psi(\widehat F)$, and the formal chain rule gives $D\widehat F'=d\psi(\widehat F)D\widehat F$. The matrix of $P_2$ changes by the corresponding conjugation, so <a href="#eq:cauchy" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:cauchy">[eq:cauchy]</a> is preserved. For a source change $s'=h(s)$, $t'=h(t)$, one has $$u'=h(u),\qquad v'=h(u+v)-h(u).$$ The new normal parameter is $v$ times a unit, so this substitution identifies the two completions. With $$a=\frac1{h'(u)},\qquad b=\frac1{h'(u+v)},$$ the continuous source derivations transform as $$\partial_{v'}=b\partial_v,\qquad
\partial_{u'}=a\partial_u+(b-a)\partial_v.$$ Since $P_2(\widehat F)\partial_v\widehat F=\partial_v\widehat F$, these formulas imply $$P_2(\widehat F)\partial_{u'}\widehat F
 =b\partial_v\widehat F=\partial_{v'}\widehat F.$$ The initial value also transforms to the same diagonal map. Local uniqueness therefore identifies the coordinate series on every overlap. Their Taylor substitution maps agree as well, since those maps are determined by the coordinate series. This proves both global existence and uniqueness. ◻

</details>

#### Rational reconstruction along the diagonal

We next recover an ordinary rational map from the formal one. The positive normal bundle of $\Delta$ bounds the number of formal sections after twisting; comparison with pairs of polynomial sections then forces each formal coordinate ratio to be rational. The classical study of algebraic formal rational functions provides background for this mechanism; see Hironaka–Matsumura <a id="pm-cite-11"></a>[[HM68](#ref-HM68)] and Bădescu <a id="pm-cite-12"></a>[[Bad96](#ref-Bad96)]. The proof below works directly in the analytic completion just defined, including the passage to an ordinary holomorphic map near the diagonal.

We use the notation $\mathcal O_S(m,m)=\operatorname{pr}_1^*\mathcal O_C(m)\otimes\operatorname{pr}_2^*\mathcal O_C(m)$.

<a id="stmt-lem-rational"></a>
<div id="lem:rational" class="lemma">

**Lemma 5**. *Let $Y\subset\mathbb P^N$ be a closed complex submanifold and let $\widehat G$ be a formal holomorphic map from the formal neighborhood of $\Delta\subset S$ to $Y$. There is a rational map $R:S\dashrightarrow\mathbb P^N$ that is holomorphic on a neighborhood of a nonempty open subset of $\Delta$, where its formal expansion agrees with $\widehat G$. On that neighborhood it takes values in $Y$.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-rational"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-rational</summary>

*Proof.* Write $g_0=\widehat G|_\Delta$. We first construct the formal pullback of $\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^N}(1)$. Choose a cover of $g_0(\Delta)$ by affine projective charts $U_a$ with holomorphic frames $e_a$ of this line bundle, and write $e_b=t_{ab}e_a$ on overlaps. Restricting $t_{ab}$ to $Y$ and applying $\widehat G^\#$ gives formal functions $\widehat t_{ab}$ on $g_0^{-1}(U_a\cap U_b)$. Their constant terms are $t_{ab}\circ g_0$, which are nowhere zero. Thus each $\widehat t_{ab}$ is a unit in the completion. The substitutions preserve the transition cocycle identities, so the relations $\widehat e_b=\widehat t_{ab}\widehat e_a$ glue free rank-one modules into a locally free $\widehat{\mathcal O}_{S,\Delta}$-module $L$.

If the homogeneous coordinate section $x_\nu$ is $c_{\nu,a}e_a$ on $U_a$, evaluate $c_{\nu,a}|_Y$ by the same substitution. The resulting local sections $\widehat G^\#(c_{\nu,a}|_Y)\widehat e_a$ agree on overlaps, giving global sections $S_0,\ldots,S_N$ of this very module $L$. Its reduction $L_0=L/\widehat{\mathcal I}L$ is the ordinary holomorphic line bundle $g_0^*\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^N}(1)$ on $\Delta\simeq C$. The reductions of the coordinate sections have no common zero, so after reordering, $S_0|_\Delta$ is not identically zero. Its zero divisor has some degree $d\ge0$ and identifies $$L_0\simeq\mathcal O_C(d).$$ For example, a degree-$d$ homogeneous polynomial with the same zero divisor gives this identification directly.

<a id="eq:graded"></a>
For a fixed $m\ge0$, let $\widehat{\mathcal O}_S(m,m)$ denote the restriction of the ordinary line bundle to the completion, and put $$\mathcal M_m=L\otimes_{\widehat{\mathcal O}_{S,\Delta}}\widehat{\mathcal O}_S(m,m),
\qquad V=H^0(\Delta,\mathcal M_m),$$ $$V_k=H^0(\Delta,\widehat{\mathcal I}^{\,k}\mathcal M_m)\subset V
\quad(k\ge0).$$ Thus $V_0=V$, and $V_k$ consists precisely of global formal sections vanishing to normal order at least $k$. Passing a section to its class modulo $\widehat{\mathcal I}^{k+1}\mathcal M_m$ has kernel $V_{k+1}$. It gives the leading-coefficient injection $$\label{eq:graded}
V_k/V_{k+1}\hookrightarrow
H^0\bigl(C,\mathcal O_C(d+2m-2k)\bigr).$$ Indeed, the leading-coefficient sheaf is $$\frac{\widehat{\mathcal I}^{\,k}\mathcal M_m}
     {\widehat{\mathcal I}^{\,k+1}\mathcal M_m}
\simeq L_0\otimes\mathcal O_S(m,m)|_\Delta
             \otimes\iota^{-1}(\mathcal I^k/\mathcal I^{k+1}).$$ Only the constant terms of the frame transitions act on this quotient. Since $\Delta$ has bidegree $(1,1)$, its conormal bundle $\mathcal I/\mathcal I^2$ is $\mathcal O_C(-2)$; the displayed sheaf is therefore $\mathcal O_C(d+2m-2k)$, as asserted.

<a id="eq:sectionbound"></a>
This filtration is separated. In a formal frame a section is a series in $v$, so membership in every $V_k$ makes every coefficient zero. For all sufficiently large $k$, the space on the right side of <a href="#eq:graded" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:graded">[eq:graded]</a> is zero. Hence, for some $K$, $V_k=V_{k+1}$ for every $k\ge K$, and $V_K=\bigcap_{k\ge K}V_k=0$ by separatedness. The finite remaining filtration gives $$\label{eq:sectionbound}
\dim_\mathbb CV
\le\sum_{k\ge0}\max\{d+2m-2k+1,0\}
=m^2+O(m+1).$$ Here $d$ is fixed. More explicitly, the sum is $(m+q+1)^2$ for $d=2q$ and $(m+q+1)(m+q+2)$ for $d=2q+1$.

Restrict ordinary polynomial sections of $\mathcal O_S(m,m)$ to the completion. For each $\nu=1,\ldots,N$, consider $$H^0(S,\mathcal O_S(m,m))^{\oplus2}\longrightarrow V,\qquad
(A,B)\longmapsto AS_0+BS_\nu.$$ Its source has dimension $2(m+1)^2$. Choose $m$ large enough that this exceeds the bound in <a href="#eq:sectionbound" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:sectionbound">[eq:sectionbound]</a>, and choose a nonzero kernel pair $(A_\nu,B_\nu)$ for each $\nu$.

Fix a connected diagonal coordinate disk $D$ on which $S_0|_\Delta$ is nowhere zero, contained in one product affine chart. The section $S_0$ is then a formal frame for $L$ along $D$, and $S_\nu/S_0$ is the substituted affine coordinate $x_\nu/x_0$. Necessarily $B_\nu\ne0$: otherwise $A_\nu S_0=0$ would say that the ordinary section $A_\nu$ has zero formal expansion along $D$. Its convergent local Taylor series would make it zero on a neighborhood of $D$, and the identity principle on connected $S$ would give $A_\nu=0$, a contradiction. The ratios $$q_\nu=-A_\nu/B_\nu\in\mathbb C(S)$$ therefore define a rational map $R=[1:q_1:\cdots:q_N]$.

It remains to show that these ratios are holomorphic simultaneously near one point of $D$. Use $u=s$, $v=t-s$ and an ordinary frame for $\mathcal O_S(m,m)$ on the chosen product chart, and represent $A_\nu,B_\nu$ by holomorphic functions $a_\nu(u,v)$, $b_\nu(u,v)$. Write their normal Taylor coefficients on $D$ as $a_{\nu,k}(u)$, $b_{\nu,k}(u)$. For each $\nu$, the generic normal order $$r_\nu=\min\{k\ge0:b_{\nu,k}\text{ is not identically zero on }D\}$$ is finite. Otherwise Taylor uniqueness near $D$, followed by the identity principle on $S$, would make the nonzero ordinary section $B_\nu$ vanish identically. Each leading coefficient $b_{\nu,r_\nu}$ is a nonzero holomorphic function on the connected disk $D$. Its zeros are discrete, so a single point $p\in D$ can be chosen where all the finitely many leading coefficients are nonzero.

In the formal frame $S_0$, the kernel relations become $$a_\nu+b_\nu(S_\nu/S_0)=0
\quad\text{in }\mathcal O_D[[v]].$$ By the definition of $r_\nu$, all coefficients of $b_\nu$ below that order vanish on $D$; the relation makes the same true of $a_\nu$. Since $a_\nu$ and $b_\nu$ are ordinary holomorphic functions, this formal vanishing implies actual holomorphic divisibility near $p$: $$a_\nu=v^{r_\nu}\widetilde a_\nu,\qquad
b_\nu=v^{r_\nu}\widetilde b_\nu,
\qquad
\widetilde b_\nu(p)=b_{\nu,r_\nu}(p)\ne0.$$ Indeed, their convergent Taylor expansions in $v$ have the first $r_\nu$ coefficients zero. Shrink to one neighborhood of $p$ where every $\widetilde b_\nu$ is a holomorphic unit. Multiplication by $v$ is injective on formal series, so the same power can be canceled in each formal relation. It follows that $$q_\nu=-\widetilde a_\nu/\widetilde b_\nu$$ is holomorphic there and has formal expansion $S_\nu/S_0$. Thus $R$ is holomorphic on this common neighborhood. Its affine coordinate series agree with those of the composition of $\widehat G$ with $Y\hookrightarrow\mathbb P^N$. The Taylor substitution rule then makes these formal maps agree on every holomorphic function on the projective target chart.

Finally, $R(p)=g_0(p)$, so shrink the source neighborhood until its image lies in a target chart with holomorphic defining equations for $Y$. Their compositions with $R$ have zero normal Taylor series, because their substitutions by $\widehat G$ are zero. The convergent Taylor series of these ordinary holomorphic functions make them vanish on a neighborhood of $p$. There $R$ takes values in $Y$. In local submanifold coordinates, every holomorphic function on $Y$ extends to the ambient chart. The ambient formal equality therefore also gives equality of the formal maps to $Y$, as required. ◻

</details>

<div id="rem:g3" class="remark">

*Remark 6*. The normal-bundle calculation in <a href="#eq:sectionbound" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:sectionbound">[eq:sectionbound]</a> is the rationality mechanism in the proof. The related algebraic language uses the $G_3$ property: the restriction map from rational functions on the ambient variety to formal rational functions along the subvariety is an isomorphism. The proof above does not invoke a $G_3$ theorem. Its section count and cancellation work directly with the given holomorphic formal series, without an analytic-to-algebraic comparison.

</div>

<a id="pm-h-9"></a>
#### Complementary tangency removes indeterminacy

Rational reconstruction has produced a map only away from its base points. We next use the specified splitting to show that no base point can remain: each exceptional curve would have to map tangentially to both complementary summands, forcing its image to be a point.

<a id="stmt-lem-removal"></a>
<div id="lem:removal" class="lemma">

**Lemma 7**. *Let $R:S\dashrightarrow X$ be a rational map such that, on its domain of definition, $$dR(T^{(1)})\subset R^*E_1,\qquad
dR(T^{(2)})\subset R^*E_2.$$ Then $R$ extends to a holomorphic map $S\to X$.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-removal"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-removal</summary>

*Proof.* Choose a projective embedding $X\subset\mathbb P^N$. By <a href="#lem:surface" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:surface">11</a>, there are a finite set $B\subset S$, a composition of point blowups $\mu:S'\to S$ over $B$, and a holomorphic map $F':S'\to\mathbb P^N$ extending $R\circ\mu$. Since $X$ is closed and $F'$ maps a dense open set into $X$, the entire image lies in $X$.

<a id="eq:wedges"></a>
We show that $F'$ is constant on each exceptional component. Let $H$ be such a component, lying over $p\in B$, and let $s,t$ be coordinates on the two source factors near $p$. On the inverse image of this coordinate neighborhood, $P_i dF'$ is a holomorphic one-form with values in $F'^*T_X$. The tangency assumptions imply the identities $$\label{eq:wedges}
(P_1dF')\wedge d(s\circ\mu)=0,\qquad
(P_2dF')\wedge d(t\circ\mu)=0.$$ The two wedge expressions are sections of the locally free sheaf $\Omega^2_{S'}\otimes F'^*T_X$. The identities first hold off the exceptional curves, where $\mu$ is an isomorphism, and then hold everywhere by holomorphicity.

Both coordinate functions $s\circ\mu-s(p)$ and $t\circ\mu-t(p)$ vanish along $H$, since $\mu(H)=\{p\}$. Their generic orders along $H$ are positive and finite. Indeed, in a normal coordinate along a connected disk of $H$, the first normal Taylor coefficient that is not identically zero gives that order. If no such coefficient existed, the pullback would vanish on an open neighborhood meeting the dense locus where $\mu$ is an isomorphism, which is impossible for a coordinate function. Denote the two respective orders by $\alpha,\beta$. This reasoning applies also to components created by later blowups. At a general point of $H$, avoiding intersections with other components and zeros of the two leading coefficients, choose a local equation $r=0$. We can then write $$s\circ\mu-s(p)=r^\alpha h,\qquad
t\circ\mu-t(p)=r^\beta\widetilde h$$ with holomorphic units $h,\widetilde h$.

The first identity in <a href="#eq:wedges" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:wedges">[eq:wedges]</a> is therefore $$r^{\alpha-1}(P_1dF')\wedge(\alpha h\,dr+r\,dh)=0.$$ The holomorphic local ring of the smooth surface is a domain, so multiplication by $r^{\alpha-1}$ is injective on the locally free module of bundle-valued two-forms. It can therefore be canceled. At $r=0$, evaluate the resulting ambient two-form on a vector $w$ tangent to $H$ and a vector $n$ with $dr(n)=1$. The result is $\alpha h\,P_1dF'(w)=0$, hence $P_1dF'(w)=0$. Canceling $r^{\beta-1}$ in the second identity gives $P_2dF'(w)=0$ in the same way. Since $P_1+P_2=\operatorname{id}$, the derivative of $F'|_H$ vanishes on a dense open subset of $H$, and hence everywhere. Since $H$ is connected, $F'|_H$ is constant.

The fibers of $\mu$ away from $B$ are points. Each nontrivial fiber over $B$ is a connected union of exceptional components: the first blowup creates an exceptional line, and every later blowup over that point replaces its center by a new line meeting the strict transforms of the components through it, as in <a href="#lem:surface" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:surface">11</a>. The constants on intersecting components agree, so $F'$ is constant on every fiber.

The map $\mu$ is a continuous surjection from a compact space to a Hausdorff space. It is therefore a quotient map, and $F'$ descends to a continuous map $F:S\to X$, holomorphic off $B$. Near each point of $B$, continuity places the image in a single target coordinate chart. The coordinate functions are continuous and holomorphic away from the isolated point. One-variable removability on coordinate lines makes them separately holomorphic, and the iterated Cauchy formula makes them holomorphic. Hence $F$ is holomorphic everywhere. ◻

</details>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-removal-2"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-removal</summary>

*Proof of <a href="#prop:box" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:box">3</a>.* Apply <a href="#lem:formal" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:formal">4</a> and then <a href="#lem:rational" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:rational">5</a> to a projective embedding of $X$. The resulting rational map is holomorphic near a nonempty diagonal patch and has formal expansion $\widehat F$ there. Continuous differentiation and Taylor substitution identify the expansions of $P_1(R)\partial_tR$ and $P_2(R)\partial_sR$ with the zero formal tangency expressions of $\widehat F$. These are ordinary holomorphic expressions, so Taylor uniqueness makes them zero on a neighborhood of that patch. Let $B$ be the finite base set from <a href="#lem:surface" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:surface">11</a>. The complement $S\setminus B$ is connected; one can perturb any path in the connected real four-manifold $S$ inside coordinate balls to avoid the finitely many points. The inverse image of $X$ under the holomorphic map $R:S\setminus B\to\mathbb P^N$ is an analytic subset containing a nonempty open set, so it is all of $S\setminus B$. The two tangency conditions are then vanishing identities for holomorphic bundle morphisms on this connected complement, and continue there by the identity principle.

Now <a href="#lem:removal" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:removal">7</a> gives a holomorphic extension $F:S\to X$. Tangency extends by holomorphicity. The maps $F|_\Delta$ and $g$ agree on the original nonempty diagonal patch, so they agree on all of $\Delta$. ◻

</details>

<a id="sec:transport"></a>
### Bundles on a tangent box

A tangent box separates the two bundles as well as the two tangent directions. We show that each pulled-back summand comes from its corresponding factor. Transport from the diagonal gives a natural normalization of this identification at the point where the bracket will be tested.

<a id="eq:partial"></a>
For $i\ne j$, the splitting defines a projected-bracket partial connection on $E_i$ in directions of $E_j$: $$\label{eq:partial}
\nabla^i_\xi e=P_i[\xi,e],$$ where $\xi$ and $e$ are local holomorphic sections of $E_j$ and $E_i$, respectively.

<a id="stmt-prop-transport"></a>
<div id="prop:transport" class="proposition">

**Proposition 8**. *Let $F:S\to X$ be a tangent box of $g:C\to X$. Transport along the fibers of $\operatorname{pr}_i$ gives holomorphic bundle isomorphisms <a id="eq:transport"></a>$$\label{eq:transport}
\Theta_i:\operatorname{pr}_i^*(g^*E_i)\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }F^*E_i,
\qquad i=1,2,$$ whose restrictions to the diagonal are the identity.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-prop-transport"><summary>Proof of stmt-prop-transport</summary>

*Proof.* The bracket rules and $P_i\xi=0$ give $$\nabla^i_{f\xi}e=f\nabla^i_\xi e,\qquad
\nabla^i_\xi(fe)=\xi(f)e+f\nabla^i_\xi e.$$ Thus <a href="#eq:partial" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:partial">[eq:partial]</a> is tensorial in its $E_j$ direction and obeys the connection Leibniz rule in its $E_i$ argument. These two rules use no bracket of two $E_j$ sections and require no integrability of either summand.

Fix $i$ and let $j\ne i$. The tangency of $F$ sends the vertical line bundle $\ker(d\operatorname{pr}_i)=T^{(j)}$ into $F^*E_j$. The partial connection therefore pulls back to a holomorphic connection on $F^*E_i$ relative to $\operatorname{pr}_i$. We spell out this construction because $F$ may have critical points. Take local frames $e_\lambda$ of $E_i$ and $\xi_\gamma$ of $E_j$, and write $$\nabla^i_{\xi_\gamma}e_\lambda
 =\sum_\nu\Gamma_{\gamma\lambda}^{\nu}e_\nu,\qquad
dF(V)=\sum_\gamma h_\gamma F^*\xi_\gamma$$ for a local holomorphic vertical vector field $V$ on $S$. Define $$D_V\!\left(\sum_\lambda f_\lambda F^*e_\lambda\right)
=\sum_\lambda V(f_\lambda)F^*e_\lambda
 +\sum_{\gamma,\lambda,\nu}
 f_\lambda h_\gamma
 (\Gamma_{\gamma\lambda}^{\nu}\circ F)F^*e_\nu.$$ The two connection rules and the chain rule $V(A\circ F)=dA(dF(V))$ give the usual connection transformation under a change of the $E_i$ frame; tensoriality in the first argument handles a change of the $E_j$ frame. Hence these formulas agree on overlapping product neighborhoods. They define a holomorphic relative connection $$D:\mathcal O(F^*E_i)\longrightarrow
\mathcal O(F^*E_i\otimes\Omega^1_{S/C}),$$ where the relative differential is for $\operatorname{pr}_i:S\to C$. At a point where $dF(V)=0$, all $h_\gamma$ vanish there and the formula still applies; no inverse of $dF$ is used.

The restriction of $D$ to any fiber $C=\mathbb P^1$ is a holomorphic connection. Its curvature is a holomorphic two-form on that curve and therefore vanishes. Horizontal sections satisfy an everywhere nonsingular holomorphic linear ordinary differential equation. Local existence and uniqueness give continuation along every path, with reverse transport as its inverse. Flatness and simple connectedness of $\mathbb P^1$ make this transport path-independent. Starting at the fiber’s diagonal point therefore identifies its bundle with the corresponding fiber of $g^*E_i$.

These identifications depend holomorphically on both variables. Fix a base point and a path in its fiber from the diagonal to a desired endpoint. Cover the path by a finite chain of coordinate disks and bundle frames, and shrink the base neighborhood so that the same chain applies to nearby fibers. In each disk the coefficient matrix of the horizontal equation is holomorphic in the base and fiber coordinates. Picard iteration converges normally on smaller disks by the exponential-series estimate, so its solution matrix is holomorphic in the base and the start and end coordinates. Composing these local transports and frame changes gives a holomorphic identification near the chosen point of $S$. Path independence makes the local identifications agree globally. Transport at the starting diagonal point is the identity, giving $\Theta_i$ and its stated normalization. ◻

</details>

<a id="sec:vanishing"></a>
### Vanishing of the bracket tensors

We now test the bracket obstruction on sections supplied by deformations of a rational curve. The tangent box makes the complementary target bundle trivial along the curve where the test is performed. This use of rational curves to force bracket vanishing has an antecedent in <a id="pm-cite-13"></a>[[Hor07](#ref-Hor07), Section 2.B and Lemma 3.18].

Return to the hypotheses of <a href="#thm:main" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:main">1</a>. For $i\ne j$, define $$\mathcal B_i:\bigwedge\nolimits^2E_i\longrightarrow E_j,\qquad
\mathcal B_i(e,e')=P_j[e,e'].$$ The extra terms in the bracket of scalar multiples lie in $E_i$, so $P_j$ kills them. Thus $\mathcal B_i$ is a holomorphic bundle morphism, and $E_i$ is integrable exactly when $\mathcal B_i=0$.

The following consequence of rational connectedness gives the needed sections. Its direct parameter-space proof is in Appendix <a href="#app:variation" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="app:variation">7</a>.

<a id="stmt-lem-variation"></a>
<div id="lem:variation" class="lemma">

**Lemma 9**. *Fix distinct $a,b\in C$. There are a nonempty complex manifold $T$ and a holomorphic map $G:C\times T\to X$ such that $$(\operatorname{ev}_a,\operatorname{ev}_b):T\longrightarrow X\times X,\qquad
\tau\longmapsto (G(a,\tau),G(b,\tau))$$ is a submersion. In particular, for $g=G(\,\cdot\,,\tau)$, the global sections of $g^*E_i$ vanishing at $a$ have values spanning $E_i$ at $g(b)$.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-variation"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-variation</summary>

*Proof of <a href="#thm:main" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:main">1</a>.* Choose the family in <a href="#lem:variation" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:variation">9</a>. For each member $g$, take a tangent box $F:S\to X$ from <a href="#prop:box" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:box">3</a>; the boxes need not be chosen holomorphically as the member varies. For $j\ne i$, consider $$L_i=\{(s_1,s_2)\in S:s_j=b\}.$$ The free coordinate identifies $L_i$ with $C$. As shown in <a href="#fig:box" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="fig:box">1</a>, its marked endpoint $(b,b)$ lies on the diagonal. By <a href="#prop:transport" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:transport">8</a>, $\Theta_i$ identifies $F^*E_i|_{L_i}$ with $g^*E_i$, while $\Theta_j$ identifies $F^*E_j|_{L_i}$ with the trivial bundle having fiber $E_{j,g(b)}$.

Let $u,v$ be any two global sections of $g^*E_i$ vanishing at $a$. Restricting the transport maps to $L_i$, the tensor value $$\sigma_{u,v}
=\Theta_j^{-1}\!\left((F^*\mathcal B_i)(\Theta_i u\wedge\Theta_i v)\right)
\in H^0\bigl(C,\mathcal O_C\otimes E_{j,g(b)}\bigr)$$ is a holomorphic section of a trivial bundle on $\mathbb P^1$, hence is constant. It vanishes at the point with free coordinate $a$, so it also vanishes at the point with free coordinate $b$. At this latter point, $F(b,b)=g(b)$ and both transport maps are the identity. Consequently $$\mathcal B_i(g(b))\bigl(u(b)\wedge v(b)\bigr)=0.$$ By <a href="#lem:variation" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:variation">9</a>, the values $u(b)$ of sections vanishing at $a$ span $E_{i,g(b)}$. Their pairwise wedges span its exterior square, and therefore $\mathcal B_i(g(b))=0$.

This applies to both $i$ and every member of the family. The map $\operatorname{ev}_b$ is a submersion, being the composition of the two-point submersion with the second projection. Its image is a nonempty open subset of $X$, on which both bracket tensors vanish. The identity principle for holomorphic bundle morphisms on connected $X$ makes them vanish everywhere. This is precisely integrability of both specified summands. ◻

</details>

<a id="sec:consequences"></a>
### Consequences for the rationally connected quotient

The main theorem also applies to the general fiber of the rationally connected quotient of a uniruled compact Kähler manifold. That fiber is a smooth projective manifold even when the ambient manifold is not projective. Its tangent splitting is therefore integrable, which supplies the premise in Höring’s structure theorems <a id="pm-cite-14"></a>[[Hor08](#ref-Hor08)].

<a id="stmt-cor-rational-quotient"></a>
<div id="cor:rational-quotient" class="corollary">

**Corollary 10**. *Let $X$ be a connected uniruled compact Kähler manifold with a holomorphic splitting $T_X=V_1\oplus V_2$. Let $Z$ be a general fiber of its rationally connected quotient and set $W_i=T_Z\cap V_i|_Z$. For each $i=1,2$, there is an almost-holomorphic fibration $\phi_i:X\dashrightarrow Y_i$ whose general fiber $F_i$ is rationally connected and, for compatible choices $F_i\subset Z$, satisfies $$T_{F_i}=W_i|_{F_i}\subset V_i|_{F_i}.$$ If $W_i=0$, one may take $\phi_i=\operatorname{id}_X$, with point fibers.*

*If $\operatorname{rank}V_1=2$, exactly one of the following holds:*

1.  *$W_1=V_1|_Z$, and $X$ admits an analytic fiber bundle $X\to Y$ with rationally connected general fiber and $T_{X/Y}=V_1$.*

2.  *$W_1$ is a line bundle, and there is an equidimensional holomorphic map $X\to Y$ whose general fiber $F$ is a rational curve with $T_F\subset V_1|_F$.*

3.  *$W_1=0$, equivalently $T_Z\subset V_2|_Z$.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-cor-rational-quotient"><summary>Proof of stmt-cor-rational-quotient</summary>

*Proof.* Use the published version of <a id="pm-cite-15"></a>[[Hor08](#ref-Hor08)]; its definition of an almost-holomorphic fibration includes the identity case. Choose $Z$ over a regular value in the fibration locus of the quotient supplied by <a id="pm-cite-16"></a>[[Hor08](#ref-Hor08), Definition 2.9 and Theorem 2.10]. Then $Z$ is an actual smooth connected compact submanifold of $X$. It inherits a Kähler form and is rationally connected. Campana’s theorem, recalled in the remark after <a id="pm-cite-17"></a>[[Hor08](#ref-Hor08), Definition 2.8], makes $Z$ projective.

By <a id="pm-cite-18"></a>[[Hor08](#ref-Hor08), Proposition 3.16], the ambient complementary projections preserve $T_Z$. Their restrictions are complementary holomorphic idempotents. An idempotent has locally constant rank, so on connected $Z$ their images are the holomorphic subbundles $W_i$, and $$T_Z=W_1\oplus W_2.$$ If both ranks are positive, then $\dim Z\ge2$, and <a href="#thm:main" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:main">1</a> makes both $W_i$ integrable. Otherwise they are $0$ and $T_Z$, which are integrable as well.

This is the general-fiber integrability hypothesis of <a id="pm-cite-19"></a>[[Hor08](#ref-Hor08), Theorem 1.3], which gives the fibrations $\phi_i$ and the stated tangent identities. Its proof includes the identity when $W_i=0$. If $\operatorname{rank}V_1=2$, <a id="pm-cite-20"></a>[[Hor08](#ref-Hor08), Theorem 1.4] gives the three conclusions according as $\operatorname{rank}W_1$ is $2$, $1$, or $0$. These ranks are mutually exclusive and exhaustive, proving the stated trichotomy. ◻

</details>

<a id="app:surface"></a>
### Elementary elimination of surface base points

Elimination of indeterminacy by point blowups is a classical part of the birational theory of smooth surfaces; see <a id="pm-cite-21"></a>[[Bea96](#ref-Bea96)]. The next lemma supplies exactly the resolution statement used in <a href="#lem:removal" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:removal">7</a>. We include the argument to make explicit the finite termination of the point-blowup process, using the colength of the local base ideal as the decreasing positive integer.

<a id="stmt-lem-surface"></a>
<div id="lem:surface" class="lemma">

**Lemma 11**. *A rational map $R:\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1\dashrightarrow\mathbb P^N$ is holomorphic outside a finite set $B$. A finite sequence of point blowups over $B$ gives a smooth projective surface $\mu:S'\to\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1$ on which $R\circ\mu$ extends holomorphically. The fibers of $\mu$ are connected; its exceptional components are smooth curves.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-surface"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-surface</summary>

*Proof.* Clear denominators to represent $R$ by a tuple of bihomogeneous polynomials of the same bidegree, and remove all common factors. The factors can be taken bihomogeneous: the highest and lowest degrees in each variable group are additive in products.

In any product affine chart, the dehomogenized tuple $h_0(x,y),\ldots,h_N(x,y)$ has no common nonconstant factor. Otherwise bihomogenizing such a factor would give a common factor of the original tuple. In $\mathbb C(x)[y]$, the tuple therefore has gcd one. A univariate Bézout identity, with denominators cleared, puts a nonzero polynomial in $x$ in the ideal $(h_0,\ldots,h_N)$. Interchanging the variables gives a nonzero polynomial in $y$. The common zero set in the affine chart is finite. At each common zero, the ideal in centered formal coordinates contains powers of both coordinates and consequently has finite colength. There are four product affine charts, so the global base set $B$ is finite.

If $B=\varnothing$, the map is already holomorphic, and $S'=S$, $\mu=\operatorname{id}_S$ give the conclusion. Assume from now on that $B\ne\varnothing$.

It remains to resolve such a base point. Let $$D=\mathbb C[[x,y]],\qquad \mathfrak m=(x,y),$$ and let $I\subset D$ be the ideal of a representing holomorphic tuple $f_0,\ldots,f_N$ at the point. Assume $I$ is proper and has finite colength, and let $k=\min_\nu\operatorname{ord}(f_\nu)\ge1$. Blow up the point. In a chart $(x,z)\mapsto(x,xz)$, divide the pulled-back tuple by $x^k$; the divided functions are holomorphic. The other chart gives the same map, since the two tuples differ by a unit on their overlap.

On the exceptional projective line, the remaining base points are common zeros of the degree-$k$ initial forms. At least one such form is nonzero, so there are finitely many. Fix one. A linear change of the old coordinates lets us place it at $(x,z)=(0,0)$ in the displayed chart. Let $$J=(x^{-k}f_\nu(x,xz))_\nu\subset\mathbb C[[x,z]],
\qquad D'=\mathbb C[[x,z]]/J.$$ A power $x^\ell$ belongs to $I$; applying the substitution and division gives $x^{\ell-k}\in J$. Thus the class of $x$ in $D'$ is nilpotent. Modulo $x$, a nonzero degree-$k$ initial form gives a nonzero polynomial in $z$ of degree at most $k$. Its germ at the new base point is $z^q$ times a unit, with $1\le q\le k$. Therefore $D'/(x)$ is spanned by $1,z,\ldots,z^{k-1}$. Successively lifting modulo the powers of the nilpotent $x$ shows that $D'$ is spanned by finitely many classes $x^az^b$, with $0\le b<k$.

<a id="eq:transform"></a>
There is a well-defined $\mathbb C$-linear map $$\label{eq:transform}
\mathfrak m^k/I\longrightarrow D',\qquad
h\longmapsto x^{-k}h(x,xz).$$ Indeed a monomial $x^ay^b$ of total degree at least $k$ maps to $x^{a+b-k}z^b$. All exponents are nonnegative, and the resulting orders tend to infinity with the original orders, so the operation is defined on arbitrary formal series. If $h=\sum_\nu q_\nu f_\nu$ lies in $I$, its transform is $\sum_\nu q_\nu(x,xz)x^{-k}f_\nu(x,xz)\in J$. This proves descent to the quotient; no multiplicativity is needed.

<a id="eq:lengthdrop"></a>
Each spanning monomial $x^az^b$, $b<k$, is the image of $x^{a+k-b}y^b\in\mathfrak m^k$, so <a href="#eq:transform" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:transform">[eq:transform]</a> is surjective. It follows that $$\label{eq:lengthdrop}
\dim_\mathbb CD'
\le \dim_\mathbb C(\mathfrak m^k/I)
=\dim_\mathbb C(D/I)-\frac{k(k+1)}2
<\dim_\mathbb C(D/I).$$ In particular, the new base ideals have finite colength, and that positive integer decreases strictly along every surviving branch of the blowup process.

There are finitely many initial base points and finitely many children at each step. The maximum initial colength bounds the length of every branch by <a href="#eq:lengthdrop" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:lengthdrop">[eq:lengthdrop]</a>. Thus the entire tree is finite, and finitely many point blowups remove all base points. The local divided tuples define the desired holomorphic map.

Finally, a point blowup is proper, has a projective line over its center, and is an isomorphism elsewhere. The strict transform of a smooth curve remains smooth, and a new exceptional line meets the strict transforms of the components through the center. Induction gives smooth exceptional components and connected fibers of the composite. ◻

</details>

<a id="app:variation"></a>
### Two-point evaluation from rational connectedness

We prove <a href="#lem:variation" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:variation">9</a> using only polynomial presentations of maps from $\mathbb P^1$, analytic subsets, and the holomorphic constant-rank theorem. This isolates the precise two-point variation property used in the proof. For broader background on rationally connected varieties, see <a id="pm-cite-22"></a>[[KMM92](#ref-KMM92), [Kol99](#ref-Kol99)].

We first record an elementary covering observation. If $A$ is a proper closed analytic subset of a connected second-countable complex manifold $M$, then $A$ is covered by countably many complex submanifolds of dimension smaller than $\dim M$. The covering submanifolds need not be contained in $A$. To see this, choose a countable cover of $A$ by sufficiently small connected coordinate neighborhoods on which $A$ is defined by holomorphic functions. In each neighborhood choose a non-identically-zero defining function $f$. Such a function exists because a proper analytic subset has empty interior. At a zero of $f$, let $r\ge1$ be its finite order. Some derivative of order $r-1$ vanishes there and has nonzero differential. Thus that point belongs to the regular zero locus of one of the partial derivatives of $f$. There are countably many such derivatives and charts. Their regular zero loci, with connected components separated, give the asserted covering.

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-surface-2"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-surface</summary>

*Proof of <a href="#lem:variation" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:variation">9</a>.* Embed $X$ in $\mathbb P^N$. Every holomorphic map $C=\mathbb P^1\to\mathbb P^N$ is represented by a tuple of homogeneous polynomials of a degree $d\ge0$, with no common zero. Indeed its pullback of $\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^N}(1)$ is $\mathcal O_C(d)$, and its homogeneous coordinate sections supply such a tuple. For fixed $d$, these tuples form an open subset $M_d$ of a finite-dimensional vector space. Openness follows from compactness of $C$. There is a universal holomorphic evaluation map $C\times M_d\to\mathbb P^N$.

We cover the tuples whose maps land in $X$ by countably many manifold families all of whose maps land in $X$. Start with the connected components of $M_d$. For a current connected submanifold $M$, either every tuple maps $C$ into $X$, in which case accept this family, or there are $\tau\in M$ and $c\in C$ with $G(c,\tau)\notin X$. In the latter case $$\{\eta\in M:G(c,\eta)\in X\}$$ is a proper closed analytic subset of $M$, and contains every desired tuple in $M$. Use the covering observation to replace it by countably many lower-dimensional submanifolds, and repeat the same test on each. All desired tuples survive. Extra points introduced by the covering cause no problem: each new manifold is tested anew. Dimension strictly decreases whenever a family is not accepted. In dimension zero the test accepts precisely the desired points. Finite depth and countable branching give countably many accepted families covering all the desired tuples. On an accepted family the universal evaluation factors holomorphically through the embedded submanifold $X$.

Perform this construction for every $d$. The resulting countably many two-point evaluation maps to $X\times X$ have images containing general pairs: a rational curve through two distinct points can be reparametrized to take those values at the fixed distinct points $a,b$. By the convention in the introduction, these images contain a nonempty Zariski open subset of $X\times X$. It is also open in the usual topology and has positive measure in smooth local coordinates.

Suppose, for a contradiction, that every one of these evaluation maps has complex rank everywhere less than $2\dim X$. The image of each is then measure zero. Here is a direct verification of that fact for a holomorphic map $\phi:M\to X\times X$ with the stated rank bound. We may take $M$ connected. On the open set of maximum rank, the constant-rank theorem places the image locally in a submanifold of smaller dimension than $X\times X$. Countably many local charts give a measure-zero image. If the maximum rank is zero, this already treats all of $M$. Otherwise its complement is a proper closed analytic subset. Cover that subset by countably many lower-dimensional submanifolds and apply induction on $\dim M$. The strict rank bound persists on every such restriction, including the extra points of a covering submanifold. The zero-dimensional case is immediate. This proves the asserted measure-zero property.

A countable union of measure-zero images cannot contain that open subset. Some family therefore has a point where two-point evaluation is submersive. Restrict its parameter space to the nonempty open set where the differential is surjective; call the resulting manifold $T$.

Finally, a parameter tangent vector differentiates the holomorphic family to a global section of $g^*T_X$. Surjectivity of the two-point evaluation differential provides, for every $w\in T_{g(b)}X$, such a section with values $0,w$ at $a,b$. Projecting with $P_i$ gives the last assertion of <a href="#lem:variation" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:variation">9</a>. ◻
