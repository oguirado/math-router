## 1. Abstract and result contract

> Let <i>K</i> and <i>L</i> be finitely generated extensions of transcendence degree at least two over algebraically closed fields of characteristic <i>p</i>. We prove that every isomorphism of their first Milnor <i>K</i>-groups modulo <i>p</i> preserving the degree-two Steinberg relations is a nonzero scalar multiple of the map induced by a unique field isomorphism. The proof recovers projective lines over the subfields of <i>p</i>th powers by an elementary calculation with derivations.

- **C1** (theorem, [source](#stmt-thm-main)): Let $K/k$ and $L/l$ be finitely generated extensions of algebraically closed fields of characteristic $p$, with $$\mathop{\mathrm{trdeg}}(K/k)\ge2,\qquad \mathop{\mathrm{trdeg}}(L/l)\ge2.$$ The canonical map $$\mathop{\mathrm{Isom}}(K,k;L,l)\longrightarrow \mathop{\mathrm{Isom}}_{\mathrm M}(V_K,V_L)/\Lambda^\times, \qquad \alpha\longmapsto[\alpha_*],$$ is bijective.
  - Explanation: The stated field hypotheses identify compatible Milnor-group isomorphisms modulo nonzero prime-field scaling with unique base-preserving field isomorphisms.
- **C2** (theorem, [source](#stmt-lem-p-degree)): With the notation above, $[F:C]=p^d$.
  - Explanation: The field has finite dimension over its subfield of pth powers, determined by its transcendence degree.
- **C3** (theorem, [source](#stmt-lem-p-basis)): Let $F$ be any field of characteristic $p$, and put $C=F^p$. If $C\subseteq M\subseteq F$ and $s\in F\setminus M$, then $[M(s):M]=p$. If $[F:C]$ is finite, every $p$-independent list extends to a $p$-basis $s_1,\ldots,s_e$ of $F/C$, with $[F:C]=p^e$ and monomial basis $$\{s_1^{i_1}\cdots s_e^{i_e}:0\leq i_j<p\}.$$ For such a basis there are commuting $C$-linear derivations $D_1,\ldots,D_e$ on $F$ satisfying $$D_j(s_1^{i_1}\cdots s_e^{i_e}) =i_j s_1^{i_1}\cdots s_e^{i_e}.$$ On $C(s_j)$ the kernel of $D_j$ is $C$, and its nonzero eigenvectors with eigenvalue in $C$ are precisely the elements $c s_j^i$, where $c\in C^\times$ and $0\leq i<p$; their eigenvalues are $i\in\mathbf F_p$. Moreover, $C(s_j)\cap C(s_k)=C$ when $j\ne k$.
  - Explanation: Strict adjunctions over the pth-power subfield have prime degree; monomial bases produce commuting Euler derivations and identify their kernels, eigenvectors and subfield intersections.
- **C4** (theorem, [source](#stmt-lem-zero-symbol)): For $s,t\in F^\times$, one has $$s\notin C,\qquad \{s,t\}_F=0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad t\in C(s).$$
  - Explanation: A vanishing symbol constrains its second entry to the simple subfield generated by the first, provided the first entry is outside the pth-power subfield.
- **C5** (theorem, [source](#stmt-lem-affine-symbol)): If $c,d\in C^\times$ and $t,c+dt\in F^\times$, then $$\{t,c+dt\}_F=0.$$
  - Explanation: The Steinberg relation makes the symbol vanish for the indicated nonzero affine combination with nonzero pth-power coefficients.
- **C6** (theorem, [source](#stmt-prop-intersection)): Let $F$ be a field of characteristic $p$ with $[F:F^p]<\infty$, and put $C=F^p$. Let $X,Y\in F^\times$ be $p$-independent. Suppose $$U\in C(X)^\times,\qquad V\in C(Y)^\times, \qquad [U]\notin\mathbf F_p[X],\qquad [V]\notin\mathbf F_p[Y].$$ If $$\label{eq:intersection-witness} X\,C(Y/X)^\times\ \cap\ U\,C(V/U)^\times\ne\varnothing,$$ then there is a unique integer $n$, $1\le n<p$, such that $$\label{eq:intersection-conclusion} U^n\in C+CX^n,\qquad V^n\in C+CY^n.$$ In fact, either inclusion in <a href="#eq:intersection-conclusion" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:intersection-conclusion">[eq:intersection-conclusion]</a> determines $n$ uniquely. In each inclusion, both coefficients are nonzero.
  - Explanation: The indicated intersection and nonmonomial hypotheses determine a unique common exponent taking both elements onto their corresponding projective lines.
- **C7** (theorem, [source](#stmt-lem-scalar-line)): Let $F$ have characteristic $p$, with $F\ne F^p$. If multiplication by $r\in\mathbf F_p^\times$ on $V_F$ maps every projective line over $F^p$ into a projective line, then $r=1$.
  - Explanation: Only the identity prime-field scalar preserves all projective lines over the pth-power subfield of an imperfect field.
- **C8** (theorem, [source](#stmt-prop-lines)): Let $K/k$ and $L/l$ be finitely generated extensions of algebraically closed fields of characteristic $p$, both of transcendence degree at least two. For every compatible isomorphism $\Theta:V_K\to V_L$, there is a unique $n\in\mathbf F_p^\times$ such that $\Psi=n\Theta$ sends every projective line over $K^p$ onto a projective line over $L^p$.
  - Explanation: A compatible isomorphism has one unique scalar correction that maps projective lines onto projective lines.
- **C9** (theorem, [source](#stmt-prop-projective-lift)): Let $E$ and $E'$ be finite-dimensional vector spaces over fields $H$ and $C$, respectively, with dimensions at least three. Every bijection $\psi:\mathbf P_H(E)\to\mathbf P_C(E')$ that maps lines onto lines is induced by an additive bijection $S:E\to E'$ satisfying $$S(av)=\sigma(a)S(v)\qquad(a\in H,\ v\in E)$$ for a field isomorphism $\sigma:H\to C$. The map $\sigma$ is unique, and $S$ is unique up to multiplication by an element of $C^\times$.
  - Explanation: A line-preserving projective bijection lifts to a semilinear additive bijection, with unique coefficient-field isomorphism and scalar ambiguity in the lift.
- **C10** (theorem, [source](#stmt-lem-perfect-core)): If $F/\kappa$ is a finitely generated extension of an algebraically closed field of characteristic $p$, then $$\bigcap_{m\ge1} F^{p^m}=\kappa.$$
  - Explanation: The intersection of all iterated pth-power subfields recovers the algebraically closed base under finite generation.

## 2. Definitions, validity and bounds

- **prime field Lambda** ([source](#sec:introduction)): Fix a prime $p$ and write $\Lambda=\mathbf F_p$.
- **V_F** ([source](#sec:introduction)): For a field $F$ of characteristic $p$, set $$V_F=F^\times/(F^\times)^p=\mathrm K^{\mathrm M}_1(F)/p.$$
- **additive notation on V_F** ([source](#sec:introduction)): We write $[f]$ for the class of $f\in F^\times$ and use additive notation in $V_F$: thus $[f]+[g]=[fg]$, $r[f]=[f^r]$ for $r\in\mathbf Z$, and $[1]=0$.
- **R_F** ([source](#sec:introduction)): Define the Steinberg relation subspace $$R_F=\mathop{\mathrm{Span}}_{\Lambda} \{[f]\otimes[1-f]:f\in F\setminus\{0,1\}\} \subset V_F\otimes_{\Lambda}V_F.$$
- **W_F** ([source](#sec:introduction)): The quotient $W_F=(V_F\otimes_{\Lambda}V_F)/R_F$ is $\mathrm K^{\mathrm M}_2(F)/p$ by the tensor presentation of Milnor $K$-theory (Milnor 1970, sec. 1) [[Milnor1970](#ref-Milnor1970)].
- **Milnor symbol** ([source](#sec:introduction)): We denote the image of $[f]\otimes[g]$ in $W_F$ by $\{f,g\}_F$.
- **compatible isomorphism** ([source](#sec:introduction)): Let $\mathop{\mathrm{Isom}}_{\mathrm M}(V_K,V_L)$ be the set of $\Lambda$-linear isomorphisms $\Theta:V_K\to V_L$ satisfying $$(\Theta\otimes\Theta)(R_K)=R_L.$$
- **base-preserving field isomorphisms** ([source](#sec:introduction)): For specified subfields $k\subset K$ and $l\subset L$, write $\mathop{\mathrm{Isom}}(K,k;L,l)$ for the field isomorphisms $\alpha:K\to L$ with $\alpha(k)=l$.
- **C and d** ([source](#sec:symbols)): Let $F/\kappa$ be a finitely generated extension of an algebraically closed field of characteristic $p$, and put $$C=F^p,\qquad d=\mathop{\mathrm{trdeg}}(F/\kappa).$$
- **projective line through the identity** ([source](#sec:intersection)): For $X\in F\setminus C$, the projective line through $[1]$ and $[X]$ consists of the nonzero elements of $C+CX$, modulo $C^\times$.

## 4. Engineering Triptych

### Panel 1 - epistemic contract

- **P3-I1**: Compatible degree-one isomorphisms determine unique base-respecting field isomorphisms after quotienting by nonzero prime-field scalars.
- **P3-I2**: Only the identity nonzero prime-field scalar can map every projective line over F^p into a projective line.
- **P3-I3**: Line-preserving projective bijections lift semilinearly, uniquely up to target scalars, in the stated finite-dimensional regime.

### Panel 2 - negative knowledge

- **P3-A1**: Apply reconstruction to arbitrary fields or dimensions. -> The theorem supplies no conclusion outside its hypotheses.
- **P3-A2**: Reconstruct from an arbitrary isomorphism of V_K and V_L. -> Multiplicative classes alone do not establish the required compatibility.
- **P3-A3**: Treat the absolute Frobenius self-map as an invertible map on V_F. -> Its induced map on V_F is zero.
- **P3-A4**: Promote finite coefficient tests to a certificate of field reconstruction. -> The tests establish no field encoding, projective collineation, or global semilinear lift.

### Panel 3 - reference implementation and oracle

Applicability: `constructive`. Constructive finite polynomial calculation in the proof of Lemma 7 only: expand (x+1)^r modulo p and test support outside degrees 0 and r. The field reconstruction theorem has no supplied finite input encoding or end-to-end executable algorithm; it is not certified by this code.

# PRECONDITIONS: p prime, 2 <= p <= 101, 1 <= r < p.
# OUTPUTS: JSON coefficients of (x+1)^r modulo p and obstruction boolean.
# TIME: O(r) exact factorial operations plus O(sqrt(p)) trial divisions.
# SPACE: O(r) exact integers; factorial intermediates have O(r log r) bits.
# ARITHMETIC: python-flint fmpz factorials, exact integer division, reduction modulo p.
# FAILURE: rejected input raises an exception; no approximation or solver witness.
import json, sys
from flint import fmpz
from math import isqrt
data = json.loads(sys.stdin.read())
p, r = data['p'], data['r']
assert type(p) is int and type(r) is int
assert 2 <= p <= 101 and 1 <= r < p
assert all(p % d for d in range(2, isqrt(p) + 1))

coeffs = [int(numerator // (fmpz.fac_ui(i) * fmpz.fac_ui(r-i))) % p for i in range(r+1)]
obstruction = any(coeffs[i] != 0 for i in range(1, r))
print(json.dumps({'coefficients': coeffs, 'obstruction': obstruction}, sort_keys=True))

## 6. Applicability limits and search

- **L-field-hypotheses** (hypothesis): The reconstruction theorem requires finitely generated extensions of algebraically closed base fields in the same positive characteristic p. Failure or absence of a hypothesis prevents invoking this theorem; it does not prove a counterexample outside its scope. Blocked use: ['Arbitrary fields', 'Characteristic-zero reconstruction', 'Non-algebraically-closed bases without a separate theorem']
- **L-transcendence** (hypothesis): Both transcendence degrees must be at least two. The paper does not establish the main reconstruction theorem for curves or algebraic extensions. Blocked use: ['Applying Theorem 1 in transcendence degree below two']
- **L-compatible-input** (hypothesis): Input must be a prime-field-linear isomorphism preserving the entire Steinberg relation subspace in both directions. Multiplicative information alone or a sampled list of relations does not establish compatibility. Blocked use: ['Reconstruction from an arbitrary group bijection', 'Treating sampled relation checks as complete compatibility']
- **L-scalar-quotient** (edge_case): Uniqueness is stated after quotienting compatible maps by nonzero prime-field scalars. A raw compatible map need not itself be the induced field map. Blocked use: ['Ignoring scalar normalization', 'Claiming every compatible map is already a field-induced map']
- **L-projective-base** (edge_case): Projective incidence uses the subfield of pth powers, while the multiplicative quotient has a prime-field vector-space structure. These scalar fields must remain distinct. Blocked use: ['Replacing projective lines over F^p by lines over the original base field']
- **L-symbol-direction** (counterexample_scope): The zero-symbol lemma gives a necessary containment under s outside C. It does not state that every t in C(s) has vanishing symbol. Blocked use: ['Invoking a converse not established by Lemma 4', 'Dropping s outside C']
- **L-intersection-preconditions** (hypothesis): The exponent conclusion requires finite p-degree, a p-independent pair, nonmonomial projective classes, and a nonempty translated-subfield intersection. Mere membership U in C(X) and V in C(Y) does not discharge those conditions. Blocked use: ['Using the exponent conclusion for dependent pairs', 'Omitting the intersection witness', 'Admitting monomial U or V']
- **L-projective-lift** (hypothesis): Projective lifting requires finite-dimensional vector spaces of dimension at least three and a bijection mapping lines onto lines. One-way containment alone is not the input contract. Blocked use: ['Lifting arbitrary point maps', 'Skipping bijectivity or onto-line verification', 'Applying Proposition 9 in vector dimension below three']
- **L-perfect-core** (edge_case): Base recovery uses the intersection of all iterated pth-power subfields under finite generation and algebraic closedness. A finite truncation of that intersection is not the asserted identity. Blocked use: ['Replacing the infinite perfect-core intersection by a finite stopping criterion without proof']
- **L-frobenius-target** (edge_case): Frobenius F to F is not a compatible isomorphism of V_F: it induces the zero map and is not surjective on F. Frobenius F to F^p is a field isomorphism with a different actual target quotient. Blocked use: ['Adding a further Frobenius ambiguity', 'Treating Frobenius as an automorphism of the original field invariant']
- **L-no-complexity-contract** (unknown): ABSENT: a finite encoding of the full compatible-isomorphism input, an end-to-end executable reconstruction algorithm, and computational time or space bounds in the provided paper. Finite-dimensionality over an infinite field is not a finite input enumeration. Blocked use: ['Claiming a runtime guarantee for full field reconstruction', 'Presenting finite sublemma experiments as certification of Theorem 1']
- **L-no-formal-certificate** (formal_scope): ABSENT: a machine-checked proof of this characteristic-p reconstruction theorem in the provided source. Exact finite coefficient tests can certify their declared finite scope but do not verify the full field theorem. Blocked use: ['Promoting an unrelated comparator formalization to a proof of Theorem 1', 'Calling bounded binomial tests a theorem-level certificate']

## 7. Complete mathematical paper

We prove that every isomorphism of their first Milnor <i>K</i>-groups modulo <i>p</i> preserving the degree-two Steinberg relations is a nonzero scalar multiple of the map induced by a unique field isomorphism. The proof recovers projective lines over the subfields of <i>p</i>th powers by an elementary calculation with derivations.

<a id="sec:introduction"></a>
### Introduction

Milnor $K$-theory records multiplication in a field together with relations arising from addition. A reconstruction problem asks whether this information determines the field, and whether isomorphisms of the invariant come from field isomorphisms. We study this question in characteristic $p$ using only the first two Milnor $K$-groups modulo $p$.

Fix a prime $p$ and write $\Lambda=\mathbf F_p$. For a field $F$ of characteristic $p$, set $$V_F=F^\times/(F^\times)^p=\mathrm K^{\mathrm M}_1(F)/p.$$ We write $[f]$ for the class of $f\in F^\times$ and use additive notation in $V_F$: thus $[f]+[g]=[fg]$, $r[f]=[f^r]$ for $r\in\mathbf Z$, and $[1]=0$. Define the Steinberg relation subspace $$R_F=\mathop{\mathrm{Span}}_{\Lambda}
       \{[f]\otimes[1-f]:f\in F\setminus\{0,1\}\}
       \subset V_F\otimes_{\Lambda}V_F.$$ The quotient $W_F=(V_F\otimes_{\Lambda}V_F)/R_F$ is $\mathrm K^{\mathrm M}_2(F)/p$ by the tensor presentation of Milnor $K$-theory (Milnor 1970, sec. 1) <a id="pm-cite-1"></a>[[Milnor1970](#ref-Milnor1970)]. We denote the image of $[f]\otimes[g]$ in $W_F$ by $\{f,g\}_F$.

Let $\mathop{\mathrm{Isom}}_{\mathrm M}(V_K,V_L)$ be the set of $\Lambda$-linear isomorphisms $\Theta:V_K\to V_L$ satisfying $$(\Theta\otimes\Theta)(R_K)=R_L.$$ We call these isomorphisms *compatible*. Equivalently, $\Theta$ extends to an isomorphism of the degree-one and degree-two groups respecting their product pairing. Scalar multiplication by $\Lambda^\times$ acts on this set. For specified subfields $k\subset K$ and $l\subset L$, write $\mathop{\mathrm{Isom}}(K,k;L,l)$ for the field isomorphisms $\alpha:K\to L$ with $\alpha(k)=l$. Such an isomorphism induces $\alpha_*([f])=[\alpha(f)]$ and carries $R_K$ onto $R_L$.

<a id="stmt-thm-main"></a>
<div id="thm:main" class="theorem">

**Theorem 1**. *Let $K/k$ and $L/l$ be finitely generated extensions of algebraically closed fields of characteristic $p$, with $$\mathop{\mathrm{trdeg}}(K/k)\ge2,\qquad \mathop{\mathrm{trdeg}}(L/l)\ge2.$$ The canonical map $$\mathop{\mathrm{Isom}}(K,k;L,l)\longrightarrow
 \mathop{\mathrm{Isom}}_{\mathrm M}(V_K,V_L)/\Lambda^\times,
 \qquad \alpha\longmapsto[\alpha_*],$$ is bijective.*

</div>

The invariant therefore determines the field and its isomorphisms, with exactly the scalar identification in the displayed target. The argument also recovers the named algebraically closed base from the field itself.

<a id="pm-h-3"></a>
#### Context and method

Bogomolov and Tschinkel developed a reconstruction method that recovers projective lines from multiplication and algebraic dependence, and applied it to Milnor $K$-theory in characteristic zero (Bogomolov and Tschinkel 2009) <a id="pm-cite-2"></a>[[BogomolovTschinkel2009](#ref-BogomolovTschinkel2009)]. Cadoret and Pirutka extended reconstruction from the multiplicative group modulo constants and algebraic dependence to finitely generated regular extensions of perfect fields (Cadoret and Pirutka 2021, Theorem 4) <a id="pm-cite-3"></a>[[CadoretPirutka2021](#ref-CadoretPirutka2021)]. For algebraically closed or finite base fields, they also obtained reconstruction from suitable quotients of the Milnor $K$-ring (Cadoret and Pirutka 2021, Corollary 10) <a id="pm-cite-4"></a>[[CadoretPirutka2021](#ref-CadoretPirutka2021)].

Reconstruction from other reduced forms of $K$-theory has also been studied. For a prime $\ell$ different from the characteristic, Topaz recovers perfect closures from mod-$\ell$ data together with rational subgroups for function fields over algebraically closed bases in transcendence degree at least five (Topaz 2016, Theorem B) <a id="pm-cite-5"></a>[[Topaz2016](#ref-Topaz2016)] <a id="pm-cite-6"></a>[[Topaz2023](#ref-Topaz2023)]. His work on rational Milnor $K$-theory reconstructs perfect closures for fields of absolute transcendence degree at least five (Topaz 2023, Main Theorem). Theorem <a href="#thm:main" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:main">1</a> concerns reduction modulo the characteristic and starts directly from $V_F$ and $R_F$; neither rational subgroups nor algebraic-dependence data are supplied.

The geometric framework of our proof has close predecessors. A differential approach to line recovery appears in an argument of Rovinsky recorded by Bogomolov and Tschinkel (Bogomolov and Tschinkel 2012, Proposition 9) <a id="pm-cite-7"></a>[[BogomolovTschinkel2012](#ref-BogomolovTschinkel2012)]. In positive characteristic, Cadoret and Pirutka recover projective lines over the subfield of $p$th powers after a common power correction, using a differential intersection calculation (Cadoret and Pirutka 2021, sec. 1.3, Lemma 23, and Proposition 27) <a id="pm-cite-8"></a>[[CadoretPirutka2021](#ref-CadoretPirutka2021)]. Here the intersection calculation involves only degree-$p$ extensions of the subfield of $p$th powers. The resulting intersection statement, Proposition <a href="#prop:intersection" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:intersection">6</a>, and the passage from vanishing symbols to these subextensions are given complete elementary proofs below. These are the two structural ingredients that allow the degree-two mod-$p$ relations to supply the required projective geometry.

<a id="pm-h-4"></a>
#### Outline of the proof

Put $C_F=F^p$. Since $(F^\times)^p=C_F^\times$, the underlying set of $V_F$ is the projective point set $\mathbf P_{C_F}(F)$. Its point $[1]$ is the zero of the group $V_F$; its projective lines are the sets of one-dimensional subspaces in two-dimensional $C_F$-subspaces of $F$. The field degree is finite: $$[F:C_F]=p^{\mathop{\mathrm{trdeg}}(F/\kappa)}$$ for $F/\kappa$ as in the theorem. This is a projective geometry over $C_F$, not over the original constant field $\kappa$.

The first step constrains pairs with vanishing symbol. If $s\notin C_F$ and $\{s,t\}_F=0$, then $t\in C_F(s)$. To prove this implication, consider a $p$-independent pair $s,t$, meaning that $[C_F(s,t):C_F]=p^2$. Extend it to a monomial basis and take the determinant of two logarithmic derivatives. This pairing kills Steinberg relations and is nonzero on the independent pair. Section <a href="#sec:symbols" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:symbols">2</a> supplies the details.

The fields $C_F(s)$ need not themselves be two-dimensional over $C_F$, so this implication does not yet recover projective lines. The key calculation in Section <a href="#sec:intersection" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:intersection">3</a> uses intersections of two multiplicative translates of such subfields. For a $p$-independent pair $X,Y$, suppose $U\in C_F(X)^\times$ and $V\in C_F(Y)^\times$ are not scalar multiples of powers of $X,Y$, respectively. A nonzero element of $$X\,C_F(Y/X)\ \cap\ U\,C_F(V/U)$$ forces, for a unique $1\le n<p$, $$U^n\in C_F+C_FX^n,
                    \qquad V^n\in C_F+C_FY^n.$$ Euler derivations give this common exponent and show that either inclusion determines it.

Section <a href="#sec:lines" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:lines">4</a> produces the required intersections from Steinberg relations. Uniqueness propagates the exponent across a connected graph, giving one scalar $n$ for which $n\Theta$ preserves every projective line. Applying the argument to the inverse, and using the fact that no nontrivial scalar preserves projective lines, yields preservation in both directions. Section <a href="#sec:fields" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:fields">5</a> then proves the needed projective lifting theorem and uses the multiplicative group law to turn the normalized semilinear lift into a field isomorphism. Finally, the identity $\bigcap_{m\ge1}F^{p^m}=\kappa$ recovers the original base and completes the theorem.

<a id="sec:symbols"></a>
### Symbols and projective points over $p$th powers

Let $F/\kappa$ be a finitely generated extension of an algebraically closed field of characteristic $p$, and put $$C=F^p,\qquad d=\mathop{\mathrm{trdeg}}(F/\kappa).$$ Since $(F^\times)^p=C^\times$, the underlying set of $V_F$ is the point set of the projective space $\mathbf P_C(F)$: the class $[f]$ represents the one-dimensional $C$-subspace $Cf$. These projective points also carry the $\mathbf F_p$-vector-space structure $[f]+[g]=[fg]$, whose zero is $[1]$. We will use both structures, keeping their two scalar fields distinct. Our first task is to extract from a vanishing symbol the field-theoretic constraint that will allow us to recover projective lines.

<a id="stmt-lem-p-degree"></a>
<div id="lem:p-degree" class="lemma">

**Lemma 2**. *With the notation above, $[F:C]=p^d$.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-p-degree"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-p-degree</summary>

*Proof.* Choose a transcendence basis $t_1,\ldots,t_d$ over $\kappa$, and write $F_0=\kappa(t_1,\ldots,t_d)$. Perfectness of $\kappa$ gives $F_0^p=\kappa(t_1^p,\ldots,t_d^p)$. The monomials $t_1^{i_1}\cdots t_d^{i_d}$ with $0\leq i_j<p$ form a basis of $F_0$ over $F_0^p$: group polynomial exponents modulo $p$, and for rational functions use $a/b=ab^{p-1}/b^p$. Linear independence follows by clearing denominators and separating the exponent classes. Thus $[F_0:F_0^p]=p^d$.

The extension $F/F_0$ is finite, and Frobenius induces an isomorphism of extensions $F/F_0\simeq F^p/F_0^p$. Computing $[F:F_0^p]$ in the two towers through $F_0$ and $F^p$ gives $$[F:F_0]p^d=[F:F^p][F^p:F_0^p]
           =[F:F^p][F:F_0].$$ Cancellation proves the assertion without a separability assumption. ◻

</details>

A list $s_1,\ldots,s_r\in F$ is *$p$-independent over $C$* if $[C(s_1,\ldots,s_r):C]=p^r$. A $p$-independent list generating $F$ over $C$ is called a *$p$-basis*. Its monomials supply derivations that record one exponent at a time.

<a id="stmt-lem-p-basis"></a>
<div id="lem:p-basis" class="lemma">

**Lemma 3**. *Let $F$ be any field of characteristic $p$, and put $C=F^p$. If $C\subseteq M\subseteq F$ and $s\in F\setminus M$, then $[M(s):M]=p$. If $[F:C]$ is finite, every $p$-independent list extends to a $p$-basis $s_1,\ldots,s_e$ of $F/C$, with $[F:C]=p^e$ and monomial basis $$\{s_1^{i_1}\cdots s_e^{i_e}:0\leq i_j<p\}.$$ For such a basis there are commuting $C$-linear derivations $D_1,\ldots,D_e$ on $F$ satisfying $$D_j(s_1^{i_1}\cdots s_e^{i_e})
       =i_j s_1^{i_1}\cdots s_e^{i_e}.$$ On $C(s_j)$ the kernel of $D_j$ is $C$, and its nonzero eigenvectors with eigenvalue in $C$ are precisely the elements $c s_j^i$, where $c\in C^\times$ and $0\leq i<p$; their eigenvalues are $i\in\mathbf F_p$. Moreover, $C(s_j)\cap C(s_k)=C$ when $j\ne k$.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-p-basis"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-p-basis</summary>

*Proof.* Since $s^p\in C$, the minimal polynomial of $s$ over $M$ divides $T^p-s^p=(T-s)^p$. If its degree were $1\leq e<p$, it would be $(T-s)^e$, and its coefficient $-es$ of $T^{e-1}$ would force $s\in M$. Thus every strict adjunction has degree $p$. When $[F:C]$ is finite, successive adjunctions must terminate, so they extend any $p$-independent list to a generating list. If its length is $e$, the tower formula gives $[F:C]=p^e$, and the usual basis of each simple extension gives the asserted monomial basis.

Define the $D_j$ on these monomials and extend $C$-linearly. When two monomials are multiplied, reducing an exponent by $p$ only removes a factor in $C$ and leaves its value in $\mathbf F_p$ unchanged. This proves the product rule. The operators commute because they are diagonal on the same basis. On $C(s_j)$ their eigenvector assertions follow from the distinct eigenvalues $0,1,\ldots,p-1$ on $1,s_j,\ldots,s_j^{p-1}$. Finally, the expansions of an element in both $C(s_j)$ and $C(s_k)$ can have only their constant term in common. ◻

</details>

For a $p$-independent pair $s,t$, write $D_s,D_t$ for the corresponding derivations obtained from any extending $p$-basis. The following proof uses a coordinate form of the logarithmic differential symbol; the much stronger injectivity theorem for that symbol is the Bloch–Gabber–Kato theorem (Bloch and Kato 1986, Theorem (2.1)) <a id="pm-cite-9"></a>[[BlochKato1986](#ref-BlochKato1986)]. Here we check the needed implication directly.

<a id="stmt-lem-zero-symbol"></a>
<div id="lem:zero-symbol" class="lemma">

**Lemma 4**. *For $s,t\in F^\times$, one has $$s\notin C,\qquad \{s,t\}_F=0
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad t\in C(s).$$*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-zero-symbol"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-zero-symbol</summary>

*Proof.* Suppose instead that $s\notin C$ and $t\notin C(s)$. By Lemma <a href="#lem:p-basis" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:p-basis">3</a>, the pair $s,t$ is $p$-independent. Consider $$\beta([f],[g])=
 \frac{D_s f}{f}\frac{D_t g}{g}
 -\frac{D_t f}{f}\frac{D_s g}{g},
 \qquad f,g\in F^\times.$$ Each logarithmic derivative is additive on products and vanishes on $C^\times$. Hence $\beta$ is a well-defined $\mathbf F_p$-bilinear map $V_F\times V_F\to F$. For $f\ne0,1$, the identities $D_s(1-f)=-D_s f$ and $D_t(1-f)=-D_t f$ give $\beta([f],[1-f])=0$. Thus $\beta$ descends through the Steinberg relations to an $\mathbf F_p$-linear map $W_F\to F$. But $D_s s=s$, $D_t t=t$, and $D_s t=D_t s=0$, so this map sends $\{s,t\}_F$ to $1$. The symbol cannot vanish. ◻

</details>

The symbols arising from an affine line do vanish, by the defining Steinberg relation itself.

<a id="stmt-lem-affine-symbol"></a>
<div id="lem:affine-symbol" class="lemma">

**Lemma 5**. *If $c,d\in C^\times$ and $t,c+dt\in F^\times$, then $$\{t,c+dt\}_F=0.$$*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-affine-symbol"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-affine-symbol</summary>

*Proof.* Put $q=-dt/c$. The hypotheses give $q\ne0,1$, while $[q]=[t]$ and $[1-q]=[c+dt]$, since the omitted factors lie in $C^\times$. Therefore the identity is the Steinberg relation $\{q,1-q\}_F=0$. ◻

</details>

<a id="sec:intersection"></a>
### An intersection that determines an exponent

For $X\in F\setminus C$, the projective line through $[1]$ and $[X]$ consists of the nonzero elements of $C+CX$, modulo $C^\times$. To recover such lines from degree-$p$ subfields, we need to pass from membership in $C(X)$ to membership in $C+CX$, possibly after taking a power. The following intersection provides such a condition for two $p$-independent elements at once. Its uniqueness assertion will allow the exponent to be compared between different pairs.

The use of differential equations to control such intersections, including the power correction in positive characteristic, appears in Cadoret and Pirutka (2021, secs. 3.4–3.5) <a id="pm-cite-10"></a>[[CadoretPirutka2021](#ref-CadoretPirutka2021)]. Here the degree-$p$ subextensions allow a direct proof using commuting Euler derivations.

<a id="stmt-prop-intersection"></a>
<div id="prop:intersection" class="proposition">

<a id="eq:intersection-witness"></a>
<a id="eq:intersection-conclusion"></a>
**Proposition 6** (Intersection and exponent). *Let $F$ be a field of characteristic $p$ with $[F:F^p]<\infty$, and put $C=F^p$. Let $X,Y\in F^\times$ be $p$-independent. Suppose $$U\in C(X)^\times,\qquad V\in C(Y)^\times,
 \qquad [U]\notin\mathbf F_p[X],\qquad [V]\notin\mathbf F_p[Y].$$ If $$\label{eq:intersection-witness}
 X\,C(Y/X)^\times\ \cap\ U\,C(V/U)^\times\ne\varnothing,$$ then there is a unique integer $n$, $1\le n<p$, such that $$\label{eq:intersection-conclusion}
 U^n\in C+CX^n,\qquad V^n\in C+CY^n.$$ In fact, either inclusion in <a href="#eq:intersection-conclusion" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:intersection-conclusion">[eq:intersection-conclusion]</a> determines $n$ uniquely. In each inclusion, both coefficients are nonzero.*

</div>

The shape of <a href="#eq:intersection-witness" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:intersection-witness">[eq:intersection-witness]</a> reflects the inclusion $CX+CY\subseteq X\,C(Y/X)$, and its counterpart for $U,V$. Thus a common nonzero vector of the two spans yields the required intersection [(eq:intersection-witness)](#eq:intersection-witness).

<details id="proof-stmt-prop-intersection"><summary>Proof of stmt-prop-intersection</summary>

*Proof.* Choose $Z$ in the intersection <a href="#eq:intersection-witness" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:intersection-witness">[eq:intersection-witness]</a>. Extend $X,Y$ to a $p$-basis and let $D_X,D_Y$ be the commuting Euler derivations of Lemma <a href="#lem:p-basis" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:p-basis">3</a>. We first translate the two descriptions of $Z$ into differential equations. Since $U$ and $V$ are not scalar multiples of monomials in $X$ and $Y$, respectively, the eigenvector description in that lemma gives $$D_XU\ne0,\qquad D_YV\ne0.$$ Define $$B_X=\frac{U}{D_XU},\qquad B_Y=\frac{V}{D_YV},
 \qquad A_X=B_X-1,\qquad A_Y=B_Y-1.$$ Here $A_X\in C(X)\setminus C$ and $A_Y\in C(Y)\setminus C$: if, for example, $B_X\in C$, then $D_XU=B_X^{-1}U$, making $U$ an eigenvector and hence a scalar multiple of a monomial in $X$. In particular, $A_X,A_Y,B_X,B_Y$ are all nonzero.

<a id="eq:intersection-q"></a>
The derivations $$\delta=D_X+D_Y,
 \qquad \delta'=B_XD_X+B_YD_Y$$ satisfy $\delta X=X$, $\delta Y=Y$, $\delta'U=U$, and $\delta'V=V$. Thus $\delta$ kills $Y/X$, whereas $\delta'$ kills $V/U$; both kill $C$. The two expressions for $Z$ therefore give $$\delta Z=Z=\delta'Z.$$ Set $$q_X=\frac{D_XZ}{Z},\qquad q_Y=\frac{D_YZ}{Z}.$$ Dividing the preceding equations by $Z$ and subtracting yields $$\label{eq:intersection-q}
 q_X+q_Y=1,\qquad A_Xq_X+A_Yq_Y=0.$$ Both $q_X$ and $q_Y$ are nonzero: if one vanished, these two equations would force the other $A$ to vanish.

<a id="eq:intersection-monomials"></a>
Since $\delta$ commutes with $D_X$ and $D_Y$, the equation $\delta Z=Z$ implies that $q_X,q_Y$ are killed by $\delta$: $$\delta q_X=\frac{D_X(\delta Z)}{Z}
              -\frac{D_XZ\,\delta Z}{Z^2}=0,
 \qquad \delta q_Y=0.$$ By <a href="#eq:intersection-q" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:intersection-q">[eq:intersection-q]</a>, $A_X/A_Y=-q_Y/q_X$, so $\delta(A_X/A_Y)=0$. Since $D_YA_X=D_XA_Y=0$, we obtain <a id="eq:intersection-eigenvalue"></a>$$\label{eq:intersection-eigenvalue}
 \frac{D_XA_X}{A_X}=\frac{D_YA_Y}{A_Y}.$$ The left side lies in $C(X)$ and the right side in $C(Y)$. Their intersection is $C$, by the $p$-monomial basis. Consequently $A_X$ and $A_Y$ are eigenvectors of their respective Euler derivations with the same eigenvalue. The distinct eigenvalues on $1,X,\ldots,X^{p-1}$, and on $1,Y,\ldots,Y^{p-1}$, now give $$\label{eq:intersection-monomials}
 A_X=c_XX^m,\qquad A_Y=c_YY^m$$ for $c_X,c_Y\in C^\times$ and one integer $m$, $1\le m<p$. The eigenvalue cannot be zero because $A_X,A_Y\notin C$.

It remains to integrate <a href="#eq:intersection-monomials" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:intersection-monomials">[eq:intersection-monomials]</a> inside the one-variable subfields. Put $n=p-m$. The identity $1+c_XX^m=B_X\ne0$ ensures that all terms in $$\frac{D_X(U^n)}{U^n}
 =\frac{n}{1+c_XX^m}
 =\frac{D_X(X^n+c_XX^p)}{X^n+c_XX^p}$$ are defined. Thus $D_X$ kills $U^n/(X^n+c_XX^p)\in C(X)^\times$. Its kernel on $C(X)$ is $C$, so this quotient belongs to $C^\times$. Since $X^p\in C^\times$, we have $U^n\in C+CX^n$ with both coefficients nonzero. Replacing $X,U,c_X,D_X$ by $Y,V,c_Y,D_Y$ proves the second inclusion.

Finally, suppose $U^r=h+jX^r$ with $h,j\in C$ and $1\le r<p$. Both coefficients must be nonzero: otherwise $r[U]\in\mathbf F_p[X]$, contrary to $[U]\notin\mathbf F_p[X]$. Taking logarithmic derivatives gives $$\frac{D_XU}{U}=\frac{jX^r}{h+jX^r},
 \qquad A_X=\frac{h}{jX^r}.$$ The last expression is a nonzero scalar multiple of $X^{p-r}$, since $X^p\in C^\times$. Comparison with <a href="#eq:intersection-monomials" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:intersection-monomials">[eq:intersection-monomials]</a> and linear independence of the $p$ monomials forces $p-r=m$, hence $r=n$. The argument for the inclusion involving $V$ is identical. ◻

</details>

All hypotheses and conclusions of Proposition <a href="#prop:intersection" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:intersection">6</a> are unchanged when $X,Y,U,V$ are multiplied by elements of $C^\times$. In particular, its exponent depends on projective points, rather than on the representatives used in the calculation.

<a id="sec:lines"></a>
### One scalar recovers all projective lines

The intersection calculation produces a power correction for two points at a time. We now show that a single correction works throughout the projective space. The passage from local power corrections to a global one parallels Cadoret and Pirutka (2021, sec. 3.5) <a id="pm-cite-11"></a>[[CadoretPirutka2021](#ref-CadoretPirutka2021)]; here the degree-$p$ subfields give a particularly simple connected graph on which to propagate the correction.

We first record why a nontrivial scalar cannot preserve projective lines. This will rule out a new scalar when we apply the line-recovery argument to the inverse map.

<a id="stmt-lem-scalar-line"></a>
<div id="lem:scalar-line" class="lemma">

**Lemma 7**. *Let $F$ have characteristic $p$, with $F\ne F^p$. If multiplication by $r\in\mathbf F_p^\times$ on $V_F$ maps every projective line over $F^p$ into a projective line, then $r=1$.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-scalar-line"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-scalar-line</summary>

*Proof.* Represent $r$ by an integer $1\le r<p$ and take $x\in F\setminus F^p$. The points $[1],[x],[x+1]$ lie on one line. Their images under multiplication by $r$ are $[1],[x^r],[(x+1)^r]$. The first two are distinct, so the asserted line containment implies $$(x+1)^r\in F^p+F^p x^r.$$ If $1<r<p$, the binomial expansion has nonzero coefficient $r$ at $x^{r-1}$. This contradicts the linear independence of $1,x,\ldots,x^{p-1}$ over $F^p$ from Lemma <a href="#lem:p-basis" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:p-basis">3</a>. ◻

</details>

<a id="stmt-prop-lines"></a>
<div id="prop:lines" class="proposition">

**Proposition 8**. *Let $K/k$ and $L/l$ be finitely generated extensions of algebraically closed fields of characteristic $p$, both of transcendence degree at least two. For every compatible isomorphism $\Theta:V_K\to V_L$, there is a unique $n\in\mathbf F_p^\times$ such that $\Psi=n\Theta$ sends every projective line over $K^p$ onto a projective line over $L^p$.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-prop-lines"><summary>Proof of stmt-prop-lines</summary>

*Proof.* Put $H=K^p$ and $C=L^p$. We first find one scalar for which lines map into lines. Only after this construction will we apply it to the inverse.

*A connected graph of independent image pairs.* On the nonzero elements of $V_K$, join $[x]$ and $[y]$ by an edge when $$C(X)\ne C(Y),\qquad [X]=\Theta([x]),\quad [Y]=\Theta([y]).$$ This condition is independent of the chosen representatives. Since $X,Y\notin C$, the fields $C(X)$ and $C(Y)$ have degree $p$ over $C$. An edge therefore means that $X,Y$ are $p$-independent. The graph is complete between distinct parts of the partition by the fields $C(X)$. It has at least two parts: by Lemma <a href="#lem:p-degree" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:p-degree">2</a>, $[L:C]\ge p^2$, so for any $X\notin C$ there is an element of $L\setminus C(X)$, and $\Theta$ is surjective. Thus every vertex has a neighbor, and any two vertices are joined by a path of length at most two.

*An affine configuration along an edge.* Fix an edge $[x],[y]$. The elements $1,x,y$ are linearly independent over $H$. Indeed, a dependence would give $y=c+dx$ with $c,d\in H$ and $d\ne0$, since $x,y\notin H$. If $c=0$, the two vertices would coincide. Otherwise Lemma <a href="#lem:affine-symbol" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:affine-symbol">5</a> gives $\{x,y\}_K=0$, and compatibility followed by Lemma <a href="#lem:zero-symbol" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:zero-symbol">4</a> gives $Y\in C(X)$, again contradicting the edge condition.

<a id="eq:affine-configuration"></a>
<a id="eq:nonmonomial-source"></a>
For arbitrary $a,b\in H^\times$, set $$\label{eq:affine-configuration}
 u=x+a,\qquad v=y+b,\qquad z=bx-ay=bu-av.$$ These are nonzero: $u,v$ are nonzero because $x,y\notin H$, and $z\ne0$ because $x,y$ are $H$-linearly independent. Moreover, $$\label{eq:nonmonomial-source}
 [u]\notin\mathbf F_p[x],\qquad [v]\notin\mathbf F_p[y].$$ For example, $[u]=i[x]$ with $0\le i<p$ would give $x+a=h x^i$ for some $h\in H^\times$, contrary to the linear independence of $1,x,\ldots,x^{p-1}$ and the condition $a\ne0$. Figure <a href="#fig:affine-configuration" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="fig:affine-configuration">1</a> shows the incidence that explains the two expressions for $z$.

<a id="eq:configuration-symbols"></a>
<a id="eq:local-line-powers"></a>
Lemma <a href="#lem:affine-symbol" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:affine-symbol">5</a>, applied also to $z/x=b-a(y/x)$ and $z/u=b-a(v/u)$, gives $$\label{eq:configuration-symbols}
 \{x,u\}_K=\{y,v\}_K
 =\{y/x,z/x\}_K=\{v/u,z/u\}_K=0.$$ Write $[U]=\Theta([u])$, $[V]=\Theta([v])$, and $[Z]=\Theta([z])$. Compatibility and Lemma <a href="#lem:zero-symbol" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:zero-symbol">4</a> applied to the first two symbols show that $$U\in C(X)^\times,\qquad V\in C(Y)^\times.$$ Linearity and injectivity transport <a href="#eq:nonmonomial-source" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:nonmonomial-source">[eq:nonmonomial-source]</a> to $[U]\notin\mathbf F_p[X]$ and $[V]\notin\mathbf F_p[Y]$. In particular $U,V\notin C$. Also $Y/X\notin C$, since $X,Y$ are $p$-independent, and $V/U\notin C$: otherwise $U$ would belong to $C(X)\cap C(Y)=C$. We may therefore apply Lemma <a href="#lem:zero-symbol" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:zero-symbol">4</a> to the last two symbols in <a href="#eq:configuration-symbols" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:configuration-symbols">[eq:configuration-symbols]</a>. The result is $$Z\in X C(Y/X)^\times\cap U C(V/U)^\times.$$ All hypotheses of Proposition <a href="#prop:intersection" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:intersection">6</a> now hold. It gives a common integer $1\le n<p$ such that $$\label{eq:local-line-powers}
 U^n\in C+C X^n,\qquad V^n\in C+C Y^n,$$ and either inclusion uniquely determines $n$.

*Propagation of the scalar.* Fix representatives for all vertices and their images. For a vertex $[x]$, fix one neighbor $[y]$ and one $b\in H^\times$. As $a\in H^\times$ varies, Proposition <a href="#prop:intersection" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:intersection">6</a> applies to <a href="#eq:affine-configuration" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:affine-configuration">[eq:affine-configuration]</a>. The second inclusion in <a href="#eq:local-line-powers" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:local-line-powers">[eq:local-line-powers]</a>, whose pair $Y,V$ stays fixed, forces the same $n$ for every $a$. To compare choices of neighbor and $b$, specialize to $a=1$, keeping $X$ and a representative $U$ of $\Theta([x+1])$ fixed. Uniqueness for the first inclusion shows that these choices cannot change $n$. Thus each vertex has a well-defined scalar. Adjacent vertices have the same scalar by <a href="#eq:local-line-powers" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:local-line-powers">[eq:local-line-powers]</a>. Connectedness consequently supplies a single $n$ for all vertices and all $a\in H^\times$.

Set $\Psi=n\Theta$, with $n$ viewed in $\mathbf F_p^\times$. The line joining $[1]$ and $[x]$ consists of those two points and $[x+a]$ for $a\in H^\times$. The first inclusion in <a href="#eq:local-line-powers" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:local-line-powers">[eq:local-line-powers]</a> says exactly that its image is contained in the line joining $[1]$ and $[X^n]$. Every projective line is a multiplicative translate of one through $[1]$: multiply the line joining $[s],[t]$ by $s^{-1}$. Multiplication by a nonzero field element is linear over the field of $p$-th powers, and $\Psi$ respects the multiplicative group law. Thus $\Psi$ maps every line into a line.

*Equality of lines and uniqueness.* Scalar multiplication preserves compatibility, since it scales the tensor square by the nonzero scalar $n^2$. Hence $\Psi^{-1}$ is compatible. Apply the construction just completed, with the fields reversed, to obtain $r\in\mathbf F_p^\times$ for which $r\Psi^{-1}$ maps lines into lines. Its composition with $\Psi$ is multiplication by $r$ on $V_K$ and has the same line-containment property. Lemma <a href="#lem:scalar-line" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:scalar-line">7</a> forces $r=1$. Thus both $\Psi$ and its inverse map lines into lines. If $\Psi(\ell)\subseteq\ell'$, choose a line $\ell''$ containing $\Psi^{-1}(\ell')$. Then $$\ell\subseteq\Psi^{-1}(\ell')\subseteq\ell''.$$ Two projective lines cannot properly contain one another, so $\ell=\ell''$ and both inclusions are equalities. Applying $\Psi$ gives $\Psi(\ell)=\ell'$.

Finally, if $m\Theta$ also sends lines onto lines, then $(m\Theta)\circ\Psi^{-1}$ is multiplication by $mn^{-1}$ on $V_L$ and preserves lines. Another application of Lemma <a href="#lem:scalar-line" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:scalar-line">7</a> gives $m=n$. ◻

</details>

<a id="sec:fields"></a>
### From projective lines to the field

Proposition <a href="#prop:lines" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:lines">8</a> has recovered a bijection of projective spaces that preserves both lines and the multiplicative group law. The fundamental theorem of projective geometry (Hall 2015, sec. 8.2, Theorem 8.2) <a id="pm-cite-12"></a>[[Hall2015](#ref-Hall2015)] recovers addition from the lines; the group law then forces the resulting additive map to be multiplicative. This last passage also appears in Cadoret and Pirutka (2021, sec. 4, Lemma 29) <a id="pm-cite-13"></a>[[CadoretPirutka2021](#ref-CadoretPirutka2021)]. We include the coordinate argument to make both the reconstruction and its uniqueness explicit.

<a id="stmt-prop-projective-lift"></a>
<div id="prop:projective-lift" class="proposition">

**Proposition 9** (Projective lifting). *Let $E$ and $E'$ be finite-dimensional vector spaces over fields $H$ and $C$, respectively, with dimensions at least three. Every bijection $\psi:\mathbf P_H(E)\to\mathbf P_C(E')$ that maps lines onto lines is induced by an additive bijection $S:E\to E'$ satisfying $$S(av)=\sigma(a)S(v)\qquad(a\in H,\ v\in E)$$ for a field isomorphism $\sigma:H\to C$. The map $\sigma$ is unique, and $S$ is unique up to multiplication by an element of $C^\times$.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-prop-projective-lift"><summary>Proof of stmt-prop-projective-lift</summary>

*Proof.* The inverse also maps lines onto lines: a target line is the image of the line joining the preimages of any two distinct points on it. Projective spans are obtained by repeatedly joining pairs of points by lines, so $\psi$ and its inverse preserve spans. Minimal spanning sets therefore correspond, and $\dim_H E=\dim_C E'=N+1$ for some $N\ge2$. Choose a basis $e_0,\ldots,e_N$ of $E$ and representatives $e'_0,\ldots,e'_N$ of their images. They form a basis of $E'$. Keeping $e'_0$ fixed, scale each $e'_i$, $i\ge1$, so that $$\psi([e_0+e_i])=[e'_0+e'_i].$$ This is possible because $[e_0+e_i]$ lies on the line joining $[e_0]$ to $[e_i]$ and differs from both endpoints.

The hyperplanes spanned by $e_1,\ldots,e_N$ and by $e'_1,\ldots,e'_N$ correspond. On their affine complements use the coordinates $$(t_1,\ldots,t_N)\longleftrightarrow
 [e_0+t_1e_1+\cdots+t_Ne_N].$$ The induced affine bijection preserves parallelism: parallel lines have the same point on the corresponding hyperplane at infinity. On the $i$th coordinate axis it is a bijection $\sigma_i:H\to C$ fixing $0$ and $1$. Varying only the $j$th input coordinate moves along a line with point at infinity $[e_j]$, whose image has point at infinity $[e'_j]$. Hence all other output coordinates are unchanged. It follows that the affine map is $$(t_1,\ldots,t_N)\longmapsto
 (\sigma_1(t_1),\ldots,\sigma_N(t_N)).$$ In each plane of two coordinate axes, the diagonal through $(0,0)$ and $(1,1)$ maps to the corresponding diagonal. Thus all $\sigma_i$ equal one bijection $\sigma:H\to C$. In the first two coordinates, the line $(t,t+a)$ is parallel to this diagonal and passes through $(0,a)$. Its image therefore gives $$\sigma(t+a)=\sigma(t)+\sigma(a).$$ The line $(t,at)$ passes through $(0,0)$ and $(1,a)$, so its image gives $$\sigma(at)=\sigma(a)\sigma(t).$$ Since $\sigma(1)=1$, it is a field isomorphism.

Define $S(\sum_{i=0}^N t_i e_i)=\sum_{i=0}^N\sigma(t_i)e'_i$. This is an additive, $\sigma$-semilinear bijection inducing $\psi$ on the affine chart. Every point at infinity is the direction of an affine line through the origin, so it induces $\psi$ there as well.

For uniqueness, suppose that $T$ is another semilinear lift, possibly with a different field isomorphism. For every nonzero $v\in E$ write $T(v)=c_vS(v)$ with $c_v\in C^\times$. If $v,w$ are independent, additivity applied to $v+w$ and independence of $S(v),S(w)$ give $c_v=c_w=c_{v+w}$. Two dependent nonzero vectors can each be compared to a vector outside their common line. Thus $c_v$ is a single scalar $c$, and $T=cS$. Comparing the images of $av$ then shows that the two field isomorphisms also agree. ◻

</details>

We will also recover the prescribed algebraically closed base directly from the field.

<a id="stmt-lem-perfect-core"></a>
<div id="lem:perfect-core" class="lemma">

**Lemma 10**. *If $F/\kappa$ is a finitely generated extension of an algebraically closed field of characteristic $p$, then $$\bigcap_{m\ge1} F^{p^m}=\kappa.$$*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-perfect-core"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-perfect-core</summary>

*Proof.* Perfectness gives the inclusion from right to left. If $f\in
F\setminus\kappa$, algebraic closedness makes $f$ transcendental over $\kappa$. Complete $f$ to a transcendence basis $f,t_2,\ldots,t_d$, and put $B=\kappa(t_2,\ldots,t_d)$ and $E=B(f)$. The extension $F/E$ is finite; write $D=[F:E]$. For every $m\ge1$, the polynomial $T^{p^m}-f$ is Eisenstein at the prime $f$ of $B[f]$, and is therefore irreducible over $E$. If $f=g^{p^m}$ with $g\in F$, it follows that $$p^m=[E(g):E]\le D.$$ This fails for all sufficiently large $m$, so $f$ does not belong to the intersection. ◻

</details>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-perfect-core-2"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-perfect-core</summary>

*Proof of Theorem <a href="#thm:main" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:main">1</a>.* A field isomorphism $\alpha:K\to L$ sends $K^p$ to $L^p$ and Steinberg pairs to Steinberg pairs. It therefore induces an element of $\mathop{\mathrm{Isom}}_{\mathrm M}(V_K,V_L)$, and the canonical map in the theorem is well defined.

For surjectivity, take $\Theta\in\mathop{\mathrm{Isom}}_{\mathrm M}(V_K,V_L)$. Proposition <a href="#prop:lines" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:lines">8</a> gives $n\in\mathbf F_p^\times$ such that $\Psi=n\Theta$ maps lines onto lines. Put $H=K^p$ and $C=L^p$. By Lemma <a href="#lem:p-degree" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:p-degree">2</a>, $\dim_H K$ and $\dim_C L$ are at least $p^2\ge4$, so Proposition <a href="#prop:projective-lift" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:projective-lift">9</a> gives a semilinear lift $S:K\to L$. Since $\Psi([1])=[1]$, scale $S$ to arrange $S(1)=1$. For each $f\in K^\times$, the two semilinear bijections $$w\longmapsto S(fw),\qquad w\longmapsto S(f)S(w)$$ induce the same projective map: the equality is precisely $\Psi([fw])=\Psi([f])+\Psi([w])$ in the additive notation for $V_L$. Their values at $1$ agree, so the uniqueness assertion in Proposition <a href="#prop:projective-lift" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:projective-lift">9</a> makes them equal. Thus $S$ is multiplicative, and hence a field isomorphism. It maps every $K^{p^m}$ onto $L^{p^m}$, so Lemma <a href="#lem:perfect-core" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:perfect-core">10</a> gives $S(k)=l$. Its induced map is $n\Theta$, proving surjectivity onto the scalar quotient.

For injectivity, suppose that field isomorphisms $\alpha,\beta:K\to L$ induce maps with $\beta_*=r\alpha_*$ for $r\in\mathbf F_p^\times$. Both preserve projective lines, so multiplication by $r$ on $V_L$ also preserves lines. Lemma <a href="#lem:scalar-line" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:scalar-line">7</a> gives $r=1$. The maps $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are therefore semilinear lifts of the same projective bijection. Proposition <a href="#prop:projective-lift" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:projective-lift">9</a> makes them scalar multiples, and $\alpha(1)=\beta(1)=1$ makes them equal. ◻

</details>

<a id="stmt-auto-remark-1"></a>
<div class="remark">

*Remark 11* (Frobenius and the target quotient). There is no further Frobenius ambiguity. For a field $F/\kappa$ as in the theorem, the Frobenius map $F\to F$, $f\mapsto f^p$, is not surjective and induces the zero map on $V_F$. By contrast, $F\to F^p$ is a field isomorphism, whose induced isomorphism has target $$V_{F^p}=(F^p)^\times/(F^{p^2})^\times.$$ It is already covered by the theorem, with its actual target field $F^p$.
