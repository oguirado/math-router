## 1. Abstract and result contract

> We construct a two-dimensional ramified regular local ring <i>A</i> in mixed characteristic $`(0,5)`$ for which the integral map $`K_3(A)\to K_3(\mathop{\mathrm{Frac}}\nolimits A)`$ has nonzero kernel. This gives a negative answer to the unrestricted integral Gersten conjecture.

- **C1** (counterexample, [source](#stmt-thm-main)): The ring $A$ is a two-dimensional Noetherian regular local domain of mixed characteristic $(0,5)$, with $\pi\in\mathfrak m_A^2$. There is an integral class $c\in K_3(A)$ such that $$c\|_{\mathop{\mathrm{Frac}}A}=0, \qquad c\|_{A[1/\pi]}\bmod5\ne0 \quad\text{in }G_3(A[1/\pi];\mathbb Z/5).$$ In particular, $K_3(A)\to K_3(\mathop{\mathrm{Frac}}A)$ is not injective.
  - Explanation: An integral class on the specified ramified regular local ring vanishes on the fraction field, but its coefficient reduction after inverting the uniformizer is nonzero. This refutes unrestricted integral Gersten injectivity.
- **C2** (construction, [source](#stmt-lem-model)): The generic fiber $C=\mathcal X_L$ is an integral projective curve. The ring $A=\mathcal O_{\mathcal X,m}$ is a two-dimensional Noetherian regular local domain of mixed characteristic $(0,5)$ with $\pi\in\mathfrak m_A^2$. For every closed point $q\in C$ with residue field $E=\kappa(q)$, the $E$-point extends to $\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}\mathcal O_E\to\mathcal X$. If its closed image is not $m$, then $5\mid[k_E:\mathbb F_5]$.* *On the chart $Z\ne0$, set $x=X/Z$, $y=Y/Z$, and let $t$ be the image of $x$ in $S=A/yA$. Then $$\label{eq:divisor} S\cong V[t]/\bigl((t-1)^5+\zeta\bigr)$$ is a DVR with uniformizer $t$. Its fraction field $D$ is totally ramified of degree five over $L$, and $$\label{eq:norm-t} N_{D/L}(t)=\pi,\qquad \eta=1-t,\qquad \eta^5=\zeta.$$ The equation $Y=0$ cuts out a single closed point $q_0\in C$ with residue field $D$.
  - Explanation: The model is integral and regular at the chosen special-fiber point. The distinguished divisor has a totally ramified fraction field with uniformizer norm pi, while points specializing away have residue degree divisible by five.
- **C3** (construction, [source](#stmt-prop-integral)): There exists $b\in K_3(S)$ whose restriction to $D$, followed by coefficient reduction, is $a_D$. If $j:\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}S\hookrightarrow\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}A$ and $c=j_*b\in G_3(A)=K_3(A)$, then $c\|_{\mathop{\mathrm{Frac}}A}=0$. Moreover, for $T=\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}A[1/\pi]$ and $i:\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}D\hookrightarrow T$, $$c\|_T\bmod5=i_*a_D\quad\text{in }G_3(T;\mathbb Z/5).$$
  - Explanation: Two integral lifts followed by coherent pushforward produce the supported candidate. Its generic restriction vanishes integrally, and its localized coefficient image is the divisor pushforward.
- **C4** (theorem, [source](#stmt-lem-field)): For $L=\mathbb Q_5(\zeta)$ and the fixed $\beta$ above, every finite extension $E/L$ has an isomorphism $$\label{eq:theta} \theta_E:E^\times/E^{\times5}\xrightarrow{\sim}K_3(E;\mathbb Z/5), \qquad u\bmod E^{\times5}\longmapsto\beta_E\cdot[u].$$ The degree-three motivic edge identifies the target with $H^1_{\mathrm{et}}(E,\mu_5^{\otimes2})$. Under this edge, the displayed map is Kummer reduction followed by cup product with the nonzero weight-one edge image $\alpha_E\in H^0(E,\mu_5)$ of $\beta_E$.
  - Explanation: Multiplication by the restricted coefficient class identifies units modulo fifth powers with the degree-three coefficient group. The motivic edge identifies this multiplication with a nonzero weight-one cup product.
- **C5** (theorem, [source](#stmt-lem-norm-detector)): For every finite extension $E/L$ and every $u\in E^\times$, $$\label{eq:transfer} \mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}_{E/L}\theta_E(u)=\theta_L(N_{E/L}u).$$ Consequently $$\lambda_L=(v_L\bmod5)\circ\theta_L^{-1}: K_3(L;\mathbb Z/5)\longrightarrow\mathbb Z/5$$ satisfies $$\lambda_L\bigl(\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}_{E/L}(a)\bigr)=0 \quad\text{for every }a\in K_3(E;\mathbb Z/5) \quad\text{if }5\mid[k_E:k_L].$$
  - Explanation: The comparison intertwines transfer and norm. Normalized valuation modulo five kills transfers from fields whose residue degree is divisible by five.
- **C6** (construction, [source](#stmt-lem-opens)): Let $\Sigma=R\setminus\mathfrak m$, where $R=V[x,y]/(F)$ and $\mathfrak m=(\pi,x,y)$. For $h\in\Sigma$ put $$U_h=\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}R[1/\pi,1/h]\subset C.$$ Then the refinement maps $U_{hk}\to U_h$ are affine and flat, and $$\label{eq:continuity} T=\varprojlim_{h\in\Sigma}U_h, \qquad \varinjlim_{h\in\Sigma}G_3(U_h;\mathbb Z/5) \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }G_3(T;\mathbb Z/5).$$ Every $U_h$ contains $q_0$. Its complement in $C$ is a finite set of closed points $q$, and their residue fields $E_q=\kappa(q)$ satisfy $5\mid[k_{E_q}:\mathbb F_5]$.
  - Explanation: The inverted local scheme is a limit of affine flat refinements, with coherent coefficient continuity. Every stage contains the distinguished point; omitted points have the residue degrees needed by the detector.

## 2. Definitions, validity and bounds

- **Cyclotomic base field, valuation ring and uniformizer** ([source](#sec:introduction)): Fix a primitive fifth root of unity $\zeta$, and define $$L=\mathbb Q_5(\zeta),\qquad V=\mathcal O_L,\qquad \pi=1-\zeta.$$
- **Projective quintic model** ([source](#sec:introduction)): Let $\mathcal X\subset\mathbb P^2_V$ be the projective hypersurface $$(X-Z)^5-XY^4+Y^5+\zeta Z^5=0.$$
- **Local ring A** ([source](#sec:introduction)): On its special fiber take $m=[0:0:1]$, and set $A=\mathcal O_{\mathcal X,m}$.
- **Coherent K-theory and coefficient notation** ([source](#sec:introduction)): We use $G_i$ for coherent $K$-theory and $G_i(-;\mathbb Z/5)$ for its mod-five coefficient groups.
- **Generic curve** ([source](#pm-h-4)): Write $C=\mathcal X_L$ for the projective generic curve.
- **Distinguished divisor ring** ([source](#pm-h-4)): The divisor $y=0$ in $\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}A$ has ring $S=A/yA$.
- **Moore coefficient groups** ([source](#eq:coeff)): We use $K_i(B;\mathbb Z/5)=\pi_i(K(B)\wedge M_5)$, and the same convention for $G$-theory.
- **Restricted beta class** ([source](#sec:integral)): For every finite extension $E/L$, write $\beta_E$ for its restriction.
- **Divisor coefficient class** ([source](#sec:integral)): The class to be lifted is $$a_D=\beta_D\cdot[t]\in K_3(D;\mathbb Z/5).$$
- **Normalized valuations** ([source](#sec:field)): For every finite extension $E/L$, let $k_E$ be its residue field, and normalize $v_E$ so that a uniformizer has valuation one.

## 3. Proof roadmap

- **R1** [claim](#stmt-lem-model) / [proof](#proof-stmt-lem-model): Eisenstein and special-fiber Frobenius analysis; local regularity and norm computation. -> The model is integral and regular at the chosen special-fiber point. The distinguished divisor has a totally ramified fraction field with uniformizer norm pi, while points specializing away have residue degree divisible by five.
- **R2** [claim](#stmt-prop-integral) / [proof](#proof-stmt-prop-integral): Coefficient exactness, Steinberg vanishing, DVR localization and support pushforward. -> Two integral lifts followed by coherent pushforward produce the supported candidate. Its generic restriction vanishes integrally, and its localized coefficient image is the divisor pushforward.
- **R3** [claim](#stmt-lem-field) / [proof](#proof-stmt-lem-field): Multiplicative motivic-to-K comparison and weight filtration. -> Multiplication by the restricted coefficient class identifies units modulo fifth powers with the degree-three coefficient group. The motivic edge identifies this multiplication with a nonzero weight-one cup product.
- **R4** [claim](#stmt-lem-norm-detector) / [proof](#proof-stmt-lem-norm-detector): Projection formula, field K1 norm and normalized valuation. -> The comparison intertwines transfer and norm. Normalized valuation modulo five kills transfers from fields whose residue degree is divisible by five.
- **R5** [claim](#stmt-lem-opens) / [proof](#proof-stmt-lem-opens): Flat localization refinements and coherent coefficient continuity. -> The inverted local scheme is a limit of affine flat refinements, with coherent coefficient continuity. Every stage contains the distinguished point; omitted points have the residue degrees needed by the detector.
- **R6** [claim](#stmt-thm-main) / [proof](#proof-stmt-lem-opens-2): Coherent localization and proper pushforward contradict the detector values. -> An integral class on the specified ramified regular local ring vanishes on the fraction field, but its coefficient reduction after inverting the uniformizer is nonzero. This refutes unrestricted integral Gersten injectivity.

## 4. Engineering Triptych

### Panel 1 - epistemic contract

- **P3-I1**: The specified ramified regular local ring admits an integral kernel class with nonzero coefficient reduction after inverting pi.
- **P3-I2**: The class is lifted integrally on the divisor before pushforward; support gives integral generic vanishing.
- **P3-I3**: Multiplication by the fixed beta identifies units modulo fifth powers with the degree-three coefficient group for every finite extension of L.
- **P3-I4**: The transfer detector annihilates every coefficient class when the residue degree is divisible by five.
- **P3-I5**: The affine stages contain the distinguished point and their omitted closed points have residue degree divisible by five.

### Panel 2 - negative knowledge

- **P3-A1**: Apply the counterexample to unramified or smooth-over-V rings. -> The constructed ring lies outside those positive-result hypotheses.
- **P3-A2**: Infer the integral kernel directly from a finite-coefficient kernel. -> A coefficient class need not lift, and a lift may have nonzero integral generic restriction.
- **P3-A3**: Replace the residue degree in the valuation test with total extension degree. -> This would kill the distinguished contribution because D has total degree five but residue degree one.
- **P3-A4**: Treat T as an affine open stage of C. -> The argument needs passage to a finite stage via continuity.
- **P3-A5**: Infer torsion order, rational nonvanishing, or the smallest failing degree. -> Those conclusions are not established by the detector.

### Panel 3 - reference implementation and oracle

Applicability: `not_applicable`. At proof-stmt-prop-integral the paper states "Exactness of" the coefficient sequence yields an integral lift and then "We may therefore choose $b\in K_3(S)$ restricting to $\widetilde a_D$." At proof-stmt-lem-opens-2 it uses a filtered-colimit finite-stage existence argument and arbitrary classes supplied by localization. These steps prove existence and nonvanishing of a K-theory class but give no finite effective representation or terminating procedure computing that class. The finite polynomial and valuation identities are supporting checks, not an algorithm for the theorem. A finite computational contract and complete oracle for this construction are ABSENT.

## 6. Applicability limits and search

- **L1** (counterexample_scope): The failure is exhibited at the specified ramified regular local ring and at its initial integral Gersten injection. The result does not establish failure for every regular local domain. Blocked use: ['Treating all regular local domains as counterexamples', 'Extending the construction to arbitrary residue characteristics or K-theory degrees']
- **L2** (unknown): The order of the integral kernel class and its rational image are undetermined. Blocked use: ['Claiming that the class has finite order', 'Claiming rational Gersten failure', 'Claiming rational vanishing of the class']
- **L3** (hypothesis): The constructed ring lies outside the unramified and smooth-over-the-chosen-valuation-ring positive settings discussed in the paper. Blocked use: ['Using this example to refute those positive results under their stated hypotheses']
- **L4** (counterexample_scope): The companion construction uses a different ring; neither result establishes the smallest degree of integral Gersten failure. Blocked use: ['Claiming minimality of the failure degree', 'Identifying the two counterexample rings']
- **L5** (nonconstructive): The proof chooses an integral lift by exactness and then another lift by localization. An executable construction of these K-theory representatives is ABSENT from this argument. Blocked use: ['Presenting polynomial arithmetic as a construction of the integral kernel class', 'Claiming a finite algorithm or complexity bound for constructing the class']
- **L6** (edge_case): Being a fifth power does not make the integral multiplicative-group class vanish; the boundary of the product is killed by the Steinberg relation. Blocked use: ['Declaring the boundary of the Bott-type coefficient class zero solely because the root is a fifth power']
- **L7** (hypothesis): The multiplicative field comparison is stated for finite extensions of the fixed cyclotomic local field and the fixed coefficient class. Blocked use: ['Using this comparison for arbitrary fields or coefficient primes without another proof']
- **L8** (edge_case): The norm valuation detector uses residue degree, not total extension degree. Blocked use: ['Replacing residue degree by total degree in the transfer detector', 'Killing the distinguished transferred class because its field extension is totally ramified']
- **L9** (edge_case): The localized scheme is an inverse limit of affine opens and need not be an open subset of the projective curve. Blocked use: ['Applying an open-subscheme argument directly to the localized scheme without the continuity passage']
- **L10** (hypothesis): Detection uses coherent G-theory on the projective curve without assuming that the curve is smooth. Blocked use: ['Replacing coherent G-theory by vector-bundle K-theory on the curve without proving the required comparison', 'Adding a smoothness hypothesis to the source theorem']
- **L11** (formal_scope): The earlier preprints are contextual antecedents rather than theorem dependencies of this proof. Blocked use: ['Listing results from Feld or Druzhinin as required proof inputs']

## 7. Complete mathematical paper

### Introduction

For a regular local domain $B$ with fraction field $F_B$, the integral Gersten conjecture asks whether, for each $n\geq0$, the augmented complex of Quillen groups $$0\longrightarrow K_n(B)\longrightarrow K_n(F_B)
 \longrightarrow
 \bigoplus_{\operatorname{ht}\mathfrak p=1}
 K_{n-1}(\kappa(\mathfrak p))\longrightarrow\cdots$$ is exact. Here $\kappa(\mathfrak p)$ is the residue field at a prime, and the later maps are the codimension-residue maps. In particular, exactness requires the first map $K_n(B)\to K_n(F_B)$ to be injective. We construct a ring for which this requirement fails in degree three.

Fix a primitive fifth root of unity $\zeta$, and define $$L=\mathbb Q_5(\zeta),\qquad V=\mathcal O_L,\qquad \pi=1-\zeta.$$ Let $\mathcal X\subset\mathbb P^2_V$ be the projective hypersurface $$(X-Z)^5-XY^4+Y^5+\zeta Z^5=0.$$ On its special fiber take $m=[0:0:1]$, and set $A=\mathcal O_{\mathcal X,m}$. We use $G_i$ for coherent $K$-theory and $G_i(-;\mathbb Z/5)$ for its mod-five coefficient groups.

<a id="stmt-thm-main"></a>
<div id="thm:main" class="theorem">

**Theorem 1**. *The ring $A$ is a two-dimensional Noetherian regular local domain of mixed characteristic $(0,5)$, with $\pi\in\mathfrak m_A^2$. There is an integral class $c\in K_3(A)$ such that $$c|_{\mathop{\mathrm{Frac}}A}=0,
 \qquad
 c|_{A[1/\pi]}\bmod5\ne0
 \quad\text{in }G_3(A[1/\pi];\mathbb Z/5).$$ In particular, $K_3(A)\to K_3(\mathop{\mathrm{Frac}}A)$ is not injective.*

</div>

Thus the unrestricted integral Gersten complex can fail already at its initial injection. The class $c$ is not in $5K_3(A)$: any such multiple would have zero coefficient reduction, and hence zero reduction after restriction to $A[1/\pi]$. This conclusion does not determine the order of $c$ or its image in $K_3(A)\otimes\mathbb Q$.

<a id="pm-h-3"></a>
#### Context and earlier results

Gersten asked, among other questions, whether the transfer from the residue field to a discrete valuation ring vanishes (Gersten 1973, Problem 9) <a id="pm-cite-1"></a>[[GerstenProblems](#ref-GerstenProblems)]. Localization connects this question to injectivity of restriction to the fraction field: the image of the residue-field transfer is its kernel. Quillen formulated the general regular-local conjecture and proved it for regular semilocalizations of finite-type algebras over a field (Quillen 1973, sec. 7, Conjecture 5.10 and Theorem 5.11) <a id="pm-cite-2"></a>[[QuillenHigher](#ref-QuillenHigher)]. Sherman established the equicharacteristic DVR case (Sherman 1978, 498) <a id="pm-cite-3"></a>[[Sherman](#ref-Sherman)], and Panin proved the full equicharacteristic case (Panin 2003, Theorem A) <a id="pm-cite-4"></a>[[Panin](#ref-Panin)]. These results place the remaining integral question in mixed characteristic.

In mixed characteristic, smoothness over a DVR and the choice of coefficients lead to different positive results. Gillet and Levine’s relative theory reduces the smooth-over-DVR setting to the DVR case (Gillet and Levine 1987) <a id="pm-cite-5"></a>[[GilletLevine](#ref-GilletLevine)]. Geisser and Levine proved finite-coefficient Gersten exactness for semilocal rings essentially smooth over a DVR, including coefficients of residue-characteristic order (Geisser and Levine 2000, Theorem 8.2) <a id="pm-cite-6"></a>[[GeisserLevine](#ref-GeisserLevine)]. Druzhinin’s preprint states exactness for essentially smooth local Henselian schemes over a DVR (Druzhinin 2025, Theorem 4.5) <a id="pm-cite-7"></a>[[Druzhinin](#ref-Druzhinin)]. A separate result of Skalit gives finite-coefficient exactness for every unramified regular local ring (Skalit 2017, Corollary 5.7) <a id="pm-cite-8"></a>[[Skalit](#ref-Skalit)]; in mixed characteristic $(0,p)$, unramified here means $p\in\mathfrak m\setminus\mathfrak m^2$. His integral result is a reduction to DVRs essentially smooth over $\mathbb Z$ (Skalit 2017, Theorem 5.4) <a id="pm-cite-9"></a>[[Skalit](#ref-Skalit)]. Our ring satisfies $5\in\mathfrak m_A^2$, and its special fiber over the chosen $V$ is singular at $m$; it lies outside the unramified and smooth-over-$V$ settings just described.

The distinction between integral and coefficient classes governs the proof. A finite-coefficient kernel class may have a nonzero coefficient boundary and hence fail to lift integrally. Even when it lifts, its integral generic restriction may be nonzero but divisible by the coefficient prime. Feld’s mod-three degree-two example and its integral divisibility corollary illustrate this second issue (Feld 2026, Theorem 1.1 and Corollary 5.1) <a id="pm-cite-10"></a>[[Feld](#ref-Feld)].

Feld’s construction also provides a methodological antecedent: push a Bott-type class from a divisor and detect its image after a flat cyclotomic base change and inversion of three. The symbol sketch in (Feld 2026, Remark 5.3, footnote 3) <a id="pm-cite-11"></a>[[Feld](#ref-Feld)] gives a related use of symbols. Here we arrange the integral lift on the divisor itself, so support proves integral generic vanishing before the norm test is applied. No result from Feld’s or Druzhinin’s preprint is an input to this proof.

The companion paper *An integral counterexample to Gersten’s conjecture* (OpenAI 2026, Theorem 1.1) <a id="pm-cite-12"></a>[[IntegralDegreeFive](#ref-IntegralDegreeFive)] gives an integral degree-five counterexample at $A_5=(V_0[x,y]/(5+x^4+y^4))_{(5,x,y)}$, where $V_0$ is the ring of integers of the unramified degree-eight extension of $\mathbb Q_5$. Its integral cyclotomic lift and Picard-valued specialization test form a different proof route. Here the quintic model supports a Steinberg lift and a norm valuation test, proved entirely within this paper. The two results concern different rings and do not determine the smallest degree in which integral Gersten injectivity can fail.

<a id="pm-h-4"></a>
#### The route through the proof

Write $C=\mathcal X_L$ for the projective generic curve. On the chart $Z\ne0$ let $x=X/Z$ and $y=Y/Z$. The divisor $y=0$ in $\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}A$ has ring $S=A/yA$. Section <a href="#sec:model" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:model">2</a> proves that $S$ is a DVR with uniformizer $t=x\bmod y$, and that its fraction field $D$ is a totally ramified degree-five extension of $L$ satisfying $$N_{D/L}(t)=\pi,\qquad (1-t)^5=\zeta.$$ For a finite extension $E/L$, write $k_E$ for the residue field of its valuation ring; its residue degree is $[k_E:\mathbb F_5]$. The corresponding closed point $q_0\in C$ has field $\kappa(q_0)=D$ and specializes to $m$, with $[D:L]=5$ but $[k_D:\mathbb F_5]=1$. Every closed point $q$ specializing elsewhere satisfies $5\mid[k_{\kappa(q)}:\mathbb F_5]$. This difference in residue degrees supplies the nonvanishing test.

Write $[u]$ for the integral $K_1$ class of a nonzero field element. Section <a href="#sec:integral" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:integral">3</a> chooses a class $\beta\in K_2(L;\mathbb Z/5)$ whose coefficient boundary in $K_1(L)$ is $[\zeta]$ and constructs an integral lift of $a_D=\beta_D\cdot[t]$ in two steps, where $\beta_D$ is the restriction of $\beta$ to $D$. The identity $(1-t)^5=\zeta$ turns the coefficient boundary of $a_D$ into a multiple of the Steinberg symbol $\{1-t,t\}=0$ in $K_2(D)$ (Matsumoto 1969) <a id="pm-cite-13"></a>[[Matsumoto](#ref-Matsumoto)]. Hence $a_D$ lifts to an integral class in $K_3(D)$. Quillen’s finite-field calculation gives $K_2(\mathbb F_5)=0$ (Quillen 1972) <a id="pm-cite-14"></a>[[QuillenFinite](#ref-QuillenFinite)], so localization then lifts this integral class to $K_3(S)$. Pushforward from $S$ produces $c\in K_3(A)$, and restriction to $\mathop{\mathrm{Frac}}A$ kills it because its support is the divisor $y=0$.

To detect this candidate, Section <a href="#sec:field" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:field">4</a> proves that for every finite extension $E/L$, multiplication by the restriction $\beta_E$ identifies $E^\times/E^{\times5}$ with $K_3(E;\mathbb Z/5)$. This calculation combines the multiplicative motivic-to-$K$ spectral sequence of Friedlander and Suslin, Suslin’s product-preserving comparison, and the norm-residue comparison (Friedlander and Suslin 2002; Suslin 2003; Voevodsky 2011) <a id="pm-cite-15"></a>[[FriedlanderSuslin](#ref-FriedlanderSuslin), [Suslin](#ref-Suslin), [Voevodsky](#ref-Voevodsky)]. The projection formula identifies field transfer with norm. Normalized valuation modulo five consequently kills every transferred coefficient class from a field of residue degree divisible by five, whereas $a_D$ has transferred valuation one because $N_{D/L}(t)=\pi$.

Section <a href="#sec:detection" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:detection">5</a> proves that this integral class is nonzero. The scheme $T=\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}A[1/\pi]$ is an inverse limit of affine opens of $C$ with flat transition maps. These opens contain every closed point specializing to $m$, so their omitted points have residue degree divisible by five. If the coefficient reduction of $c|_T$ vanished, continuity of coherent $K$-theory would make it vanish on one such open. Localization would then express the supported coefficient class $(q_0)_*(\beta_D\cdot[t])$ as a sum of classes supported at omitted points. Proper pushforward to $L$ and the norm valuation give zero for every term on that side and one for $q_0$, a contradiction. We work in coherent $G$-theory on $C$, which need not be smooth.

<a id="sec:model"></a>
### The model and its distinguished divisor

We verify the local ring in Theorem <a href="#thm:main" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:main">1</a> and construct the divisor on which the integral class will be lifted. The special fiber forces a factor of five in residue degrees away from $m$, while the divisor through $m$ has residue degree one and a uniformizer of norm $\pi$. These properties will distinguish its class from classes whose supporting points specialize away from $m$. For a finite extension $E/L$, write $\mathcal O_E$ for its valuation ring and $k_E$ for its residue field.

<a id="stmt-lem-model"></a>
<div id="lem:model" class="lemma">

**Lemma 2**. *The generic fiber $C=\mathcal X_L$ is an integral projective curve. The ring $A=\mathcal O_{\mathcal X,m}$ is a two-dimensional Noetherian regular local domain of mixed characteristic $(0,5)$ with $\pi\in\mathfrak m_A^2$. For every closed point $q\in C$ with residue field $E=\kappa(q)$, the $E$-point extends to $\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}\mathcal O_E\to\mathcal X$. If its closed image is not $m$, then $5\mid[k_E:\mathbb F_5]$.*

<a id="eq:divisor"></a>
<a id="eq:norm-t"></a>
*On the chart $Z\ne0$, set $x=X/Z$, $y=Y/Z$, and let $t$ be the image of $x$ in $S=A/yA$. Then $$\label{eq:divisor}
 S\cong V[t]/\bigl((t-1)^5+\zeta\bigr)$$ is a DVR with uniformizer $t$. Its fraction field $D$ is totally ramified of degree five over $L$, and $$\label{eq:norm-t}
 N_{D/L}(t)=\pi,\qquad \eta=1-t,\qquad \eta^5=\zeta.$$ The equation $Y=0$ cuts out a single closed point $q_0\in C$ with residue field $D$.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-model"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-model</summary>

*Proof.* The polynomial $$\Phi_5(1-T)=T^4-5T^3+10T^2-10T+5$$ is Eisenstein at five. Therefore $\pi=1-\zeta$ is a uniformizer of $V$, its residue field is $\mathbb F_5$, and $5=u\pi^4$ for some $u\in V^\times$. The homogeneous equation $P$ defining $\mathcal X$ reduces to $$\overline P=X^5-XY^4+Y^5.$$ If $\alpha$ is a root of $s^5-s+1$, then Frobenius sends $\alpha$ to $\alpha-1$. Its orbit has length exactly five: the $r$th iterate is $\alpha-r$, which first returns at $r=5$. Thus $s^5-s+1$ is irreducible over $\mathbb F_5$, and so is its homogenization $\overline P$. This remains irreducible on adjoining the independent variable $Z$. A factorization of the primitive homogeneous polynomial $P$ over $L$ would, by Gauss’ lemma, give primitive positive-degree homogeneous factors over $V$ and hence a factorization of $\overline P$. Consequently $C$ is integral. It is a projective plane curve, so it has dimension one.

Put $$F=(x-1)^5-xy^4+y^5+\zeta,\qquad
 R=V[x,y]/(F),\qquad \mathfrak m=(\pi,x,y)\subset R.$$ In the regular local ring $V[x,y]_{(\pi,x,y)}$ of dimension three, $F\equiv-\pi$ modulo the square of the maximal ideal. Thus quotienting by $F$ gives the regular local ring $A=R_{\mathfrak m}$ of dimension two. A regular local ring is a domain. Since $F$ is monic in $x$, the ring $R$ is free over $V[y]$ and hence flat over $V$. Its localization $A$ is also $V$-flat, so $V$ embeds into $A$. Together with $A/\mathfrak m_A=\mathbb F_5$, this proves mixed characteristic $(0,5)$. Expanding $F=0$ gives $$\pi=x^5-5x^4+10x^3-10x^2+5x-xy^4+y^5.$$ In particular, $\pi\in(x,y)A$ and $\mathfrak m_A=(x,y)A$. Since $5$ is divisible by $\pi$, the term $5x$ is in $(x,y)^2A$, as are all remaining terms on the right. Hence $\pi\in\mathfrak m_A^2$. Since $5=u\pi^4$, also $5\in\mathfrak m_A^2$, so $A$ is ramified in the usual mixed-characteristic sense. The reduction of the equation has no linear term at $m$, so the one-dimensional special fiber is singular there; $A$ is not smooth over $V$.

Modulo $y$, the equation becomes $$f(t)=(t-1)^5+\zeta
 =t^5-5t^4+10t^3-10t^2+5t-\pi.$$ This is Eisenstein at $\pi$. The finite free $V$-algebra $B=V[t]/(f)$ is a domain. Every maximal ideal lies over the maximal ideal of $V$, and $B/\pi B=\mathbb F_5[t]/(t^5)$, so $B$ is local. Since $f(t)=0$ implies $\pi\in(t)B$, its maximal ideal is $(t)$. Its dimension is one, making it a DVR. The localization defining $A/yA$ leaves this local ring unchanged, proving <a href="#eq:divisor" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:divisor">[eq:divisor]</a>. Its fraction field has degree five over $L$ and residue degree one, hence total ramification. The constant term gives $$N_{D/L}(t)=(-1)^5f(0)=\pi.$$ The relation $(1-t)^5=\zeta$ also follows from $f(t)=0$. As $S[1/\pi]=D$, the equation $y=0$ on the generic affine chart gives a single closed point. There is no additional point with $Y=Z=0$ on $C$, since substitution in $P$ would force $X=0$. This proves the assertion about $q_0$ on all of $C$.

We have constructed the distinguished point with residue degree one. It remains to establish the residue-degree constraint on points that specialize away from $m$. A closed point of $C$ has residue field finite over $L$. Properness of $\mathcal X/V$ extends its $E$-point to $\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}\mathcal O_E$. On the special fiber $Y=0$ forces $X=0$, so $m$ is the only point with $Y=0$. If the closed image is elsewhere, $X/Y$ in $k_E$ is a root of $s^5-s+1$. Thus $k_E$ contains $\mathbb F_{5^5}$, and $5\mid[k_E:\mathbb F_5]$. ◻

</details>

<a id="sec:integral"></a>
### Lifting on the divisor

The divisor $\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}S\subset\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}A$ now supplies an integral class with zero generic restriction. We first lift a coefficient class to $K_3(D)$ and then extend that lift across the closed point of $S$. Its coefficient image will be tested for nonvanishing only after this construction.

<a id="eq:coeff"></a>
Let $M_5$ be the mod-five Moore spectrum. We use $K_i(B;\mathbb Z/5)=\pi_i(K(B)\wedge M_5)$, and the same convention for $G$-theory. The coefficient sequence is $$\label{eq:coeff}
 K_i(B)\xrightarrow{5}K_i(B)\xrightarrow{\rho_B}K_i(B;\mathbb Z/5)
 \xrightarrow{\partial_{\mathrm{coef}}}K_{i-1}(B)
 \xrightarrow{5}K_{i-1}(B).$$ Here $\rho_B$ denotes coefficient reduction, and $\partial_{\mathrm{coef}}$ denotes the connecting map. Multiplication of a coefficient class by an integral class uses the natural $K(B)$-module structure on $K(B)\wedge M_5$.

For an element $u$ of a field’s multiplicative group, write $[u]$ for its integral $K_1$ class. Since $[\zeta]\in K_1(L)=L^\times$ has order five, exactness permits us to fix $$\beta\in K_2(L;\mathbb Z/5),\qquad
 \partial_{\mathrm{coef}}\beta=[\zeta].$$ For every finite extension $E/L$, write $\beta_E$ for its restriction. The class to be lifted is $$a_D=\beta_D\cdot[t]\in K_3(D;\mathbb Z/5).$$

Resolution identifies $K$-theory with coherent $G$-theory for the regular rings $S$ and $A$; closed-immersion pushforward is taken in $G$-theory (Quillen 1973, sec. 7.1 and §7, paragraph 2.7 and the discussion following (2.8)) <a id="pm-cite-16"></a>[[QuillenHigher](#ref-QuillenHigher)].

<a id="stmt-prop-integral"></a>
<div id="prop:integral" class="proposition">

**Proposition 3**. *There exists $b\in K_3(S)$ whose restriction to $D$, followed by coefficient reduction, is $a_D$. If $j:\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}S\hookrightarrow\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}A$ and $c=j_*b\in G_3(A)=K_3(A)$, then $c|_{\mathop{\mathrm{Frac}}A}=0$. Moreover, for $T=\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}A[1/\pi]$ and $i:\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}D\hookrightarrow T$, $$c|_T\bmod5=i_*a_D\quad\text{in }G_3(T;\mathbb Z/5).$$*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-prop-integral"><summary>Proof of stmt-prop-integral</summary>

*Proof.* The coefficient cofiber sequence is a sequence of $K(D)$-modules. Its connecting map is therefore compatible with multiplication by an integral $K(D)$ class, up to the suspension sign. By <a href="#eq:norm-t" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:norm-t">[eq:norm-t]</a>, $\zeta=\eta^5$ and $t=1-\eta$. Both $\eta$ and $1-\eta$ are nonzero: the first has fifth power $\zeta\ne0$, and the second is the uniformizer $t$ of $S$. Matsumoto’s Steinberg relation (Matsumoto 1969, sec. 5, Lemma 5.6, Theorem 5.10, and Corollary 5.11) <a id="pm-cite-17"></a>[[Matsumoto](#ref-Matsumoto)], with the identification of symbols and products in Quillen $K_2(D)$ (Weibel 2013, 145:III.6.1, IV.1.7.1, IV.1.10.1, and IV.7.2) <a id="pm-cite-18"></a>[[Weibel](#ref-Weibel)], gives <a id="eq:steinberg"></a>$$\label{eq:steinberg}
 \partial_{\mathrm{coef}}a_D
 =\pm[\zeta]\cdot[t]
 =\pm5\{\eta,1-\eta\}=0.$$ The sign does not affect the vanishing. Exactness of <a href="#eq:coeff" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:coeff">[eq:coeff]</a> yields an integral lift $\widetilde a_D\in K_3(D)$ of $a_D$. The boundary $[\zeta]\in K_1(D)[5]$ of $\beta_D$ itself is still nonzero, even though $\zeta=\eta^5$. Being a fifth power makes the class of $\zeta$ vanish in $D^\times/D^{\times5}$, not in the integral group $K_1(D)=D^\times$. The Steinberg relation is what kills the boundary of the product $a_D$.

Localization and dévissage for the DVR $S$ give (Quillen 1973, sec. 7, Proposition 3.2) <a id="pm-cite-19"></a>[[QuillenHigher](#ref-QuillenHigher)] $$K_3(S)\longrightarrow K_3(D)\longrightarrow K_2(\mathbb F_5).$$ The last group is zero by Quillen’s finite-field computation (Quillen 1972) <a id="pm-cite-20"></a>[[QuillenFinite](#ref-QuillenFinite)]. We may therefore choose $b\in K_3(S)$ restricting to $\widetilde a_D$. This is a second lift, distinct from the coefficient lift to $K_3(D)$.

Let $c=j_*b\in G_3(A)=K_3(A)$. Its restriction to $\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}A[1/y]$ is zero because the closed immersion $j$ has empty pullback there. The element $y$ is nonzero in the domain $A$: its quotient $S$ has dimension one, whereas $A$ has dimension two. Hence $y$ becomes invertible in $\mathop{\mathrm{Frac}}A$, and $c|_{\mathop{\mathrm{Frac}}A}=0$ integrally.

Base change of $j$ along $T\to\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}A$ is $i:\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}D\hookrightarrow T$, since $S[1/\pi]=D$. Flat base change for coherent pushforward (Weibel 2013, vol. 145, v. 3.7.2) <a id="pm-cite-21"></a>[[Weibel](#ref-Weibel)] here is the natural isomorphism $$A[1/\pi]\otimes_A M\cong D\otimes_S M$$ for a finite $S$-module $M$, with both sides viewed as $A[1/\pi]$-modules. Thus restriction of scalars commutes with localization. Naturality of coefficient reduction gives the commutative diagram $$\begin{array}{ccc}
 K_3(S)&\xrightarrow{\ j_*\ }&G_3(A)\\
 \big\downarrow&&\big\downarrow\\
 K_3(D;\mathbb Z/5)&\xrightarrow{\ i_*\ }&G_3(T;\mathbb Z/5).
\end{array}$$ Both vertical arrows mean restriction followed by coefficient reduction. The left arrow sends $b$ to $a_D$, which proves the stated identity. ◻

</details>

<a id="sec:field"></a>
### The field comparison and norm detector

Proposition <a href="#prop:integral" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:integral">3</a> constructs an integral class $c$ with zero generic restriction. To prove it nonzero, we will test its coefficient image by proper pushforward to $L$ and a valuation. Localization on the projective curve will introduce arbitrary classes in $K_3(E;\mathbb Z/5)$, for finite extensions $E/L$ arising as closed-point fields. We therefore need a description of all these groups that identifies field transfer with norm.

Keep the class $\beta$ and its restrictions $\beta_E$ fixed as in Section <a href="#sec:integral" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:integral">3</a>. For every finite extension $E/L$, let $k_E$ be its residue field, and normalize $v_E$ so that a uniformizer has valuation one.

<a id="stmt-lem-field"></a>
<div id="lem:field" class="lemma">

<a id="eq:theta"></a>
**Lemma 4** (Multiplicative field comparison). *For $L=\mathbb Q_5(\zeta)$ and the fixed $\beta$ above, every finite extension $E/L$ has an isomorphism $$\label{eq:theta}
 \theta_E:E^\times/E^{\times5}\xrightarrow{\sim}K_3(E;\mathbb Z/5),
 \qquad u\bmod E^{\times5}\longmapsto\beta_E\cdot[u].$$ The degree-three motivic edge identifies the target with $H^1_{\mathrm{et}}(E,\mu_5^{\otimes2})$. Under this edge, the displayed map is Kummer reduction followed by cup product with the nonzero weight-one edge image $\alpha_E\in H^0(E,\mu_5)$ of $\beta_E$.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-field"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-field</summary>

*Proof.* We first identify the degree-three group, then locate the image of $\beta_E$ in degree two, and finally compute multiplication by $\beta_E$ on integral $K_1$. The strongly convergent motivic-to-$K$ spectral sequence for a characteristic-zero field is <a id="eq:motivic"></a>$$\label{eq:motivic}
 E_2^{a,b}=H^{a-b}(E,\mathbb Z/5(-b))
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad K_{-a-b}(E;\mathbb Z/5).$$ It has a pairing with the integral spectral sequence that induces the integral/coefficient $K$-module product on the abutment (Friedlander and Suslin 2002, Theorem 16.2 and Appendix C.2–C.3) <a id="pm-cite-22"></a>[[FriedlanderSuslin](#ref-FriedlanderSuslin)]. The comparison with motivic complexes preserves products (Suslin 2003, Lemma 3.4 and Theorem 6.1) <a id="pm-cite-23"></a>[[Suslin](#ref-Suslin)]. Write $s=a-b$ and $j=-b$, so a term $H^s(E,\mathbb Z/5(j))$ has total $K$-degree $2j-s$. Negative weights vanish. For $j\geq0$, the field motivic groups vanish for $s>j$, and the norm-residue and motivic–étale comparison theorems identify them, for every $s\leq j$, with $$H^s_{\mathrm{et}}(E,\mu_5^{\otimes j});$$ see (Voevodsky 2011, Theorems 6.16–6.17) <a id="pm-cite-24"></a>[[Voevodsky](#ref-Voevodsky)] and (Weibel 2013, 145:VI.4.1–4.2) <a id="pm-cite-25"></a>[[Weibel](#ref-Weibel)]. In particular, the groups vanish for $s<0$. These comparisons apply because $5$ is invertible in $E$.

<a id="eq:degree-three-edge"></a>
The $5$-cohomological dimension of a finite extension of $\mathbb Q_5$ is two (Weibel 2013, VI.§7, before Proposition 7.3) <a id="pm-cite-26"></a>[[Weibel](#ref-Weibel)]. Thus the only potentially nonzero terms satisfy $0\leq s\leq\min(j,2)$. For $r\geq2$ a differential changes $(s,j)$ to $(s+2r-1,j+r-1)$, so none can join two nonzero terms. Total degree three has the single term $(s,j)=(1,2)$, yielding the natural identification $$\label{eq:degree-three-edge}
 K_3(E;\mathbb Z/5)\simeq H^1_{\mathrm{et}}(E,\mu_5^{\otimes2}).$$ In particular, this $K$-group is killed by five. In total degree two the weight-one quotient of the filtration is $H^0(E,\mu_5)$; the only other possible piece is $H^2(E,\mu_5^{\otimes2})$ in weight two.

Let $\alpha_E$ be the weight-one image of $\beta_E$. To see that it is nonzero, restrict to an algebraic closure $\overline E$. The same comparison there has only $s=0$ terms, so its total degree-two group has just the weight-one piece $H^0(\overline E,\mu_5)$. Matsumoto’s presentation of field $K_2$ ((Matsumoto 1969, sec. 5); see also (Weibel 2013, 145:III.6.1) <a id="pm-cite-27"></a>[[Weibel](#ref-Weibel)]) gives $K_2(\overline E)/5=0$: every symbol $\{u,v\}$ equals $5\{r,v\}$ after choosing $r^5=u$. The coefficient sequence therefore identifies $K_2(\overline E;\mathbb Z/5)$ with $K_1(\overline E)[5]$ through $\partial_{\mathrm{coef}}$. The restriction of $\beta_E$ is nonzero, since its boundary is the primitive root $[\zeta]$. Naturality of the filtration shows that $\alpha_E$ has nonzero image in $H^0(\overline E,\mu_5)$. It is consequently a generator of $H^0(E,\mu_5)$; only this nonvanishing is needed.

It remains to determine the actual product from these filtration pieces. Integral $K_1(E)=E^\times$ has only the weight-one piece $H^1(E,\mathbb Z(1))$: on the diagonal $2j-s=1$, weights $j\geq2$ give $s>j$ and vanish, weight zero gives $H^{-1}(E,\mathbb Z(0))=0$, and negative weights vanish. The identification $\mathbb Z(1)\simeq\mathbb G_m[-1]$ identifies reduction of $[u]$ with its Kummer class in $H^1(E,\mu_5)=E^\times/E^{\times5}$.

Write $F^j$ for the decreasing weight filtration. The preceding calculations give $$F^1K_1(E)=K_1(E),\quad F^2K_1(E)=0,\qquad
 F^2K_3(E;\mathbb Z/5)=K_3(E;\mathbb Z/5),\quad F^3K_3(E;\mathbb Z/5)=0.$$ The class $\beta_E$ lies in $F^1K_2(E;\mathbb Z/5)$ and has image $\alpha_E$ in its quotient by $F^2$. The mixed filtered pairing satisfies $$F^2K_2(E;\mathbb Z/5)\cdot F^1K_1(E)
 \subseteq F^3K_3(E;\mathbb Z/5)=0.$$ Thus the possible weight-two part of $\beta_E$ contributes nothing: the product $\beta_E\cdot[u]$ is determined by $\alpha_E$ and the weight-one class of $[u]$. Product compatibility identifies it, under <a href="#eq:degree-three-edge" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:degree-three-edge">[eq:degree-three-edge]</a>, with $\alpha_E\cup[u]$. Since $\alpha_E$ generates the constant one-dimensional $\mathbb F_5$-module $\mu_5$, this cup product is an isomorphism. The product factors through fifth powers because its target is killed by five and $[u^5]=5[u]$. This proves <a href="#eq:theta" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:theta">[eq:theta]</a> with its asserted multiplicative identification. ◻

</details>

In particular, the coefficient class already constructed on the divisor is $a_D=\theta_D(t)$. We now relate the comparison maps for different extensions of $L$.

<a id="stmt-lem-norm-detector"></a>
<div id="lem:norm-detector" class="lemma">

<a id="eq:transfer"></a>
**Lemma 5** (Norm detector). *For every finite extension $E/L$ and every $u\in E^\times$, $$\label{eq:transfer}
 \mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}_{E/L}\theta_E(u)=\theta_L(N_{E/L}u).$$ Consequently $$\lambda_L=(v_L\bmod5)\circ\theta_L^{-1}:
 K_3(L;\mathbb Z/5)\longrightarrow\mathbb Z/5$$ satisfies $$\lambda_L\bigl(\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}_{E/L}(a)\bigr)=0
 \quad\text{for every }a\in K_3(E;\mathbb Z/5)
 \quad\text{if }5\mid[k_E:k_L].$$*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-norm-detector"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-norm-detector</summary>

*Proof.* Restriction of scalars obeys the tensor projection formula at the level of $K$-theory spectra (Weibel 2013, vol. 145, v. 3.3.2) <a id="pm-cite-28"></a>[[Weibel](#ref-Weibel)]. Smashing its base-field factor with $M_5$ gives the mixed pairing identity $$\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}_{E/L}(\beta_E\cdot[u])
 =\beta\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}_{E/L}[u]
 =\beta\cdot[N_{E/L}u].$$ The second equality uses the norm description of field $K_1$ transfer (Weibel 2013, 145:III.1.7.1 and the following paragraph) <a id="pm-cite-29"></a>[[Weibel](#ref-Weibel)]. This proves <a href="#eq:transfer" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:transfer">[eq:transfer]</a>.

For normalized valuations, the norm of the prime ideal of $E$ is the $[k_E:k_L]$th power of the prime ideal of $L$. Hence $$v_L(N_{E/L}u)=[k_E:k_L]v_E(u).$$ Every $a\in K_3(E;\mathbb Z/5)$ has the form $\theta_E(u)$ by Lemma <a href="#lem:field" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:field">4</a>. Applying the formula to that $u$ proves the assertion for all $a$. The factor is the residue degree, not the total degree $[E:L]$. ◻

</details>

<a id="sec:detection"></a>
### Detection on the projective curve

Proposition <a href="#prop:integral" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:integral">3</a> has constructed an integral class $c$ with $c|_{\mathop{\mathrm{Frac}}A}=0$ and coefficient image $i_*a_D$ on $$T=\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}A[1/\pi].$$ By Lemma <a href="#lem:field" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:field">4</a>, $a_D=\beta_D\cdot[t]=\theta_D(t)$. To prove its pushforward $i_*a_D$ nonzero, we extend the supported class to the projective curve $C$, where proper pushforward permits the norm test. The scheme $T$ is an inverse limit of affine opens of $C$ with flat transition maps, and need not itself be an open subset. The following lemma makes that passage explicit.

<a id="stmt-lem-opens"></a>
<div id="lem:opens" class="lemma">

<a id="eq:continuity"></a>
**Lemma 6**. *Let $\Sigma=R\setminus\mathfrak m$, where $R=V[x,y]/(F)$ and $\mathfrak m=(\pi,x,y)$. For $h\in\Sigma$ put $$U_h=\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}R[1/\pi,1/h]\subset C.$$ Then the refinement maps $U_{hk}\to U_h$ are affine and flat, and $$\label{eq:continuity}
 T=\varprojlim_{h\in\Sigma}U_h,
 \qquad
 \varinjlim_{h\in\Sigma}G_3(U_h;\mathbb Z/5)
   \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }G_3(T;\mathbb Z/5).$$ Every $U_h$ contains $q_0$. Its complement in $C$ is a finite set of closed points $q$, and their residue fields $E_q=\kappa(q)$ satisfy $5\mid[k_{E_q}:\mathbb F_5]$.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-opens"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-opens</summary>

*Proof.* The multiplicative set $\Sigma$ is directed by taking products: $hk$ gives a common refinement of $h$ and $k$. Transitivity of localization gives $$\varinjlim_{h\in\Sigma}R[1/\pi,1/h]
 =R_{\mathfrak m}[1/\pi]=A[1/\pi].$$ Each refinement is a principal localization, so the corresponding scheme map is affine and flat. The stages and their limit are affine Noetherian schemes, hence separated. Quillen’s continuity theorem, in its coherent $K'$ conclusion (Quillen 1973, sec. 7, Proposition 2.2) <a id="pm-cite-30"></a>[[QuillenHigher](#ref-QuillenHigher)], therefore applies to their integral $G$-groups.

To pass to coefficients, use the natural short exact sequence $$0\longrightarrow G_3(Y)/5G_3(Y)
 \longrightarrow G_3(Y;\mathbb Z/5)
 \longrightarrow G_2(Y)[5]\longrightarrow0$$ for each stage $Y=U_h$ and for $Y=T$. Filtered colimits of abelian groups are exact, and thus commute with the quotient by five and the kernel of multiplication by five. Integral continuity in degrees three and two identifies the outer terms in the resulting comparison of short exact sequences. It consequently identifies the middle terms, proving the coefficient assertion in <a href="#eq:continuity" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:continuity">[eq:continuity]</a>.

Since $h\notin\mathfrak m$, its image in the local divisor ring $S$ is a unit. Thus $q_0=\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}D$ belongs to $U_h$. More generally, consider a closed point $q\in C$ with residue field $E$ whose extension $\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}\mathcal O_E\to\mathcal X$ specializes to $m$. The inverse image of the chart $Z\ne0$ contains the closed point of $\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}\mathcal O_E$, and hence is all of $\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}\mathcal O_E$. On that chart the reduction of $h$ at $m$ is nonzero. Its pullback to $\mathcal O_E$ is therefore a unit, so $q$ lies in $U_h$. It follows that each point omitted from $U_h$ specializes away from $m$. Lemma <a href="#lem:model" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:model">2</a> then gives $5\mid[k_E:\mathbb F_5]$. Finally, $C$ is an integral projective curve and $U_h$ is nonempty, so its closed complement consists of finitely many closed points. ◻

</details>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-opens-2"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-opens</summary>

*Proof of Theorem <a href="#thm:main" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:main">1</a>.* Take the integral class $c\in K_3(A)$ constructed in Proposition <a href="#prop:integral" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:integral">3</a>, and put $$w=(q_0)_*\theta_D(t)\in G_3(C;\mathbb Z/5).$$ Coherent $G$-theory provides proper pushforward even when $C$ is singular. The point immersion is finite, and the structural map $p:C\to\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}L$ is proper with $C$ carrying the ample line bundle $\mathcal O_C(1)$. Coherent pushforward and its composition law therefore apply to both maps (Quillen 1973, sec. 7, paragraph 2.7 and the discussion following (2.8)) <a id="pm-cite-31"></a>[[QuillenHigher](#ref-QuillenHigher)].

Flat base change for the point immersion on each $U_h$ (Weibel 2013, vol. 145, v. 3.7.2) <a id="pm-cite-32"></a>[[Weibel](#ref-Weibel)], followed by <a href="#eq:continuity" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:continuity">[eq:continuity]</a>, identifies $w|_T$ with the pushforward of $\theta_D(t)$ along $\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}D\hookrightarrow T$. By Proposition <a href="#prop:integral" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:integral">3</a>, this is precisely the coefficient reduction of $c|_T$.

<a id="eq:omitted-relation"></a>
Suppose that $w|_T=0$. The element $w|_{U_1}$ represents this class in the filtered colimit <a href="#eq:continuity" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:continuity">[eq:continuity]</a>. A zero element in that colimit becomes zero at a finite stage, so $w|_{U_h}=0$ for some $h\in\Sigma$. Give $Z_h=C\setminus U_h$ its reduced closed structure. Apply Moore coefficients to coherent localization and dévissage at these closed points (Quillen 1973, sec. 7, Proposition 3.2 and paragraph 3.4) <a id="pm-cite-33"></a>[[QuillenHigher](#ref-QuillenHigher)]. The resulting exact segment is $$\bigoplus_{q\in Z_h}K_3(E_q;\mathbb Z/5)
 \longrightarrow G_3(C;\mathbb Z/5)
 \longrightarrow G_3(U_h;\mathbb Z/5).$$ Consequently there are classes $a_q\in K_3(E_q;\mathbb Z/5)$ such that $$\label{eq:omitted-relation}
 w=\sum_{q\in Z_h}q_*a_q.$$ These are arbitrary coefficient classes supplied by localization. Lemma <a href="#lem:field" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:field">4</a> applies to every finite extension $E_q/L$, so its surjectivity writes each of them as $a_q=\theta_{E_q}(u_q)$ for some $u_q\in E_q^\times$.

Apply the proper pushforward $p_*:G_3(C;\mathbb Z/5)\to K_3(L;\mathbb Z/5)$ to <a href="#eq:omitted-relation" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:omitted-relation">[eq:omitted-relation]</a>. For every closed point, the composite $p_*q_*$ is the field transfer. Lemma <a href="#lem:opens" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:opens">6</a> gives $5\mid[k_{E_q}:\mathbb F_5]$, and Lemma <a href="#lem:norm-detector" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:norm-detector">5</a> therefore gives $$\lambda_L\bigl(p_*q_*a_q\bigr)
 =v_L\bigl(N_{E_q/L}(u_q)\bigr)\bmod5
 =[k_{E_q}:\mathbb F_5]v_{E_q}(u_q)\bmod5=0.$$ Thus <a href="#eq:omitted-relation" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:omitted-relation">[eq:omitted-relation]</a> forces $\lambda_L(p_*w)=0$. On the other hand, the distinguished point has residue field $D$, and its parameter satisfies $N_{D/L}(t)=\pi$. The same transfer formula yields $$\lambda_L(p_*w)
 =\lambda_L\bigl(\operatorname{Tr}_{D/L}\theta_D(t)\bigr)
 =v_L\bigl(N_{D/L}(t)\bigr)\bmod5
 =v_L(\pi)\bmod5=1.$$ This contradiction proves $w|_T\ne0$. Hence the coefficient reduction of $c|_T$ is nonzero, so the integral class $c\in K_3(A)$ is itself nonzero. Its image at the fraction field is zero by Proposition <a href="#prop:integral" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:integral">3</a>; together with the properties of $A$ in Lemma <a href="#lem:model" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:model">2</a>, this proves the theorem. ◻

</details>
