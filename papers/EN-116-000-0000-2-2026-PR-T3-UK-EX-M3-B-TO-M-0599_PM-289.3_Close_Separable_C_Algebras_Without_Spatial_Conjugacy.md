## 1. Abstract and result contract

> We disprove the separable <i>C</i><sup>∗</sup>-algebraic spatial form of the Kadison–Kastler conjecture. For every <i>ε</i> &gt; 0, we construct unital, norm-separable <i>C</i><sup>∗</sup>-algebras on a common separable complex Hilbert space, with the same identity and Kadison–Kastler distance less than <i>ε</i>, that are not conjugate by any unitary. The two algebras have the same von Neumann closure.

- **main** (counterexample, [source](#stmt-thm-main)): For every $\varepsilon>0$ there are a separable complex Hilbert space $H$ and unital, norm-separable $C^*$-algebras $A,B\subseteq\mathcal B(H)$ with common identity $I_H$ such that $$d_{\mathrm{KK}}(A,B)<\varepsilon,\qquad A''=B'',$$ but no unitary $u\in\mathcal B(H)$ satisfies $uAu^*=B$.
  - Explanation: Arbitrarily close norm-separable represented algebras can share a bicommutant while admitting no spatial unitary conjugacy.
- **sequences** (construction, [source](#stmt-prop-sequences)): Let $L$ be a nonzero separable Hilbert space, let $D\subseteq\mathcal B(L)$ be a unital norm-separable $C^*$-algebra with $Z(D)=\mathbb CI_L$, and let $P,Q\subseteq D$ be unital $C^*$-subalgebras containing $I_L$. On $H=\bigoplus_{n\ge1}L$, define $$\label{eq:sequence-definition} \mathcal E_D(P)=\{(x_n)_{n\ge1}:x_n\in D,\ x_n\to p\in P\text{ in norm}\},$$ and define $\mathcal E_D(Q)$ similarly. Then:* 1. *These are unital norm-separable $C^*$-algebras with common identity, and $$\label{eq:distance-equality} d_{\mathrm{KK}}(\mathcal E_D(P),\mathcal E_D(Q))=d_{\mathrm{KK}}(P,Q).$$* 2. *If a unitary conjugates $\mathcal E_D(P)$ onto $\mathcal E_D(Q)$, there are unitaries $U_n\in\mathcal B(L)$ with $U_nDU_n^*=D$ such that, for every $b\in Q$, some $p_b\in P$ satisfies $$\label{eq:pointwise-normalizers} \left\\|b-U_np_bU_n^*\right\\|\longrightarrow0.$$ The same sequence $(U_n)$ works for every $b$.* 3. *The two algebras have the same commutant, and hence the same von Neumann closure.
  - Explanation: The convergent-sequence construction preserves distance and commutant, and forces simultaneous normalizer approximation under spatial conjugacy.
- **invariant** (conditional_result, [source](#stmt-lem-invariant)): Let $P,Q\subseteq D\subseteq\mathcal B(L)$ be unital $C^*$-algebras. The identity in Definition <a href="#def:tensor" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="def:tensor">3</a> is preserved by conjugating $P$ with any unitary normalizing $D$. If $(U_n)$ is a sequence of unitaries in $\mathcal B(L)$ satisfying $U_nDU_n^*=D$ and every $b\in Q$ admits the approximation <a href="#eq:pointwise-normalizers" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:pointwise-normalizers">[eq:pointwise-normalizers]</a>, then the identity for $P$ implies the identity for $Q$.
  - Explanation: The relative tensor-norm identity survives normalizer conjugation and simultaneous finite-family norm limits.
- **obstruction** (conditional_result, [source](#stmt-cor-obstruction)): Under the hypotheses of Proposition <a href="#prop:sequences" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:sequences">2</a>, suppose that $P$ has the spatial tensor-norm identity relative to $D$, whereas $Q$ does not. Then $\mathcal E_D(P)$ and $\mathcal E_D(Q)$ are not spatially conjugate. If $W\in\mathcal B(L)$ is unitary and $WPW^*=Q$, then nevertheless $$d_{\mathrm{KK}}(\mathcal E_D(P),\mathcal E_D(Q))\le2\left\\|W-I_L\right\\|.$$
  - Explanation: Different relative tensor-norm identities obstruct spatial conjugacy of sequence algebras despite a perturbation bound.
- **tree** (construction, [source](#stmt-lem-tree)): For every $0<t<1$, there is a unitary representation $\pi_t:G\to\mathcal B(K)$ such that, with $r=\sqrt{1-t^2}$, $$\begin{aligned} \left\langle \pi_t(s_i)\delta_e,\delta_e\right\rangle&=t\quad(i\ge1),\label{eq:coefficient}\\ \sup_{g\in G}\left\\|\pi_t(g)-\lambda(g)\right\\|&\le c(t),\qquad c(t)=2(1-r)+\frac{t}{1-t}+t.\label{eq:uniform} \end{aligned}$$ In particular, $c(t)\to0$ as $t\downarrow0$.
  - Explanation: The tree deformation gives a unitary representation with a uniform perturbation estimate.
- **absorption** (construction, [source](#stmt-lem-absorption)): Let $\sigma$ be a unitary representation of $G$ on a nonzero Hilbert space $J$. The operator $$W_\sigma(\delta_x\otimes v)=\delta_x\otimes\sigma(x)v \quad(x\in G,\ v\in J)$$ is unitary on $K\otimes J$ and satisfies $$\label{eq:absorption} W_\sigma(\lambda(g)\otimes I_J)W_\sigma^* =\lambda(g)\otimes\sigma(g)\quad(g\in G).$$
  - Explanation: An explicitly defined direct-sum unitary absorbs an auxiliary representation.
- **ambient** (construction, [source](#stmt-lem-ambient)): The algebra $D$ in <a href="#eq:D" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:D">[eq:D]</a> has scalar center, and $$\label{eq:commutant} D'=\{Y\otimes I_K:Y\in\lambda(G)'\}.$$ There is a unitary $W\in\mathcal B(L)$ such that $$\label{eq:close-W} WP_0W^*=P_t,\qquad \left\\|W-I_L\right\\|\le c(t).$$
  - Explanation: The ambient algebra has scalar center and an explicit commutant; a small unitary intertwines the inclusions.
- **good** (conditional_result, [source](#stmt-lem-good-inclusion)): The inclusion $P_0\subseteq D$ has the spatial tensor-norm identity.
  - Explanation: The regular inclusion has the relative tensor-norm identity.
- **free-norm** (bound, [source](#stmt-lem-free-norm)): For every positive integer $m$, $$\label{eq:free-norm} \left\\|\sum_{i=1}^m\lambda(s_i)\right\\|\le2\sqrt m.$$
  - Explanation: The regular generator sum has the stated square-root upper bound.
- **bad** (conditional_result, [source](#stmt-lem-bad-inclusion)): For every $0<t<1$, the inclusion $P_t\subseteq D$ fails the spatial tensor-norm identity.
  - Explanation: The deformed inclusion fails the identity by a finite-family norm separation.

## 2. Definitions, validity and bounds

- **spatial tensor-norm identity** ([source](#stmt-def-tensor)): For a unital inclusion $P\subseteq D\subseteq\mathcal B(L)$, say that $P$ has the *spatial tensor-norm identity relative to $D$* if $$\label{eq:tensor-property} \left\\|\sum_{i=1}^m p_i z_i\right\\|_{\mathcal B(L)} =\left\\|\sum_{i=1}^m p_i\otimes z_i\right\\|_{\mathcal B(L\otimes L)}$$ for every positive integer $m$, all $p_1,\ldots,p_m\in P$, and all $z_1,\ldots,z_m\in D'$.
- **Kadison–Kastler distance** ([source](#sec:intro)): For $C^*$-algebras $A,B\subseteq\mathcal B(H)$, write $A_1$ and $B_1$ for their closed unit balls. Their Kadison–Kastler distance is $$\label{eq:kk} d_{\mathrm{KK}}(A,B)=\max\left\{ \sup_{a\in A_1}\inf_{b\in B_1}\left\\|a-b\right\\|,\, \sup_{b\in B_1}\inf_{a\in A_1}\left\\|b-a\right\\|\right\}.$$
- **commutant** ([source](#pm-h-3)): Write $D'$ for the commutant of $D$ on $L$, consisting of the bounded operators commuting with every element of $D$.
- **free group and regular Hilbert space** ([source](#sec:tree)): Let $G$ be the free group on the countable set $\{s_1,s_2,\ldots\}$, let $e$ denote its identity, and put $K=\ell^2(G)$.

## 4. Engineering Triptych

### Panel 1 - epistemic contract

- **spatial-obstruction**: Arbitrarily small Kadison–Kastler distance and equality of von Neumann closures do not guarantee spatial unitary conjugacy.
- **relative-invariant**: The spatial tensor-norm identity is preserved under ambient normalizers and simultaneous finite-family norm limits.
- **path-matrices**: The finite path recursion yields X=R(I-tS*)^{-1} and T=XR′-tS on the path span.

### Panel 2 - negative knowledge

- **closure-equivalence**: Infer spatial conjugacy from equal closures and small distance. -> Theorem 1 excludes every implementing unitary.
- **scope-extension**: Conclude abstract nonisomorphism or a von Neumann algebra counterexample. -> Neither conclusion is asserted by the source.
- **global-X**: Extend the finite path X to a bounded global change of basis on the infinitely branching tree. -> Root coefficients accumulate over infinitely many generator vectors.
- **normalizer-confusion**: Treat the close intertwiner W as an ambient normalizer. -> This would contradict the tensor-norm obstruction.

### Panel 3 - reference implementation and oracle

Applicability: `constructive`. Finite path matrix construction only (eq:X, eq:path-action); infinite-dimensional spatial nonconjugacy is outside its scope.

```python
# INPUTS: JSON [a,b,d,l] of integers on stdin.
# PRECONDITIONS: a,b,d positive, a*a+b*b=d*d, 0<=l<=8; t=a/d, r=b/d.
# OUTPUTS: JSON rational-entry matrices X and T for the path h_0,...,h_l.
# TIME: O((l+1)^3) exact rational operations using dense matrix inversion.
# SPACE: O((l+1)^2) rational entries; bit sizes depend on d and l.
# ARITHMETIC: python-flint fmpq and fmpq_mat, exact; no floating point.
# FAILURE: invalid input raises ValueError; no infinite-space certificate is returned.
import sys, json
from flint import fmpq, fmpq_mat
v = json.loads(sys.stdin.read())
if len(v) != 4 or any(type(z) is not int for z in v):
    raise ValueError('four integers required')
a,b,d,l = v
if not (a>0 and b>0 and d>0 and a*a+b*b==d*d and 0<=l<=8):
    raise ValueError('invalid rational circle point or path length')
t,r = fmpq(a,d), fmpq(b,d)
n = l+1
I = fmpq_mat([[int(i==j) for j in range(n)] for i in range(n)])
S = fmpq_mat([[int(i==j+1) for j in range(n)] for i in range(n)])
R = fmpq_mat([[((1 if i==0 else r) if i==j else 0) for j in range(n)] for i in range(n)])
Rp = fmpq_mat([[((1 if i==l else r) if i==j else 0) for j in range(n)] for i in range(n)])
X = R * (I-t*S.transpose()).inv()
T = X*Rp-t*S
encode = lambda A: [[str(A[i,j]) for j in range(n)] for i in range(n)]
print(json.dumps({'X':encode(X),'T':encode(T)},sort_keys=True))
```

## 6. Applicability limits and search

- **scope-spatial-only** (counterexample_scope): The theorem excludes spatial unitary conjugacy; it establishes neither abstract nonisomorphism nor a counterexample for von Neumann algebras. Blocked use: ['Inferring abstract nonisomorphism.', 'Claiming failure of a von Neumann algebra perturbation conjecture.']
- **scope-nuclearity** (counterexample_scope): The theorem imposes no nuclearity assumption. It cannot be used to refute the cited positive result for sufficiently close separable nuclear algebras. Blocked use: ['Advertising the examples as separable nuclear counterexamples.']
- **scope-represented-setting** (hypothesis): The main conclusion concerns represented unital norm-separable algebras on a separable complex Hilbert space with a common identity. Blocked use: ['Removing the represented-algebra setting or treating the result as a classification of abstract algebras.']
- **reduction-hypotheses** (hypothesis): The convergent-sequence reduction requires a nonzero separable Hilbert space, a unital norm-separable ambient algebra with scalar center, and unital subalgebras containing its identity. Blocked use: ['Using the same minimal-central-projection argument for an ambient algebra with nonscalar center without a new proof.', 'Dropping the common ambient inclusion or unitality hypotheses.']
- **simultaneous-normalizers** (approximation): The approximation uses a single sequence of ambient-algebra normalizers for every element. Independently chosen sequences do not supply the simultaneous finite-family limit used in the proof. Blocked use: ['Substituting unrelated elementwise approximating normalizer sequences.']
- **relative-invariant** (hypothesis): The tensor-norm identity is relative to the ambient inclusion and uses elements of its commutant; it is not asserted to be an invariant of the abstract subalgebra alone. Blocked use: ['Comparing arbitrary algebras without a specified common ambient algebra and commutant.']
- **normalizer-versus-close-unitary** (edge_case): The unitary proving closeness need not normalize the ambient algebra. Under the tensor-norm obstruction, a normalizing unitary cannot conjugate the two inclusions. Blocked use: ['Applying normalizer invariance to the closeness unitary without establishing that it normalizes the ambient algebra.']
- **deformation-open-regime** (edge_case): The deformed representation and bad-inclusion conclusion are stated for the open parameter regime; endpoint substitution is not covered. Blocked use: ['Applying the deformation formulas at the singular endpoint.', 'Claiming that the undeformed inclusion fails the tensor-norm identity.']
- **finite-path-only** (approximation): The finite path operator used in the estimate must not be promoted to a bounded global change of basis on the infinitely branching tree. Blocked use: ['Building the representation by assuming a globally bounded map sending basis vectors to the recursively defined vectors.']

<a id="sec:intro"></a>
### Introduction

For $C^*$-algebras $A,B\subseteq\mathcal B(H)$, write $A_1$ and $B_1$ for their closed unit balls. Their Kadison–Kastler distance is <a id="eq:kk"></a>$$\label{eq:kk}
d_{\mathrm{KK}}(A,B)=\max\left\{
\sup_{a\in A_1}\inf_{b\in B_1}\left\|a-b\right\|,\,
\sup_{b\in B_1}\inf_{a\in A_1}\left\|b-a\right\|\right\}.$$ The spatial form of the perturbation problem asks whether sufficiently close algebras must be related by $uAu^*=B$ for a unitary $u\in\mathcal B(H)$. Kadison and Kastler initiated this problem in <a id="pm-cite-1"></a>[[KK72](#ref-KK72)]. In its separable $C^*$-algebraic form, one asks for a constant $\delta>0$ valid for all norm-separable $C^*$-algebras on separable Hilbert spaces; no bound on $\left\|u-I\right\|$ is required. The following theorem resolves negatively this separable $C^*$-algebraic spatial form of the Kadison–Kastler conjecture, even for unital algebras with a common identity.

<a id="stmt-thm-main"></a>
<div id="thm:main" class="theorem">

**Theorem 1**. *For every $\varepsilon>0$ there are a separable complex Hilbert space $H$ and unital, norm-separable $C^*$-algebras $A,B\subseteq\mathcal B(H)$ with common identity $I_H$ such that $$d_{\mathrm{KK}}(A,B)<\varepsilon,\qquad A''=B'',$$ but no unitary $u\in\mathcal B(H)$ satisfies $uAu^*=B$.*

</div>

The scope of this statement is important. Johnson constructed arbitrarily close separable nuclear algebras for which implementing unitaries cannot be chosen close to the identity <a id="pm-cite-2"></a>[[Johnson82](#ref-Johnson82)]; those examples are nevertheless spatially conjugate. Choi and Christensen constructed arbitrarily close nonisomorphic algebras <a id="pm-cite-3"></a>[[CC83](#ref-CC83)], but norm nonseparability is essential to those examples. These distinctions, and the resulting separable spatial formulation, are discussed in <a id="pm-cite-4"></a>[[CSSWW10](#ref-CSSWW10)]. In the positive direction, Christensen, Sinclair, Smith, White, and Winter proved spatial conjugacy for sufficiently close separable nuclear $C^*$-algebras on a separable Hilbert space <a id="pm-cite-5"></a>[[CSSWW10](#ref-CSSWW10), Theorem 4]; see <a id="pm-cite-6"></a>[[CSSWW12](#ref-CSSWW12), Theorem 5.4] for the full proof. Theorem <a href="#thm:main" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:main">1</a> imposes no nuclearity assumption. It makes no assertion of abstract nonisomorphism and does not give a counterexample for von Neumann algebras.

<a id="pm-h-3"></a>
###### The construction.

We first isolate a way to turn an obstruction for inclusions into an obstruction to arbitrary spatial conjugacy. Let $D\subseteq\mathcal B(L)$ be unital, norm-separable, and have scalar center. For unital subalgebras $P,Q\subseteq D$, consider the algebra $$\mathcal E_D(P)=\{(x_n):x_n\in D,\ x_n\longrightarrow p\in P
\text{ in norm}\}$$ acting diagonally on $\bigoplus_{n\ge1}L$. Changing $P$ changes only the condition at the limit. The coordinate projections are intrinsic minimal central projections of this algebra. Consequently, a unitary conjugating $\mathcal E_D(P)$ onto $\mathcal E_D(Q)$ must permute the coordinates and normalize $D$ on every coordinate. Pulling back constant sequences then forces each finite family in $Q$ to be a simultaneous norm limit of families in unitary conjugates of $P$ by normalizers of $D$.

Write $D'$ for the commutant of $D$ on $L$, consisting of the bounded operators commuting with every element of $D$. The obstruction we use is the equality <a id="eq:intro-test"></a>$$\label{eq:intro-test}
\left\|\sum_i p_i z_i\right\|_{\mathcal B(L)}
=\left\|\sum_i p_i\otimes z_i\right\|_{\mathcal B(L\otimes L)},
\qquad p_i\in P,\quad z_i\in D'.$$ This equality is invariant under normalizers of $D$ and passes to simultaneous finite-family norm limits. We construct very close subalgebras $P_0,P_t\subseteq D$ for which it holds for $P_0$ but fails for $P_t$. In fact, $P_0$ and $P_t$ themselves are conjugate by a unitary close to the identity. The obstruction concerns their positions inside $D$, and the sequence construction makes it an obstruction to every spatial conjugacy of the resulting algebras.

For the close inclusions we use a real-parameter deformation of the regular representation of the free group on countably many generators. This representation-theoretic ingredient belongs to the Pytlik–Szwarc family <a id="pm-cite-7"></a>[[PS86](#ref-PS86)]; its uniform-topology behavior at the regular representation is discussed by Burger, Ozawa, and Thom <a id="pm-cite-8"></a>[[BOT13](#ref-BOT13), Theorem 4.6]. We give a direct tree construction and prove the uniform estimate needed here. The additional mechanism is the combination of the relative tensor-norm test with the convergent-sequence reduction. The reduction is stated separately, so it can be used for other pairs of inclusions.

Section <a href="#sec:sequences" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:sequences">2</a> proves the reduction. Section <a href="#sec:tree" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:tree">3</a> constructs the representations, and Section <a href="#sec:inclusions" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:inclusions">4</a> establishes the tensor-norm obstruction. Section <a href="#sec:assembly" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:assembly">5</a> assembles the counterexamples. All Hilbert spaces are complex, and all operator tensor products below act on the indicated Hilbert-space tensor products. No abstract choice of $C^*$-tensor norm is needed. Unital subalgebras are understood to contain the identity of their ambient operator algebra.

<a id="sec:sequences"></a>
### A convergent-sequence obstruction

We explain how the limit condition records an inclusion $P\subseteq D$. The resulting algebras preserve the distance between limit algebras, but their spatial conjugacies are constrained by the coordinate algebra $D$.

<a id="stmt-prop-sequences"></a>
<div id="prop:sequences" class="proposition">

<a id="eq:sequence-definition"></a>
**Proposition 2**. *Let $L$ be a nonzero separable Hilbert space, let $D\subseteq\mathcal B(L)$ be a unital norm-separable $C^*$-algebra with $Z(D)=\mathbb CI_L$, and let $P,Q\subseteq D$ be unital $C^*$-subalgebras containing $I_L$. On $H=\bigoplus_{n\ge1}L$, define $$\label{eq:sequence-definition}
\mathcal E_D(P)=\{(x_n)_{n\ge1}:x_n\in D,\ x_n\to p\in P\text{ in norm}\},$$ and define $\mathcal E_D(Q)$ similarly. Then:*

<a id="eq:distance-equality"></a>
1.  *These are unital norm-separable $C^*$-algebras with common identity, and $$\label{eq:distance-equality}
    d_{\mathrm{KK}}(\mathcal E_D(P),\mathcal E_D(Q))=d_{\mathrm{KK}}(P,Q).$$*

<a id="eq:pointwise-normalizers"></a>
2.  *If a unitary conjugates $\mathcal E_D(P)$ onto $\mathcal E_D(Q)$, there are unitaries $U_n\in\mathcal B(L)$ with $U_nDU_n^*=D$ such that, for every $b\in Q$, some $p_b\in P$ satisfies $$\label{eq:pointwise-normalizers}
    \left\|b-U_np_bU_n^*\right\|\longrightarrow0.$$ The same sequence $(U_n)$ works for every $b$.*

3.  *The two algebras have the same commutant, and hence the same von Neumann closure.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-prop-sequences"><summary>Proof of stmt-prop-sequences</summary>

*Proof.* A norm-convergent sequence is bounded, so <a href="#eq:sequence-definition" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:sequence-definition">[eq:sequence-definition]</a> defines a diagonal operator on $H$, with norm $\sup_n\left\|x_n\right\|$. Algebra operations and adjoints preserve the limit condition, and the constant identity belongs to the algebra. To check norm closedness, suppose $x^{(k)}\to x$ in the supremum norm, with coordinate limits $p_k\in P$. Then $$\left\|p_k-p_j\right\|\le\sup_n\left\|x_n^{(k)}-x_n^{(j)}\right\|.$$ Thus $p_k\to p\in P$. The estimate $$\left\|x_n-p\right\|\le\left\|x_n-x_n^{(k)}\right\|
+\left\|x_n^{(k)}-p_k\right\|+\left\|p_k-p\right\|$$ shows that $x_n\to p$. Moreover, <a id="eq:sum-decomposition"></a>$$\label{eq:sum-decomposition}
\mathcal E_D(P)=c_0(\mathbb N,D)+\{(p,p,\ldots):p\in P\}.$$ The two summands are norm-separable: $P$ is a subspace of the separable metric space $D$, and finite-support sequences from a countable dense subset of $D$ are dense in $c_0(\mathbb N,D)$. Their sum is norm-separable.

For the metric assertion, write $\alpha(P,Q)=\sup_{p\in P_1}\mathop{\mathrm{dist}}(p,Q_1)$. Given a contraction $x\in\mathcal E_D(P)$ with limit $p$ and $\eta>0$, choose $q\in Q_1$ with $\left\|p-q\right\|<\alpha(P,Q)+\eta$, and choose $N$ so that $\sup_{n>N}\left\|x_n-p\right\|<\eta$. Keeping the first $N$ entries of $x$ and putting $q$ thereafter gives a contraction $y\in\mathcal E_D(Q)$ with $\left\|x-y\right\|<\alpha(P,Q)+2\eta$. This proves the upper bound for the directed distance. Conversely, for a constant contraction $(p,p,\ldots)$ and any contraction $y\in\mathcal E_D(Q)$ with limit $q\in Q_1$, $$\left\|(p,p,\ldots)-y\right\|=\sup_n\left\|p-y_n\right\|\ge\left\|p-q\right\|
\ge\mathop{\mathrm{dist}}(p,Q_1).$$ Taking infima and suprema proves equality of the directed distances. Interchanging $P,Q$ gives <a href="#eq:distance-equality" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:distance-equality">[eq:distance-equality]</a>.

Let $e_n$ be the projection onto the $n$th copy of $L$. Every $e_n$ is a central element of $\mathcal E_D(P)$. Conversely, a central element $(x_n)$ commutes with all finite-support $D$-valued sequences, so each $x_n$ lies in $Z(D)=\mathbb CI_L$. Thus the center consists exactly of convergent scalar sequences. Every nonzero central projection contains an $e_n$, and hence the minimal nonzero central projections are precisely the $e_n$. The same holds for $\mathcal E_D(Q)$.

If $S\mathcal E_D(P)S^*=\mathcal E_D(Q)$, conjugation by $S$ therefore permutes the projections $e_n$. Its restriction from the $n$th coordinate to its image is a unitary $U_n:L\to L$. The corresponding coordinate corners of both algebras are exactly $D$, since every finitely supported $D$-valued sequence is allowed. Consequently $U_nDU_n^*=D$. For $b\in Q$, pull back the constant sequence $(b,b,\ldots)$ under $S$. Its $n$th entry is $U_n^*bU_n$, regardless of the coordinate permutation. It belongs to $\mathcal E_D(P)$, so its entries converge in norm to some $p_b\in P$. This is equivalent to <a href="#eq:pointwise-normalizers" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:pointwise-normalizers">[eq:pointwise-normalizers]</a> and proves (ii).

Finally, an operator commuting with $\mathcal E_D(P)$ commutes with all $e_n$, so it is block diagonal. Commutation with the finite-support $D$ corners means that each block lies in $D'$. These conditions are also sufficient, and do not depend on $P$. Thus both commutants equal $$\{(z_n):z_n\in D',\ \sup_n\left\|z_n\right\|<\infty\}.$$ This proves (iii). ◻

</details>

We next give a concrete condition preserved by the approximation in Proposition <a href="#prop:sequences" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:sequences">2</a>(ii).

<a id="stmt-def-tensor"></a>
<div id="def:tensor" class="definition">

<a id="eq:tensor-property"></a>
**Definition 3**. For a unital inclusion $P\subseteq D\subseteq\mathcal B(L)$, say that $P$ has the *spatial tensor-norm identity relative to $D$* if $$\label{eq:tensor-property}
\left\|\sum_{i=1}^m p_i z_i\right\|_{\mathcal B(L)}
=\left\|\sum_{i=1}^m p_i\otimes z_i\right\|_{\mathcal B(L\otimes L)}$$ for every positive integer $m$, all $p_1,\ldots,p_m\in P$, and all $z_1,\ldots,z_m\in D'$.

</div>

<a id="stmt-lem-invariant"></a>
<div id="lem:invariant" class="lemma">

**Lemma 4**. *Let $P,Q\subseteq D\subseteq\mathcal B(L)$ be unital $C^*$-algebras. The identity in Definition <a href="#def:tensor" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="def:tensor">3</a> is preserved by conjugating $P$ with any unitary normalizing $D$. If $(U_n)$ is a sequence of unitaries in $\mathcal B(L)$ satisfying $U_nDU_n^*=D$ and every $b\in Q$ admits the approximation <a href="#eq:pointwise-normalizers" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:pointwise-normalizers">[eq:pointwise-normalizers]</a>, then the identity for $P$ implies the identity for $Q$.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-invariant"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-invariant</summary>

*Proof.* If $UDU^*=D$, then $UD'U^*=D'$. For $q_i=Up_iU^*$ and $z_i\in D'$, apply <a href="#eq:tensor-property" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:tensor-property">[eq:tensor-property]</a> to $p_i$ and $U^*z_iU$. Conjugate its product sum by $U$ and its tensor sum by $U\otimes U$. This gives <a href="#eq:tensor-property" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:tensor-property">[eq:tensor-property]</a> for $q_i,z_i$.

Now fix finite families $b_i\in Q$ and $z_i\in D'$. Put $q_{i,n}=U_np_{b_i}U_n^*$. For each $n$, the first part gives the identity for the entire list $(q_{i,n})_i$, since its entries all lie in $U_nPU_n^*$. Both errors $$\left\|\sum_i(q_{i,n}-b_i)z_i\right\|,\qquad
\left\|\sum_i(q_{i,n}-b_i)\otimes z_i\right\|$$ are at most $\sum_i\left\|q_{i,n}-b_i\right\|\left\|z_i\right\|$, which tends to zero. Taking the limit proves the identity for the prescribed families in $Q$ and $D'$. ◻

</details>

<a id="stmt-cor-obstruction"></a>
<div id="cor:obstruction" class="corollary">

**Corollary 5**. *Under the hypotheses of Proposition <a href="#prop:sequences" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:sequences">2</a>, suppose that $P$ has the spatial tensor-norm identity relative to $D$, whereas $Q$ does not. Then $\mathcal E_D(P)$ and $\mathcal E_D(Q)$ are not spatially conjugate. If $W\in\mathcal B(L)$ is unitary and $WPW^*=Q$, then nevertheless $$d_{\mathrm{KK}}(\mathcal E_D(P),\mathcal E_D(Q))\le2\left\|W-I_L\right\|.$$*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-cor-obstruction"><summary>Proof of stmt-cor-obstruction</summary>

*Proof.* A spatial conjugacy would give the unitaries in Proposition <a href="#prop:sequences" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:sequences">2</a>(ii), contradicting Lemma <a href="#lem:invariant" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:invariant">4</a>. For the distance bound, $p\mapsto WpW^*$ bijects the closed unit balls and $$\left\|WpW^*-p\right\|\le2\left\|p\right\|\left\|W-I_L\right\|.$$ Apply Proposition <a href="#prop:sequences" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:sequences">2</a>(i). ◻

</details>

The unitary $W$ used for the distance estimate is not required to normalize $D$. Indeed, under the stated tensor-norm obstruction, no unitary normalizing $D$ can conjugate $P$ onto $Q$.

### A uniformly small free-group deformation

We now construct the representations used to produce the inclusions in Corollary <a href="#cor:obstruction" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="cor:obstruction">5</a>. Let $G$ be the free group on the countable set $\{s_1,s_2,\ldots\}$, let $e$ denote its identity, and put $K=\ell^2(G)$. On its usual orthonormal basis define <a id="eq:regular"></a>$$\label{eq:regular}
\lambda(g)\delta_x=\delta_{gx},\qquad
\rho(g)\delta_x=\delta_{xg^{-1}}.$$ Both are unitary representations, and every left translation commutes with every right translation. The group $G$ is countable, so $K$ is separable.

The following elementary version of the real Pytlik–Szwarc deformation <a id="pm-cite-9"></a>[[PS86](#ref-PS86), [BOT13](#ref-BOT13)] records exactly the coefficient and uniform estimate that the proof needs.

<a id="stmt-lem-tree"></a>
<div id="lem:tree" class="lemma">

<a id="eq:coefficient"></a>
<a id="eq:uniform"></a>
**Lemma 6**. *For every $0<t<1$, there is a unitary representation $\pi_t:G\to\mathcal B(K)$ such that, with $r=\sqrt{1-t^2}$, $$\begin{aligned}
\left\langle \pi_t(s_i)\delta_e,\delta_e\right\rangle&=t\quad(i\ge1),\label{eq:coefficient}\\
\sup_{g\in G}\left\|\pi_t(g)-\lambda(g)\right\|&\le c(t),\qquad
c(t)=2(1-r)+\frac{t}{1-t}+t.\label{eq:uniform}
\end{aligned}$$ In particular, $c(t)\to0$ as $t\downarrow0$.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-tree"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-tree</summary>

<a id="eq:xi"></a>
<a id="eq:gram"></a>
*Proof.* Use the tree of reduced words with edges given by right multiplication by a generator or its inverse, rooted at $e$. Its distance is $d(x,y)=|x^{-1}y|$, so left translations preserve distance. For $x\ne e$, let $p(x)$ be the neighbor of $x$ toward $e$, obtained by deleting the last letter of its reduced word. Recursively define $$\label{eq:xi}
\xi_e=\delta_e,\qquad \xi_x=t\xi_{p(x)}+r\delta_x\quad(x\ne e).$$ These are unit vectors supported on their root paths. If $z$ is the last common vertex of the root paths to $x$ and $y$, the common-support components are $t^{d(x,z)}\xi_z$ and $t^{d(y,z)}\xi_z$, respectively. The remaining supports are disjoint. Hence $$\label{eq:gram}
\left\langle \xi_x,\xi_y\right\rangle=t^{d(x,y)}.$$ Also $\delta_e=\xi_e$ and $\delta_x=(\xi_x-t\xi_{p(x)})/r$ for $x\ne e$, so their span is dense. The assignment $\xi_x\mapsto\xi_{gx}$ preserves every finite Gram matrix by <a href="#eq:gram" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:gram">[eq:gram]</a>. It therefore defines an isometry on the span, and the inverse assignment for $g^{-1}$ makes the extension unitary. Denote this unitary by $\pi_t(g)$. The representation law holds on the dense span and hence on $K$. Since $\pi_t(s_i)\delta_e=\xi_{s_i}=t\delta_e+r\delta_{s_i}$, <a href="#eq:coefficient" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:coefficient">[eq:coefficient]</a> follows.

<a id="eq:path-action"></a>
It remains to prove that the perturbation is uniformly small over all group elements, rather than just over the generators. Fix $g\ne e$, write $h_0=e,h_1,\ldots,h_l=g$ for its root path, and put $T=\pi_t(g)\lambda(g)^*$. For $y\ne g$, <a href="#eq:xi" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:xi">[eq:xi]</a> gives <a id="eq:Ty"></a>$$\label{eq:Ty}
T\delta_y=\frac{\xi_y-t\xi_{g p(g^{-1}y)}}{r}.$$ The vertex $g p(g^{-1}y)$ is the neighbor of $y$ toward $g$. If $y$ is off the path $[e,g]$, this neighbor is also $p(y)$, the neighbor toward $e$; see Figure <a href="#fig:tree" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="fig:tree">1</a>. Thus $T\delta_y=\delta_y$ off that path. On the path, the neighbor toward $g$ of $h_k$, for $k<l$, is $h_{k+1}$, so $$\label{eq:path-action}
T\delta_{h_k}=r\xi_{h_k}-t\delta_{h_{k+1}}\quad(0\le k<l),
\qquad T\delta_{h_l}=\xi_{h_l}.$$

X=R(I-tS^*)^{-1}.$$ Indeed, its $k$th column is $t^k\delta_e+r\sum_{j=1}^k t^{k-j}\delta_{h_j}$. Since $\left\|S\right\|\le1$ and $\left\|R\right\|=1$, <a id="eq:Xbound"></a>$$\label{eq:Xbound}

<a id="sec:inclusions"></a>
### Close inclusions distinguished by a tensor norm

Fix $0<t<1$, let $\pi_t$ be given by Lemma <a href="#lem:tree" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:tree">6</a>, and write $\pi_0=\lambda$. We construct a common coordinate algebra $D$ and subalgebras $P_0,P_t$ satisfying Corollary <a href="#cor:obstruction" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="cor:obstruction">5</a>. First we record the absorption calculation that will be used both for closeness and for the tensor-norm estimate.

<a id="stmt-lem-absorption"></a>
<div id="lem:absorption" class="lemma">

<a id="eq:absorption"></a>
**Lemma 8** (Absorption). *Let $\sigma$ be a unitary representation of $G$ on a nonzero Hilbert space $J$. The operator $$W_\sigma(\delta_x\otimes v)=\delta_x\otimes\sigma(x)v
\quad(x\in G,\ v\in J)$$ is unitary on $K\otimes J$ and satisfies $$\label{eq:absorption}
W_\sigma(\lambda(g)\otimes I_J)W_\sigma^*
=\lambda(g)\otimes\sigma(g)\quad(g\in G).$$*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-absorption"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-absorption</summary>

*Proof.* The operator is the orthogonal direct sum of the unitaries $\sigma(x)$. On $\delta_x\otimes v$, the left side of <a href="#eq:absorption" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:absorption">[eq:absorption]</a> equals $$\delta_{gx}\otimes\sigma(gx)\sigma(x)^*v
=\delta_{gx}\otimes\sigma(g)v.
\qedhere$$ ◻

</details>

<a id="eq:D"></a>
On $L=K\otimes K$, put <a id="eq:P"></a>$$\label{eq:P}
Q_a(g)=\lambda(g)\otimes\pi_a(g),\qquad
P_a=C^*(Q_a(g):g\in G)\quad(a=0,t).$$ For the matrix units $E_{xy}\delta_z=\mathbf1_{\{z=y\}}\delta_x$ on $K$, define $D\subseteq\mathcal B(L)$ to be generated by $$\label{eq:D}
\lambda(g)\otimes I_K,\quad I_K\otimes\pi_0(g),\quad
I_K\otimes\pi_t(g)\quad(g\in G),
\qquad I_K\otimes E_{xy}\quad(x,y\in G).$$ The generators include the identity, and $P_0,P_t\subseteq D$. All three algebras are norm-separable, since their generating sets are countable.

<a id="stmt-lem-ambient"></a>
<div id="lem:ambient" class="lemma">

<a id="eq:commutant"></a>
**Lemma 9**. *The algebra $D$ in <a href="#eq:D" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:D">[eq:D]</a> has scalar center, and $$\label{eq:commutant}
D'=\{Y\otimes I_K:Y\in\lambda(G)'\}.$$ There is a unitary $W\in\mathcal B(L)$ such that <a id="eq:close-W"></a>$$\label{eq:close-W}
WP_0W^*=P_t,\qquad \left\|W-I_L\right\|\le c(t).$$*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-ambient"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-ambient</summary>

*Proof.* Commutation with all $I_K\otimes E_{xy}$ forces a bounded operator to be $Y\otimes I_K$: commutation with the diagonal matrix units removes off-diagonal blocks, and commutation with the remaining units makes the diagonal blocks identical. The first-factor generators then impose exactly $Y\in\lambda(G)'$. Such operators commute with all the second-factor generators, proving <a href="#eq:commutant" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:commutant">[eq:commutant]</a>. In particular, $\rho(g)\otimes I_K\in D'$.

If $Y\otimes I_K\in Z(D)$, then $Y$ commutes with $\lambda(G)$ by <a href="#eq:commutant" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:commutant">[eq:commutant]</a>. It also commutes with $\rho(G)$, since $Y\otimes I_K$ belongs to $D$ and the right translations belong to $D'$. Thus $Y\delta_e$ is invariant under $\lambda(g)\rho(g)$, which acts on the basis by conjugation $x\mapsto gxg^{-1}$. Every nonidentity conjugacy class in $G$ is infinite: conjugate a fixed nonidentity reduced word by distinct generators absent from that word. A square-summable vector invariant under conjugation must therefore be supported at $e$. Write $Y\delta_e=\alpha\delta_e$. Commuting with left translations gives $Y\delta_x=\alpha\delta_x$ for every $x$, proving $Z(D)=\mathbb CI_L$.

For the final assertion, Lemma <a href="#lem:absorption" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:absorption">8</a> shows that $W=W_{\pi_t}W_{\pi_0}^*$ sends every $Q_0(g)$ to $Q_t(g)$ by conjugation. Moreover, the direct-sum description of these unitaries gives $$\left\|W-I_L\right\|=\left\|W_{\pi_t}-W_{\pi_0}\right\|
=\sup_{x\in G}\left\|\pi_t(x)-\lambda(x)\right\|\le c(t).
\qedhere$$ ◻

</details>

<a id="stmt-lem-good-inclusion"></a>
<div id="lem:good-inclusion" class="lemma">

**Lemma 10**. *The inclusion $P_0\subseteq D$ has the spatial tensor-norm identity.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-good-inclusion"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-good-inclusion</summary>

*Proof.* Define a unitary on $L$ by $$V(v\otimes\delta_x)=\lambda(x)^*v\otimes\delta_x.$$ It commutes with every element of $D'$ by <a href="#eq:commutant" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:commutant">[eq:commutant]</a>. On elementary vectors one computes $$VQ_0(g)V^*(v\otimes\delta_x)=v\otimes\delta_{gx}.$$ Thus $VP_0V^*=I_K\otimes C^*(\lambda(G))$. Given finite families $p_i\in P_0$ and $z_i\in D'$, write $$Vp_iV^*=I_K\otimes X_i,\qquad z_i=Y_i\otimes I_K.$$ Conjugation by $V$ gives $$\left\|\sum_i p_i z_i\right\|=\left\|\sum_i Y_i\otimes X_i\right\|.$$ On $L\otimes L=K^{\otimes4}$, conjugation by $V\otimes I_L$ gives $$\left\|\sum_i p_i\otimes z_i\right\|
=\left\|\sum_iI_K\otimes X_i\otimes Y_i\otimes I_K\right\|
=\left\|\sum_iY_i\otimes X_i\right\|.$$ The last equality permutes the four tensor factors and uses the fact that tensoring with a nonzero identity operator does not change norm. This proves <a href="#eq:tensor-property" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:tensor-property">[eq:tensor-property]</a>. ◻

</details>

For $P_t$, the identity will fail because the product norm has a lower bound linear in the number of generators, while absorption gives a square-root upper bound on the tensor norm.

<a id="stmt-lem-free-norm"></a>
<div id="lem:free-norm" class="lemma">

<a id="eq:free-norm"></a>
**Lemma 11**. *For every positive integer $m$, $$\label{eq:free-norm}
\left\|\sum_{i=1}^m\lambda(s_i)\right\|\le2\sqrt m.$$*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-free-norm"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-free-norm</summary>

*Proof.* Let $F_i$ be the orthogonal projection onto the closed span of words beginning with $s_i^{-1}$. The $F_i$ are pairwise orthogonal. Write $\lambda(s_i)=A_i+B_i$, where $$A_i=\lambda(s_i)(I_K-F_i),\qquad B_i=\lambda(s_i)F_i.$$ There is no cancellation when $s_i$ is prepended to a word not beginning with $s_i^{-1}$. Thus the range of $A_i$ consists of vectors supported on words beginning with $s_i$, and these ranges are mutually orthogonal. For every $v\in K$, $$\left\|\sum_i A_iv\right\|^2=\sum_i\left\|(I_K-F_i)v\right\|^2
\le m\left\|v\right\|^2,$$ while $$\left\|\sum_i B_iv\right\|\le\sum_i\left\|F_iv\right\|
\le\sqrt m\left(\sum_i\left\|F_iv\right\|^2\right)^{1/2}
\le\sqrt m\left\|v\right\|.$$ Adding the two bounds proves <a href="#eq:free-norm" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:free-norm">[eq:free-norm]</a>. ◻

</details>

<a id="stmt-lem-bad-inclusion"></a>
<div id="lem:bad-inclusion" class="lemma">

**Lemma 12**. *For every $0<t<1$, the inclusion $P_t\subseteq D$ fails the spatial tensor-norm identity.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-bad-inclusion"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-bad-inclusion</summary>

<a id="eq:large-norm"></a>
<a id="eq:small-norm"></a>
*Proof.* Choose an integer $m>4/t^2$, so $mt>2\sqrt m$, and take $$p_i=Q_t(s_i),\qquad z_i=\rho(s_i)\otimes I_K,
\qquad 1\le i\le m.$$ These belong to $P_t$ and $D'$, respectively. Since $\lambda(s_i)\rho(s_i)\delta_e=\delta_e$, <a href="#eq:coefficient" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:coefficient">[eq:coefficient]</a> implies $$\label{eq:large-norm}
\left\|\sum_i p_i z_i\right\|
\ge\left|\left\langle \left(\sum_i p_i z_i\right)
(\delta_e\otimes\delta_e),\delta_e\otimes\delta_e\right\rangle\right|
=mt.$$ On the other hand, the tensor sum on $L\otimes L$ is $$\sum_{i=1}^m\lambda(s_i)\otimes\pi_t(s_i)
\otimes\rho(s_i)\otimes I_K.$$ Apply Lemma <a href="#lem:absorption" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:absorption">8</a> to the representation $\sigma=\pi_t\otimes\rho\otimes1$ on $K^{\otimes3}$, where $1$ is the trivial representation on the last copy of $K$. Together with Lemma <a href="#lem:free-norm" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:free-norm">11</a>, it gives $$\label{eq:small-norm}
\left\|\sum_i p_i\otimes z_i\right\|
=\left\|\sum_{i=1}^m\lambda(s_i)\right\|\le2\sqrt m<mt.$$ Equations <a href="#eq:large-norm" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:large-norm">[eq:large-norm]</a> and <a href="#eq:small-norm" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:small-norm">[eq:small-norm]</a> contradict the identity for this finite list. ◻

</details>

<a id="sec:assembly"></a>
### The counterexamples

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-bad-inclusion-2"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-bad-inclusion</summary>

*Proof of Theorem <a href="#thm:main" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:main">1</a>.* Given $\varepsilon>0$, choose $0<t<1$ so small that $2c(t)<\varepsilon$. Use the algebras $P_0,P_t\subseteq D\subseteq\mathcal B(L)$ from Section <a href="#sec:inclusions" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:inclusions">4</a>, and put $$H=\bigoplus_{n\ge1}L,\qquad A=\mathcal E_D(P_0),\qquad B=\mathcal E_D(P_t).$$ The space $H$ is separable. Lemma <a href="#lem:ambient" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:ambient">9</a> supplies the scalar center of $D$, so Proposition <a href="#prop:sequences" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:sequences">2</a> shows that $A,B$ are unital norm-separable $C^*$-algebras with common identity and that $A''=B''$. The same lemma supplies a unitary $W$ with $WP_0W^*=P_t$ and $\left\|W-I_L\right\|\le c(t)$. By Corollary <a href="#cor:obstruction" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="cor:obstruction">5</a>, $$d_{\mathrm{KK}}(A,B)\le2c(t)<\varepsilon.$$ Finally, Lemmas <a href="#lem:good-inclusion" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:good-inclusion">10</a> and <a href="#lem:bad-inclusion" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:bad-inclusion">12</a> show that the relative tensor-norm identity holds for $P_0$ but not for $P_t$. Corollary <a href="#cor:obstruction" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="cor:obstruction">5</a> therefore excludes every unitary conjugating $A$ onto $B$. ◻

</details>

The common ideal $c_0(\mathbb N,D)$ makes the von Neumann closures agree, while the norm-limit condition retains the distinction between the two inclusions.
