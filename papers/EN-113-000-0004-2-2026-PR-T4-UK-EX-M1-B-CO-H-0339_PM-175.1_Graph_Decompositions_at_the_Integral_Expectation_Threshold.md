## 1. Abstract and result contract

> We prove the graph-decomposition conjecture of Ascoli, He, Park, and Talagrand. Every graph admits a partition into a universally bounded number of fixed edge pieces, each having ordinary containment threshold at most a universal constant times the original graph's integral expectation threshold. The partition is chosen before sampling the random host, and the separate embeddings of the pieces need not agree on shared vertices.

- **C1** (theorem, [source](#stmt-thm-main)): There exist an absolute integer $k\geq2$ and an absolute constant $L\geq1$ such that, for every $n\geq2$ and every graph $H$ on at most $n$ vertices, the edges of $H$ admit a partition $$E(H)=E(H_1)\,\dot\cup\,\cdots\,\dot\cup\,E(H_k) \quad\text{with}\quad p_c(H_i;n)\leq Lq(H;n)\qquad(1\leq i\leq k).$$ The pieces may be empty and may share vertices. The partition depends only on $H,n$ and is fixed before the random host is sampled.
  - Explanation: A fixed edge partition gives separate ordinary containment threshold guarantees; compatible embeddings of shared vertices are not asserted.
- **C2** (theorem, [source](#stmt-thm-discrete)): Set $K_0=2^{75}$. For every nonempty finite set $X$, every $r\in(0,1)$, and every family $\mathcal D\subseteq2^X$ with $\mu_r(\mathcal D)\geq1-1/K_0$, the family $$\mathcal E_{K_0}(\mathcal D)= \{S\subseteq X:S\not\subseteq D_1\cup\cdots\cup D_{K_0} \text{ for every }D_1,\ldots,D_{K_0}\in\mathcal D\}$$ has a cover of cost at most $1/2$ at the same parameter $r$. The family $\mathcal D$ need not be monotone, and members of a tuple may repeat.
  - Explanation: External same-parameter discrete-convexity input gives an integral cover for sets excluded from every bounded union of typical sets.
- **C3** (conditional_result, [source](#stmt-cor-typical-cover)): Let $n\geq2$, let $H$ have an edge and at most $n$ vertices, and suppose $q(H;n)<r<1$. If a family of graphs on $[n]$ satisfies $\mu_r(\mathcal D)\geq1-1/K_0$, then some copy of $H$ has its edges contained in the union of $K_0$ members of $\mathcal D$.
  - Explanation: The covering input forces a labelled copy into a union of typical graphs whenever the density exceeds the integral expectation threshold.
- **C4** (bound, [source](#stmt-lem-q-lower)): If $H$ has an edge, $\|V(H)\|\leq n$, and $d=\mathop{\mathrm{degen}}(H)$, then $$\label{eq:q-lower} q(H;n)\geq\frac1{2\binom n2},\qquad q(H;n)\geq\frac12 n^{-2/d}.$$
  - Explanation: Single-edge covers and a minimum-degree subgraph give two explicit lower bounds on the integral expectation threshold.
- **C5** (construction, [source](#stmt-lem-split)): For every positive integer $m$, the edges of a $d$-degenerate graph can be partitioned into $m$ graphs, each of degeneracy at most $\lceil d/m\rceil$.
  - Explanation: Distributing backward edges of a degeneracy order gives a deterministic partition with bounded piece degeneracy.
- **C6** (construction, [source](#stmt-lem-compression)): Let $Y$ be finite, $r\in(0,1)$, and $s\geq1$ an integer. For every nonempty family $$\mathcal R\subseteq\{T\subseteq Y:1\leq\|T\|\leq s\}$$ there is a nonempty subfamily $\mathcal Q\subseteq\mathcal R$ with $\|\mathcal Q\|\leq\lceil2r^{-s}\rceil$ such that $$\label{eq:compression} S\text{ satisfies }\mathcal R\quad\Longrightarrow\quad f_{\mathcal Q}(S)\geq1/2.$$
  - Explanation: Greedy addition compresses a finite requirement family into a bounded subfamily with a uniform smoothed satisfaction guarantee.
- **C7** (conditional_result, [source](#stmt-prop-transfer)): There is an absolute integer $N_1$ such that the following holds for every $n\geq N_1$ and every $r,s$ satisfying <a href="#eq:parameters" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:parameters">[eq:parameters]</a>. There exists $\mathcal D=\mathcal D(n,r,s)\subseteq2^{E(K_n)}$ with $\mu_r(\mathcal D)\geq1-1/K_0$ such that, whenever* - *$Y\subseteq[n]$ has $\|Y\|\leq n^{2/3}$;* - *$J$ is a labelled bipartite graph with all edges between $Y$ and $[n]\setminus Y$, and $E(J)\subseteq E(G)$ for some $G\in\mathcal D$;* - *at most $n/8$ vertices outside $Y$ have positive degree in $J$, and all of those degrees are at most $s$;* *one has $$\label{eq:transfer} p_c(J;n)\leq r_*:=1-(1-r)^{32}\leq32r.$$
  - Explanation: Simultaneous tests and compression establish Hall conditions for cut pieces contained in a typical source graph.
- **C8** (bound, [source](#stmt-prop-degree-embedding)): There is an absolute integer $N_2$ such that, for $n\geq N_2$ and $r,s$ satisfying <a href="#eq:parameters" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:parameters">[eq:parameters]</a>, every graph $J$ with $$\|V_+(J)\|\leq n/4,\qquad \mathop{\mathrm{degen}}(J)\leq s, \qquad\Delta(J)\leq n^{2/3}$$ and at most $n$ vertices satisfies $p_c(J;n)\leq r$.
  - Explanation: Independent layers and fresh bin exposures yield an ordinary threshold bound for graphs with small support and controlled degrees.
- **C9** (bound, [source](#stmt-prop-stars)): Put $A=400e^4$. There is an absolute integer $N_3$ such that, for every $n\geq N_3$ and every star forest $J$ on at most $n$ vertices with $\|V_+(J)\|\leq n/100$, $$\label{eq:star-threshold} p_c(J;n)\leq2Aq(J;n).$$ If $q(J;n)<1/(2A)$, the stronger bound $p_c(J;n)\leq Aq(J;n)$ holds.
  - Explanation: Fresh-center trials embed small star forests with a threshold controlled by their integral expectation threshold.

## 2. Definitions, validity and bounds

- **ordinary containment threshold** ([source](#sec:intro)): For a graph $J$ on at most $n$ vertices, let $$p_c(J;n)=\min\{p\in[0,1]: \mathbb P(G(n,p)\text{ contains a copy of }J)\geq1/2\}.$$
- **binomial random graph** ([source](#sec:intro)): Here $G(n,p)$ has vertex set $[n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}$, with edges present independently with probability $p$.
- **containment family** ([source](#sec:intro)): To define the integral expectation threshold, put $X=E(K_n)$ and let $\mathcal F_H\subseteq2^X$ be the increasing family of edge sets whose graphs contain $H$.
- **integral cover** ([source](#sec:intro)): A family $\mathcal C\subseteq2^X$ *covers* $\mathcal F_H$ if every $A\in\mathcal F_H$ contains some $S\in\mathcal C$.
- **cover cost** ([source](#sec:intro)): Its cost at $p$ is $$c_p(\mathcal C)=\sum_{S\in\mathcal C}p^{\|S\|}.$$
- **integral expectation threshold** ([source](#sec:intro)): For $H$ with at least one edge, define $$\label{eq:q-definition} q(H;n)=\max\{p\in[0,1]: \mathcal F_H\text{ has a cover }\mathcal C \text{ with }c_p(\mathcal C)\leq1/2\}.$$
- **Bernoulli subset law** ([source](#sec:prelim)): For a finite ground set $X$, write $\mu_r$ for the law of its Bernoulli-$r$ random subset.
- **nonisolated support** ([source](#eq:monotonicity)): For a graph $J$, let $$V_+(J)=\{v\in V(J):\deg_J(v)>0\}$$ be its set of nonisolated vertices.
- **degeneracy** ([source](#eq:edge-bound)): The *degeneracy* $d=\mathop{\mathrm{degen}}(H)$ is the maximum of the minimum degrees of nonempty subgraphs of $H$.
- **requirement satisfaction** ([source](#eq:smoothed-test)): For a family $\mathcal Q$ of subsets of a finite set $Y$, say that $S\subseteq Y$ *satisfies* $\mathcal Q$ if it contains some member of $\mathcal Q$.
- **smoothed satisfaction probability** ([source](#eq:smoothed-test)): Given $r\in(0,1)$, define $$\label{eq:smoothed-test} f_{\mathcal Q}(S)=\mathbb P(S\cup Z\text{ satisfies }\mathcal Q), \qquad Z\sim\mu_r\text{ on }Y.$$
- **star forest** ([source](#pm-h-14)): A star forest is a graph whose nontrivial components are stars.

## 3. Proof roadmap

- **R1** [claim](#stmt-thm-main) / [proof](#sec:intro): State fixed-partition goal. -> A fixed edge partition gives separate ordinary containment threshold guarantees; compatible embeddings of shared vertices are not asserted.
- **R2** [claim](#stmt-thm-discrete) / [proof](#sec:prelim): Use external same-parameter integral covering theorem. -> External same-parameter discrete-convexity input gives an integral cover for sets excluded from every bounded union of typical sets.
- **R3** [claim](#stmt-cor-typical-cover) / [proof](#proof-stmt-cor-typical-cover): Contradict maximality of the integral expectation threshold. -> The covering input forces a labelled copy into a union of typical graphs whenever the density exceeds the integral expectation threshold.
- **R4** [claim](#stmt-lem-q-lower) / [proof](#proof-stmt-lem-q-lower): Count single edges and copies of a minimum-degree subgraph. -> Single-edge covers and a minimum-degree subgraph give two explicit lower bounds on the integral expectation threshold.
- **R5** [claim](#stmt-lem-split) / [proof](#proof-stmt-lem-split): Distribute preceding-neighbor edges along a degeneracy order. -> Distributing backward edges of a degeneracy order gives a deterministic partition with bounded piece degeneracy.
- **R6** [claim](#stmt-lem-compression) / [proof](#proof-stmt-lem-compression): Greedily add requirements while conditional satisfaction is too small. -> Greedy addition compresses a finite requirement family into a bounded subfamily with a uniform smoothed satisfaction guarantee.
- **R7** [claim](#stmt-prop-transfer) / [proof](#proof-stmt-prop-transfer): Concentrate all bounded tests simultaneously, compress requirements, and apply Hall matching. -> Simultaneous tests and compression establish Hall conditions for cut pieces contained in a typical source graph.
- **R8** [claim](#stmt-prop-degree-embedding) / [proof](#proof-stmt-prop-degree-embedding): Construct independent layers, use fresh disjoint bins, and exclude Hall failures. -> Independent layers and fresh bin exposures yield an ordinary threshold bound for graphs with small support and controlled degrees.
- **R9** [claim](#stmt-prop-stars) / [proof](#proof-stmt-prop-stars): Use integral star-copy covers and fresh-center trials. -> Fresh-center trials embed small star forests with a threshold controlled by their integral expectation threshold.
- **R10** [claim](#stmt-thm-main) / [proof](#proof-stmt-prop-stars-2): Assemble high-degeneracy cut pieces or bounded-degeneracy star forests and pad with empty pieces. -> A fixed edge partition gives separate ordinary containment threshold guarantees; compatible embeddings of shared vertices are not asserted.

## 4. Engineering Triptych

### Panel 1 - epistemic contract

- **I1**: The main theorem supplies a fixed edge partition with separate ordinary containment threshold bounds.
- **I2**: Piece embeddings need not agree on shared vertices.
- **I3**: Partition every edge exactly once into m pieces, each with degeneracy at most ceil(d/m).

### Panel 2 - negative knowledge

- **A1**: Combine separate successful piece embeddings into a single embedding of H. -> Shared vertices may receive different images.
- **A2**: Use the partition proof for an induced-containment workload. -> Extra host edges invalidate induced containment.
- **A3**: Choose pieces after inspecting the random host and attribute the theorem bound to that selection. -> An adaptive selection has no stated guarantee.
- **A4**: Treat the finite splitting self-test as certification of the complete threshold theorem. -> The code never checks covering families, asymptotic cutoffs or random containment thresholds.

### Panel 3 - reference implementation and oracle

Applicability: `constructive`. Lemma 5 proof gives a finite constructive edge assignment along a degeneracy order. Only this sublemma is implemented, not the main threshold theorem.

```python
# INPUTS: JSON {n,m,graphs}, graphs are lists of undirected edges on range(n).
# PRECONDITIONS: 0 <= n <= 6, 1 <= m <= 8; simple graphs; at most 32768 graphs.
# OUTPUTS: JSON list of partitions, each containing exactly m edge lists.
# TIME: O(g*(n^3 + e + m)); g graphs, e edges per graph, naive peeling.
# SPACE: O(g*(e+m) + n^2), including all output partitions.
# ARITHMETIC: exact Python integers and python-flint fmpz ceiling division.
# FAILURE: malformed or out-of-contract input raises ValueError and exits nonzero.
import json, sys
from flint import fmpz

def split(n, edges, m):
    adj = [set() for _ in range(n)]
    for u, v in edges:
        adj[u].add(v)
        adj[v].add(u)
    alive = set(range(n))
    removed = []
    d = 0
    while alive:
        v = min(alive, key=lambda u: (len(adj[u] & alive), u))
        d = max(d, len(adj[v] & alive))
        removed.append(v)
        alive.remove(v)
    order = list(reversed(removed))
    rank = {v: i for i, v in enumerate(order)}
    cap = max(1, int((fmpz(d) + m - 1) // m))
    pieces = [[] for _ in range(m)]
    for v in order:
        earlier = sorted(u for u in adj[v] if rank[u] < rank[v])
        for j, u in enumerate(earlier):
            bucket = j // cap
            pieces[bucket].append(sorted([u, v]))
    return pieces

x = json.load(sys.stdin)
n, m, graphs = x['n'], x['m'], x['graphs']
if type(n) is not int or type(m) is not int or not 0 <= n <= 6 or not 1 <= m <= 8:
    raise ValueError('n or m out of contract')
if type(graphs) is not list or len(graphs) > 32768:
    raise ValueError('graph batch out of contract')
for edges in graphs:
    seen = set()
    for edge in edges:
        if type(edge) is not list or len(edge) != 2:
            raise ValueError('edge shape')
        u, v = edge
        if type(u) is not int or type(v) is not int or not 0 <= u < v < n or (u,v) in seen:
            raise ValueError('graph not simple canonical')
        seen.add((u,v))
print(json.dumps([split(n, edges, m) for edges in graphs]))
```

## 6. Applicability limits and search

- **L1** (hypothesis): The setting is finite simple graphs with ordinary non-induced containment. An induced-containment guarantee, multigraph extension, or infinite-graph extension is ABSENT. Blocked use: ['Induced motif containment', 'Multigraph or infinite-graph targets']
- **L2** (hypothesis): The host model has independent identically distributed Bernoulli edges on the prescribed vertex set. Guarantees for dependent, heterogeneous, directed, or empirical network hosts are ABSENT. Blocked use: ['Dependent-edge reliability models', 'Inhomogeneous or directed host models']
- **L3** (counterexample_scope): The separate embeddings of the pieces need not agree on shared vertices. The decomposition does not provide one consistent embedding of the whole target at the piecewise threshold. Blocked use: ['Assembly of piece witnesses into one global target embedding']
- **L4** (hypothesis): The partition uses only the target and host order and is fixed before sampling. Guarantees for host-adaptive partitions are ABSENT. Blocked use: ['Selecting a partition after inspecting the sampled host']
- **L5** (counterexample_scope): The containment threshold uses probability at least one half for each piece individually. A high-reliability guarantee or a simultaneous all-pieces guarantee at the same parameter is ABSENT. Blocked use: ['Interpreting the threshold as near-certain success', 'Claiming simultaneous piece containment without a separate probability argument']
- **L6** (unknown): Numerical values of the absolute embedding cutoffs and the resulting numerical threshold constant are ABSENT. The proof defines L through a sufficiently large n_0; a deployable numerical L cannot be read off from that expression alone. Blocked use: ['Numerical deployment guarantees using an invented cutoff or constant']
- **L7** (hypothesis): Neighborhood transfer requires a small fixed part, a labelled bipartite subgraph contained in a member of the typical class, bounded outside support, and bounded outside degrees. Arbitrary sparse cut graphs do not satisfy the membership hypothesis automatically. Blocked use: ['Applying transfer from degree bounds alone']
- **L8** (hypothesis): Transfer and the maximum-degree embedding proposition require the displayed joint density and degree regime as well as their sufficiently large host-order cutoff. Their stated bounds cannot be extended to arbitrary parameters. Blocked use: ['Using the embedding propositions outside their parameter regime']
- **L9** (counterexample_scope): Compression preserves smoothed satisfaction after an independent random extension. It does not preserve every original requirement by deterministic satisfaction of the selected subfamily. Blocked use: ['Replacing all requirements by a deterministic logically equivalent subfamily']
- **L10** (edge_case): Edgeless targets use zero thresholds and empty pieces. Arguments dividing by positive expectation thresholds or invoking positive degeneracy require the non-edgeless case. Blocked use: ['Dividing by q for an edgeless target']
- **L11** (hypothesis): The graph-decomposition proof relies on the companion discrete-convexity covering theorem stated as its covering input. Verifying a local graph routine does not certify this external input. Blocked use: ['Treating a certificate for degeneracy splitting as a certificate of the main theorem']
- **L12** (unknown): A practical runtime bound for computing the main partition, a numerical oracle for the integral expectation threshold, and a production implementation are ABSENT. Finite local constructions in the proof do not supply those engineering contracts. Blocked use: ['Claiming a polynomial-time implementation of the entire theorem', 'Reporting measured software performance from the existence proof']

## 7. Complete mathematical paper


We prove the graph-decomposition conjecture of Ascoli, He, Park, and Talagrand. Every graph admits a partition into a universally bounded number of fixed edge pieces, each having ordinary containment threshold at most a universal constant times the original graph's integral expectation threshold. The partition is chosen before sampling the random host, and the separate embeddings of the pieces need not agree on shared vertices.

<a id="sec:intro"></a>
### Introduction

All graphs are finite and simple, and copies are not required to be induced. For a graph $J$ on at most $n$ vertices, let $$p_c(J;n)=\min\{p\in[0,1]:
           \mathbb P(G(n,p)\text{ contains a copy of }J)\geq1/2\}.$$ Here $G(n,p)$ has vertex set $[n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}$, with edges present independently with probability $p$. We set $p_c(J;n)=0$ when $J$ is edgeless.

To define the integral expectation threshold, put $X=E(K_n)$ and let $\mathcal F_H\subseteq2^X$ be the increasing family of edge sets whose graphs contain $H$. A family $\mathcal C\subseteq2^X$ *covers* $\mathcal F_H$ if every $A\in\mathcal F_H$ contains some $S\in\mathcal C$. Its cost at $p$ is $$c_p(\mathcal C)=\sum_{S\in\mathcal C}p^{|S|}.$$ For $H$ with at least one edge, define <a id="eq:q-definition"></a>$$\label{eq:q-definition}
 q(H;n)=\max\{p\in[0,1]:
    \mathcal F_H\text{ has a cover }\mathcal C
    \text{ with }c_p(\mathcal C)\leq1/2\}.$$ The maximum exists because there are only finitely many families of subsets of $X$. Each cover gives a first-moment bound on containment, so $q(H;n)\leq p_c(H;n)$. For convenience set $q(H;n)=0$ for edgeless graphs as well.

<a id="stmt-thm-main"></a>
<div id="thm:main" class="theorem">

**Theorem 1**. *There exist an absolute integer $k\geq2$ and an absolute constant $L\geq1$ such that, for every $n\geq2$ and every graph $H$ on at most $n$ vertices, the edges of $H$ admit a partition $$E(H)=E(H_1)\,\dot\cup\,\cdots\,\dot\cup\,E(H_k)
 \quad\text{with}\quad
 p_c(H_i;n)\leq Lq(H;n)\qquad(1\leq i\leq k).$$ The pieces may be empty and may share vertices. The partition depends only on $H,n$ and is fixed before the random host is sampled.*

</div>

This proves Conjecture 8.1 of Ascoli, He, Park, and Talagrand (Ascoli et al. 2026) <a id="pm-cite-1"></a>[[AHPT26](#ref-AHPT26)]. Their formulation allows overlapping edge sets. Assigning each edge to one piece containing it gives a partition and cannot increase any piece’s containment threshold. Thus the two formulations are equivalent.

<a id="pm-h-3"></a>
#### History and significance

Kahn and Kalai (Kahn and Kalai 2007) <a id="pm-cite-2"></a>[[KK07](#ref-KK07)] proposed a general comparison between ordinary thresholds and integral expectation thresholds with a logarithmic loss. Following the fractional version established by Frankston, Kahn, Narayanan, and Park (Frankston et al. 2021) <a id="pm-cite-3"></a>[[FKNP21](#ref-FKNP21)], Park and Pham (Park and Pham 2024) <a id="pm-cite-4"></a>[[PP24](#ref-PP24)] proved the integral comparison. For graph containment it gives $$p_c(H;n)\leq Cq(H;n)\log\max\{2,|E(H)|\}$$ with an absolute constant $C$. Theorem <a href="#thm:main" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:main">1</a> removes this logarithmic loss after partitioning the target into a bounded number of pieces. The assertion concerns each fixed piece’s ordinary threshold; their embeddings need not agree on shared vertices.

Ascoli, He, Park, and Talagrand introduced generalized thresholds based on unions of likely graphs, related them to Talagrand’s discrete-convexity conjecture, and formulated the decomposition problem above (Ascoli et al. 2026) <a id="pm-cite-5"></a>[[AHPT26](#ref-AHPT26)]. They proved bounded-degeneracy cases and further cases with both degeneracy and maximum-degree restrictions (Ascoli et al. 2026, Theorems 1.12–1.13) <a id="pm-cite-6"></a>[[AHPT26](#ref-AHPT26)]. Their Section 8 isolates the bipartite edges between high- and low-degree vertices as a principal remaining difficulty.

We use the same-parameter integral covering theorem from *Talagrand’s discrete-convexity conjecture* (OpenAI 2026, Theorem 1.1) <a id="pm-cite-7"></a>[[OpenAI26DC](#ref-OpenAI26DC)], which proves the discrete conjecture formulated in (Talagrand 2010, Conjecture 7.1) <a id="pm-cite-8"></a>[[Talagrand10](#ref-Talagrand10)]. It is stated precisely in Section <a href="#sec:prelim" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:prelim">2</a>; this is an essential companion input. All remaining graph-decomposition and embedding arguments are proved below.

<a id="pm-h-4"></a>
#### Method

The covering theorem places one labelled copy of $H$ inside the union of a bounded number of deterministic graphs from a class $\mathcal D$ having high probability at a density $r$ comparable to $q(H;n)$. Assigning each edge to one of these graphs gives initial pieces. In the large-degeneracy case we isolate a small set $Y$ containing the high-degree vertices and enlarge it until each outside vertex has few neighbors in $Y$. Subsequent splitting produces pieces inside $Y$, outside $Y$, and across the cut.

To embed a cut piece while fixing $Y$, each nonisolated outside vertex needs a distinct image adjacent to its prescribed set of at most $s$ vertices in $Y$. Hall’s criterion asks for enough candidate images for every collection of outside vertices. Membership in a typical class alone does not ensure this; the class must control unions of arbitrarily many such conditions.

The compression lemma selects at most $\lceil2r^{-s}\rceil$ requirements from any family so that every neighborhood containing an original requirement contains a selected requirement with probability at least $1/2$ after adding each vertex of $Y$ independently with probability $r$. This principle is independent of graphs. It permits simultaneous concentration over all subfamilies of this bounded size while still controlling every Hall condition. We define $\mathcal D$ using these tests for all small $Y$ at once, so that transfer applies to cut pieces chosen after the deterministic cover.

For the two other regions, a direct embedding lemma uses successive matchings of layers of pairwise nonadjacent vertices. Graphs of bounded degeneracy are handled by small star forests, adapting the degeneracy splitting and center-testing arguments of (Ascoli et al. 2026, Claim 3.9 and Section 6) <a id="pm-cite-9"></a>[[AHPT26](#ref-AHPT26)].

Section <a href="#sec:prelim" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:prelim">2</a> collects the covering input and elementary estimates. Section <a href="#sec:transfer" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:transfer">3</a> proves neighborhood transfer, Section <a href="#sec:direct" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:direct">4</a> gives the direct embedding lemmas, and Section <a href="#sec:assembly" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:assembly">5</a> constructs the partition and proves Theorem <a href="#thm:main" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:main">1</a>.

<a id="sec:prelim"></a>
### Covering input and elementary estimates

The first step converts a family of typical labelled graphs into a deterministic cover of one copy of the target. We then record the elementary degree and threshold bounds used to subdivide that copy.

For a finite ground set $X$, write $\mu_r$ for the law of its Bernoulli-$r$ random subset. Covering and the cost $c_r$ have the same meaning as in the introduction, with $X$ in place of $E(K_n)$.

<a id="stmt-thm-discrete"></a>
<div id="thm:discrete" class="theorem">

**Theorem 2** (Discrete convexity, (OpenAI 2026, Theorem 1.1) <a id="pm-cite-10"></a>[[OpenAI26DC](#ref-OpenAI26DC)]). *Set $K_0=2^{75}$. For every nonempty finite set $X$, every $r\in(0,1)$, and every family $\mathcal D\subseteq2^X$ with $\mu_r(\mathcal D)\geq1-1/K_0$, the family $$\mathcal E_{K_0}(\mathcal D)=
 \{S\subseteq X:S\not\subseteq D_1\cup\cdots\cup D_{K_0}
                   \text{ for every }D_1,\ldots,D_{K_0}\in\mathcal D\}$$ has a cover of cost at most $1/2$ at the same parameter $r$. The family $\mathcal D$ need not be monotone, and members of a tuple may repeat.*

</div>

<a id="stmt-cor-typical-cover"></a>
<div id="cor:typical-cover" class="corollary">

**Corollary 3**. *Let $n\geq2$, let $H$ have an edge and at most $n$ vertices, and suppose $q(H;n)<r<1$. If a family of graphs on $[n]$ satisfies $\mu_r(\mathcal D)\geq1-1/K_0$, then some copy of $H$ has its edges contained in the union of $K_0$ members of $\mathcal D$.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-cor-typical-cover"><summary>Proof of stmt-cor-typical-cover</summary>

*Proof.* If no such copy exists, no graph containing $H$ can be contained in such a union. Thus $\mathcal F_H\subseteq\mathcal E_{K_0}(\mathcal D)$. Theorem <a href="#thm:discrete" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:discrete">2</a> supplies a cover of $\mathcal F_H$ of cost at most $1/2$ at $r$, contrary to $r>q(H;n)$. ◻

</details>

<a id="eq:monotonicity"></a>
For a graph $J$, let $$V_+(J)=\{v\in V(J):\deg_J(v)>0\}$$ be its set of nonisolated vertices. Isolated vertices can be deleted during an embedding and added afterward: any embedding of the remaining vertices extends injectively when $|V(J)|\leq n$. If $J\subseteq H$, then $\mathcal F_H\subseteq\mathcal F_J$, so every cover of $\mathcal F_J$ covers $\mathcal F_H$. In particular, $$\label{eq:monotonicity}
 q(J;n)\leq q(H;n),\qquad p_c(J;n)\leq p_c(H;n).$$

<a id="eq:edge-bound"></a>
The *degeneracy* $d=\mathop{\mathrm{degen}}(H)$ is the maximum of the minimum degrees of nonempty subgraphs of $H$. Equivalently, the vertices admit an order with at most $d$ preceding neighbors per vertex. To obtain the order, repeatedly remove a vertex of degree at most $d$ and place it last. Conversely, the last vertex of any subgraph in such an order has degree at most $d$. Counting each edge at its later endpoint shows that every subgraph $J$ of $H$ satisfies $$\label{eq:edge-bound}
 |E(J)|\leq d|V(J)|.$$

<a id="stmt-lem-q-lower"></a>
<div id="lem:q-lower" class="lemma">

**Lemma 4**. *If $H$ has an edge, $|V(H)|\leq n$, and $d=\mathop{\mathrm{degen}}(H)$, then <a id="eq:q-lower"></a>$$\label{eq:q-lower}
 q(H;n)\geq\frac1{2\binom n2},\qquad
 q(H;n)\geq\frac12 n^{-2/d}.$$*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-q-lower"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-q-lower</summary>

*Proof.* The family of all single edges covers $\mathcal F_H$, proving the first bound. For the second, choose a subgraph of minimum degree $d$, with $v$ vertices and $a\geq dv/2$ edges. Its copies on $[n]$ form a cover of $\mathcal F_H$ with cost at most $n^vp^a$. At $p=2^{-1/a}n^{-v/a}\in(0,1)$ this is at most $1/2$. Since $a\geq1$ and $v/a\leq2/d$, this parameter is at least $\frac12n^{-2/d}$. ◻

</details>

We use the following elementary splitting from (Ascoli et al. 2026, Claim 3.9) <a id="pm-cite-11"></a>[[AHPT26](#ref-AHPT26)].

<a id="stmt-lem-split"></a>
<div id="lem:split" class="lemma">

**Lemma 5** (Degeneracy splitting). *For every positive integer $m$, the edges of a $d$-degenerate graph can be partitioned into $m$ graphs, each of degeneracy at most $\lceil d/m\rceil$.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-split"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-split</summary>

*Proof.* Fix a degeneracy order. At each vertex, partition its edges to preceding neighbors into $m$ groups of size at most $\lceil d/m\rceil$, and assign each group to its corresponding piece. Each edge is assigned once. The same order certifies the claimed bound in every piece. ◻

</details>

<a id="sec:transfer"></a>
### Uniform neighborhood transfer

We construct a typical class whose small-neighborhood bipartite subgraphs embed in a slightly denser random graph. To embed a bipartite graph $J$ while fixing one part $Y$, each nonisolated vertex $w$ outside $Y$ needs a distinct image adjacent to every vertex of $T_w=N_J(w)\subseteq Y$. For every set $U$ of vertices to be matched, Hall’s criterion asks for at least $|U|$ candidate images whose neighborhoods contain at least one $T_w$ with $w\in U$. The family of requirements indexed by $U$ can be large. The next lemma reduces it to a bounded subfamily after an independent random extension.

<a id="pm-h-7"></a>
#### Compressing requirements

<a id="eq:smoothed-test"></a>
For a family $\mathcal Q$ of subsets of a finite set $Y$, say that $S\subseteq Y$ *satisfies* $\mathcal Q$ if it contains some member of $\mathcal Q$. Given $r\in(0,1)$, define $$\label{eq:smoothed-test}
 f_{\mathcal Q}(S)=\mathbb P(S\cup Z\text{ satisfies }\mathcal Q),
 \qquad Z\sim\mu_r\text{ on }Y.$$ A small subfamily need not be satisfied by every set that satisfies the original family. The lemma replaces this implication by a uniform probability bound after the extension.

<a id="stmt-lem-compression"></a>
<div id="lem:compression" class="lemma">

<a id="eq:compression"></a>
**Lemma 6** (Compression after a random extension). *Let $Y$ be finite, $r\in(0,1)$, and $s\geq1$ an integer. For every nonempty family $$\mathcal R\subseteq\{T\subseteq Y:1\leq|T|\leq s\}$$ there is a nonempty subfamily $\mathcal Q\subseteq\mathcal R$ with $|\mathcal Q|\leq\lceil2r^{-s}\rceil$ such that $$\label{eq:compression}
 S\text{ satisfies }\mathcal R\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 f_{\mathcal Q}(S)\geq1/2.$$*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-compression"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-compression</summary>

*Proof.* Start with $\mathcal Q=\varnothing$. While some $T\in\mathcal R$ satisfies $$\mathbb P(Z\text{ satisfies }\mathcal Q\mid Z\supseteq T)<1/2,$$ add $T$ to $\mathcal Q$. The probability that $Z$ satisfies the current family increases by $$\mathbb P(Z\supseteq T,\ Z\text{ does not satisfy }\mathcal Q)
 >\tfrac12r^{|T|}\geq\tfrac12r^s$$ at each addition. A previously added set cannot be chosen again, and the process makes fewer than $2r^{-s}$ additions. It makes at least one, because $\mathcal R$ is nonempty.

At termination the conditional probability is at least $1/2$ for every $T\in\mathcal R$. The conditional law of $Z$ given $Z\supseteq T$ is the law of $T\cup Z$ with a fresh Bernoulli sample. Hence $f_{\mathcal Q}(T)\geq1/2$. The function $f_{\mathcal Q}$ is increasing in $S$, proving <a href="#eq:compression" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:compression">[eq:compression]</a> whenever $S$ contains such a $T$. ◻

</details>

<a id="pm-h-8"></a>
#### The transfer lemma

<a id="eq:parameters"></a>
All logarithms below are natural. The parameter range used in both this section and the next is $$\label{eq:parameters}
 0<r<1,\qquad s\in\mathbb N,\qquad
 s\leq10\log n,\qquad r^s\geq n^{-1/100}.$$ The bounds will be uniform over this range.

<a id="stmt-prop-transfer"></a>
<div id="prop:transfer" class="proposition">

**Proposition 7** (Neighborhood transfer). *There is an absolute integer $N_1$ such that the following holds for every $n\geq N_1$ and every $r,s$ satisfying <a href="#eq:parameters" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:parameters">[eq:parameters]</a>. There exists $\mathcal D=\mathcal D(n,r,s)\subseteq2^{E(K_n)}$ with $\mu_r(\mathcal D)\geq1-1/K_0$ such that, whenever*

- *$Y\subseteq[n]$ has $|Y|\leq n^{2/3}$;*

- *$J$ is a labelled bipartite graph with all edges between $Y$ and $[n]\setminus Y$, and $E(J)\subseteq E(G)$ for some $G\in\mathcal D$;*

- *at most $n/8$ vertices outside $Y$ have positive degree in $J$, and all of those degrees are at most $s$;*

*one has <a id="eq:transfer"></a>$$\label{eq:transfer}
 p_c(J;n)\leq r_*:=1-(1-r)^{32}\leq32r.$$*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-prop-transfer"><summary>Proof of stmt-prop-transfer</summary>

*Proof.* We impose upper bounds on source sums of $f_{\mathcal Q}$ and lower bounds on target counts satisfying $\mathcal Q$. Compression makes each vertex in a set $U$ to be matched contribute at least $1/2$ to an appropriate source sum, while each target neighborhood counted by the corresponding $\mathcal Q$ is usable for at least one vertex of $U$.

<a id="pm-h-9"></a>
###### The test families.

<a id="eq:source-test"></a>
<a id="eq:target-test"></a>
Use every $Y\subseteq[n]$ of size at most $n^{2/3}$ and every nonempty family $$\mathcal Q\subseteq\{T\subseteq Y:1\leq|T|\leq s\},
 \qquad |\mathcal Q|\leq\lceil2r^{-s}\rceil.$$ Write $m=n-|Y|$, put $\tau=1-(1-r)^2$, and let $x(\mathcal Q)$ and $x_*(\mathcal Q)$ be the probabilities that Bernoulli-$\tau$ and Bernoulli-$r_*$ subsets of $Y$ satisfy $\mathcal Q$, respectively. Both are at least $r^s$. Define $\mathcal D$ by requiring, for every test $(Y,\mathcal Q)$, $$\label{eq:source-test}
 \frac1m\sum_{w\in[n]\setminus Y}
 f_{\mathcal Q}(N_G(w)\cap Y)\leq2x(\mathcal Q).$$ Also define a target event by the inequalities $$\label{eq:target-test}
 \frac1m\bigl|\{w\in[n]\setminus Y:
       N_{G_*}(w)\cap Y\text{ satisfies }\mathcal Q\}\bigr|
 \geq\frac34x_*(\mathcal Q)$$ for all of the same tests.

<a id="pm-h-10"></a>
###### Simultaneous concentration.

For fixed $Y,\mathcal Q$ and $G\sim G(n,r)$, the summands in <a href="#eq:source-test" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:source-test">[eq:source-test]</a> are independent and lie in $[0,1]$. Their mean is $x(\mathcal Q)$: the neighborhood sample and the independent sample in <a href="#eq:smoothed-test" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:smoothed-test">[eq:smoothed-test]</a> have union density $\tau$. Their independence follows because the edges from distinct vertices outside $Y$ into $Y$ are disjoint sets of random variables.

<a id="eq:test-count"></a>
The logarithm of the number of tests is at most $$\label{eq:test-count}
 C\bigl(n^{2/3}\log n+n^{1/100}(\log n)^2\bigr)$$ with an absolute $C$. Indeed, choosing $Y$ costs at most $(\lfloor n^{2/3}\rfloor+1)n^{n^{2/3}}$ possibilities. One can then list at most $\lceil2n^{1/100}\rceil$ subsets, each of size at most $s\leq10\log n$. This overcounts families and gives <a href="#eq:test-count" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:test-count">[eq:test-count]</a>.

For large $n$, $m\geq n/2$. Since $x(\mathcal Q)\geq n^{-1/100}$, Hoeffding’s inequality (Hoeffding 1963) <a id="pm-cite-12"></a>[[Hoeffding63](#ref-Hoeffding63)] bounds the failure probability of <a href="#eq:source-test" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:source-test">[eq:source-test]</a> for a fixed test by $\exp(-c n^{98/100})$. The indicators in <a href="#eq:target-test" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:target-test">[eq:target-test]</a>, for $G_*\sim G(n,r_*)$, are likewise independent, with mean $x_*(\mathcal Q)\geq n^{-1/100}$. The same bound, with another absolute $c>0$, applies to their relative deviation by $1/4$. Thus each simultaneous event has probability at least $1-\varepsilon_n$, where uniformly in $r,s$, <a id="eq:uniform-error"></a>$$\label{eq:uniform-error}
 \varepsilon_n\leq
 2\exp\!\left(
 C\bigl(n^{2/3}\log n+n^{1/100}(\log n)^2\bigr)
 -c n^{98/100}\right)\longrightarrow0.$$ Increase $N_1$ so that this error is at most $\min\{1/K_0,1/2\}$ for $n\geq N_1$.

The relation between the two test means is important. A Bernoulli-$r_*$ set is the union of $16$ independent Bernoulli-$\tau$ sets. Satisfaction is increasing, so <a id="eq:amplification"></a>$$\label{eq:amplification}
 x_*(\mathcal Q)\geq1-(1-x(\mathcal Q))^{16}.$$

<a id="pm-h-11"></a>
###### Hall’s criterion.

Fix $J,Y,G$ as in the statement and a target graph $G_*$ satisfying <a href="#eq:target-test" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:target-test">[eq:target-test]</a>. Keep $Y$ fixed and match the positive-degree vertices of $J$ outside $Y$ to distinct vertices of $[n]\setminus Y$. A candidate image $a$ is usable for a vertex $w$ when $N_J(w)\subseteq N_{G_*}(a)\cap Y$.

For any nonempty set $U$ of vertices to be matched, apply Lemma <a href="#lem:compression" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:compression">6</a> to $$\mathcal R=\{N_J(w):w\in U\}.$$ It gives an admissible test $\mathcal Q\subseteq\mathcal R$. Every $N_G(w)\cap Y$, for $w\in U$, satisfies $\mathcal R$, because $G$ contains the labelled edges of $J$. Writing $x=x(\mathcal Q)$, <a href="#eq:source-test" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:source-test">[eq:source-test]</a> therefore yields <a id="eq:hall-source"></a>$$\label{eq:hall-source}
 |U|/2\leq\sum_{w\in[n]\setminus Y}
       f_{\mathcal Q}(N_G(w)\cap Y)\leq2xm.$$ Repeated neighborhoods disappear from $\mathcal R$, but their distinct vertices still contribute separately to this sum.

Every target vertex counted in <a href="#eq:target-test" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:target-test">[eq:target-test]</a> is usable for some vertex of $U$, because $\mathcal Q\subseteq\mathcal R$. If $x\leq1/16$, then $$x_*(\mathcal Q)\geq1-e^{-16x}\geq8x,$$ so there are at least $6xm\geq|U|$ usable vertices. If $x>1/16$, there are at least $$\frac34(1-e^{-1})m\geq\frac38(1-e^{-1})n>n/8\geq|U|.$$ Hall’s theorem (Hall 1935) <a id="pm-cite-13"></a>[[Hall35](#ref-Hall35)] gives the matching in both cases. All choices of $\mathcal Q$ are legitimate even though they depend on $J,U,G$: the inequalities were established simultaneously for every test.

The matching embeds every edge of $J$ while fixing $Y$. Any remaining isolated vertices can be added afterward. The target event has probability at least $1/2$, giving $p_c(J;n)\leq r_*$. Finally, the union bound for $32$ independent Bernoulli-$r$ samples gives $r_*\leq32r$. ◻

</details>

<a id="sec:direct"></a>
### Direct embedding lemmas

Neighborhood transfer will handle edges between a small high-degree set and its complement. Two direct estimates cover the other parts: one for graphs with bounded maximum degree and one for small star forests. Neither requires membership in the typical family.

<a id="pm-h-13"></a>
#### A maximum-degree bound

<a id="stmt-prop-degree-embedding"></a>
<div id="prop:degree-embedding" class="proposition">

**Proposition 8**. *There is an absolute integer $N_2$ such that, for $n\geq N_2$ and $r,s$ satisfying <a href="#eq:parameters" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:parameters">[eq:parameters]</a>, every graph $J$ with $$|V_+(J)|\leq n/4,\qquad \mathop{\mathrm{degen}}(J)\leq s,
 \qquad\Delta(J)\leq n^{2/3}$$ and at most $n$ vertices satisfies $p_c(J;n)\leq r$.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-prop-degree-embedding"><summary>Proof of stmt-prop-degree-embedding</summary>

<a id="eq:layers"></a>
*Proof.* Discard isolated vertices and assume that an edge remains. We partition the vertices into layers that are independent sets, constructed in reverse order. In any residual subgraph with $m'$ vertices, the average degree is at most $2s$. At least $m'/2$ vertices therefore have degree at most $4s$. The graph they induce has an independent set of size at least $m'/(2(4s+1))$, by greedily choosing a vertex and deleting it and its neighbors. Take such a set as the last remaining layer and repeat on the residual graph. Geometric decrease gives $$\label{eq:layers}
 t\leq C s\log n\leq C'(\log n)^2$$ layers. Every vertex has at most $4s$ neighbors in earlier layers.

For each layer choose a disjoint bin in $[n]$ of size equal to the layer size plus $$b=\left\lfloor\frac{n}{4t}\right\rfloor.$$ The bins fit, since their total size is at most $n/4+tb\leq n/2$. Embed the layers in order. At a given step expose the edges between the current bin and the earlier images, and seek a matching of the layer into its bin. These edges are fresh independent Bernoulli-$r$ variables conditional on the entire past: all previous exposures were between earlier bins.

Write $\Delta=\Delta(J)$. Fix a subset $U$ of the current layer with $|U|=u\geq1$. Each vertex of $U$ requires adjacency to at most $4s$ earlier images. Each earlier image occurs in at most $\Delta$ requirements, so the conflict graph joining intersecting requirements has maximum degree at most $4s\Delta$. It has an independent set of size at least $u/(1+4s\Delta)$, whose requirements are pairwise disjoint.

<a id="eq:hall-failure"></a>
For a set $W$ of $u-1$ vertices in the current bin, at least $b$ vertices of the bin remain outside $W$. The probability that none is usable for any vertex of $U$ is at most $$\label{eq:hall-failure}
 \exp\!\left(-\frac{bu r^{4s}}{1+4s\Delta}\right).$$ Indeed, restrict to the disjoint requirements just selected. Each succeeds at a given candidate with probability at least $r^{4s}$, independently over those requirements and over candidates. If an empty requirement is present, the failure event is impossible, so the bound remains valid.

<a id="eq:hall-exponent"></a>
Uniformly under <a href="#eq:parameters" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:parameters">[eq:parameters]</a> and <a href="#eq:layers" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:layers">[eq:layers]</a>, for sufficiently large $n$, $$\label{eq:hall-exponent}
 \frac{b r^{4s}}{1+4s\Delta}
 \geq c\frac{n^{1-4/100-2/3}}{(\log n)^3}
 =c\frac{n^{22/75}}{(\log n)^3}\geq5\log n.$$ Here $b\geq c'n/(\log n)^2$ and $r^{4s}\geq n^{-4/100}$. There are at most $n^{2u}$ choices of $U,W$ for each $u$. Failure of Hall’s condition implies such a pair with all usable images of $U$ in $W$. By <a href="#eq:hall-failure" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:hall-failure">[eq:hall-failure]</a>–<a href="#eq:hall-exponent" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:hall-exponent">[eq:hall-exponent]</a>, the conditional failure probability at a layer is at most $$\sum_{u\geq1}n^{2u}e^{-5u\log n}
 =\sum_{u\geq1}n^{-3u}=O(n^{-3}).$$ Summing this bound over the $O((\log n)^2)$ layers shows that the embedding succeeds with probability tending to one, uniformly over the allowed parameters. Taking an absolute $N_2$ so that it succeeds with probability at least $1/2$ proves the proposition. ◻

</details>

<a id="pm-h-14"></a>
#### Small star forests

A star forest is a graph whose nontrivial components are stars. The next argument adapts the fresh-center embedding in (Ascoli et al. 2026, Claim 6.6) <a id="pm-cite-14"></a>[[AHPT26](#ref-AHPT26)]. Small support is imposed directly, so it is unnecessary to divide the stars by their degrees in advance.

<a id="stmt-prop-stars"></a>
<div id="prop:stars" class="proposition">

<a id="eq:star-threshold"></a>
**Proposition 9**. *Put $A=400e^4$. There is an absolute integer $N_3$ such that, for every $n\geq N_3$ and every star forest $J$ on at most $n$ vertices with $|V_+(J)|\leq n/100$, $$\label{eq:star-threshold}
 p_c(J;n)\leq2Aq(J;n).$$ If $q(J;n)<1/(2A)$, the stronger bound $p_c(J;n)\leq Aq(J;n)$ holds.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-prop-stars"><summary>Proof of stmt-prop-stars</summary>

*Proof.* The edgeless case is immediate. Put $q_J=q(J;n)>0$. If $q_J\geq1/(2A)$, then $2Aq_J\geq1$, so <a href="#eq:star-threshold" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:star-threshold">[eq:star-threshold]</a> is automatic. We may therefore assume $q_J<1/(2A)$ and set $p=Aq_J\in(0,1/2)$.

<a id="eq:star-count"></a>
For $\ell\geq1$, let $m_\ell$ be the number of components of $J$ with exactly $\ell$ leaves, ignoring isolated vertices. If $m=m_\ell>0$, the copies on $[n]$ of $m$ disjoint $\ell$-edge stars cover $\mathcal F_J$. Their number is at most $$C_\ell=\frac{n^{m(\ell+1)}}{(\ell!)^m m!}.$$ This follows by counting vertex injections and dividing by permutations of the leaves and of the stars. For $\ell=1$ there are additional automorphisms, which only decrease the actual count. Since such a star forest exists on at most $n$ vertices, $C_\ell\geq1$. Using its cover at $(1/(2C_\ell))^{1/(m\ell)}\in(0,1)$ yields $$\label{eq:star-count}
 q_J^\ell\geq\frac{\ell!}{n^{\ell+1}}(m!/2)^{1/m}
 \geq\frac{\ell!}{n^{\ell+1}}\frac{m_\ell}{2e}.$$ The last step uses $m!\geq(m/e)^m$ and $2^{1/m}\leq2$.

Partition $[n]$ into a center bin of size $\lfloor n/2\rfloor$ and a leaf bin containing the remaining vertices. Process the components of $J$ one at a time. For a star with $\ell$ leaves, test unused centers successively until one has at least $\ell$ neighbors among the unused leaf vertices. Use that center and any $\ell$ such neighbors, and never test that center again. Failed centers are also discarded.

<a id="eq:expected-trials"></a>
Throughout the process, at least $b'=\lfloor n/3\rfloor$ leaf vertices remain. The unused leaf set is determined by previously tested center rows, and a fresh center row has not been exposed. Thus each trial, conditional on the past, has success probability at least <a id="eq:star-success"></a>$$\label{eq:star-success}
 u_\ell=\mathbb P(\mathop{\mathrm{Bin}}(b',p)\geq\ell).$$ For the purpose of counting trials, append infinitely many auxiliary centers with fresh independent rows to the same leaf bin. This enlarged process agrees with the actual process until the original center bin is exhausted. The number $T$ of trials needed to finish all stars in the enlarged process satisfies $$\label{eq:expected-trials}
 \mathbb ET\leq\sum_{\ell:m_\ell>0}\frac{m_\ell}{u_\ell},$$ because the conditional expected waiting time for each success is at most $1/u_\ell$.

If $\mu=b'p\geq2\ell$, the second-moment bound $\mathbb E[\mathop{\mathrm{Bin}}(b',p)^2]\leq\mu^2+\mu$ and the Paley–Zygmund inequality give $$u_\ell\geq\frac14\frac{\mu^2}{\mu^2+\mu}\geq\frac16,$$ since $\mu\geq2$. Otherwise $b'p<2\ell$. For every occupied star size, $\ell\leq n/100$, and for large $n$, $b'-\ell+1\geq n/4$. As $p<1/2$, the inequality $\log(1-p)\geq-2p$ implies <a id="eq:star-small-mean"></a>$$\begin{aligned}
 u_\ell
 &\geq\binom{b'}{\ell}p^\ell(1-p)^{b'-\ell}
 \geq\frac{(n/4)^\ell p^\ell}{\ell!}e^{-4\ell}\notag\\
 &\geq\left(\frac{A}{4e^4}\right)^\ell
        \frac{m_\ell}{2e n}
 =100^\ell\frac{m_\ell}{2e n},
 \label{eq:star-small-mean}
\end{aligned}$$ where the last inequality uses <a href="#eq:star-count" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:star-count">[eq:star-count]</a>.

There are at most $n/200$ nontrivial components. Combining the two bounds in <a href="#eq:expected-trials" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:expected-trials">[eq:expected-trials]</a> gives $$\mathbb ET\leq6\frac{n}{200}+2e n\sum_{\ell\geq1}100^{-\ell}
 =\left(\frac3{100}+\frac{2e}{99}\right)n<\frac n8.$$ For large $n$, Markov’s inequality shows that $T\leq\lfloor n/2\rfloor$ with probability at least $1/2$. On this event all centers used belong to the actual random graph, so the construction embeds $J$ in $G(n,p)$. This proves $p_c(J;n)\leq Aq_J$ in the remaining case. ◻

</details>

<a id="sec:assembly"></a>
### Constructing the fixed partition

We now prove Theorem <a href="#thm:main" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:main">1</a>. The high-degeneracy case first uses the typical-family cover and isolates a small set $Y$ so that every vertex outside $Y$ has few neighbors in $Y$ and bounded total degree. Neighborhood transfer handles edges across this cut, while the maximum-degree estimate handles edges inside and outside $Y$ after further splitting. The bounded-degeneracy case uses only small star forests. The partition choices in both cases are deterministic.

<details id="proof-stmt-prop-stars-2"><summary>Proof of stmt-prop-stars</summary>

<a id="eq:deterministic-cutoffs"></a>
<a id="eq:nontrivial-range"></a>
*Proof of Theorem <a href="#thm:main" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:main">1</a>.* Fix <a id="eq:constants"></a>$$\label{eq:constants}
 M=B=1000,\qquad k=2K_0M(B+1)^2.$$ Choose an absolute integer $n_0\geq2$ large enough for Propositions <a href="#prop:transfer" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:transfer">7</a>, <a href="#prop:degree-embedding" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:degree-embedding">8</a>, and <a href="#prop:stars" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:stars">9</a>, and so that, for every $n\geq n_0$, $$\label{eq:deterministic-cutoffs}
 \frac{8\log n}{\log2}\sqrt n\leq n^{2/3},\qquad
 n^{2/3}\leq n/4,\qquad
 2\lceil n/B\rceil\leq n/100.$$ All these conditions have absolute cutoffs. Set $$L=\max\{2A,64,n_0^2\}.$$ If $H$ is edgeless, use only empty pieces. Otherwise write $q=q(H;n)>0$ and $d=\mathop{\mathrm{degen}}(H)\geq1$. When $n<n_0$, Lemma <a href="#lem:q-lower" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:q-lower">4</a> gives $Lq\geq n_0^2/(n(n-1))\geq1$. When $q\geq1/(2A)$, we also have $Lq\geq1$. In either case put all edges in one piece and use empty pieces for the rest. Henceforth $$\label{eq:nontrivial-range}
 n\geq n_0,\qquad q<1/(2A).$$

<a id="pm-h-16"></a>
###### Large degeneracy: $d\geq M$.

<a id="eq:d-upper"></a>
Set $$r=2q,\qquad s=\lceil2d/M\rceil.$$ By Lemma <a href="#lem:q-lower" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:q-lower">4</a>, $r\geq n^{-2/d}$, whereas <a href="#eq:nontrivial-range" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:nontrivial-range">[eq:nontrivial-range]</a> gives $r<1/A<1/2$. Consequently $$\label{eq:d-upper}
 d<\frac{2\log n}{\log2},\qquad s\leq3d/M,
 \qquad r^s\geq n^{-6/M}\geq n^{-1/100}.$$ These bounds also give $s\leq10\log n$, so <a href="#eq:parameters" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:parameters">[eq:parameters]</a> holds.

Take the family $\mathcal D$ from Proposition <a href="#prop:transfer" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:transfer">7</a>. Corollary <a href="#cor:typical-cover" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="cor:typical-cover">3</a> supplies a copy of $H$ covered by $K_0$ members $G_1,\ldots,G_{K_0}$ of $\mathcal D$. Fix those graphs and the labelled copy, and use this copy to label $H$ on a subset of $[n]$. Assign each edge of $H$ to one $G_i$ containing it. This gives $K_0$ initial edge pieces, each contained in its specified typical graph.

<a id="eq:outside-bounds"></a>
We separate the high-degree vertices, following the obstacle identified in (Ascoli et al. 2026, sec. 8) <a id="pm-cite-15"></a>[[AHPT26](#ref-AHPT26)]. Initially let $$Y_0=\{v\in V(H):\deg_H(v)>\sqrt n\}.$$ By <a href="#eq:edge-bound" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:edge-bound">[eq:edge-bound]</a>, $|Y_0|\leq2d\sqrt n$. Starting from $Y_0$, add an outside vertex whenever it has more than $2d$ neighbors in the current set, and stop when no such vertex remains. Let $Y$ be the resulting set and let $a=|Y\setminus Y_0|$. Each added vertex contributes more than $2d$ edges to earlier vertices of the set; no edge is counted twice. Thus $$2da\leq|E(H[Y])|\leq d(|Y_0|+a),$$ giving $|Y|\leq2|Y_0|\leq4d\sqrt n\leq n^{2/3}$ by <a href="#eq:d-upper" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:d-upper">[eq:d-upper]</a> and <a href="#eq:deterministic-cutoffs" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:deterministic-cutoffs">[eq:deterministic-cutoffs]</a>. The conclusion also holds if $Y_0$ is empty, in which case no vertex is added. At termination every $w\notin Y$ satisfies $$\label{eq:outside-bounds}
 \deg_H(w)\leq\sqrt n,\qquad |N_H(w)\cap Y|\leq2d.$$

Partition $V(H)\setminus Y$ into $B$ possibly empty blocks of size at most $\lceil n/B\rceil$. Subdivide each initial edge piece as follows.

*Edges inside $Y$.* Apply Lemma <a href="#lem:split" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:split">5</a> with $m=M$ to obtain $M$ pieces of degeneracy at most $\lceil d/M\rceil\leq s$. Each is supported on at most $|Y|\leq n^{2/3}\leq n/4$ vertices and has maximum degree at most $n^{2/3}$. Proposition <a href="#prop:degree-embedding" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:degree-embedding">8</a> gives threshold at most $r$.

*Edges outside $Y$.* First partition by the unordered pair of endpoint blocks, allowing both endpoints in the same block. There are $B(B+1)/2$ such pairs. Apply Lemma <a href="#lem:split" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:split">5</a> with $m=M$ to each resulting graph. The pieces have degeneracy at most $s$, maximum degree at most $\sqrt n$, and support size at most $2\lceil n/B\rceil\leq n/100$. Proposition <a href="#prop:degree-embedding" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:degree-embedding">8</a> again gives threshold at most $r$.

*Edges between $Y$ and its complement.* First partition by the block of the endpoint outside $Y$. At each such endpoint, distribute its edges in the initial piece among $M$ groups of size at most $\lceil2d/M\rceil=s$, using <a href="#eq:outside-bounds" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:outside-bounds">[eq:outside-bounds]</a>. This assigns every cut edge once. Each resulting bipartite piece is still contained in its specified $G_i\in\mathcal D$, has at most $\lceil n/B\rceil\leq n/8$ positive-degree vertices outside $Y$, and has all those degrees at most $s$. Proposition <a href="#prop:transfer" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:transfer">7</a> gives threshold at most $r_*\leq32r=64q$.

All pieces therefore have threshold at most $64q\leq Lq$. Their number is at most $$K_0M\left(1+\frac{B(B+1)}2+B\right)
 =\frac{K_0M(B+1)(B+2)}2\leq k.$$

<a id="pm-h-17"></a>
###### Bounded degeneracy: $1\leq d<M$.

Lemma <a href="#lem:split" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:split">5</a>, with $m=d$, partitions $E(H)$ into $d$ graphs of degeneracy at most one, hence into forests. Indeed, the last vertex of a cycle in the certifying order would have two preceding neighbors.

Root every nontrivial tree. Color each parent–child edge by the parity of the depth of its parent. Each color class is a star forest: its centers occur at one depth parity, and their children have the other parity and no other incident edge of that color. This is the parity splitting used in (Ascoli et al. 2026, Claim 6.2) <a id="pm-cite-16"></a>[[AHPT26](#ref-AHPT26)].

Partition $V(H)$ into $B$ blocks of size at most $\lceil n/B\rceil$, and split each star forest by unordered endpoint-block pairs. Every resulting piece $J$ remains a star forest and has $|V_+(J)|\leq2\lceil n/B\rceil\leq n/100$. By Proposition <a href="#prop:stars" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:stars">9</a> and <a href="#eq:monotonicity" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:monotonicity">[eq:monotonicity]</a>, $$p_c(J;n)\leq2Aq(J;n)\leq2Aq(H;n)\leq Lq.$$ The number of pieces is at most $2d\,B(B+1)/2=dB(B+1)\leq k$.

Finally, add empty pieces if necessary. In the large-degeneracy case, pull the labelled edge pieces back to the original $H$. The typical graphs, the copy of $H$, all vertex orders, and all assignments have been fixed using only $H,n$; none depends on the random host used to test a piece’s threshold. This completes the proof. ◻

</details>
