## 1. Abstract and result contract

> We prove a bounded spectral recovery theorem for a von Neumann algebra with a faithful normal state whose centralizer consists only of scalars. A positive averaged squared norm for an arbitrary bounded Hilbert-space operator on shrinking modular spectral bands can be recovered on uniformly bounded algebra elements with shrinking spectral support. We apply this theorem to spectral intertwining rigidity and obtain an alternative proof of bicentralizer triviality for type III<sub>1</sub> factors with separable predual.

- **C1** (theorem, [source](#stmt-thm-main)): Let $M$ be a type $\mathrm{III}_1$ factor with separable predual and let $\phi$ be any faithful normal state on $M$. Then $$\mathop{\mathrm{BC}}(M,\phi)=\mathbb C1.$$
  - Explanation: The bicentralizer is scalar for every faithful normal state under the stated factor and separable-predual hypotheses.
- **C2** (theorem, [source](#stmt-thm-rigidity)): Let $M$ be a von Neumann algebra with a faithful normal state $\phi$ such that $M_\phi=\mathbb C1$. Let $b:\mathbb R\curvearrowright M$ be a continuous $\phi$-preserving action. Suppose that, for every $s\in\mathbb R$, every uniformly bounded sequence $(w_n)$ in $M$, and every $a\in M$, $$\label{eq:flow-property} \norm{\phi w_n-e^s w_n\phi}_{M_*}\longrightarrow0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad w_na-b_s(a)w_n\longrightarrow0 \quad\text{strongly}.$$ Then $b_s=\mathop{\mathrm{id}}_M$ for every $s\in\mathbb R$.
  - Explanation: A continuous state-preserving action satisfying the quantified asymptotic intertwining law is trivial when the modular centralizer is scalar.
- **C4** (conditional_result, [source](#stmt-mod-predual)): For $w\in M$ and $s\in\mathbb R$, $$\label{eq:predual-bound} \norm{\phi w-e^sw\phi}_{M_*} \le (1+e^{s/2})\norm{(e^{D/2}-e^{s/2})w\xi}.$$ Consequently, if $(w_n)$ is uniformly bounded and the $D$-spectral support of $w_n\xi$ is contained in $[s-d_n,s+d_n]$ with $d_n\to0$, then the left side of <a href="#eq:predual-bound" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:predual-bound">[eq:predual-bound]</a> tends to zero.
  - Explanation: Modular spectral localization controls the predual defect; shrinking bands give convergence for uniformly bounded sequences.
- **C5** (conditional_result, [source](#stmt-mod-localization)): Let $f\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R)$, with Fourier convention $f(u)=\int_{\mathbb R}g(t)e^{itu}\,dt$. For $y\in M$, the weak integral $$y_f=\int_{\mathbb R}g(t)\sigma_t(y)\,dt$$ belongs to $M$ and satisfies $\norm{y_f}\le\norm{g}_1\norm{y}$ and $y_f\xi=f(D)y\xi$. If $a\in M$ and $a\xi$ has compact $D$-spectral support, there is $a^\flat\in M$ such that $$\label{eq:right-multiplier} a\xi=\xi a^\flat.$$ The same Fourier construction applies to any continuous state-preserving action and its unitary implementation.
  - Explanation: Smooth Fourier filtering realizes spectral localization inside the algebra and supplies bounded right multipliers for compact spectral support.
- **C6** (conditional_result, [source](#stmt-mod-means)): For every $y\in M$ and $\psi\in M_*$, $$\begin{aligned} \int\sigma_t(y)\xi\,d\mu_k(t)&\longrightarrow\phi(y)\xi &&\text{in }H,\label{eq:hilbert-mean}\\ \int\psi\circ\sigma_{-t}\,d\mu_k(t)&\longrightarrow\psi(1)\phi &&\text{in }M_*.\label{eq:predual-mean} \end{aligned}$$
  - Explanation: Modular averages converge in Hilbert-space norm and in predual norm under the scalar-centralizer assumption.
- **C7** (conditional_result, [source](#stmt-mod-average)): For $S\in\mathcal B(H)$, define $P(S)$ by $$\langle P(S)h,k\rangle =m_t\langle U_t^*SU_th,k\rangle.$$ Then $P$ is a positive unital contraction, $P(S)$ commutes with every spectral projection of $D$, and $$\label{eq:scalar-average} P(L(a))=\phi(a)1\qquad(a\in M).$$ In particular, $$\label{eq:quadratic-average} \norm{t\mapsto SU_th}_{\mathrm{av}}^2 =\langle P(S^*S)h,h\rangle.$$
  - Explanation: The fixed invariant mean defines a positive unital contraction commuting with modular spectral projections; averaged left multiplication becomes scalar.
- **C8** (theorem, [source](#stmt-rec-bounded)): Let $M$ be a von Neumann algebra with a faithful normal state $\phi$ such that $M_\phi=\mathbb C1$. Use the standard-space notation and the fixed mean $m$ of [mod:average](#mod:average). Fix $T\in\mathcal B(H)$ and $s\in\mathbb R$. Suppose that $\delta_n>0$ tends to zero and that $h_n\in H$ are unit vectors with $D$-spectral support in $[s-\delta_n,s+\delta_n]$. If $$\label{eq:recovery-positive} \limsup_{n\to\infty}m_t\norm{TU_th_n}^{2}>0,$$ then there are a subsequence $(n_j)$, elements $v_j\in M$, and constants $C_*<\infty$ and $\eta>0$ such that, for every $j$, $$\norm{v_j}\le C_*,\qquad \norm{Tv_j\xi}\ge\eta,$$ and $v_j\xi$ has $D$-spectral support in $[s-4\delta_{n_j},s+4\delta_{n_j}]$.
  - Explanation: A positive averaged defect on shrinking modular bands yields uniformly bounded algebra witnesses on shrinking enlarged bands after taking a subsequence.
- **C9** (conditional_result, [source](#stmt-spec-point)): Let $a,a^\flat\in M$ satisfy $a\xi=\xi a^\flat$. For $s\in\mathbb R$, set $$T_s=R(a^\flat)-L(b_s(a))\in\mathcal B(H).$$ If $h_n\in H$ are unit vectors with $D$-spectral supports contained in $[s-d_n,s+d_n]$, where $d_n>0$ and $d_n\to0$, then $$\norm{t\mapsto T_sU_th_n}_{\mathrm{av}}\longrightarrow0. \label{eq:point-estimate}$$
  - Explanation: Bounded recovery contradicts the intertwining hypothesis if the averaged defect persists at a fixed modular energy.
- **C10** (conditional_result, [source](#stmt-spec-interval)): Let $a,a^\flat\in M$ satisfy $a\xi=\xi a^\flat$. For every nonempty compact interval $I\subset\mathbb R$, every $c\in\mathbb C$, and every $h\in E_D(I)H$, $$\norm{t\mapsto (R(a^\flat)-cL(a))U_th}_{\mathrm{av}} \leq \norm{h}\sup_{s\in I}\norm{(b_s(a)-ca)\xi}. \label{eq:interval-estimate}$$
  - Explanation: Spectral partitioning extends the point estimate to a compact interval without requiring a rate uniform in the modular energy.
- **C11** (conditional_result, [source](#stmt-spec-pairing)): For every $(r,p),(q,\ell)\in\mathcal S$, $$\exp\bigl(i(r\ell+qp)\bigr)=1. \label{eq:spectral-pairing}$$
  - Explanation: Interchanging localized products and reversing modular time imposes the symmetric phase relation on joint spectral support.
- **C12** (conditional_result, [source](#stmt-spec-eigenvalues)): If $M_\phi=\mathbb C1$, then $D$ has no nonzero eigenvalue. If, in addition, $D$ has pure point spectrum, then $M=\mathbb C1$.
  - Explanation: A scalar modular centralizer excludes nonzero modular eigenvalues; pure point modular spectrum forces the algebra to be scalar.

## 2. Definitions, validity and bounds

- **modular centralizer** ([source](#pm-h-2)): Write $M_\phi$ for the fixed-point algebra of its modular automorphism group $\sigma^\phi$.
- **ergodic faithful normal state** ([source](#pm-h-2)): We call $\phi$ ergodic when $M_\phi=\mathbb C1$.
- **standard-space notation** ([source](#pm-h-2)): On the standard Hilbert space $H=L^2(M)$, put $\xi=\phi^{1/2}$, $D=\log\Delta_\phi$, and $U_t=e^{itD}$.
- **predual multiplication convention** ([source](#pm-h-3)): We use the predual convention $$(w\phi)(y)=\phi(yw),\qquad (\phi w)(y)=\phi(wy).$$
- **bicentralizer** ([source](#pm-h-3)): The bicentralizer $\mathop{\mathrm{BC}}(M,\phi)$ consists of those $a\in M$ for which $[a,w_n]\to0$ strongly whenever $(w_n)$ is uniformly bounded and $\norm{w_n\phi-\phi w_n}_{M_*}\to0$.
- **continuous action** ([source](#pm-h-5)): A continuous action on $M$ means a $u$-continuous action on its automorphism group.
- **state seminorm** ([source](#sec:bicentralizers)): For a faithful normal state $\phi$, put $\norm{x}_\phi=\phi(x^*x)^{1/2}$.
- **averaged vector seminorm** ([source](#sec:modular)): For a bounded continuous $H$-valued function $F$, put $$\norm{F}_{\mathrm{av}} =\bigl(m_t\norm{F(t)}^2\bigr)^{1/2}.$$
- **operator averaging** ([source](#stmt-mod-average)): For $S\in\mathcal B(H)$, define $P(S)$ by $$\langle P(S)h,k\rangle =m_t\langle U_t^*SU_th,k\rangle.$$
- **joint spectral support** ([source](#pm-h-13)): The self-adjoint operators $D$ and $Q$ strongly commute; let $E$ be their joint spectral resolution and let $\mathcal S\subset\mathbb R^2$ be its closed support.

## 3. Proof roadmap

- **R1** [claim](#stmt-mod-predual) / [proof](#proof-stmt-mod-predual): Replace the two state-vector expressions using the modular identity and bound the total error. -> A predual norm estimate from spectral localization.
- **R2** [claim](#stmt-mod-means) / [proof](#proof-stmt-mod-means): Use the Hilbert-space mean ergodic theorem and the predual annihilator argument. -> Norm limits that control both terms in the moment calculation.
- **R3** [claim](#stmt-mod-average) / [proof](#proof-stmt-mod-average): Average modular conjugations against the fixed invariant mean. -> A positive contraction commuting with modular spectral projections.
- **R4** [claim](#stmt-rec-bounded) / [proof](#proof-stmt-rec-bounded): Filter bounded approximations, average their moments, randomize circle phases, truncate and restore the band. -> Uniformly bounded witnesses with a positive defect on shrinking bands.
- **R5** [claim](#stmt-spec-point) / [proof](#proof-stmt-spec-point): Apply bounded recovery to a fixed defect operator and contradict asymptotic intertwining. -> Vanishing averaged defect at a fixed modular energy.
- **R6** [claim](#stmt-spec-interval) / [proof](#proof-stmt-spec-interval): Partition a compact spectral interval, select a normalized piece and use compactness. -> An interval estimate without a uniform pointwise rate.
- **R7** [claim](#stmt-spec-pairing) / [proof](#proof-stmt-spec-pairing): Localize algebra vectors jointly, interchange their products and reverse modular time. -> The symmetric phase relation on joint spectral support.
- **R8** [claim](#stmt-spec-eigenvalues) / [proof](#proof-stmt-spec-eigenvalues): Extract a bounded eigenoperator through twisted means and weak compactness; compare its two state masses. -> No nonzero modular eigenvalue; pure point spectrum forces scalarity.
- **R9** [claim](#stmt-thm-rigidity) / [proof](#proof-stmt-spec-eigenvalues-2): Constrain the joint support by the symmetric pairing and eliminate the pure point and modular-axis cases. -> Triviality of the state-preserving action.
- **R10** [claim](#stmt-thm-main) / [proof](#proof-stmt-bc-ergodicity-2): Reduce to a self-bicentralizing factor and combine triviality of the flow with its ergodicity. -> Scalarity of the original bicentralizer.

## 4. Engineering Triptych

### Panel 1 - epistemic contract

- **recovery**: A positive limiting averaged squared norm for a fixed operator on unit vectors in shrinking modular bands yields a subsequence of uniformly bounded algebra elements with a positive lower bound on the transformed vector norms and spectral support localized within narrow symmetric intervals around the target spectral parameter.
- **kernel**: The Fourier kernel norm is independent of the filter scale, and the filtered operator norm is bounded by a constant multiple of the element norm.
- **averaging**: Operator averaging is positive, unital and contractive; its output commutes with every spectral projection of $D$.
- **rigidity**: The continuous state-preserving action is trivial under the asymptotic spectral intertwining hypothesis.

### Panel 2 - negative knowledge

- **localization**: Infer uniformly bounded algebra representatives from localized Hilbert-space vectors alone. -> Spectral localization alone does not produce uniform algebra norm bounds.
- **uniform-time**: Choose one averaging time for every initial representative. -> The initial representatives can have growing operator norms, and the averaging limits are taken with the representative fixed.
- **cutoff-band**: Treat polar truncation as preserving the original modular spectral band. -> The polar cutoff need not preserve spectral localization.
- **normal-average**: Use the invariant-mean average as a normal operator map. -> Normality is not asserted by the averaging result.
- **finite-certificate**: Use a finite spectral model or finite phase sampling to certify the full recovery or rigidity theorem. -> The proof relies on limiting spectral bands and a fixed free-ultrafilter mean, together with existential selection of realizations.

### Panel 3 - reference implementation and oracle

Applicability: `not_applicable`. The bounded recovery and rigidity results concern asymptotic spectral bands and infinite operator-algebraic data. The proof fixes a free ultrafilter to define the averaging mean, chooses averaging times separately for initial representatives, and selects phase realizations by existence of a maximum. Its finite averages are intermediate existence statements, with no finite effective selection or termination contract for the theorem inputs. Therefore no finite constructive implementation of the stated result is supplied.

## 6. Applicability limits and search

- **L1** (hypothesis): Bounded recovery requires a faithful normal state whose modular centralizer is scalar. Blocked use: ['Do not apply bounded recovery when the scalar-centralizer hypothesis is unproved or fails.']
- **L2** (hypothesis): The bicentralizer application proved here assumes a type $\mathrm{III}_1$ factor with separable predual. Blocked use: ['Do not attribute a proof without the separable-predual hypothesis to this application.']
- **L3** (counterexample_scope): A scalar centralizer and an irreducible centralizer are distinct assumptions in the stated results. Blocked use: ['Do not replace the scalar-centralizer hypothesis by irreducibility of the centralizer.']
- **L4** (hypothesis): Spectral intertwining rigidity requires the stated law for every modular energy, every uniformly bounded sequence, and every algebra element. Blocked use: ['Do not infer rigidity from state preservation alone or from a finite sample of intertwining tests.']
- **L5** (approximation): Spectral localization alone does not produce uniformly bounded algebra elements. Blocked use: ['Do not identify arbitrary localized Hilbert-space vectors with uniformly bounded algebra representatives.']

The absolute bicentralizer conjecture is not included.

## 7. Complete mathematical paper


We prove a bounded spectral recovery theorem for a von Neumann algebra with a faithful normal state whose centralizer consists only of scalars. A positive averaged squared norm for an arbitrary bounded Hilbert-space operator on shrinking modular spectral bands can be recovered on uniformly bounded algebra elements with shrinking spectral support. We apply this theorem to spectral intertwining rigidity and obtain an alternative proof of bicentralizer triviality for type III<sub>1</sub> factors with separable predual.

### Introduction

Spectral localization in the standard Hilbert space of a von Neumann algebra does not by itself produce uniformly bounded elements of the algebra. This distinction matters when an argument starts with spectral vectors but its asymptotic identities apply only to bounded operator sequences. Our main analytical result, [rec:bounded](#rec:bounded), provides a bounded recovery principle for this situation.

Let $M$ be a von Neumann algebra with a faithful normal state $\phi$. Write $M_\phi$ for the fixed-point algebra of its modular automorphism group $\sigma^\phi$. We call $\phi$ ergodic when $M_\phi=\mathbb C1$. On the standard Hilbert space $H=L^2(M)$, put $\xi=\phi^{1/2}$, $D=\log\Delta_\phi$, and $U_t=e^{itD}$. For an arbitrary fixed $T\in\mathcal B(H)$, consider unit vectors supported in spectral intervals of $D$ shrinking to a real number $s$. [rec:bounded](#rec:bounded) shows that a positive limiting averaged value of $\norm{TU_th}^2$ on these vectors can be recovered as a positive value of $\norm{Tv\xi}^2$ on a sequence of uniformly bounded $v\in M$. The recovered vectors remain in intervals shrinking to $s$, with widths at most four times the original widths. The averaging uses a fixed translation-invariant mean obtained from symmetric interval averages. The exact quantifiers and mean are given in [mod:average](#mod:average), [rec:bounded](#rec:bounded).

The only structural assumption in this recovery theorem is $M_\phi=\mathbb C1$; neither a bicentralizer condition nor a second action is required. This assumption supplies a broad class of applications: Marrakchi and Vaes proved that ergodic faithful normal states form a dense $G_\delta$ in the normal state space of every type $\mathrm{III}_1$ factor with separable predual (Marrakchi and Vaes 2024, Theorem A) <a id="pm-cite-1"></a>[[MarrakchiVaes2024](#ref-MarrakchiVaes2024)].

<a id="pm-h-3"></a>
###### Bicentralizers and prior work.

We use the predual convention $$(w\phi)(y)=\phi(yw),\qquad
  (\phi w)(y)=\phi(wy).$$ The bicentralizer $\mathop{\mathrm{BC}}(M,\phi)$ consists of those $a\in M$ for which $[a,w_n]\to0$ strongly whenever $(w_n)$ is uniformly bounded and $\norm{w_n\phi-\phi w_n}_{M_*}\to0$. Connes’ bicentralizer conjecture asserts that this algebra is scalar for every type $\mathrm{III}_1$ factor with separable predual. Connes developed the problem in his classification program for injective factors (Connes 1982, 1985) <a id="pm-cite-2"></a>[[Connes1980](#ref-Connes1980), [Connes1985](#ref-Connes1985)]. Haagerup proved the amenable case, completing the uniqueness theorem for the injective type $\mathrm{III}_1$ factor (Haagerup 1987) <a id="pm-cite-3"></a>[[Haagerup1987](#ref-Haagerup1987)].

Haagerup also related triviality to the existence of a faithful normal state with irreducible centralizer, meaning $(M_\phi)'\cap M=\mathbb C1$ (Haagerup 1987, Theorem 3.1) <a id="pm-cite-4"></a>[[Haagerup1987](#ref-Haagerup1987)]. Together with Popa’s work on maximal abelian subalgebras (Popa 1981) <a id="pm-cite-5"></a>[[Popa1981](#ref-Popa1981)], this connects the problem to expected subalgebras; the expected-masa equivalence is stated in (Houdayer and Isono 2017, Corollary 3.6) <a id="pm-cite-6"></a>[[HI2017](#ref-HI2017)]. Important nonamenable cases include Houdayer’s theorem for free Araki–Woods factors (Houdayer 2009) <a id="pm-cite-7"></a>[[Houdayer2009](#ref-Houdayer2009)] and the semisolid factors treated by Houdayer and Isono (Houdayer and Isono 2017, Theorem 3.7) <a id="pm-cite-8"></a>[[HI2017](#ref-HI2017)]. An irreducible centralizer in these results is different from the scalar centralizer assumed in our recovery theorem.

Houdayer and Isono proved that a nonscalar bicentralizer of a separable type $\mathrm{III}_1$ factor is itself a type $\mathrm{III}_1$ factor and equals its own bicentralizer for the restricted state (Houdayer and Isono 2017, Proposition 3.4 and Theorem 3.5) <a id="pm-cite-9"></a>[[HI2017](#ref-HI2017)]. Ando, Haagerup, Houdayer, and Marrakchi established the canonical relative bicentralizer flow (Ando et al. 2020, Theorem A) <a id="pm-cite-10"></a>[[AHHM2020](#ref-AHHM2020)], following the flow construction of Haagerup and its independent discovery by Marrakchi. Marrakchi proved that every nonidentity time of the absolute flow is ergodic, without a fullness assumption (Marrakchi 2020, Theorem D) <a id="pm-cite-11"></a>[[Marrakchi2020](#ref-Marrakchi2020)]. His subsequent work develops the binormal-state and relative-core machinery (Marrakchi 2025, 2026) <a id="pm-cite-12"></a>[[Marrakchi2025](#ref-Marrakchi2025), [Marrakchi2026](#ref-Marrakchi2026)].

<a id="pm-h-4"></a>
###### The absolute application and the recent announcement.

Houdayer and Marrakchi have announced triviality of the bicentralizer for every type $\mathrm{III}_1$ factor admitting a faithful normal state, with no separability condition in the theorem statement (Houdayer and Marrakchi 2026, Theorem G) <a id="pm-cite-13"></a>[[HoudayerMarrakchi2026](#ref-HoudayerMarrakchi2026)]. The application proved here is an alternative proof of the separable-predual instance of that conclusion.

<a id="stmt-thm-main"></a>
<div id="thm:main" class="theorem">

**Theorem 1**. *Let $M$ be a type $\mathrm{III}_1$ factor with separable predual and let $\phi$ be any faithful normal state on $M$. Then $$\mathop{\mathrm{BC}}(M,\phi)=\mathbb C1.$$*

</div>

By (Houdayer and Isono 2017, Corollary 3.6) <a id="pm-cite-14"></a>[[HI2017](#ref-HI2017)], also implies that every type $\mathrm{III}_1$ factor with separable predual admits a maximal abelian subalgebra with a faithful normal conditional expectation. This is an existence statement for the factor; the expectation need not preserve the scalar-centralizer state used in bounded recovery.

The independent analytical contribution is the bounded recovery theorem, which applies to arbitrary $T$ without assuming a bicentralizer intertwining law. Houdayer and Marrakchi obtain the same symmetric spectral relation used below through multiplication isometries and a singular KMS state on the continuous core (Houdayer and Marrakchi 2026, sec. 8.4, Propositions 8.8–8.10) <a id="pm-cite-15"></a>[[HoudayerMarrakchi2026](#ref-HoudayerMarrakchi2026)]. Here modular averaging and bounded recovery lead to that relation through two interval estimates.

Houdayer and Marrakchi explicitly disclose assistance from *GPT-5.6 Sol* and *GPT-6 Astra* (Houdayer and Marrakchi 2026, 7) <a id="pm-cite-16"></a>[[HoudayerMarrakchi2026](#ref-HoudayerMarrakchi2026)]. They credit Sol with counterexample exploration and an initial strict-outerness argument, and Astra with the singular tracial-state ingredient in their resonance strategy. They describe checking and rewriting the proofs and retaining responsibility for them. These are their stated contributions to that work, whose relative tracial-action theory is also developed in the paper.

<a id="pm-h-5"></a>
###### The rigidity statement.

A continuous action on $M$ means a $u$-continuous action on its automorphism group. Bounded recovery gives the following spectral proof of rigidity.

<a id="stmt-thm-rigidity"></a>
<div id="thm:rigidity" class="theorem">

<a id="eq:flow-property"></a>
**Theorem 2** (Spectral intertwining rigidity). *Let $M$ be a von Neumann algebra with a faithful normal state $\phi$ such that $M_\phi=\mathbb C1$. Let $b:\mathbb R\curvearrowright M$ be a continuous $\phi$-preserving action. Suppose that, for every $s\in\mathbb R$, every uniformly bounded sequence $(w_n)$ in $M$, and every $a\in M$, $$\label{eq:flow-property}
  \norm{\phi w_n-e^s w_n\phi}_{M_*}\longrightarrow0
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  w_na-b_s(a)w_n\longrightarrow0
  \quad\text{strongly}.$$ Then $b_s=\mathop{\mathrm{id}}_M$ for every $s\in\mathbb R$.*

</div>

The conclusion also follows from the announced absolute theorem. Indeed, at $s=0$, hypothesis <a href="#eq:flow-property" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:flow-property">[eq:flow-property]</a> implies $\mathop{\mathrm{BC}}(M,\phi)=M$. If $M\ne\mathbb C1$, its scalar centralizer makes it a type $\mathrm{III}_1$ factor by (Marrakchi and Vaes 2024, Lemma 2.1) <a id="pm-cite-17"></a>[[MarrakchiVaes2024](#ref-MarrakchiVaes2024)]; the announced theorem would then force $M=\mathbb C1$. Our proof instead derives [thm:rigidity](#thm:rigidity) directly from bounded recovery and uses it to prove [thm:main](#thm:main). For that application to a self-bicentralizing factor, the modular centralizer is scalar, the bicentralizer flow satisfies <a href="#eq:flow-property" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:flow-property">[eq:flow-property]</a>, and that flow is ergodic. A trivial ergodic action has scalar underlying algebra.

<a id="pm-h-6"></a>
###### Recovery and the spectral argument.

The recovery proof combines modular averaging with the fourth-moment and polar-cutoff devices of Haagerup and Musat (Haagerup and Musat 2007, Equation (2.10), Lemma 2.6, and Remark 2.8) <a id="pm-cite-18"></a>[[HaagerupMusat2007](#ref-HaagerupMusat2007)]. The strategy of recovering bounded witnesses by randomization, fourth-moment control, and truncation appears in the tracial spectral-gap argument of Ioana and Vaes (Ioana and Vaes 2015, 127–30) <a id="pm-cite-19"></a>[[IoanaVaes2015](#ref-IoanaVaes2015)]. Related fourth-moment control underlies Marrakchi’s binormal-state implementation (Marrakchi 2025, sec. 2, Claim 2.2) <a id="pm-cite-20"></a>[[Marrakchi2025](#ref-Marrakchi2025)]. Here modular orbit averages supply the moment bounds, and a final Fourier filter restores the shrinking spectral bands. The averaging time is chosen separately for each initial bounded representative of a spectral vector; these representatives need not be uniformly bounded. Norm convergence in the predual controls a varying second-moment operator, and independent circle phases give a uniform fourth-moment bound. One fixed polar truncation threshold then suffices. Fourier localization restores the spectral support without increasing operator norms by a factor depending on the width of the spectral interval. The Fourier operations belong to the standard spectral-subspace framework for automorphism groups (Arveson 1974) <a id="pm-cite-21"></a>[[Arveson1974](#ref-Arveson1974)]; the bounds needed here are proved explicitly.

For the spectral application, implement the state-preserving action by $V_s(a\xi)=b_s(a)\xi$ and write $V_s=e^{isQ}$. These unitaries commute with $U_t$, so $(D,Q)$ has a joint spectral support $\mathcal S\subset\mathbb R^2$. The recovered bounded sequences let <a href="#eq:flow-property" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:flow-property">[eq:flow-property]</a> force averaged intertwining defects to vanish on bands shrinking to a fixed modular energy. A spectral partition then gives an interval estimate without requiring a rate uniform in that energy. Applying this estimate twice, with the operators interchanged and the time reversed, gives $$\exp\bigl(i(r\ell+qp)\bigr)=1
  \qquad ((r,p),(q,\ell)\in\mathcal S).$$ This symmetric relation is the final rigidity mechanism. It forces the joint support to be countable or to lie on a coordinate axis. Scalarity of the modular centralizer excludes nonzero modular eigenvalues, by the bounded-eigenoperator extraction and KMS argument of (Marrakchi and Vaes 2024, Lemma 2.1) <a id="pm-cite-22"></a>[[MarrakchiVaes2024](#ref-MarrakchiVaes2024)], leaving only the axis on which the flow generator vanishes.

### Bicentralizers and the reduction

We recall the definition to fix both the topology and the quantifiers. For a faithful normal state $\phi$, put $\norm{x}_\phi=\phi(x^*x)^{1/2}$. The bicentralizer consists of those $a\in M$ for which, for every $\varepsilon>0$, there is $\delta>0$ such that $$u\in\mathcal U(M),\quad
  \norm{u\phi-\phi u}_{M_*}<\delta
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  \norm{u^*au-a}_\phi<\varepsilon.$$ This is Haagerup’s unitary formulation of the usual bicentralizer (Haagerup 1987, proof of Lemma 1.2) <a id="pm-cite-23"></a>[[Haagerup1987](#ref-Haagerup1987)]. Equivalently, its elements asymptotically commute strongly with every uniformly bounded sequence that asymptotically centralizes $\phi$ in predual norm. We use established structural results in this equivalent form.

<a id="sec:modular"></a>
### Modular localization and averaging

The recovery argument requires two kinds of control: modular spectral localization must imply a predual norm estimate, and modular averaging must control the moments of an operator even when its norm is large. We establish these facts here. The two norm limits in [mod:means](#mod:means) will control different terms in the fourth-moment expansion in [sec:recovery](#sec:recovery).

<a id="eq:modular-identities"></a>
Throughout this Section $M$ is a von Neumann algebra with a faithful normal state $\phi$. Let $H=L^2(M)$ be its standard Hilbert space, $\xi=\phi^{1/2}$ its cyclic separating unit vector, and $$L(a)h=ah,\qquad R(a)h=ha,\qquad
  D=\log\Delta_\phi,\qquad U_t=e^{itD}.$$ Write $E_D$ for the spectral resolution of $D$ and $J$ for the modular conjugation. Inner products are linear in the first variable. We use the standard modular theory of (Takesaki 2003) <a id="pm-cite-30"></a>[[Takesaki2003](#ref-Takesaki2003)]. We write $\sigma_t=\sigma^\phi_t$. Standard modular theory gives $$\label{eq:modular-identities}
  U_t(a\xi)=\sigma_t(a)\xi,\qquad
  e^{D/2}a\xi=\xi a,\qquad
  R(a)=JL(a)^*J.$$ Indeed, $M\xi$ is contained in the domain of the closed Tomita operator $S=J\Delta_\phi^{1/2}$, with $S(a\xi)=a^*\xi$. Thus $\Delta_\phi^{1/2}a\xi=Ja^*\xi=\xi a$. These standard-form conventions are also those of (Ando et al. 2020, sec. 2.1) <a id="pm-cite-31"></a>[[AHHM2020](#ref-AHHM2020)].

<a id="stmt-mod-predual"></a>
<div id="mod:predual" class="lemma">

<a id="eq:predual-bound"></a>
**Lemma 4** (From modular localization to predual norm). *For $w\in M$ and $s\in\mathbb R$, $$\label{eq:predual-bound}
  \norm{\phi w-e^sw\phi}_{M_*}
  \le (1+e^{s/2})\norm{(e^{D/2}-e^{s/2})w\xi}.$$ Consequently, if $(w_n)$ is uniformly bounded and the $D$-spectral support of $w_n\xi$ is contained in $[s-d_n,s+d_n]$ with $d_n\to0$, then the left side of <a href="#eq:predual-bound" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:predual-bound">[eq:predual-bound]</a> tends to zero.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-mod-predual"><summary>Proof of stmt-mod-predual</summary>

*Proof.* Put $c=e^{s/2}$ and $\eta=\xi w-cw\xi$. By <a href="#eq:modular-identities" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:modular-identities">[eq:modular-identities]</a>, $\eta=(e^{D/2}-c)w\xi$, and $J\eta=w^*\xi-c\xi w^*$. For $\norm{y}\le1$, commutation of the left and right actions gives $$\langle y\xi,\xi w^*\rangle
  =\langle y(\xi w),\xi\rangle.$$ Starting from $\phi(wy)=\langle y\xi,w^*\xi\rangle$, replace $w^*\xi$ by $c\xi w^*$, and then $\xi w$ by $cw\xi$. The total error is at most $(1+c)\norm{\eta}$, while the resulting expression is $c^2\phi(yw)$. Taking the supremum over $y$ proves the estimate. The last assertion follows from $$\norm{(e^{D/2}-e^{s/2})w_n\xi}
  \le \sup_{\abs{u-s}\le d_n}\abs{e^{u/2}-e^{s/2}}\,
       \norm{w_n\xi}.
  \qedhere$$ ◻

</details>

<a id="stmt-mod-localization"></a>
<div id="mod:localization" class="lemma">

**Lemma 5** (Smooth spectral localization). *Let $f\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R)$, with Fourier convention $f(u)=\int_{\mathbb R}g(t)e^{itu}\,dt$. For $y\in M$, the weak integral $$y_f=\int_{\mathbb R}g(t)\sigma_t(y)\,dt$$ belongs to $M$ and satisfies $\norm{y_f}\le\norm{g}_1\norm{y}$ and $y_f\xi=f(D)y\xi$. If $a\in M$ and $a\xi$ has compact $D$-spectral support, there is $a^\flat\in M$ such that <a id="eq:right-multiplier"></a>$$\label{eq:right-multiplier}
  a\xi=\xi a^\flat.$$ The same Fourier construction applies to any continuous state-preserving action and its unitary implementation.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-mod-localization"><summary>Proof of stmt-mod-localization</summary>

*Proof.* The inverse Fourier transform $g$ is integrable. The bounded weak integral is defined by integration against normal functionals, and its operator norm has the stated bound. Applying it to $\xi$ and using the spectral theorem gives $y_f\xi=f(D)y\xi$. This uses no operator norm continuity of the orbit.

If $K$ is a compact support for $a\xi$, choose $f\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R)$ equal to $e^{-u/2}$ on a neighborhood of $K$, and put $a^\flat=a_f$. Then <a href="#eq:modular-identities" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:modular-identities">[eq:modular-identities]</a> and functional calculus give $$\xi a^\flat=e^{D/2}a^\flat\xi
  =e^{D/2}f(D)a\xi=a\xi.$$ All vectors in this computation belong to the domain of $e^{D/2}$; in particular the filtered vector has compact spectral support. The Fourier argument for another state-preserving action is identical. ◻

</details>

For the rest of this Section assume $M_\phi=\mathbb C1$. Let $\mu_k$ be uniform probability measure on $[-k,k]$, for $k\in\mathbb N$.

<a id="stmt-mod-means"></a>
<div id="mod:means" class="lemma">

<a id="eq:hilbert-mean"></a>
<a id="eq:predual-mean"></a>
**Lemma 6** (Two norm ergodic limits). *For every $y\in M$ and $\psi\in M_*$, $$\begin{aligned}
  \int\sigma_t(y)\xi\,d\mu_k(t)&\longrightarrow\phi(y)\xi
       &&\text{in }H,\label{eq:hilbert-mean}\\
  \int\psi\circ\sigma_{-t}\,d\mu_k(t)&\longrightarrow\psi(1)\phi
       &&\text{in }M_*.\label{eq:predual-mean}
\end{aligned}$$*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-mod-means"><summary>Proof of stmt-mod-means</summary>

*Proof.* The unitary mean ergodic theorem gives a norm limit for the vectors in <a href="#eq:hilbert-mean" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:hilbert-mean">[eq:hilbert-mean]</a>. The corresponding operator averages have norm at most $\norm{y}$. For each fixed $u$, translating the interval changes their averages in operator norm by at most $\abs{u}\norm{y}/k$ for all sufficiently large $k$. Every weak operator cluster point is consequently fixed by $\sigma$, hence scalar. Evaluation under $\phi$ identifies it as $\phi(y)1$. This identifies the Hilbert norm limit and proves <a href="#eq:hilbert-mean" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:hilbert-mean">[eq:hilbert-mean]</a>.

The action on $M_*$ is norm continuous on each functional. For positive normal functionals this follows from their realization as vector functionals in standard form and the strong continuity of $U$; linear decomposition gives the general case. The predual averages are therefore Bochner integrals and are contractions. Let $$\mathcal N=\overline{\mathop{\mathrm{span}}}^{\,\norm{\cdot}}
   \{\rho\circ\sigma_u-\rho:\rho\in M_*,\ u\in\mathbb R\}.$$ Its annihilator in $(M_*)^*=M$ is $M_\phi=\mathbb C1$. The Hahn–Banach theorem implies $\mathcal N=\{\psi\in M_*:\psi(1)=0\}$. The averages tend to zero in norm on each generator of $\mathcal N$, by the interval boundary estimate, and hence on all of $\mathcal N$ by contractivity. They fix $\phi$. Apply this to $\psi-\psi(1)\phi$ to obtain <a href="#eq:predual-mean" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:predual-mean">[eq:predual-mean]</a>. ◻

</details>

Fix a free ultrafilter $\omega$ on $\mathbb N$. For a bounded continuous scalar function $f$ define $$m_t f(t)=\lim_{k\to\omega}\int f(t)\,d\mu_k(t).$$ This positive normalized mean is invariant under translations and under $t\mapsto-t$. For a bounded continuous $H$-valued function $F$, put $$\norm{F}_{\mathrm{av}}
  =\bigl(m_t\norm{F(t)}^2\bigr)^{1/2}.$$ The triangle inequality follows from the corresponding inequalities for the finite interval integrals. Thus this is a seminorm.

<a id="stmt-mod-average"></a>
<div id="mod:average" class="lemma">

<a id="eq:scalar-average"></a>
<a id="eq:quadratic-average"></a>
**Lemma 7** (Operator averaging). *For $S\in\mathcal B(H)$, define $P(S)$ by $$\langle P(S)h,k\rangle
  =m_t\langle U_t^*SU_th,k\rangle.$$ Then $P$ is a positive unital contraction, $P(S)$ commutes with every spectral projection of $D$, and $$\label{eq:scalar-average}
  P(L(a))=\phi(a)1\qquad(a\in M).$$ In particular, $$\label{eq:quadratic-average}
  \norm{t\mapsto SU_th}_{\mathrm{av}}^2
  =\langle P(S^*S)h,h\rangle.$$*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-mod-average"><summary>Proof of stmt-mod-average</summary>

*Proof.* The bounded sesquilinear form defines $P(S)$, with $\norm{P(S)}\le\norm{S}$. Positivity and unitality follow directly from the mean. Translation invariance gives $U_u^*P(S)U_u=P(S)$ for every $u$, hence commutation with the spectral projections of $D$. For $S=L(a)$ the averages and their weak limits belong to the weakly closed algebra $L(M)$, since $U_t^*L(a)U_t=L(\sigma_{-t}(a))$. The fixed-point algebra is scalar, and evaluation at $\xi$ gives <a href="#eq:scalar-average" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:scalar-average">[eq:scalar-average]</a>. Equation <a href="#eq:quadratic-average" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:quadratic-average">[eq:quadratic-average]</a> is the definition applied to $S^*S$. No normality of $P$ is asserted or needed. ◻

</details>

<a id="sec:recovery"></a>
### Recovering bounded operators from spectral vectors

converts a positive averaged quantity on narrow modular spectral bands into a positive quantity on uniformly bounded operators. Its proof uses both norm limits in [mod:means](#mod:means): the Hilbert-space limit controls one fourth-moment term, and the predual limit controls the other. No uniform operator norm bound is required for the initial representatives of the spectral vectors. Randomization, fourth-moment control, truncation, and selection of a bounded witness occur together in the tracial spectral-gap argument of Ioana and Vaes (Ioana and Vaes 2015, proof of the appendix theorem, pp. 129–130) <a id="pm-cite-32"></a>[[IoanaVaes2015](#ref-IoanaVaes2015)]. Related fourth-moment control gives equi-integrability in Marrakchi’s binormal-state implementation (Marrakchi 2025, sec. 2, Claim 2.2) <a id="pm-cite-33"></a>[[Marrakchi2025](#ref-Marrakchi2025)]. We use the fourth-moment and polar-cutoff devices of Haagerup and Musat (Haagerup and Musat 2007, Lemma 2.6 and Remark 2.8) <a id="pm-cite-34"></a>[[HaagerupMusat2007](#ref-HaagerupMusat2007)], with moment bounds supplied by modular orbit averages against the given state. The final Fourier localization retains the shrinking spectral band.

<a id="stmt-rec-bounded"></a>
<div id="rec:bounded" class="theorem">

**Theorem 8** (Bounded recovery). *Let $M$ be a von Neumann algebra with a faithful normal state $\phi$ such that $M_\phi=\mathbb C1$. Use the standard-space notation and the fixed mean $m$ of [mod:average](#mod:average). Fix $T\in\mathcal B(H)$ and $s\in\mathbb R$. Suppose that $\delta_n>0$ tends to zero and that $h_n\in H$ are unit vectors with $D$-spectral support in $[s-\delta_n,s+\delta_n]$. If <a id="eq:recovery-positive"></a>$$\label{eq:recovery-positive}
 \limsup_{n\to\infty}m_t\norm{TU_th_n}^{2}>0,$$ then there are a subsequence $(n_j)$, elements $v_j\in M$, and constants $C_*<\infty$ and $\eta>0$ such that, for every $j$, $$\norm{v_j}\le C_*,\qquad
 \norm{Tv_j\xi}\ge\eta,$$ and $v_j\xi$ has $D$-spectral support in $[s-4\delta_{n_j},s+4\delta_{n_j}]$.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-rec-bounded"><summary>Proof of stmt-rec-bounded</summary>

*Proof.* We use the same family of Fourier filters to localize the initial bounded approximations and to restore localization after polar truncation. Choose $f_0\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R)$ such that $$0\le f_0\le1,\qquad f_0=1\text{ on }[-1,1],\qquad
 \mathop{\mathrm{supp}}f_0\subset[-2,2],$$ and write $f_0(u)=\int_\mathbb Rg_0(t)e^{itu}\,dt$, with $g_0$ a Schwartz function. For $\rho>0$, set $$f_\rho(u)=f_0\bigl((u-s)/\rho\bigr),\qquad
 g_\rho(t)=\rho e^{-ist}g_0(\rho t).$$ Then $f_\rho(u)=\int_\mathbb Rg_\rho(t)e^{itu}\,dt$ and <a id="eq:recovery-kernel"></a>$$\label{eq:recovery-kernel}
 \norm{g_\rho}_1=\norm{g_0}_1=:C_1
 \quad\text{for every }\rho>0.$$ By [mod:localization](#mod:localization), the weak integral $$\mathcal F_\rho(x)
   =\int_\mathbb Rg_\rho(t)\sigma_t(x)\,dt\in M$$ satisfies <a id="eq:recovery-filter"></a>$$\label{eq:recovery-filter}
 \mathcal F_\rho(x)\xi=f_\rho(D)x\xi,
 \qquad \norm{\mathcal F_\rho(x)}\le C_1\norm{x}.$$ In particular, its vector has spectral support in $[s-2\rho,s+2\rho]$.

*Approximation by bounded representatives.* By [eq:recovery-positive](#eq:recovery-positive), after passing to a subsequence and reindexing, there is $\gamma>0$ such that $$m_t\norm{TU_th_n}^{2}\ge4\gamma\qquad(n\ge1).$$ Since $M\xi$ is dense in $H$, approximate $h_n$ by $x_n\xi$ and apply $f_{\delta_n}(D)$. This multiplier fixes $h_n$ and is a contraction. Thus the vectors $\mathcal F_{\delta_n}(x_n)\xi$ approximate $h_n$ as closely as desired. Normalize them, obtaining $y_n\in M$ with $\norm{y_n\xi}=1$ and spectral support in $[s-d_n,s+d_n]$, where $d_n=2\delta_n$. Choose the approximations so that $\norm{y_n\xi-h_n}\to0$ and <a id="eq:recovery-defect"></a>$$\label{eq:recovery-defect}
 m_t\norm{TU_ty_n\xi}^{2}\ge2\gamma\qquad(n\ge1).$$ This is possible because the averaged seminorm in [mod:average](#mod:average) gives $$\abs{\norm{t\mapsto TU_ty_n\xi}_{\mathrm{av}}
       -\norm{t\mapsto TU_th_n}_{\mathrm{av}}}
 \le\norm{T}\norm{y_n\xi-h_n}.$$ Discard finitely many terms so that $d_n\le1$. The modular identity in [eq:modular-identities](#eq:modular-identities) gives <a id="eq:recovery-two-masses"></a>$$\label{eq:recovery-two-masses}
 \phi(y_n^*y_n)=1,\qquad
 \phi(y_ny_n^*)=\norm{e^{D/2}y_n\xi}^{2}\le e^{s+d_n}.$$ There is no asserted uniform bound on $\norm{y_n}$.

*Simultaneous defect and moment bounds.* Fix $n$ for the moment, and write $y=y_n$. With $\mu_k$ the uniform probability measure on $[-k,k]$, define $$\begin{aligned}
 A_k&=\int\sigma_t(y^*y)\,d\mu_k(t),&
 B_k&=\int\sigma_t(yy^*)\,d\mu_k(t),\\
 \psi(x)&=\phi(y^*xy),&
 \psi_k&=\int\psi\circ\sigma_{-t}\,d\mu_k(t).
 \end{aligned}$$ We seek simultaneous control of $\phi(A_k^2)$ and $\psi_k(B_k)$, which will bound the fourth moment after discretization. Here $\psi$ is a normal state. State invariance gives the exact identity <a id="eq:recovery-functional"></a>$$\label{eq:recovery-functional}
 \psi_k(x)=
 \int\phi\bigl(\sigma_t(y)^*x\sigma_t(y)\bigr)\,d\mu_k(t).$$ By the two ordinary norm limits in [mod:means](#mod:means), $$A_k\xi\longrightarrow\xi,\qquad
 \norm{\psi_k-\phi}_{M_*}\longrightarrow0.$$ Consequently $\phi(A_k^2)=\norm{A_k\xi}^{2}\to1$. Although $B_k$ depends on $k$, it satisfies $$\norm{B_k}\le\norm{y}^{2},\qquad
 \phi(B_k)=\phi(yy^*)\le e^{s+1},$$ and hence <a id="eq:recovery-varying-test"></a>$$\label{eq:recovery-varying-test}
 \abs{\psi_k(B_k)-\phi(B_k)}
 \le\norm{\psi_k-\phi}_{M_*}\norm{y}^{2}\longrightarrow0.$$ All these limits are taken with $n$ fixed. For every sufficiently large $k$ we may impose $\phi(A_k^2)\le2$ and $\norm{\psi_k-\phi}_{M_*}\le\norm{y}^{-2}$ simultaneously. It follows that <a id="eq:recovery-continuous-moments"></a>$$\label{eq:recovery-continuous-moments}
 \phi(A_k^2)+\psi_k(B_k)\le3+e^{s+1}.$$ On the other hand, [eq:recovery-defect](#eq:recovery-defect) and the definition of $m$ imply that the set of integers $k$ for which $$\int\norm{T\sigma_t(y)\xi}^{2}\,d\mu_k(t)>\frac32\gamma$$ belongs to the fixed free ultrafilter. It therefore meets the cofinite set on which [eq:recovery-continuous-moments](#eq:recovery-continuous-moments) holds. Choose one such $k$, still for this fixed $n$.

We next replace this continuous average by a finite probability average. Put $z_t=\sigma_t(y)$. The three scalar functions $$t\longmapsto\norm{Tz_t\xi}^{2},\qquad
 (t,u)\longmapsto\phi(z_t^*z_tz_u^*z_u),\qquad
 (t,u)\longmapsto\phi(z_t^*z_uz_u^*z_t)$$ are continuous on the relevant compact interval or square. Indeed, $t\mapsto z_t$ is bounded and strong-star continuous, and products on bounded sets preserve this continuity. A common sufficiently fine partition of $[-k,k]$, using its normalized interval lengths as weights, therefore approximates all three integrals. The product weights approximate the double integrals defining $\phi(A_k^2)$ and $\psi_k(B_k)$. Thus there are finitely many times $t_i$ and weights $p_i\ge0$, with $\sum_i p_i=1$, such that, writing $$z_i=\sigma_{t_i}(y),\qquad
 A=\sum_i p_i z_i^*z_i,\qquad
 B=\sum_i p_i z_iz_i^*,$$ we have <a id="eq:recovery-finite"></a>$$\label{eq:recovery-finite}
 \sum_i p_i\norm{Tz_i\xi}^{2}\ge\gamma,
 \qquad
 \phi(A^2)+\sum_i p_i\phi(z_i^*Bz_i)\le C,
 \qquad C:=4+e^{s+1}.$$ The finite averages may depend on $n$; $\gamma$ and $C$ do not. In particular, no estimate on the rate in [eq:recovery-varying-test](#eq:recovery-varying-test) uniform in $n$ has been used.

*Randomization and truncation.* For this finite average, let $\epsilon_i$ be independent random variables uniformly distributed on the complex unit circle, and set $$z=\sum_i\sqrt{p_i}\,\epsilon_i z_i.$$ The vector $z\xi$ is supported in $[s-d_n,s+d_n]$. Independence and vanishing first moments give $$\mathbb E\norm{Tz\xi}^{2}
   =\sum_i p_i\norm{Tz_i\xi}^{2}\ge\gamma.$$ The following expansion is the Steinhaus variant of the fourth-moment identity in (Haagerup and Musat 2007, equation (2.10) and Remark 2.8) <a id="pm-cite-35"></a>[[HaagerupMusat2007](#ref-HaagerupMusat2007)], with coefficients $\sqrt{p_i}z_i$. We include the expansion to keep track of the order of the noncommuting factors. The two surviving pairings give <a id="eq:recovery-fourth"></a>$$\label{eq:recovery-fourth}
 \mathbb E[(z^*z)^2]
  =A^2+\sum_i p_i z_i^*Bz_i
       -\sum_i p_i^2(z_i^*z_i)^2.$$ The last sum subtracts the overlap of the pairings, where all four indices agree. Its summands are positive, so [eq:recovery-finite](#eq:recovery-finite) implies <a id="eq:recovery-fourth-bound"></a>$$\label{eq:recovery-fourth-bound}
 \mathbb E\phi((z^*z)^2)\le C.$$

We next use the functional-calculus truncation underlying (Haagerup and Musat 2007, Lemma 2.6, equations (2.15)–(2.20)) <a id="pm-cite-36"></a>[[HaagerupMusat2007](#ref-HaagerupMusat2007)]. For $K>0$, write $z=u\abs{z}$ in its polar decomposition and put $z^{[K]}=u\min(\abs{z},K)$. Then $\norm{z^{[K]}}\le K$ and <a id="eq:recovery-truncation"></a>$$\label{eq:recovery-truncation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E\norm{(z-z^{[K]})\xi}^{2}
 &=\mathbb E\phi\bigl((\abs{z}-K)_+^2\bigr)\\
 &\le K^{-2}\mathbb E\phi(\abs{z}^{4})
 \le CK^{-2}.
 \end{aligned}$$ This controls the vector error on the side required for the bounded operator $T$. No estimate for the adjoint truncation error is needed.

*Restoring the spectral band and selecting realizations.* Define the random element $v=\mathcal F_{d_n}(z^{[K]})\in M$. By [eq:recovery-filter](#eq:recovery-filter), every realization satisfies <a id="eq:recovery-bounded-output"></a>$$\label{eq:recovery-bounded-output}
 \norm{v}\le C_1K,
 \qquad v\xi=f_{d_n}(D)z^{[K]}\xi,$$ and $v\xi$ is supported in $[s-2d_n,s+2d_n]$. Since $f_{d_n}(D)$ fixes $z\xi$ and has norm at most one, [eq:recovery-truncation](#eq:recovery-truncation) gives $$\mathbb E\norm{v\xi-z\xi}^{2}\le CK^{-2}.$$ The triangle inequality in the Hilbert space of square-integrable random $H$-vectors now yields <a id="eq:recovery-fixed-cutoff"></a>$$\label{eq:recovery-fixed-cutoff}
 \bigl(\mathbb E\norm{Tv\xi}^{2}\bigr)^{1/2}
 \ge\sqrt\gamma-\frac{\norm{T}\sqrt C}{K}.$$ Fix once and for all $$K\ge2\norm{T}\sqrt{C/\gamma}.$$ The hypothesis implies $T\ne0$, so $K$ can be chosen positive. This choice is independent of $n$ and ensures that the left-hand side of [eq:recovery-fixed-cutoff](#eq:recovery-fixed-cutoff) is at least $\sqrt\gamma/2$.

To select a realization, note that $z$ depends norm-continuously on the finite phase torus and $z^{[K]}=zq_K(z^*z)$, where $$q_K(r)=\min\{1,K/\sqrt r\}\quad(r>0),\qquad q_K(0)=1.$$ Since $q_K$ is continuous on $[0,\infty)$, continuous functional calculus makes the cutoff norm-continuous. The filter $\mathcal F_{d_n}$ is bounded and linear, so $\norm{Tv\xi}^2$ is continuous on the compact phase torus. It therefore attains a maximum at least its expectation. Choose such a realization $v_n$ for each $n$. Then $$\norm{v_n}\le C_1K,
 \qquad \norm{Tv_n\xi}\ge\frac{\sqrt\gamma}{2},$$ and its spectral support lies in $[s-2d_n,s+2d_n]=[s-4\delta_n,s+4\delta_n]$. Restoring the subsequence notation proves [rec:bounded](#rec:bounded) with $C_*=C_1K$ and $\eta=\sqrt\gamma/2$. ◻

</details>

summarizes the construction. The averaging time depends on the initial representative, whereas the moment bound, cutoff threshold, and Fourier-kernel norm are independent of the width of its spectral band.

### Spectral rigidity

We now prove [thm:rigidity](#thm:rigidity). Throughout this Section, assume its hypotheses and retain the modular notation and the symmetric mean from [mod:average](#mod:average). In particular, $M_\phi=\mathbb C1$. We use no ergodicity assumption on $b$.

<a id="pm-h-12"></a>
#### From bounded recovery to an interval estimate

When $a\xi=\xi a^\flat$, the identity $wa\xi=R(a^\flat)w\xi$ for $w\in M$ writes the intertwining defect, at fixed $s$, as a bounded operator applied to $w\xi$.

<a id="stmt-spec-point"></a>
<div id="spec:point" class="lemma">

**Lemma 9** (Estimate at a spectral point). *Let $a,a^\flat\in M$ satisfy $a\xi=\xi a^\flat$. For $s\in\mathbb R$, set $$T_s=R(a^\flat)-L(b_s(a))\in\mathcal B(H).$$ If $h_n\in H$ are unit vectors with $D$-spectral supports contained in $[s-d_n,s+d_n]$, where $d_n>0$ and $d_n\to0$, then <a id="eq:point-estimate"></a>$$\norm{t\mapsto T_sU_th_n}_{\mathrm{av}}\longrightarrow0.
 \label{eq:point-estimate}$$*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-spec-point"><summary>Proof of stmt-spec-point</summary>

*Proof.* If the conclusion fails, [rec:bounded](#rec:bounded), applied to the fixed operator $T_s$, gives a uniformly bounded sequence $v_n\in M$ such that the $D$-spectral supports of $v_n\xi$ shrink to $s$ and $$\norm{T_sv_n\xi}\geq\eta>0
 \qquad\text{for every }n.$$ By [mod:predual](#mod:predual), $$\norm{\phi v_n-e^s v_n\phi}_{M_*}\longrightarrow0.$$ The intertwining hypothesis <a href="#eq:flow-property" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:flow-property">[eq:flow-property]</a> therefore gives $(v_na-b_s(a)v_n)\xi\to0$. Since left and right multiplication commute, $$T_sv_n\xi
   =v_n\xi a^\flat-b_s(a)v_n\xi
   =(v_na-b_s(a)v_n)\xi,$$ a contradiction. ◻

</details>

The point estimate supplies no uniform rate in $s$. To pass to an interval, we use the commutation of the averaged quadratic form with the spectral projections of $D$.

<a id="stmt-spec-interval"></a>
<div id="spec:interval" class="lemma">

**Lemma 10** (Estimate on an interval). *Let $a,a^\flat\in M$ satisfy $a\xi=\xi a^\flat$. For every nonempty compact interval $I\subset\mathbb R$, every $c\in\mathbb C$, and every $h\in E_D(I)H$, <a id="eq:interval-estimate"></a>$$\norm{t\mapsto (R(a^\flat)-cL(a))U_th}_{\mathrm{av}}
 \leq \norm{h}\sup_{s\in I}\norm{(b_s(a)-ca)\xi}.
 \label{eq:interval-estimate}$$*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-spec-interval"><summary>Proof of stmt-spec-interval</summary>

<a id="eq:partition-gap"></a>
*Proof.* Put $$S=R(a^\flat)-cL(a),\qquad
 C=P(S^*S),\qquad
 B=\sup_{s\in I}\norm{(b_s(a)-ca)\xi}.$$ The number $B$ is finite, and [mod:average](#mod:average) gives $$\norm{t\mapsto SU_th}_{\mathrm{av}}^2=\langle Ch,h\rangle.$$ Suppose that the asserted estimate fails. By homogeneity there is a unit vector $h\in E_D(I)H$ with $$\langle Ch,h\rangle=B^2+\varepsilon
 \qquad(\varepsilon>0).$$ For each positive integer $n$, partition $I$ into finitely many disjoint Borel intervals $J$ of diameter at most $1/n$. Since $C$ commutes with the spectral projections of $D$, $$\langle Ch,h\rangle
   =\sum_J\langle C E_D(J)h,E_D(J)h\rangle,
 \qquad
 \sum_J\norm{E_D(J)h}^2=1.$$ Consequently, one nonzero spectral piece, after normalization, gives a unit vector $h_n$ supported in $\overline{J_n}$ such that $$\langle Ch_n,h_n\rangle\geq B^2+\varepsilon.
 \label{eq:partition-gap}$$ By compactness of $I$, after passing to a subsequence these supports lie in intervals shrinking to a single point $s\in I$. This also covers a singleton interval $I$.

Write $z=b_s(a)-ca$, so that $S=T_s+L(z)$. By [spec:point](#spec:point), $\norm{t\mapsto T_sU_th_n}_{\mathrm{av}}\to0$. On the other hand, [mod:average](#mod:average) gives the exact identity $$\norm{t\mapsto L(z)U_th_n}_{\mathrm{av}}^2
 =\langle P(L(z^*z))h_n,h_n\rangle
 =\phi(z^*z)\leq B^2.$$ The triangle inequality now yields $\limsup_n\norm{t\mapsto SU_th_n}_{\mathrm{av}}\leq B$, contrary to <a href="#eq:partition-gap" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:partition-gap">[eq:partition-gap]</a>. ◻

</details>

<a id="pm-h-13"></a>
#### The joint spectral pairing

State preservation gives a unitary implementation $$V_s(a\xi)=b_s(a)\xi\qquad(a\in M).$$ It is strongly continuous by the $u$-continuity of $b$: for $a\in M$, $$\norm{V_s(a\xi)-a\xi}^2
 =2\phi(a^*a)-2\operatorname{Re}\phi(a^*b_s(a))\longrightarrow0
 \qquad(s\to0),$$ and density extends this continuity to $H$. Since $b_s$ preserves $\phi$, it commutes with the modular automorphism group. Thus $V_sU_t=U_tV_s$ for all $s,t$. Write $V_s=e^{isQ}$. The self-adjoint operators $D$ and $Q$ strongly commute; let $E$ be their joint spectral resolution and let $\mathcal S\subset\mathbb R^2$ be its closed support.

The joint spectral measure is concentrated on $\mathcal S$. Indeed, its complement is a union of open sets with zero spectral projection, and the countable base of $\mathbb R^2$ reduces this to a countable union. Hence $E(\mathcal S)=1$, without any separability assumption on $H$.

<a id="stmt-spec-pairing"></a>
<div id="spec:pairing" class="lemma">

<a id="eq:spectral-pairing"></a>
**Lemma 11** (Joint spectral pairing). *For every $(r,p),(q,\ell)\in\mathcal S$, $$\exp\bigl(i(r\ell+qp)\bigr)=1.
 \label{eq:spectral-pairing}$$*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-spec-pairing"><summary>Proof of stmt-spec-pairing</summary>

*Proof.* Fix $\delta>0$ and write $I_v=[v-\delta,v+\delta]$. We first choose nonzero $x,a\in M$ such that $$x\xi\in E(I_r\times I_p)H,
 \qquad
 a\xi\in E(I_q\times I_\ell)H.$$ Here is the justification for this bounded localization. Choose nonnegative smooth functions $f,g$ supported in the interiors of $I_r,I_p$ and equal to one on smaller neighborhoods of $r,p$. The spectral projection of the resulting smaller rectangle is nonzero by the definition of $\mathcal S$. Consequently, $f(D)g(Q)\neq0$. Density of $M\xi$ supplies $y\in M$ with $f(D)g(Q)y\xi\neq0$. Fourier integration along $b$ realizes $g(Q)y\xi$ as a vector in $M\xi$: if $g(u)=\int_{\mathbb R}\widehat g(t)e^{itu}\,dt$, use the bounded weak integral $\int_{\mathbb R}\widehat g(t)b_t(y)\,dt$. The subsequent filter $f(D)$ also preserves $M\xi$ by [mod:localization](#mod:localization). Their composition therefore gives the required $x$. The construction of $a$ is identical. No bound uniform in $\delta$ on the resulting operators is needed.

<a id="eq:first-product-estimate"></a>
<a id="eq:second-product-estimate"></a>
Both vectors have compact modular spectral support. By [mod:localization](#mod:localization), choose $x^\flat,a^\flat\in M$ with $$x\xi=\xi x^\flat,\qquad a\xi=\xi a^\flat.$$ Set $$c=e^{ir\ell},\qquad d=e^{iqp},\qquad
 H_0=\norm{a\xi}\norm{x\xi}>0,$$ and consider the bounded continuous vector functions $$F(t)=\sigma_t(x)a\xi,\qquad G(t)=a\sigma_t(x)\xi.$$ Since $$F(t)-cG(t)=(R(a^\flat)-cL(a))U_t(x\xi),$$ [spec:interval](#spec:interval) and the $Q$-spectral support of $a\xi$ give $$\begin{aligned}
 \norm{F-cG}_{\mathrm{av}}
 &\leq\norm{x\xi}\sup_{s\in I_r}\norm{(b_s(a)-ca)\xi}\notag\\
 &\leq H_0\sup_{\substack{s\in I_r\\u\in I_\ell}}
                \abs{e^{isu}-c}.
 \label{eq:first-product-estimate}
\end{aligned}$$ Interchanging $a$ and $x$ in the same argument yields $$\norm{t\mapsto\sigma_t(a)x\xi-dx\sigma_t(a)\xi}_{\mathrm{av}}
 \leq H_0\sup_{\substack{s\in I_q\\u\in I_p}}\abs{e^{isu}-d}.$$ The pointwise identity <a id="eq:time-reversal"></a>$$U_t\bigl(\sigma_{-t}(a)x\xi-dx\sigma_{-t}(a)\xi\bigr)
       =G(t)-dF(t)
 \label{eq:time-reversal}$$ and reflection invariance of the mean identify the seminorm on the left with $\norm{G-dF}_{\mathrm{av}}$. In particular, the phase is still $d$: the unitary $U_t$ is complex linear. We have proved $$\norm{G-dF}_{\mathrm{av}}
 \leq H_0\sup_{\substack{s\in I_q\\u\in I_p}}\abs{e^{isu}-d}.
 \label{eq:second-product-estimate}$$

The averaged norm of $F$ has the same factor $H_0$ as the two error bounds. By reflection invariance and [mod:average](#mod:average), <a id="eq:product-norm"></a>$$\begin{aligned}
 \norm{F}_{\mathrm{av}}^2
 &=m_t\langle L(\sigma_t(x^*x))a\xi,a\xi\rangle\notag\\
 &=\langle P(L(x^*x))a\xi,a\xi\rangle
   =\phi(x^*x)\phi(a^*a)=H_0^2.
 \label{eq:product-norm}
\end{aligned}$$ Using $\abs{c}=1$ and $$(1-cd)F=(F-cG)+c(G-dF),$$ combine <a href="#eq:first-product-estimate" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:first-product-estimate">[eq:first-product-estimate]</a> and <a href="#eq:second-product-estimate" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:second-product-estimate">[eq:second-product-estimate]</a>, and divide by $H_0$, to obtain $$\abs{1-cd}
 \leq
 \sup_{\substack{s\in I_r\\u\in I_\ell}}\abs{e^{isu}-e^{ir\ell}}
 +\sup_{\substack{s\in I_q\\u\in I_p}}\abs{e^{isu}-e^{iqp}}.$$ The right side tends to zero as $\delta\to0$. For example, the first supremum is at most $\delta(\abs{r}+\abs{\ell})+\delta^2$, by $\abs{e^{iv}-e^{iw}}\leq\abs{v-w}$ for real $v,w$. The second has the analogous bound. Since $c$ and $d$ depend only on the original support points, this proves <a href="#eq:spectral-pairing" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:spectral-pairing">[eq:spectral-pairing]</a>. ◻

</details>

<a id="pm-h-14"></a>
#### Conclusion of the proof

We first record the restriction that a scalar modular centralizer imposes on modular eigenvalues. This is the weak-mixing argument of Marrakchi and Vaes (Marrakchi and Vaes 2024, Lemma 2.1) <a id="pm-cite-37"></a>[[MarrakchiVaes2024](#ref-MarrakchiVaes2024)]; we include the needed conclusion and its proof in our spectral notation. The bounded operator extraction is useful because a spectral projection on $H$ need not itself preserve $M\xi$.

<a id="stmt-spec-eigenvalues"></a>
<div id="spec:eigenvalues" class="lemma">

**Lemma 12** (Modular eigenvalues). *If $M_\phi=\mathbb C1$, then $D$ has no nonzero eigenvalue. If, in addition, $D$ has pure point spectrum, then $M=\mathbb C1$.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-spec-eigenvalues"><summary>Proof of stmt-spec-eigenvalues</summary>

*Proof.* Suppose $E_D(\{r\})\neq0$. Choose $x\in M$ such that $\eta=E_D(\{r\})x\xi\neq0$, using density of $M\xi$. Let $$y_k=\int_{\mathbb R}e^{-irt}\sigma_t(x)\,d\mu_k(t),$$ where $\mu_k$ is uniform probability measure on $[-k,k]$. These bounded weak integrals satisfy $\norm{y_k}\leq\norm{x}$. The Hilbert-space mean ergodic theorem for $t\mapsto e^{-irt}U_t$ gives $$y_k\xi\longrightarrow\eta\qquad\text{in }H.$$ Take a weak operator convergent subnet of $(y_k)$, with limit $y\in M$. Its vector limit is $y\xi=\eta\neq0$. Therefore, for every $t\in\mathbb R$, $$\sigma_t(y)\xi=U_ty\xi=e^{irt}y\xi.$$ Since $\xi$ is separating, $\sigma_t(y)=e^{irt}y$.

It follows that $y^*y,yy^*\in M_\phi=\mathbb C1$. Their scalar values coincide because $\norm{y^*y}=\norm{yy^*}$, and are strictly positive. Thus $y$ is a nonzero scalar multiple of a unitary, and $\phi(y^*y)=\phi(yy^*)>0$. The modular identity <a href="#eq:modular-identities" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:modular-identities">[eq:modular-identities]</a> now gives $$\phi(yy^*)
   =\norm{\xi y}^2
   =\norm{e^{D/2}y\xi}^2
   =e^r\norm{y\xi}^2
   =e^r\phi(y^*y).$$ Hence $r=0$. If $D$ has pure point spectrum, all of $H$ is consequently its zero eigenspace, so $D=0$. Then the modular action is trivial and $M=M_\phi=\mathbb C1$. ◻

</details>

<details id="proof-stmt-spec-eigenvalues-2"><summary>Proof of stmt-spec-eigenvalues</summary>

*Proof of [thm:rigidity](#thm:rigidity).* By [spec:pairing](#spec:pairing), the symmetric bilinear form $$\mathfrak b((r,p),(q,\ell))=r\ell+qp$$ takes values in $2\pi\mathbb Z$ on every pair of points of $\mathcal S$. We claim that $\mathcal S$ is countable or is contained in a coordinate axis.

If $\mathcal S$ contains linearly independent vectors $v_1,v_2$, the map $$z\longmapsto\bigl(\mathfrak b(v_1,z),\mathfrak b(v_2,z)\bigr)$$ is an invertible real linear map on $\mathbb R^2$, since the form is nondegenerate and $v_1,v_2$ are independent. It sends $\mathcal S$ into the countable set $(2\pi\mathbb Z)^2$, proving countability. Otherwise $\mathcal S$ is contained in a line through the origin. If this line is not a coordinate axis, write its points as $t(\alpha,\beta)$ with $\alpha\beta\neq0$. Pairing each point with itself gives $$\alpha\beta t^2\in\pi\mathbb Z.$$ There are at most two real values of $t$ for each integer, again proving countability. The zero-dimensional case is contained in both axes. This proves the claim.

If $\mathcal S$ is countable, its spectral projections at singletons sum strongly to $1$, because $E(\mathcal S)=1$. Each such projection has range in an eigenspace of $D$, so $D$ has pure point spectrum. By [spec:eigenvalues](#spec:eigenvalues), $M=\mathbb C1$, and $b$ is trivial.

If instead $\mathcal S\subset\{0\}\times\mathbb R$, then $D=0$, again giving $M=M_\phi=\mathbb C1$ and triviality of $b$. In the remaining case $\mathcal S\subset\mathbb R\times\{0\}$, we have $Q=0$. Thus $V_s=1$ for every $s$, and $$b_s(a)\xi=V_s(a\xi)=a\xi\qquad(a\in M).$$ Separation by $\xi$ gives $b_s(a)=a$. In all cases $b$ is the trivial action, as asserted. ◻

</details>
