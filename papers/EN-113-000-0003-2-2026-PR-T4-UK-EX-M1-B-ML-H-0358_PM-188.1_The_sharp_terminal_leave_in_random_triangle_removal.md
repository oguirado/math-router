## 1. Abstract and result contract

> Starting from the complete graph on <i>n</i> vertices, repeatedly remove the three edges of a uniformly chosen remaining triangle. We prove that the number of edges left at termination, divided by <i>n</i><sup>3/2</sup>, converges in <i>L</i><sup>2</sup> to $`1/(2\sqrt2)`$. This proves the triangle case of the sharp-constant conjecture of Joos and Kühn. In particular, the same limit holds in probability and for the normalized expectation.

- **claim-main-sharp-constant** (theorem, [source](#stmt-thm-main)): For the uniform triangle-removal process starting from $K_n$, $$\mathbb E\left[\left(\frac{F_n}{n^{3/2}}- \frac1{2\sqrt2}\right)^2\right]\longrightarrow0.$$ In particular, $$\frac{F_n}{n^{3/2}}\xrightarrow{\mathbb P}\frac1{2\sqrt2}, \qquad \frac{\mathbb EF_n}{n^{3/2}}\longrightarrow\frac1{2\sqrt2}.$$
  - Explanation: The number of edges left when uniform triangle removal from the complete graph stops, divided by n^{3/2}, converges in L^2, in probability and in expectation to the constant 1/(2 sqrt 2).
- **claim-good-prefix** (theorem, [source](#stmt-it-templates)): There are constants $c>0$ and $C_0<\infty$ and an event $\mathcal G_n$ measurable from the process through step $i_0$ such that $$\mathbb P(\mathcal G_n^c)\le \exp\bigl(- (\log n)^{4/3}\bigr)=n^{-\omega(1)}$$ for all sufficiently large $n$. On this event, $i_0$ is reached and $G=G_{i_0}$ has $m$ edges and the following simultaneous properties.* 1. * Every vertex has degree $(1\pm n^{-c})np$, and every two distinct vertices have $(1\pm n^{-c})D$ common neighbors.* 2. * For every vertex $u$ and $3\le j\le r_0$, the number of embeddings of a fixed labeled cycle $C_j$ into the link $G[N_G(u)]$ is $(1\pm n^{-c})D^j$.* 3. *For every template $(H,I)$ with $v(H)\le H_0$ and every injection $\psi:I\to[n]$, the following two bounds hold. If $S(H',I)\ge1$ for every same-root subtemplate $H'$, then $$\label{eq:template-large} X_{H,I,\psi}(G)\le(1+\log n)^{C_0}S(H,I).$$ If $n^{v(H)-\|J\|}p^{e(H)-e(H[J])}\le1$ for every $I\subseteq J\subseteq V(H)$, then $$\label{eq:template-small} X_{H,I,\psi}(G)\le(1+\log n)^{C_0}.$$* *All constants and bounds are uniform over the graph and the prescribed root images.
  - Explanation: At the deterministic early time, with superpolynomially high probability, the graph is quasirandom: degrees, codegrees, link-cycle counts and rooted-template extension counts are controlled uniformly.
- **claim-priority-representation** (theorem, [source](#stmt-lem-priority)): For any finite simple graph $G$, the graph sequence indexed by the accepted triangles in this scan has the law of uniform sequential triangle removal started from $G$.
  - Explanation: Scanning triangles in order of independent uniform priorities and accepting those whose edges remain reproduces uniform sequential triangle removal.
- **claim-exact-paired-test** (theorem, [source](#stmt-lem-finite-test)): For any finite simple graph with distinct triangle priorities, the finite test terminates. A root call at $(e,t)$ returns true exactly when $e$ is untouched before $t$ in the priority scan. A call with focus $\{f,g\}\subset T$ and threshold $U_T$ returns true exactly when both $f$ and $g$ are untouched before $U_T$.
  - Explanation: The recursive test over candidate triangles terminates and returns true exactly when the focused edge, or both focused edges, are untouched before the threshold.
- **claim-unfolding-finite** (theorem, [source](#stmt-lem-unfolding-finite)): For a fixed good graph $G$, independent unfoldings can be constructed so that, almost surely, every possible call and all thresholds $t\in[0,1]$ have finite evaluations. Their outputs are jointly measurable in the threshold and the occurrence priorities.
  - Explanation: On the occurrence trees with independent priorities, every evaluation is almost surely finite for all thresholds simultaneously, thanks to factorial decay of decreasing paths, and outputs are measurable.
- **claim-product-identities** (theorem, [source](#stmt-prop-products)): For every triangle $T=\{e,f,g\}\in\mathcal T$ and $t\in[0,1]$, the independent unfolding satisfies $$\label{eq:pair} \mathbb P(\text{the pair $\{f,g\}\subset T$ returns true at $t$}) =q_{f,T}(t)q_{g,T}(t),$$ $$\label{eq:products} \begin{aligned} q_e(t)&=\prod_{S\ni e}\bigl(1-I_{e,S}(t)\bigr),\\ q_{e,T}(t)&=\prod_{\substack{S\ni e\\S\ne T}} \bigl(1-I_{e,S}(t)\bigr). \end{aligned}$$ In the pair probability, $T$ is omitted from the pair’s candidate list. All products are over $\mathcal T$, and an empty product equals $1$. The functions $q_e$, $q_{e,T}$, and $I_{e,T}$ belong to $C^1([0,1])$. Moreover, $q_e(0)=q_{e,T}(0)=1$ and $I_{e,T}(0)=0$.
  - Explanation: In the independent unfolding, pair and edge survival probabilities satisfy exact product equations driven by integrals of products of omitted-parent survival probabilities.
- **claim-semigroup-bound** (bound, [source](#stmt-prop-semigroup)): There are fixed constants $C_1,J<\infty$ such that every graph $G$ satisfying the degree, codegree and link-cycle conditions of Proposition <a href="#prop:prefix" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:prefix">[prop:prefix]</a> obeys $$\label{eq:semigroup} \sup_{0\le s\le\log n} \left\lVert e^{-sA/D}\right\rVert_\infty \le C_1(1+\log n)^J$$ for all sufficiently large $n$.
  - Explanation: The maximum-norm growth of the exponential of the normalized edge-adjacency matrix is only polylogarithmic, which is what makes the stability argument work.
- **claim-unfolding-asymptotics** (theorem, [source](#stmt-prop-unfolding)): For every graph $G$ satisfying Proposition <a href="#prop:prefix" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:prefix">[prop:prefix]</a>, the independent-unfolding probabilities obey $$\label{eq:unfolding-asymptotics} q_e(t)=(1+o(1))q(t),\qquad q_{e,T}(t)=(1+o(1))q(t),\qquad I_{e,T}(t)=O\!\left(\frac{\log n}{D}\right).$$ The estimates hold uniformly over $G$, $0\le t\le1$, and every incident pair $e\in T\in\mathcal T$; the first estimate also holds for every $e\in\mathcal E$.
  - Explanation: Unfolded survival probabilities agree with the scalar comparison law up to relative error o(1), and the integrals are small, uniformly over good graphs.
- **claim-coupling-exposed-types** (bound, [source](#stmt-lem-coupling)): Let $X^{\mathrm{fin}},X^{\mathrm{ind}}\in\{0,1\}^k$ be the vectors of root answers in the two experiments, where $1$ denotes true. For every $H\subseteq\{0,1\}^k$, $$\bigl\|\mathbb P(X^{\mathrm{fin}}\in H) -\mathbb P(X^{\mathrm{ind}}\in H)\bigr\| \le \mathbb P_{\mathrm{ind}}(\mathcal C_k).$$
  - Explanation: The finite and independent experiments can be coupled so that their root-answer laws differ by at most the probability that some triangle type is exposed twice.
- **claim-collision-witness** (bound, [source](#stmt-lem-witness)): If the initial root labels are all distinct, then every occurrence of $\mathcal C_k$ produces an extra-edge witness. Moreover, uniformly over the graphs under consideration, $$\label{eq:collision-witness} \mathbb P_{\mathrm{ind}}(\mathcal C_k) \le O(n^{-1})+ \mathbb P_{\mathrm{ind}}(\text{an extra-edge witness exists}).$$ The $O(n^{-1})$ term may be omitted when $k=1$.
  - Explanation: With distinct initial root labels, any repeated triangle type forces an extra graph edge between labels of two formal vertices, so collision probability is controlled by root overlap and witness existence.
- **claim-embedding-extra-edge** (bound, [source](#stmt-lem-embeddings)): For every such pattern with $r\in\{1,2\}$ roots and $\ell$ non-root calls, the number of embeddings of $J^+$ into $G$ is at most $$\label{eq:embedding-bound} (2m)^r(2D)^\ell D^{-B}$$ for all sufficiently large $n$, uniformly over the pattern and $G$.
  - Explanation: The extra edge gains a polynomial factor in the codegree scale over the naive count of embeddings of the pattern graph into the good prefix graph.
- **claim-visitation-bound** (bound, [source](#stmt-lem-visitation)): Conditional on the prescribed ordered root labels, the probability that all calls in the embedded pattern are visited is at most $$\label{eq:visitation-bound} C_D^3\frac{(2\mu)^\ell}{h!\,\ell_1!\,\ell_2!}.$$ This includes zero-length segments, with the convention $0!=1$.
  - Explanation: Reaching all calls of an embedded pattern needs decreasing priorities along the paths and failures of off-pattern candidates, giving factorial decay in the segment lengths.
- **claim-uniform-collision-bound** (bound, [source](#stmt-prop-collision)): Fix $k\in\{1,2\}$ and sample $k$ independent uniform oriented edges of $G$, with replacement. Execute the independent root queries at threshold $1$, executing every root regardless of earlier outputs. Let $\mathcal C_k$ be the event that an actual triangle type is repeated among all exposed candidate lists, including candidates not traversed. Then $$\label{eq:collision-bound} \mathbb P_{\mathrm{ind}}(\mathcal C_k) =O(D^{15-B}+n^{-1}) =O(D^{-35}+n^{-1}) =o(D^{-1}).$$ The bound is uniform over the graphs in Proposition <a href="#prop:prefix" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:prefix">[prop:prefix]</a>. Consequently, by Lemma <a href="#lem:coupling" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:coupling">10</a>, the probabilities that all root queries return true in the finite and independent experiments differ by at most the same bound.
  - Explanation: Summing pattern counts, embedding bounds and visitation bounds over all lengths shows collisions have probability smaller than the reciprocal of the codegree scale, so finite and independent root-query probabilities agree to that precision.

## 2. Definitions, validity and bounds

- **Rooted template** ([source](#stmt-def-template)): A template $(H,I)$ consists of a graph $H$ and an independent set $I\subseteq V(H)$ of distinguished vertices.
- **Template scaling** ([source](#stmt-def-template)): Its scaling at density $p$ is $$S(H,I)=n^{v(H)-\|I\|}p^{e(H)}.$$
- **Balanced template** ([source](#stmt-def-template)): The template is *balanced* if every same-root subtemplate has rooted density at most that of $(H,I)$.
- **Extra-edge witness** ([source](#pm-h-23)): We call $(a,b)$ an *extra-edge witness* if $a,b$ are distinct and nonadjacent in $\Gamma$, their labels are adjacent in $G$, and $\lambda$ is injective on $V(\Gamma_{a,b})$.
- **Pattern** ([source](#pm-h-25)): A *pattern* records these rooted paths, their marks, and the choice of focus edge at each child step.

## 3. Proof roadmap

- **step-good-prefix** [claim](#stmt-it-templates) / [proof](#proof-stmt-it-templates): Specialise external stopping estimates for hypergraph removal to the triangle case and verify that the complete graph meets their pseudorandomness hypotheses. -> Good prefix event with degree, codegree, link-cycle and template bounds.
- **step-priority** [claim](#stmt-lem-priority) / [proof](#proof-stmt-lem-priority): Uniform random permutation of triangles; the next accepted triangle is uniform among present ones. -> Priority scan has the law of uniform removal.
- **step-finite-test** [claim](#stmt-lem-finite-test) / [proof](#proof-stmt-lem-finite-test): Strictly decreasing thresholds give termination; induction on the number of priorities below the threshold gives correctness. -> Recursive test decides edge survival.
- **step-unfolding-finite** [claim](#stmt-lem-unfolding-finite) / [proof](#proof-stmt-lem-unfolding-finite): Factorial decay of decreasing priority paths and a countable intersection. -> Almost surely finite, measurable evaluations.
- **step-products** [claim](#stmt-prop-products) / [proof](#proof-stmt-prop-products): Disjoint candidate sets give independence; condition on a candidate's priority to obtain integral and product identities. -> Exact product equations for survival probabilities.
- **step-semigroup** [claim](#stmt-prop-semigroup) / [proof](#proof-stmt-prop-semigroup): Trace method on vertex links, approximation by star averaging projections, maximum-norm control of the averaging semigroup and a finite perturbation expansion. -> Polylogarithmic maximum-norm semigroup bound.
- **step-unfolding-asymptotics** [claim](#stmt-prop-unfolding) / [proof](#proof-stmt-prop-unfolding): Bootstrap on the logarithmic deviation from the scalar law using the linearised equation and variation of constants. -> Unfolded probabilities uniformly asymptotic to the scalar law.
- **step-coupling** [claim](#stmt-lem-coupling) / [proof](#proof-stmt-lem-coupling): Reuse the finite priority at the first exposure of a type and a fresh variable at later ones. -> Answer laws differ by at most the collision probability.
- **step-witness** [claim](#stmt-lem-witness) / [proof](#proof-stmt-lem-witness): Analyse the first repeated label in a construction order of the formal graph of visited calls, plus a union bound on root overlap. -> Collisions reduce to an extra-edge witness or root overlap.
- **step-embeddings** [claim](#stmt-lem-embeddings) / [proof](#proof-stmt-lem-embeddings): Short patterns use the large-scaling template bound; long patterns count preceding labels directly and use the small-scaling template bound on the last births. -> Embedding count with the extra suppression factor.
- **step-visitation** [claim](#stmt-lem-visitation) / [proof](#proof-stmt-lem-visitation): Condition on selected priorities, bound off-pattern failure probabilities via the product identities, and integrate over ordered simplices. -> Visitation probability with factorial decay.
- **step-collision** [claim](#stmt-prop-collision) / [proof](#proof-stmt-prop-collision): Multiply pattern count, embedding bound, root-matching probability and visitation bound and sum over lengths. -> Collision probability smaller than the reciprocal codegree scale.
- **step-main** [claim](#stmt-thm-main) / [proof](#sec:moments): Convert root-query probabilities into first and second moments, bound the bad-prefix contribution trivially, and conclude by Markov and Cauchy-Schwarz. -> L^2, probability and expectation convergence to the sharp constant.

## 4. Engineering Triptych

### Panel 1 - epistemic contract

- **inv-finite-test-terminates**: On a finite simple graph with distinct triangle priorities, the recursive paired test always terminates.
- **inv-root-call-equals-scan**: A root call at an edge and a threshold returns true exactly when that edge is untouched before the threshold in the priority scan.

### Panel 2 - negative knowledge

- **anti-other-host-graphs**: Apply the terminal leave constant or the finite-test machinery to arbitrary starting graphs or general hypergraph removal. -> No sharp constant is asserted outside removal from the complete graph, and no fluctuation law is given.
- **anti-tied-priorities**: Run the recursive test with tied or discretised priorities. -> Correctness of the test is stated only for distinct priorities; ties can change which triangles count as earlier.

### Panel 3 - reference implementation and oracle

Applicability: `algorithmic`. The exact paired test is a finite recursive procedure; on small complete graphs it is compared with a direct priority scan.

```python
# INPUTS: stdin: 'n t' then the integer priorities of the triangles of K_n in lexicographic vertex-triple order
# PRECONDITIONS: 3<=n<=6, priorities distinct integers, t an integer threshold (a triangle is earlier iff priority<t)
# OUTPUTS: one string of 0/1, one char per edge of K_n in lexicographic order; 1 iff the root test returns true
# TIME: exponential in the recursion depth, fine for n<=6
# SPACE: recursion depth at most the number of triangles
# ARITHMETIC: exact integer comparisons only
# FAILURE: malformed input raises an exception and exits nonzero
import sys, itertools
data = sys.stdin.read().split()
n = int(data[0]); t = int(data[1]); pr = list(map(int, data[2:]))
tris = list(itertools.combinations(range(n), 3))
U = dict(zip(tris, pr))
def edges(T):
    a, b, c = T
    return [(a, b), (a, c), (b, c)]
def test(focus, thr, parent):
    cands = [S for S in tris if S != parent and any(e in focus for e in edges(S)) and U[S]<thr]
    cands.sort(key=U.get)
    for S in cands:
        child = [e for e in edges(S) if e not in focus]
        if test(child, U[S], S):
            return False
    return True
all_edges = list(itertools.combinations(range(n), 2))
print(''.join('1' if test([e], t, None) else '0' for e in all_edges))
```

## 6. Applicability limits and search

- **lim-complete-graph-start** (hypothesis): The sharp constant is proved only for uniform triangle removal started from the complete graph. The paper itself disclaims arbitrary starting graphs, general hypergraph removal and fluctuation laws. Blocked use: ['Applying the constant 1/(2 sqrt 2) to leave sizes after triangle removal from sparse or arbitrary host graphs', 'Applying the constant to hypergraph removal processes', 'Inferring a central limit theorem or other fluctuation law for the leave size']
- **lim-asymptotic-only** (approximation): The main theorem is a limit statement as n tends to infinity: the normalized leave converges in L^2, in probability and in expectation. It gives no explicit finite-n error bound. Blocked use: ['Quoting the constant as a guaranteed leave size for a specific small n', 'Treating a single simulation run at small n as confirming or refuting the constant']
- **lim-good-prefix-event** (hypothesis): All continuation estimates are conditional on a good prefix event, which has failure probability n to the minus omega(1) and holds only for all sufficiently large n. The constants and bounds are uniform over such prefix graphs. Blocked use: ['Using the degree, codegree, link-cycle and template bounds on graphs that are not good prefix graphs', 'Using the continuation estimates at small n or before the deterministic stopping time']
- **lim-external-prefix-input** (formal_scope): The early-prefix control is imported from the Joos and Kuhn removal estimates, specialised to the triangle case with fixed small parameters. The paper does not reprove it independently. Blocked use: ['Claiming a fully self-contained proof of the prefix properties', 'Reusing the specialisation with parameters outside the fixed hierarchy chosen in the proof']
- **lim-distinct-priorities** (edge_case): The exact paired test is proved correct only on the probability-one event that triangle priorities are distinct. Ties are not covered. Blocked use: ['Running the recursive test with tied or discretised priorities without a strict tie-breaking rule']
- **lim-unfolding-model-only** (formal_scope): The product identities describe the auxiliary independent unfolding only. They assert no factorisation of survival probabilities in the finite priority system, and they are linked to the finite process only through the coupling and collision bound. Blocked use: ['Assuming survival events of different edges are independent in the finite process', 'Using the unfolding probabilities as exact finite-process probabilities']
- **lim-nonconstructive-finiteness** (nonconstructive): Almost sure finiteness of the unfolding evaluations is shown by a countable intersection of probability-one events. No procedure bounding the evaluation depth is produced, so it is not a ready-made algorithm. Blocked use: ['Presenting the unfolded occurrence tree as an implementable evaluation procedure with a worst-case bound']
- **lim-collision-precision** (edge_case): The collision probability must be smaller than the reciprocal codegree scale, because the two-root survival probability is itself of that order. A bound that merely tends to zero would not control the second moment. Blocked use: ['Replacing the collision bound by a weaker vanishing bound in the second-moment step', 'Dropping repeated root samples, which would change the second moment']
- **lim-semigroup-hypotheses** (hypothesis): The polylogarithmic semigroup bound uses the degree, codegree and link-cycle conditions of the good prefix and holds for sufficiently large n. The general exponential bound would lose a factor of order D, so the link structure is essential. Blocked use: ['Applying the maximum-norm semigroup bound to graphs lacking the link-cycle conditions', 'Replacing it by the generic exponential bound in the bootstrap']
- **lim-sampling-with-replacement** (edge_case): Root edges are sampled with replacement, and the moment identities include repeated sampled edges. The collision analysis charges overlaps of initial roots to an error term. Blocked use: ['Computing the second moment from distinct sampled edges only']

abbrev Graph (n : ℕ) := Finset (Finset (Fin n))

def completeGraph (n : ℕ) : Graph n := Finset.univ.powersetCard 2

def triangles {n : ℕ} (G : Graph n) : Finset (Finset (Fin n)) :=
  (Finset.univ.powersetCard 3).filter (fun t => t.powersetCard 2 ⊆ G)

noncomputable def step {n : ℕ} (G : Graph n) : PMF (Graph n) :=
  if h : (triangles G).Nonempty then
    (PMF.uniformOfFinset (triangles G) h).map (fun t => G \ t.powersetCard 2)
  else PMF.pure G

noncomputable def evolve {n : ℕ} (G : Graph n) : ℕ → PMF (Graph n)
  | 0 => PMF.pure G
  | k + 1 => (evolve G k).bind step

noncomputable def terminalLaw (n : ℕ) : PMF (Graph n) :=
  evolve (completeGraph n) (n.choose 2)

noncomputable def expectation (n : ℕ) (f : Graph n → ℝ) : ℝ :=
  ∑ G, (terminalLaw n G).toReal * f G

## 7. Complete mathematical paper


Starting from the complete graph on <i>n</i> vertices, repeatedly remove the three edges of a uniformly chosen remaining triangle. We prove that the number of edges left at termination, divided by <i>n</i><sup>3/2</sup>, converges in <i>L</i><sup>2</sup> to $`1/(2\sqrt2)`$. This proves the triangle case of the sharp-constant conjecture of Joos and Kühn. In particular, the same limit holds in probability and for the normalized expectation.

<a id="sec:introduction"></a>
### Introduction

Begin with the complete graph $K_n$. At each step, choose uniformly from the triangles whose three edges are still present, and delete those edges. Stop when no triangle remains. The removed triangles form an edge-disjoint packing; the edges left over form its *leave*. Let $F_n$ be the number of edges in this terminal graph. The problem is to determine the typical size of this leave, including its leading constant.

The accepted triangles form a partial Steiner triple system, and $F_n$ measures its uncovered pairs. Bollobás and Erdős conjectured in 1990 that the expected leave has order $n^{3/2}$, as reported in (Bohman, Frieze, and Lubetzky 2015, Introduction) <a id="pm-cite-1"></a>[[BohmanFriezeLubetzky2015](#ref-BohmanFriezeLubetzky2015)]. Spencer’s branching-process analysis and the independent nibble argument of Rödl and Thoma showed that the leave is $o(n^2)$ with high probability (Spencer 1995; Rödl and Thoma 1996) <a id="pm-cite-2"></a>[[Spencer1995](#ref-Spencer1995), [RodlThoma1996](#ref-RodlThoma1996)]. Grable proved bounds $O(n^{11/6+\xi})$ for each fixed $\xi>0$ and outlined the higher-order calculations leading to $O(n^{7/4+\xi})$ (Grable 1997, Theorems 5 and 7) <a id="pm-cite-3"></a>[[Grable1997](#ref-Grable1997)]. Bohman, Frieze and Lubetzky gave a short proof of the bound $O(n^{7/4}\log^{5/4}n)$ (Bohman, Frieze, and Lubetzky 2010, Theorem 1) <a id="pm-cite-4"></a>[[BohmanFriezeLubetzky2010](#ref-BohmanFriezeLubetzky2010)] and subsequently proved $F_n=n^{3/2+o(1)}$ with high probability (Bohman, Frieze, and Lubetzky 2015, Theorem 1) <a id="pm-cite-5"></a>[[BohmanFriezeLubetzky2015](#ref-BohmanFriezeLubetzky2015)]. Their analysis uses self-correcting dynamic concentration and carefully chosen triangular extensions to determine the exponent.

Joos and Kühn developed removal estimates for general strictly balanced hypergraphs, including pseudorandom starting hypergraphs (Joos and Kühn 2025, Theorems 1.2–1.3) <a id="pm-cite-6"></a>[[joosKuehn2025](#ref-joosKuehn2025)]. Their Conjecture 16.2 predicts an asymptotic constant for the terminal leave; its triangle specialization is $1/(2\sqrt2)$ in probability. The following theorem proves this triangle case with $L^2$ convergence. Numbered references to Joos and Kühn refer throughout to version 2.

<a id="stmt-thm-main"></a>
<div id="thm:main" class="theorem">

**Theorem 1**. *For the uniform triangle-removal process starting from $K_n$, $$\mathbb E\left[\left(\frac{F_n}{n^{3/2}}-
                   \frac1{2\sqrt2}\right)^2\right]\longrightarrow0.$$ In particular, $$\frac{F_n}{n^{3/2}}\xrightarrow{\mathbb P}\frac1{2\sqrt2},
 \qquad
 \frac{\mathbb EF_n}{n^{3/2}}\longrightarrow\frac1{2\sqrt2}.$$*

</div>

The only external proof input is the early-prefix control from Joos and Kühn, specialized in Section <a href="#sec:prefix" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:prefix">2</a>. All subsequent continuation estimates are proved here. The theorem concerns removal from $K_n$; it does not assert a sharp constant for arbitrary starting graphs or for general hypergraph removal, or a fluctuation law.

<a id="pm-h-3"></a>
#### The continuation argument

The proof separates the part of the process governed by uniform subgraph counts from the final evolution of individual edges. Stop at a deterministic time when the edge density is approximately $p=n^{-1/2+\epsilon}$, for a small fixed $\epsilon>0$. The graph then has $$m=\frac{n^2p}{2}\quad\text{edges},\qquad D=np^2$$ triangles through each edge to relative error $o(1)$. Section <a href="#sec:prefix" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:prefix">2</a> states the exact properties needed and verifies their application from the public removal estimates.

The continuation analysis uses backward priority exploration, whose hypergraph form appears in Spencer’s work (Spencer 1995, secs. 2–4) <a id="pm-cite-7"></a>[[Spencer1995](#ref-Spencer1995)]. Grable formulated triangle packing as a uniform priority scan and used adaptive exposure decision trees in his concentration analysis (Grable 1997, sec. 1 and 4) <a id="pm-cite-8"></a>[[Grable1997](#ref-Grable1997)]. The quantitative issue here is to follow a growing rescaled time interval and to approximate joint survival on the scale of its vanishing probability. A fixed-time local approximation alone does not give the precision required for a second moment.

Give the triangles of this graph fresh independent uniform priorities and scan them in increasing order, accepting a triangle when all its edges remain. This represents the continuation of uniform removal. An edge survives precisely when a recursive test returns true. A child test asks whether the other *two* edges of a candidate triangle survive before its priority. We test the two edges with one jointly ordered list of candidates, which is essential to the later visitation estimate. Section <a href="#sec:queries" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:queries">3</a> proves this rule and constructs an auxiliary tree in which each occurrence of a candidate receives an independent priority.

In this independent model, the survival probabilities satisfy exact product equations. The scalar comparison is $$q(t)=(1+2Dt)^{-1/2},\qquad 0\le t\le1.$$ This is Spencer’s scalar branching law after the time change $c=Dt$ (Spencer 1995, sec. 3, equation (9)) <a id="pm-cite-9"></a>[[Spencer1995](#ref-Spencer1995)]. Bal and Bennett obtained related matching estimates for fixed-degree random regular hypergraphs and regular hypergraphs of growing girth (Bal and Bennett 2023, Corollaries 3–4) <a id="pm-cite-10"></a>[[BalBennett2023](#ref-BalBennett2023)]; here $D$ grows with $n$, and the terminal rescaled time is $D$. To prove a uniform approximation by $q$, one needs stability of a matrix acting on the graph’s edges. We obtain it from cycle counts in vertex links. Each normalized link matrix is close in Euclidean norm to its averaging projection; the sum of these projections has a directly controlled semigroup in the maximum norm. A finite perturbation expansion then gives the stability needed by the product equations (Section <a href="#sec:stability" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:stability">4</a>).

The final issue is the difference between independent occurrences and repeated triangle types in the finite graph. We compare the complete candidate lists exposed by one or two root queries. A repetition forces an extra graph edge in the union of at most two ancestral query paths (Section <a href="#sec:coupling" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:coupling">5</a>). Small rooted-template bounds control the number of such witnesses. Joint time ordering controls the probability that their calls are visited. Their combination gives an absolute error $o(1/D)$ (Section <a href="#sec:witnesses" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:witnesses">6</a>). This precision matters because the two-root survival probability is itself of order $1/D$.

The one- and two-root probabilities determine the first and second moments of $F_n$. Their normalization is $$mq(1)=\frac{n^2p}{2\sqrt{1+2np^2}}
       \sim\frac{n^{3/2}}{2\sqrt2}.$$ Uniformity over the good prefix graphs, together with a sufficiently small prefix failure probability, gives the unconditional conclusion in Section <a href="#sec:moments" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:moments">7</a>.

The continuation estimates developed here are the maximum-norm stability of the unfolding and the smaller than $D^{-1}$ error for adaptive paired queries. Both the numerical constant and the moment conclusion use these estimates.

<a id="pm-h-4"></a>
#### Conventions

All graphs are finite and simple. An embedding is injective and preserves edges; it need not preserve nonedges. Thus an embedding of a fixed labeled cycle counts its ordered vertex labels, without division by automorphisms. The notation $(1\pm a)b$ means a number in $[(1-a)b,(1+a)b]$. All unspecified constants below are independent of $n$ and of the realized good prefix graph. They may depend on the fixed parameters introduced next. A statement holds with superpolynomially high probability if its failure probability is $n^{-\omega(1)}$, meaning smaller than $n^{-C}$ for every fixed $C>0$ when $n$ is sufficiently large.

<a id="sec:prefix"></a>
### A uniform early prefix

This section supplies all the graph properties used in the continuation. In addition to degrees and link cycles, we need two upper bounds for small rooted graphs. The second is useful when all the remaining choices together have expected count below one.

<a id="eq:constants"></a>
<a id="eq:prefix-time"></a>
Fix $$\label{eq:constants}
 \epsilon=\frac1{2000},\qquad r_0=8000,\qquad
 L=100,\qquad B=50,\qquad
 H_0=\max\{r_0+1,3L+2\}=8001.$$ Here $r_0$ and $H_0$ bound the link-cycle lengths and template orders controlled by the prefix; $L$ and $B$ are fixed for the collision-witness count in Section <a href="#sec:witnesses" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:witnesses">6</a>. Write $G_i$ for the graph after $i$ removals, provided that step is reached, and put $$\begin{aligned}
 p_i&=1-\frac1n-\frac{6i}{n^2},\notag\\
 i_0&=\left\lfloor\frac{n^2}{6}
             \left(1-\frac1n-n^{-1/2+\epsilon}\right)\right\rfloor,
 \qquad p=p_{i_0},\qquad D=np^2,\qquad m=\frac{n^2p}{2}.
 \label{eq:prefix-time}
\end{aligned}$$ These quantities are deterministic. In particular, <a id="eq:scales"></a>$$\label{eq:scales}
 p=n^{-1/2+\epsilon}+O(n^{-2}),\qquad
 D\sim n^{2\epsilon},\qquad e(G_{i_0})=m$$ whenever $i_0$ is reached.

<a id="stmt-def-template"></a>
<div id="def:template" class="definition">

**Definition 2** (Rooted templates). A template $(H,I)$ consists of a graph $H$ and an independent set $I\subseteq V(H)$ of distinguished vertices. For an injection $\psi:I\to[n]$, let $X_{H,I,\psi}(G)$ count its edge-preserving injective extensions to $V(H)$ in $G$. Its scaling at density $p$ is $$S(H,I)=n^{v(H)-|I|}p^{e(H)}.$$ A subtemplate with the same distinguished set is any subgraph $H'\subseteq H$ whose vertex set contains $I$, distinguished by $I$. When $v(H)>|I|$, the rooted density is $e(H)/(v(H)-|I|)$; it is zero when there are no free vertices. The template is *balanced* if every same-root subtemplate has rooted density at most that of $(H,I)$.

</div>

The independence of $I$ is a property of the template. Its prescribed images may have additional edges in $G$.

<a id="stmt-it-templates"></a>
<div id="it:templates" class="proposition">

**Proposition 3** (Good prefix). *<span id="prop:prefix" label="prop:prefix"></span> There are constants $c>0$ and $C_0<\infty$ and an event $\mathcal G_n$ measurable from the process through step $i_0$ such that $$\mathbb P(\mathcal G_n^c)\le
       \exp\bigl(- (\log n)^{4/3}\bigr)=n^{-\omega(1)}$$ for all sufficiently large $n$. On this event, $i_0$ is reached and $G=G_{i_0}$ has $m$ edges and the following simultaneous properties.*

1.  *<span id="it:degrees" label="it:degrees"></span> Every vertex has degree $(1\pm n^{-c})np$, and every two distinct vertices have $(1\pm n^{-c})D$ common neighbors.*

2.  *<span id="it:cycles" label="it:cycles"></span> For every vertex $u$ and $3\le j\le r_0$, the number of embeddings of a fixed labeled cycle $C_j$ into the link $G[N_G(u)]$ is $(1\pm n^{-c})D^j$.*

<a id="eq:template-large"></a>
<a id="eq:template-small"></a>
3.  *For every template $(H,I)$ with $v(H)\le H_0$ and every injection $\psi:I\to[n]$, the following two bounds hold. If $S(H',I)\ge1$ for every same-root subtemplate $H'$, then $$\label{eq:template-large}
     X_{H,I,\psi}(G)\le(1+\log n)^{C_0}S(H,I).$$ If $n^{v(H)-|J|}p^{e(H)-e(H[J])}\le1$ for every $I\subseteq J\subseteq V(H)$, then $$\label{eq:template-small}
     X_{H,I,\psi}(G)\le(1+\log n)^{C_0}.$$*

*All constants and bounds are uniform over the graph and the prescribed root images.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-it-templates"><summary>Proof of stmt-it-templates</summary>

*Proof.* We give the specialization of Joos–Kühn’s stopping estimates, including the parameters and rooted conventions. Their Sections 5–7 (pp. 9–11) set up the trajectories and stopping times; Lemma 10.1 (p. 50) controls those times, and Lemma 7.15 (pp. 16–17) supplies the two general extension bounds (Joos and Kühn 2025) <a id="pm-cite-11"></a>[[joosKuehn2025](#ref-joosKuehn2025)].

<a id="pm-h-6"></a>
###### Public parameters and initial hypotheses.

Denote their small parameter by $\eta$, keeping it distinct from $\epsilon$ in <a href="#eq:constants" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:constants">[eq:constants]</a>. Choose $\eta$ small enough for their hierarchy and so that $$\eta<\epsilon/2,\qquad \eta^{-4}\ge H_0.$$ Choose their $\delta>0$ sufficiently small in terms of $\eta$, with $\delta<1/2$. These choices remain fixed as $n$ grows. The removed graph is a triangle, with uniformity parameter $k=2$ and 2-density $(3-1)/(3-2)=2$; it is strictly 2-balanced. The initial density in their normalization is $\theta=2e(K_n)/n^2=1-1/n$. Their density trajectory is therefore exactly $p_i$ in <a href="#eq:prefix-time" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:prefix-time">[eq:prefix-time]</a>. Their horizon has density $n^{-1/2+\eta}$, strictly below $p_{i_0}$ for large $n$.

Their Section 5 requires an $(\eta^4,\delta,2)$-pseudorandom initial graph. For completeness, $K_n$ satisfies all its defining conditions (P1)–(P4) on p. 3. The public convention permits edges among distinguished vertices and ignores them in both counts and scalings. For a template with $r$ distinguished vertices, $a$ other vertices, and $b$ edges not internal to the distinguished set, every fixed root injection has exactly $(n-r)_a$ extensions in $K_n$; here $(x)_a=x(x-1)\cdots(x-a+1)$ and $(x)_0=1$. For all templates with at most $\eta^{-4}$ vertices, $$\frac{(n-r)_a}{n^a\theta^b}=1+O_\eta(n^{-1}).$$ Their initial error parameter is $\zeta_{\mathrm{init}}=n^\delta/(\sqrt n\,\theta)$. The displayed relative error is smaller than both $\zeta_{\mathrm{init}}$ and $\zeta_{\mathrm{init}}^\delta$, which verifies the relative-error requirements (P1) and (P2). If $a\ge1$, the scaling is asymptotic to $n^a$, while the upper scaling threshold in (P3) is at most $n^{1/2}$; thus neither small-scaling condition (P3) or (P4) applies. If $a=0$, count and scaling are both exactly one, and the remaining conditions hold directly.

<a id="pm-h-7"></a>
###### Balanced counts and the stopping threshold.

The dynamic error parameter is $$\zeta_i=\frac{n^{\eta^2}}{\sqrt n\,p_i}
         =n^{\eta^2}x_i^{-1/2},\qquad x_i=np_i^2.$$ The balanced-template controls in Section 7 give relative error $\zeta_i^\delta$ until the relevant scaling reaches $\zeta_i^{-\sqrt\delta}$, simultaneously over the templates and root injections in their families. For a balanced rooted template with $a\ge1$ free vertices and density at most two, its scaling is at least $x_i^a$. Throughout $i\le i_0$, we have $x_i\ge n^{2\epsilon}$ and $$\frac{x_i^a}{\zeta_i^{-\sqrt\delta}}
  =n^{\eta^2\sqrt\delta}x_i^{a-\sqrt\delta/2}
  \longrightarrow\infty.$$ Thus each template used for parts <a href="#it:degrees" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="it:degrees">[it:degrees]</a> and <a href="#it:cycles" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="it:cycles">[it:cycles]</a> belongs to the tracked family initially and never reaches its threshold before $i_0$.

A rooted edge counts degrees. A path of length two with its endpoints distinguished counts common neighbors, even when the prescribed endpoint images are adjacent. For a link cycle of length $j$, use the wheel whose center is distinguished and whose rim is the labeled $C_j$. This template has $j$ free vertices and $2j$ edges. Any subset of $s$ free vertices has at most $s$ spokes and $s$ rim edges, so the wheel is balanced with density two. Its extensions are exactly the labeled link-cycle embeddings and have scaling $D^j$. The largest wheel has $r_0+1\le\eta^{-4}$ vertices. The degree and codegree templates are likewise balanced, with scalings $np$ and $D$ respectively.

At $i_0$, $$\zeta_{i_0}\le n^{-(\epsilon-\eta^2)}.$$ Choose any fixed $c$ with $0<c<\delta(\epsilon-\eta^2)$. The public relative errors then imply parts <a href="#it:degrees" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="it:degrees">[it:degrees]</a> and <a href="#it:cycles" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="it:cycles">[it:cycles]</a>, with slack for fixed constants.

<a id="pm-h-8"></a>
###### General rooted extensions.

On the same stopping controls, Lemma 7.15 applies to every template up to the public size cap, with no balance assumption on the target template. Its first case states that, if all same-root subtemplate scalings are at least one, the extension count is at most its scaling times $(1+\log n)^{\alpha_{H,I}}$. Its second case gives the bound $(1+\log n)^{\alpha_{H,I}}$ when every completion scaling after enlarging the distinguished set is at most one. Here $$\alpha_{H,I}=2^{v(H)-|I|+1}-2.$$ In our independent-root convention these hypotheses are exactly those in <a href="#eq:template-large" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:template-large">[eq:template-large]</a> and <a href="#eq:template-small" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:template-small">[eq:template-small]</a>. For the latter, the public convention ignores the edges internal to the enlarged root set $J$, giving precisely the exponent $e(H)-e(H[J])$. We may take $C_0=2^{H_0+1}-2$. Its size is immaterial: it is fixed independently of $n$.

<a id="pm-h-9"></a>
###### Probability and conditioning.

Lemma 10.1 bounds the probability of failure of the required stopping controls before the public horizon by $\exp(- (\log n)^{4/3})$. In particular the triangle count is controlled through $i_0$ around the positive trajectory $n^3p_i^3/6$, with relative error tending to zero. Termination at or before $i_0$ would contradict that control, so $i_0$ is reached on this event. Define $\mathcal G_n$ to be the event of reaching $i_0$ and satisfying the properties in the proposition at that time. It is determined by the prefix alone. The later public success event is a subset of $\mathcal G_n$ and proves its probability estimate. We do not condition the continuation on any future stopping control. ◻

</details>

From now through Section <a href="#sec:witnesses" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:witnesses">6</a>, fix any prefix in $\mathcal G_n$ and write $G=G_{i_0}$. All estimates will be uniform over this choice. Let $\mathcal E=E(G)$, and let $\mathcal T$ be the set of triangles of $G$, each regarded as a three-element subset of $\mathcal E$. Thus $|\mathcal E|=m$, and distinct members of $\mathcal T$ have at most one common edge. For $e\in\mathcal E$, let $$d_e=|\{T\in\mathcal T:e\in T\}|=(1\pm n^{-c})D.$$ This last equality is the common-neighbor estimate for the endpoints of $e$. The task is now to analyze the removal process started from this fixed graph.

<a id="sec:queries"></a>
### Exact queries and independent unfoldings

Fix a graph $G$ supplied by Proposition <a href="#prop:prefix" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:prefix">[prop:prefix]</a>, and retain $\mathcal E=E(G)$ and the family $\mathcal T$ of triangle edge sets. We first express edge survival by a finite recursive test. We then give each occurrence in that recursion fresh randomness. The resulting auxiliary model has exact product identities, which we will estimate before comparing it with the finite process.

<a id="pm-h-11"></a>
#### Priorities and the finite test

Independent priorities give the standard random greedy packing representation; see Spencer (Spencer 1995, sec. 1) <a id="pm-cite-12"></a>[[Spencer1995](#ref-Spencer1995)], Grable (Grable 1997, sec. 1) <a id="pm-cite-13"></a>[[Grable1997](#ref-Grable1997)], and the triangle-removal accounts (Bohman, Frieze, and Lubetzky 2015, sec. 1.2) <a id="pm-cite-14"></a>[[BohmanFriezeLubetzky2015](#ref-BohmanFriezeLubetzky2015)] <a id="pm-cite-15"></a>[[joosKuehn2025](#ref-joosKuehn2025)] (Joos and Kühn 2025, sec. 14.1).

Assign independent uniform priorities $U_T\in[0,1]$ to the triangles $T\in\mathcal T$. Scan the triangles in increasing priority order, accepting a triangle precisely when all three of its edges remain, and deleting those edges on acceptance. We work on the probability-one event that all priorities are distinct. An edge is *untouched before $t$* if no accepted triangle with priority strictly below $t$ contains it.

<a id="stmt-lem-priority"></a>
<div id="lem:priority" class="lemma">

**Lemma 4** (Priority representation). *For any finite simple graph $G$, the graph sequence indexed by the accepted triangles in this scan has the law of uniform sequential triangle removal started from $G$.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-priority"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-priority</summary>

*Proof.* The priority order is a uniform random permutation of $\mathcal T$. Conditional on any scanned prefix, the unscanned triangles have uniform relative order. Every currently present triangle is unscanned: an accepted triangle has lost its edges, and a rejected triangle already had a deleted edge and can never become present again. Until the next acceptance the graph stays fixed. Thus the next accepted triangle, if there is one, is the first currently present triangle in the remaining permutation, and is uniform among those triangles. This also identifies the absorbing state when none remains. ◻

</details>

In applying the lemma after the good prefix, the priorities are fresh random variables on the triangles of the fixed graph $G$. The original process has the same continuation law because its next-step rule depends only on its current graph.

A *call* consists of a focus (one or two edges), a threshold $t$, and, for a two-edge focus, a parent triangle. The finite test has two forms:

- A root call has focus $\{e\}$, threshold $t\in[0,1]$, and no parent. Its candidates are all triangles containing $e$.

- A non-root call has focus $\{f,g\}\subset T$, parent triangle $T$, and threshold $t=U_T$. Its candidates are all triangles meeting the focus, except $T$.

Distinct triangles meet in at most one graph edge. Consequently every candidate $S$ contains exactly one focus edge, denoted $e(S)$. Its child call has focus $S\setminus\{e(S)\}$, parent $S$, and threshold $U_S$.

At a call the test performs the following steps.

1.  Expose the priorities of *all* candidates, in a fixed deterministic order of triangle types. Each candidate occurs once in this list.

2.  Sort the candidates by priority and consider those with $U_S<t$ in that order. For a pair focus this is one joint ordering of the candidates at both focus edges.

3.  Evaluate each considered candidate’s child. At the first child returning true, stop and return false. If no child returns true, return true.

Exposing a candidate does not require visiting its child: priorities above the threshold and candidates skipped after an earlier success are also exposed. All calls use the same finite family $(U_T)_{T\in\mathcal T}$. The test has no side effects; it does not delete edges or change any priority.

<a id="stmt-lem-finite-test"></a>
<div id="lem:finite-test" class="lemma">

**Lemma 5** (Exact paired test). *For any finite simple graph with distinct triangle priorities, the finite test terminates. A root call at $(e,t)$ returns true exactly when $e$ is untouched before $t$ in the priority scan. A call with focus $\{f,g\}\subset T$ and threshold $U_T$ returns true exactly when both $f$ and $g$ are untouched before $U_T$.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-finite-test"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-finite-test</summary>

*Proof.* Along a recursive path the thresholds strictly decrease through the priorities of triangle types. No type can occur twice on that path, so its length is at most $|\mathcal T|$. The branching is finite, proving termination.

We prove correctness by induction on the number of triangle priorities strictly below the call’s threshold. Every child has a smaller threshold, so the induction hypothesis applies to it. Write $\mathcal F$ for the focus of the present call. We claim that some edge of $\mathcal F$ is deleted before $t$ if and only if some candidate $S$ with $U_S<t$ has both edges of $S\setminus\{e(S)\}$ untouched before $U_S$.

For the forward implication, take an accepted triangle that deletes a focus edge before $t$. It is a candidate and all its edges are untouched just before its acceptance. The omitted parent, when present, is at the threshold itself and cannot be this triangle. Conversely, suppose such a candidate exists and every focus edge is untouched before $t$. Its focus edge is then present at $U_S$, as are its two other edges. The scan accepts it, contradicting the assumed survival of the focus. This proves the claim. By induction the candidate condition is precisely that its child returns true, and the test returns false exactly when at least one such child exists. ◻

</details>

The parent omission is local to its call and is justified by the strict threshold. It does not erase that triangle from descendant lists. Also, the two finite survival events in a pair call need not be independent. We retain their joint chronological test, including its stopping rule, throughout the comparison argument.

<a id="pm-h-12"></a>
#### Fresh randomness at each occurrence

Backward exploration and comparison with independent branching occur in Spencer’s analysis (Spencer 1995, secs. 2–4) <a id="pm-cite-16"></a>[[Spencer1995](#ref-Spencer1995)]. We retain the paired query rule above and all candidate-list exposures for the finite comparison.

For a fixed root type, first construct the deterministic tree of all possible calls. Each node has one child for each candidate on its list, with the focus and parent specified above. The tree is countable and finitely branching. A triangle type may label many different child occurrences.

Assign an independent uniform variable to each child occurrence. This variable serves both as the candidate priority in its parent’s list and as the child’s threshold if the child is visited. Apply the same joint scanning and stopping rule on this tree. Different occurrences of a triangle type now have independent priorities; different root queries use disjoint trees with independent variables. We call this the *independent unfolding*.

We also allow a singleton root of type $e$ with a specified triangle $T\ni e$ omitted from its root list. The omission affects that list only. Descendant occurrences of $T$ remain available whenever their own parent rule allows them. A two-edge root with focus $\{f,g\}\subset T$, omitted parent $T$, and prescribed threshold $t$ is defined by the same independent construction.

The finiteness argument uses factorial decay along decreasing-priority paths, as in the local construction of graph adsorption processes by Penrose and Sudbury (Penrose and Sudbury 2005, Proposition 1 and its proof) <a id="pm-cite-17"></a>[[PenroseSudbury2005](#ref-PenroseSudbury2005)]. We give the argument for the present occurrence trees.

<a id="stmt-lem-unfolding-finite"></a>
<div id="lem:unfolding-finite" class="lemma">

**Lemma 6** (Finite evaluation). *For a fixed good graph $G$, independent unfoldings can be constructed so that, almost surely, every possible call and all thresholds $t\in[0,1]$ have finite evaluations. Their outputs are jointly measurable in the threshold and the occurrence priorities.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-unfolding-finite"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-unfolding-finite</summary>

*Proof.* Put $d_{\max}=\max_{e\in\mathcal E}d_e$ and $M=2d_{\max}$; every candidate list has at most $M$ entries. For large $n$, the good-graph estimates give $M\le4D$. From any fixed possible node there are at most $M^j$ descendant paths of length $j$. On each path the $j$ occurrence priorities are independent uniforms, and the probability that they strictly decrease is $1/j!$. Hence $$\mathbb P(\text{a decreasing path of length $j$ starts at that node})
 \le \frac{M^j}{j!}\longrightarrow0.$$ The events on the left decrease with $j$. Almost surely their depths are therefore bounded; finite branching makes the entire decreasing dependency tree finite. This argument uses threshold $1$, and thus works simultaneously for every smaller threshold. There are only countably many nodes in the forests for all possible root types, so a countable intersection gives the asserted simultaneous conclusion.

Truncate the recursion at depth $j$, using any fixed Boolean value at the boundary. Its output is a measurable function of the threshold and finitely many priorities. On the probability-one event just proved, these outputs stabilize as $j\to\infty$, for all thresholds, to the output of the full evaluation. Defining an arbitrary value on the null exceptional set proves joint measurability. ◻

</details>

We may consequently evaluate a child’s subtree hypothetically even when the chronological test never reaches that child. These hypothetical outputs characterize the root event: a call returns true if and only if no child with priority below its threshold would return true. For fixed priorities this indicator is nonincreasing in the root threshold, since each child’s threshold is its own occurrence priority.

<a id="pm-h-13"></a>
#### Exact probability identities

<a id="eq:integral"></a>
Let $q_e(t)$ be the probability that a full singleton root at $e$ returns true at threshold $t$ in the independent unfolding. Let $q_{e,T}(t)$ be the same probability when $T\ni e$ is omitted only from that singleton root list. For $T=\{e,f,g\}\in\mathcal T$, define $$\label{eq:integral}
 I_{e,T}(t)=\int_0^t q_{f,T}(v)q_{g,T}(v)\,dv,
 \qquad 0\le t\le1.$$ The integral is well defined by Lemma <a href="#lem:unfolding-finite" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:unfolding-finite">6</a>.

<a id="stmt-prop-products"></a>
<div id="prop:products" class="proposition">

<a id="eq:pair"></a>
<a id="eq:products"></a>
**Proposition 7** (Product identities). *For every triangle $T=\{e,f,g\}\in\mathcal T$ and $t\in[0,1]$, the independent unfolding satisfies $$\label{eq:pair}
 \mathbb P(\text{the pair $\{f,g\}\subset T$ returns true at $t$})
   =q_{f,T}(t)q_{g,T}(t),$$ $$\label{eq:products}
 \begin{aligned}
 q_e(t)&=\prod_{S\ni e}\bigl(1-I_{e,S}(t)\bigr),\\
 q_{e,T}(t)&=\prod_{\substack{S\ni e\\S\ne T}}
                    \bigl(1-I_{e,S}(t)\bigr).
 \end{aligned}$$ In the pair probability, $T$ is omitted from the pair’s candidate list. All products are over $\mathcal T$, and an empty product equals $1$. The functions $q_e$, $q_{e,T}$, and $I_{e,T}$ belong to $C^1([0,1])$. Moreover, $q_e(0)=q_{e,T}(0)=1$ and $I_{e,T}(0)=0$.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-prop-products"><summary>Proof of stmt-prop-products</summary>

*Proof.* After omitting $T$, the candidate sets at $f$ and $g$ are disjoint: a triangle containing both edges would equal $T$. Their occurrence variables and all descendant variables are independent. The pair returns true precisely when every candidate in both sets fails to give a successful child below $t$. The two separate events have the laws of the singleton roots with $T$ omitted, proving <a href="#eq:pair" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:pair">[eq:pair]</a>. The ordering of the joint list changes which children are visited, but not this event that all relevant children fail.

Consider a candidate $S=\{e,f,g\}$ at a singleton root $e$. Conditional on its occurrence priority being $v$, its child is a pair call at threshold $v$, with $S$ omitted and independent descendant priorities. Equation <a href="#eq:pair" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:pair">[eq:pair]</a> gives its success probability as $q_{f,S}(v)q_{g,S}(v)$. Thus $I_{e,S}(t)$ is exactly the probability that this candidate has priority below $t$ and a successful child. Different root candidates use disjoint sets of occurrence variables, including their descendants. Taking the probability that none succeeds proves both products in <a href="#eq:products" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:products">[eq:products]</a>.

Initially the probabilities are measurable and bounded between $0$ and $1$. Equation <a href="#eq:integral" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:integral">[eq:integral]</a> therefore makes each $I_{e,T}$ Lipschitz. The finite products imply continuity of all $q_e$ and $q_{e,T}$. Substitution back into <a href="#eq:integral" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:integral">[eq:integral]</a> now gives a continuous integrand, so the integrals, and then the products, are continuously differentiable. Derivatives at the endpoints have their one-sided interpretation. The initial values follow directly from the formulas. ◻

</details>

These identities describe the auxiliary independent model. They assert no factorization of survival probabilities in the finite priority system. We next estimate their solution uniformly over all edge and omitted-parent types; the finite evaluation above has already constructed this solution, so no recursive fixed-point assumption is needed.

<a id="sec:stability"></a>
### Stable unfolded probabilities

We now compute the survival probabilities in the independent unfolding. Throughout this section, $G$ is any graph satisfying Proposition <a href="#prop:prefix" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:prefix">[prop:prefix]</a>; every estimate is uniform over such graphs. Spencer’s branching model has survival probability $(1+Qc)^{-1/Q}$ (Spencer 1995, sec. 3, equation (9)) <a id="pm-cite-18"></a>[[Spencer1995](#ref-Spencer1995)]. With $Q=2$ and $c=Dt$, its scalar comparison function is <a id="eq:scalar-survival"></a>$$\label{eq:scalar-survival}
 q(t)=(1+2Dt)^{-1/2},\qquad 0\le t\le1.$$ It solves $q'(t)=-Dq(t)^3$ with $q(0)=1$. We will show that the probabilities $q_e(t)$ and $q_{e,T}(t)$ from Section <a href="#sec:queries" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:queries">3</a> are uniformly asymptotic to $q(t)$. The essential issue is stability: small discrepancies between edge types must remain small throughout the time interval.

<a id="pm-h-15"></a>
#### The exact equation and its linearization

Use the increasing change of variables $$s=\tfrac12\log(1+2Dt),\qquad
 S=\tfrac12\log(1+2D),\qquad
 t(s)=\frac{e^{2s}-1}{2D}.$$ Thus $0\le s\le S$, $q(t(s))=e^{-s}$, and $S\le\log n$ for all sufficiently large $n$. Since $0\le I_{e,T}(t)\le t$, the product identities in Proposition <a href="#prop:products" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:products">7</a> give $q_e(t)>0$ for $t<1$. On any interval on which all product factors remain positive, define the vector $z(s)\in\mathbb R^{\mathcal E}$ by $$z_e(s)=\log\frac{q_e(t(s))}{q(t(s))}.$$ In particular, $z(0)=0$. Positivity at $t=1$ will follow from the estimates below.

Let $A$ be the real symmetric matrix indexed by $\mathcal E$, with $A_{ef}=1$ if $e\ne f$ belong to a common triangle of $G$, and $A_{ef}=0$ otherwise. Each row indexed by $e$ has sum $2d_e$. Linearity of the triangle hypergraph ensures that each pair of distinct edges contributes at most once to $A$.

<a id="eq:exact-z"></a>
Differentiating the products is legitimate by Proposition <a href="#prop:products" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:products">7</a>. For $T=\{e,f,g\}$ we have $$I'_{e,T}(t)=q_{f,T}(t)q_{g,T}(t),\qquad
 q_{f,T}(t)=\frac{q_f(t)}{1-I_{f,T}(t)},\qquad
 \frac{dt}{ds}=\frac1{Dq(t)^2}.$$ Here and below a prime on $I$ denotes differentiation in $t$, and a prime on $z$ denotes differentiation in $s$. Taking the logarithmic derivative of $q_e$ consequently gives the exact identity $$\label{eq:exact-z}
 z'_e(s)=1-\frac1D
 \sum_{\substack{T\in\mathcal T\\T=\{e,f,g\}}}
 \frac{\exp(z_f(s)+z_g(s))}
 {(1-I_{e,T}(t))(1-I_{f,T}(t))(1-I_{g,T}(t))},
 \qquad t=t(s).$$ The factor indexed by $e$ comes from differentiating its product; the other two factors come from the two omitted-parent probabilities.

<a id="eq:bootstrap-exponent"></a>
<a id="eq:bootstrap-assumptions"></a>
<a id="eq:I-bootstrap"></a>
<a id="eq:linearized-error"></a>
For vectors, $\lVert\cdot\rVert_\infty$ denotes the maximum norm and $\lVert\cdot\rVert_2$ the Euclidean norm. For matrices we use the corresponding induced operator norms. Fix $$\label{eq:bootstrap-exponent}
 0<a<\min\{c,2\epsilon\},\qquad \delta_n=n^{-a}.$$ Suppose, provisionally, that on an interval starting at zero, $$\label{eq:bootstrap-assumptions}
 \lVert z(s)\rVert_\infty\le\delta_n,
 \qquad I_{e,T}(t(s))\le\tfrac12
 \quad(e\in T\in\mathcal T).$$ The omitted-parent identity then implies $q_{e,T}(t)\le2e^{\delta_n}q(t)$. Integrating yields, on the same interval, $$\label{eq:I-bootstrap}
 I_{e,T}(t)
 \le4e^{2\delta_n}\int_0^t\frac{dv}{1+2Dv}
 =\frac{2e^{2\delta_n}}D\log(1+2Dt)
 =O\!\left(\frac{\log n}{D}\right).$$ In particular, all three denominator factors in <a href="#eq:exact-z" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:exact-z">[eq:exact-z]</a> are $1+O(\log n/D)$. Expanding the exponential and reciprocal factors gives, uniformly for each summand, $$\frac{e^{z_f+z_g}}
 {(1-I_{e,T})(1-I_{f,T})(1-I_{g,T})}
 =1+z_f+z_g+
 O\!\left(\delta_n^2+\frac{\log n}{D}\right).$$ The sum of the linear terms is exactly $(Az)_e$. Since $d_e/D=1+O(n^{-c})$, we obtain the conditional estimate $$\label{eq:linearized-error}
 \left\lVert z'(s)+(A/D)z(s)\right\rVert_\infty
 \le C\left(n^{-2a}+n^{-c}+\frac{\log n}{D}\right).$$ The constant $C$ is independent of the graph and the provisional interval. The general bound $\lVert e^{-sA/D}\rVert_\infty\le
e^{s\lVert A/D\rVert_\infty}$ gives a loss of order $D$ at $s=S$, too large to guarantee control of the prefix error $n^{-c}$. We will instead prove a polylogarithmic bound, sufficient to turn <a href="#eq:linearized-error" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:linearized-error">[eq:linearized-error]</a> into a strict improvement of <a href="#eq:bootstrap-assumptions" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:bootstrap-assumptions">[eq:bootstrap-assumptions]</a>.

<a id="pm-h-16"></a>
#### From link spectra to a uniform semigroup bound

The passage from even closed-walk counts to spectral control follows the sparse quasirandom trace method; compare Chung and Graham (Chung and Graham 2002, Theorem 1 and Facts 7–8) <a id="pm-cite-19"></a>[[ChungGraham2002](#ref-ChungGraham2002)]. We prove the quantitative form required by the link hypotheses and then transfer it to the maximum norm on the full edge space.

<a id="stmt-prop-semigroup"></a>
<div id="prop:semigroup" class="proposition">

<a id="eq:semigroup"></a>
**Proposition 8**. *There are fixed constants $C_1,J<\infty$ such that every graph $G$ satisfying the degree, codegree and link-cycle conditions of Proposition <a href="#prop:prefix" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:prefix">[prop:prefix]</a> obeys $$\label{eq:semigroup}
 \sup_{0\le s\le\log n}
 \left\lVert e^{-sA/D}\right\rVert_\infty
 \le C_1(1+\log n)^J$$ for all sufficiently large $n$.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-prop-semigroup"><summary>Proof of stmt-prop-semigroup</summary>

*Proof.* We first approximate the adjacency matrix on each star by an averaging operator. We then combine these averages and estimate the effect of the error. All constants in the proof are uniform in $G$.

<a id="pm-h-17"></a>
###### Closed walks in a link.

For $u\in[n]$, let $\mathcal E_u=\{uv:v\in N_G(u)\}$ and $N_u=|\mathcal E_u|$. Identify $uv\in\mathcal E_u$ with $v\in N_G(u)$, and let $M_u$ be the adjacency matrix of the link $G[N_G(u)]$ on this index set. We have $N_u=(1\pm n^{-c})np$, and every row sum of $M_u$ is $(1\pm n^{-c})D$, by the codegree assumption. Put $r=r_0=8000$.

The trace $\operatorname{tr}(M_u^r)$ counts closed walks of length $r$ with a specified starting position. Walks whose $r$ positions are distinct are precisely embeddings of the labeled cycle $C_r$; their number is $(1\pm n^{-c})D^r$. For all other walks, classify the patterns of identifications among the $r$ positions. There are only finitely many patterns, since $r$ is fixed. The support of any realized pattern is a connected simple graph; patterns forcing a loop have no realizations.

If the support is a tree, every support edge is traversed at least twice: a walk crossing the cut obtained by deleting that edge must cross back. Thus the support has at most $r/2$ edges. Choose the image of one vertex and then expose the other vertices along this tree. The link has maximum degree at most $2D$, so this gives at most $O(np)(2D)^{r/2}$ realizations of each pattern.

If the support contains a cycle, let it have $j<r$ vertices and choose one of its simple cycles, of length $b\ge3$. The link-cycle condition gives $O(D^b)$ choices for this labeled cycle. A forest rooted at its vertices attaches all the other vertices of the connected support, using at most $(2D)^{j-b}$ choices. Ignoring its remaining edges only increases the count. The resulting bound is $O(D^j)$, and hence $O(D^{r-1})$ for each such pattern. These cases exhaust the non-injective walks. Dividing their total contribution by $D^r$ shows that <a id="eq:link-trace"></a>$$\label{eq:link-trace}
 \begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{tr}\bigl((M_u/D)^r\bigr)
 &=1+O\!\left(n^{-c}+D^{-1}+npD^{-r/2}\right)\\
 &=1+O(n^{-c_*}),\qquad c_*:=\min\{c,1/1000\}.
 \end{aligned}$$ Indeed, $D\sim n^{1/1000}$ and $npD^{-4000}\sim n^{-6999/2000}$.

<a id="pm-h-18"></a>
###### The leading eigendirection.

Write $B_u=M_u/D$, and order its real eigenvalues as $\lambda_1\ge\lambda_2\ge\cdots$. The row-sum bound gives $\lambda_1\le1+n^{-c}$; the Rayleigh quotient of the normalized all-ones vector gives $\lambda_1\ge1-n^{-c}$. Because $r$ is even, $$0\le\sum_{j\ge2}\lambda_j^r
 =\operatorname{tr}(B_u^r)-\lambda_1^r
 =O(n^{-c_*}).$$ Consequently, with the fixed positive constant $\gamma=c_*/r$, all eigenvalues other than $\lambda_1$ have absolute value $O(n^{-\gamma})$. In particular, $\lambda_1$ is simple for large $n$.

<a id="eq:star-approximation"></a>
Let $\mathbf j_u=N_u^{-1/2}\mathbf1$ and let $v_u$ be a unit eigenvector for $\lambda_1$. The row sums also give $\lVert(B_u-\mathrm{Id})\mathbf j_u\rVert_2\le n^{-c}$. Write $\mathbf j_u=\alpha v_u+w$, where $w\perp v_u$. Every eigenvalue of $B_u-\mathrm{Id}$ on $v_u^\perp$ has absolute value at least $1/2$ for large $n$, so $\lVert w\rVert_2\le2n^{-c}$. The orthogonal projectors onto the lines spanned by $v_u$ and $\mathbf j_u$ therefore differ in operator norm by at most $2n^{-c}$. Combining this with the eigenvalue estimates gives $$\label{eq:star-approximation}
 \left\lVert \frac{M_u}{D}-\frac{\mathbf1\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}}{N_u}
 \right\rVert_2=O(n^{-\gamma}).$$

<a id="pm-h-19"></a>
###### Summing the star averages.

<a id="eq:R-norms"></a>
Extend every star matrix by zero to the full edge space $\mathbb R^{\mathcal E}$, and let $P$ be the sum of the averaging matrices $\mathbf1\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}/N_u$ on the stars. Distinct edges lying in a common triangle have a unique common endpoint, so $A$ is the sum of the extended matrices $M_u$. Define $$R=A/D-P.$$ The matrix $P$ is symmetric and positive semidefinite. For every $x\in\mathbb R^{\mathcal E}$, estimate <a href="#eq:star-approximation" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:star-approximation">[eq:star-approximation]</a> gives $$|x^{\mathsf T}Rx|
 \le Cn^{-\gamma}\sum_{u\in[n]}
          \lVert x|_{\mathcal E_u}\rVert_2^2
 =2Cn^{-\gamma}\lVert x\rVert_2^2.$$ Each edge lies in exactly two stars, which explains the last equality and prevents a loss proportional to the number of vertices. Since $R$ is symmetric, it follows that $\lVert R\rVert_2=O(n^{-\gamma})$. Every row of $P$ has sum two, while a row of $A/D$ has sum $2d_e/D$. Both matrices are entrywise nonnegative, so their absolute row sums also give $$\label{eq:R-norms}
 \lVert R\rVert_2=O(n^{-\gamma}),\qquad
 \lVert R\rVert_\infty=O(1).$$

<a id="eq:average-semigroup"></a>
We have thus obtained a small Euclidean error, whereas the evolution in <a href="#eq:linearized-error" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:linearized-error">[eq:linearized-error]</a> requires the maximum norm. The special form of $P$ supplies this stronger control. Define $$\begin{aligned}
 U:\mathbb R^{[n]}&\longrightarrow\mathbb R^{\mathcal E},
 & (Uy)_{uv}&=y_u+y_v,\\
 V:\mathbb R^{\mathcal E}&\longrightarrow\mathbb R^{[n]},
 & (Vx)_u&=\frac1{N_u}\sum_{e\in\mathcal E_u}x_e.
 \end{aligned}$$ Then $P=UV$, $\lVert U\rVert_\infty=2$, and $\lVert V\rVert_\infty=1$. If $W$ is the neighbor random-walk matrix, defined by $(Wy)_u=N_u^{-1}\sum_{v\in N_G(u)}y_v$, then $VU=\mathrm{Id}+W$ and $\lVert W\rVert_\infty=1$. The identity $(UV)^j=U(VU)^{j-1}V$ for $j\ge1$, applied to the exponential power series, yields $$E_0(s):=e^{-sP}
 =\mathrm{Id}-U\int_0^s e^{-v(\mathrm{Id}+W)}\,dv\,V.$$ The exponential inside the integral satisfies $\lVert e^{-v(\mathrm{Id}+W)}\rVert_\infty
\le e^{-v}e^{v\lVert W\rVert_\infty}=1$. Hence, for every $s\ge0$, $$\label{eq:average-semigroup}
 \lVert E_0(s)\rVert_\infty\le1+2s,
 \qquad \lVert E_0(s)\rVert_2\le1.$$ The second inequality follows from the positive semidefiniteness of $P$. Likewise, $A/D=P+R$ is symmetric with smallest eigenvalue at least $-\lVert R\rVert_2$, so <a id="eq:full-semigroup-l2"></a>$$\label{eq:full-semigroup-l2}
 E(s):=e^{-sA/D},\qquad
 \lVert E(s)\rVert_2\le e^{s\lVert R\rVert_2}.$$

<a id="pm-h-20"></a>
###### A finite perturbation expansion.

It remains to transfer the maximum-norm estimate from $E_0$ to $E$. We use a finite form of the bounded-perturbation expansion for semigroups (Engel and Nagel 2000, Theorem III.1.10) <a id="pm-cite-20"></a>[[EngelNagel2000](#ref-EngelNagel2000)], estimating its initial terms and remainder in different norms. Choose a fixed integer $K$ with $K\gamma>2$. Iterating the variation-of-constants identity $$E(s)=E_0(s)-\int_0^s E_0(s-v)R E(v)\,dv$$ gives terms with $j=0,\ldots,K-1$ copies of $R$, followed by a remainder with $K$ copies. The term with $j$ copies is integrated over an ordered simplex of volume $s^j/j!$ and contains $j+1$ factors $E_0$, all at nonnegative times at most $s$. By <a href="#eq:average-semigroup" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:average-semigroup">[eq:average-semigroup]</a>, its maximum norm is at most $$(1+2s)^{j+1}\lVert R\rVert_\infty^j\frac{s^j}{j!}.$$ The remainder has the exact form $$\begin{split}
 \mathcal R_K(s)=(-1)^K
 \int_{0\le t_K\le\cdots\le t_1\le s}
 &E_0(s-t_1)R E_0(t_1-t_2)R\cdots\\[-2pt]
 &\hspace{18mm}\cdots E_0(t_{K-1}-t_K)R E(t_K)\,
 dt_K\cdots dt_1.
 \end{split}$$ Its Euclidean norm is at most $$\lVert\mathcal R_K(s)\rVert_2
 \le\lVert R\rVert_2^K e^{s\lVert R\rVert_2}\frac{s^K}{K!}.$$ For an $m\times m$ matrix $H$, Cauchy–Schwarz gives $\lVert H\rVert_\infty\le\sqrt m\,\lVert H\rVert_2$. Here $m=|\mathcal E|\le n^2/2$, so, uniformly for $s\le\log n$, $$\lVert\mathcal R_K(s)\rVert_\infty
 \le n(Cn^{-\gamma})^K
       e^{Cn^{-\gamma}\log n}\frac{(\log n)^K}{K!}
 =o(1).$$ The other $K$ terms have maximum norm bounded by a fixed power of $1+\log n$, by <a href="#eq:R-norms" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:R-norms">[eq:R-norms]</a>. This proves <a href="#eq:semigroup" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:semigroup">[eq:semigroup]</a>. The order $K$ may be large, but is fixed independently of $n$ and $G$. ◻

</details>

<a id="pm-h-21"></a>
#### Closing the probability estimate

The semigroup bound now controls the cumulative error in <a href="#eq:linearized-error" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:linearized-error">[eq:linearized-error]</a>. This is the only use of the spectral calculation in the probability argument.

<a id="stmt-prop-unfolding"></a>
<div id="prop:unfolding" class="proposition">

**Proposition 9**. *For every graph $G$ satisfying Proposition <a href="#prop:prefix" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:prefix">[prop:prefix]</a>, the independent-unfolding probabilities obey <a id="eq:unfolding-asymptotics"></a>$$\label{eq:unfolding-asymptotics}
 q_e(t)=(1+o(1))q(t),\qquad
 q_{e,T}(t)=(1+o(1))q(t),\qquad
 I_{e,T}(t)=O\!\left(\frac{\log n}{D}\right).$$ The estimates hold uniformly over $G$, $0\le t\le1$, and every incident pair $e\in T\in\mathcal T$; the first estimate also holds for every $e\in\mathcal E$.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-prop-unfolding"><summary>Proof of stmt-prop-unfolding</summary>

*Proof.* Use $a$ and $\delta_n$ from <a href="#eq:bootstrap-exponent" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:bootstrap-exponent">[eq:bootstrap-exponent]</a>. All $I_{e,T}$ vanish and all probabilities equal one at zero, so by continuity the conditions <a href="#eq:bootstrap-assumptions" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:bootstrap-assumptions">[eq:bootstrap-assumptions]</a> hold on some interval starting at zero. On every such interval, <a href="#eq:I-bootstrap" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:I-bootstrap">[eq:I-bootstrap]</a> improves the second condition to $I_{e,T}\le1/4$ for large $n$.

Write $h(s)=z'(s)+(A/D)z(s)$. As $z(0)=0$, variation of constants and Proposition <a href="#prop:semigroup" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:semigroup">8</a> give, on the same interval, $$\begin{aligned}
 \lVert z(s)\rVert_\infty
 &\le\int_0^s
       \lVert e^{-(s-v)A/D}\rVert_\infty
       \lVert h(v)\rVert_\infty\,dv\\
 &\le C(1+\log n)^{J+1}
       \left(n^{-2a}+n^{-c}+\frac{\log n}{D}\right)
 =o(n^{-a}).
\end{aligned}$$ The last equality follows from $a>0$, $c-a>0$ and $2\epsilon-a>0$, together with $D\sim n^{2\epsilon}$: every fixed power of $\log n$ is dominated by each of these positive powers of $n$. In particular, for large $n$ this improves the first condition to $\lVert z\rVert_\infty\le\delta_n/2$. The spectral exponent $\gamma$ only fixes the integer $K$ in the semigroup estimate.

If either bootstrap condition had a first exit before $s=S$, these strict improvements would hold up to that exit. All product factors would then remain at least $3/4$, so the finitely many functions and their logarithms would be continuous on a neighborhood of the exit. Both conditions would persist beyond it, a contradiction. Thus the bounds hold for $s<S$, and continuity gives them also at $s=S$; the endpoint factors remain positive. This proves $z_e(s)=o(n^{-a})$ throughout $[0,S]$ and the asserted bound on $I_{e,T}$. Finally, $$\frac{q_e(t)}{q(t)}=e^{z_e(s)},\qquad
 \frac{q_{e,T}(t)}{q(t)}
 =\frac{e^{z_e(s)}}{1-I_{e,T}(t)},$$ which establishes both probability estimates. ◻

</details>

<a id="eq:off-pattern-bound"></a>
One consequence records the precision needed for the later exploration of selected paths. Set $J_n=\max_{e\in T\in\mathcal T,\,0\le t\le1}I_{e,T}(t)$. At a paired call with focus $\{f,g\}\subset T$ and omitted parent $T$, the product of all no-success factors is $q_{f,T}(t)q_{g,T}(t)$. Deleting at most two factors from this product increases it by at most $(1-J_n)^{-2}$. Hence, uniformly in the call and threshold, for large $n$ the resulting product is at most $$\label{eq:off-pattern-bound}
 \frac{q_{f,T}(t)q_{g,T}(t)}{(1-J_n)^2}
 \le 2q(t)^2=\frac2{1+2Dt}.$$ This remains a statement about the independent unfolding. We next compare its queries with the finite priority process.

<a id="sec:coupling"></a>
### Coupling the finite and independent tests

Fix a graph $G$ satisfying Proposition <a href="#prop:prefix" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:prefix">[prop:prefix]</a>. The independent probabilities from Proposition <a href="#prop:unfolding" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:unfolding">9</a> approximate survival only if repeated uses of a finite triangle priority are sufficiently rare. We first give an exact coupling and then reduce its failure to a graph configuration supported on two visited call paths. Throughout this section, a *triangle type* means a member of $\mathcal T$, as distinguished from its possibly many occurrences in an unfolding.

Fix $k\in\{1,2\}$. Sample $k$ independent uniform oriented edges of $G$, with replacement and independently of all priorities. There are $2m$ possible oriented edges. The orientation names the two endpoints and does not affect the test. With these roots, compare two experiments: the finite tests sharing one priority for each triangle type, and the independent unfoldings with disjoint occurrence trees for different roots. Every root has threshold $1$. Execute the roots in a fixed order, including every root regardless of the preceding answers.

At each visited call, expose the priorities of *all* candidates in a fixed type order before sorting them for the test. Thus an exposed list includes candidates above the threshold and candidates never traversed because an earlier child returned true. In the independent experiment, let $\mathcal C_k$ be the event that a triangle type appears at least twice among all these exposed lists. There is no repetition within one list: a candidate meeting both edges of a pair focus would be its omitted parent triangle.

<a id="stmt-lem-coupling"></a>
<div id="lem:coupling" class="lemma">

**Lemma 10** (Coupling by exposed triangle types). *Let $X^{\mathrm{fin}},X^{\mathrm{ind}}\in\{0,1\}^k$ be the vectors of root answers in the two experiments, where $1$ denotes true. For every $H\subseteq\{0,1\}^k$, $$\bigl|\mathbb P(X^{\mathrm{fin}}\in H)
       -\mathbb P(X^{\mathrm{ind}}\in H)\bigr|
 \le \mathbb P_{\mathrm{ind}}(\mathcal C_k).$$*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-coupling"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-coupling</summary>

*Proof.* Assign independent uniform priorities $U_T$, $T\in\mathcal T$, for the finite experiment, and an independent supply of fresh uniform variables. Run the unfolding tests as follows. At the first exposure of a type $T$, use $U_T$; at each later exposure of that type, use a fresh variable instead.

This procedure has the independent-unfolding law. Indeed, the type queried next is determined by $G$, the root labels and the execution history. If $Q$ is the set of types exposed so far, that history depends only on $(U_T:T\in Q)$ and on the fresh variables already used. Every recursive computation contributing to a returned answer is part of this history, with its candidate lists exposed explicitly. Conditional on the history, the variables $(U_T:T\notin Q)$ are still independent uniforms. A fresh variable used for a repeated type is independent of the same history. Induction over exposures therefore gives independent uniform occurrence priorities. The unused occurrence variables may be filled in independently afterward.

The evaluations terminate almost surely by Section <a href="#sec:queries" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:queries">3</a>. Until a repeated type is exposed, the unfolding and finite executions use the same priority at every candidate. They consequently have the same sorted lists, recursive calls, thresholds, answers and stopping decisions. On $\mathcal C_k^c$ this agreement holds for the whole execution of all roots. The stated inequality follows from this coupling of the answer vectors. ◻

</details>

<a id="pm-h-23"></a>
#### The graph of visited calls

We now work entirely in the independent experiment. Its visited calls form a finite rooted forest almost surely. Construct a graph $\Gamma$ and a label map $\lambda:V(\Gamma)\to V(G)$ from this forest. Each root contributes two new *formal* vertices $x,y$, the edge $xy$, and labels given by its sampled ordered endpoints. Distinct roots have disjoint formal vertices even if their labels coincide. If a visited child is attached through a formal focus edge $xy$ of its parent, introduce a new formal vertex $z$, label it by the third vertex of the child’s triangle type, and add the edges $xz,yz$. These two new edges are the child’s focus. If $xy$ is ordered, order them as $(x,z),(y,z)$, so subsequent choices of focus edge have fixed names.

The *birth call* of a formal vertex or edge is the call that introduced it. Order the construction with all roots first and every parent before its children. The following properties hold in any such order:

1.  Every formal edge has adjacent labels in $G$, and the three vertices in any one gluing have distinct labels.

2.  Every formal edge is focused at exactly one call, its birth call. An attachment edge is not in the child’s focus.

3.  No step adds an edge between two vertices already present.

4.  The only triangles of $\Gamma$ are the triangles introduced by the gluings.

For the last property, consider the latest vertex of a formal triangle. The initial disjoint root edges contain no triangle. A non-root vertex has exactly two older neighbours, namely the endpoints of its attachment edge, so the triangle must be its gluing triangle.

For a formal vertex $a$, its *ancestral call path* is the path from a root to the birth call of $a$, including both ends. Given two formal vertices $a,b$, let $\Gamma_{a,b}$ be the graph constructed from the union of their ancestral call paths, using the root edges and gluings on those paths. All its calls are visited. We call $(a,b)$ an *extra-edge witness* if $a,b$ are distinct and nonadjacent in $\Gamma$, their labels are adjacent in $G$, and $\lambda$ is injective on $V(\Gamma_{a,b})$.

<a id="stmt-lem-witness"></a>
<div id="lem:witness" class="lemma">

<a id="eq:collision-witness"></a>
**Lemma 11** (A geometric witness for a collision). *If the initial root labels are all distinct, then every occurrence of $\mathcal C_k$ produces an extra-edge witness. Moreover, uniformly over the graphs under consideration, $$\label{eq:collision-witness}
 \mathbb P_{\mathrm{ind}}(\mathcal C_k)
 \le O(n^{-1})+
 \mathbb P_{\mathrm{ind}}(\text{an extra-edge witness exists}).$$ The $O(n^{-1})$ term may be omitted when $k=1$.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-witness"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-witness</summary>

*Proof.* First suppose that $\lambda$ is injective on all of $\Gamma$. If an edge of $G$ between two labels is absent from the formal graph, its two formal endpoints give the required witness. It remains to show that a collision is impossible when there is no such extra edge.

Suppose that a triangle type $T$ is exposed at two calls. It cannot occur twice in one list, so the exposing calls are distinct. Their responsible focus edges are distinct by property (ii), and their images are distinct by injectivity. Those two edges already represent all three vertices of $T$, even if neither candidate occurrence is traversed. Since there are no extra edges between labels, the three formal vertices form a triangle of $\Gamma$. By property (iv), it is a gluing triangle. Its two new edges are focused only at that gluing’s child call, which omits the triangle as its parent. Its attachment edge is focused at only one call. Hence the triangle can appear in at most one candidate list, a contradiction.

Next suppose that the labels are not injective. Choose the first repetition in a construction order with roots first and parents before children. The distinct-root assumption means that this repetition occurs when a new vertex $z$ is attached through an edge $xy$. Write $w$ for the earlier vertex with $\lambda(w)=\lambda(z)$. All earlier labels are distinct. Property (i) shows that $x,y,w$ are distinct and that $\lambda(x)\lambda(w)$ and $\lambda(y)\lambda(w)$ are edges of $G$.

If either $xw$ or $yw$ is absent formally, it gives an extra edge among the earlier vertices. The ancestral paths of its endpoints are entirely earlier in the construction, so their labels are injective. Property (iii) ensures that the pair remains nonadjacent in the completed graph. This is the desired witness.

Otherwise $xyw$ is a formal triangle and hence an earlier gluing triangle. There are two possibilities for its latest vertex. If it is $x$ or $y$, then $xy$ was born at this gluing. The current call focusing $xy$ is exactly its birth call, and omits the triangle $xyw$ as its parent. It therefore cannot have the proposed child with the same triangle type. If the latest vertex is $w$, then $w$ was attached through $xy$. The children introducing $w$ and $z$ would belong to the same unique call focusing $xy$, and would have the same triangle type. That call contains this candidate only once and traverses it at most once, again a contradiction. Thus a first repeated label always produces an earlier extra edge.

Finally, either endpoint of a uniform oriented root has distribution $$\pi(v)=\frac{\deg_G(v)}{2m},
 \qquad \max_v\pi(v)=O(n^{-1}),$$ by the degree estimate and $2m=n^2p$. For two independent roots, any fixed pair of their endpoints coincides with probability $\sum_v\pi(v)^2\le\max_v\pi(v)$. There are four pairs to consider; the two endpoints within each root are distinct. A union bound therefore gives the term $O(n^{-1})$ in <a href="#eq:collision-witness" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:collision-witness">[eq:collision-witness]</a>. ◻

</details>

The root experiment still samples with replacement; we have only charged its overlaps to the error bound. The remaining task is to bound the probability of a visited, injectively labelled pair of call paths whose two marked vertices have an extra edge. This uses both the template estimates for $G$ and the independent-test probabilities.

<a id="sec:witnesses"></a>
### Counting and visiting collision witnesses

We now bound the probability of the marked path unions furnished by Lemma <a href="#lem:witness" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:witness">11</a>. The required precision is $o(D^{-1})$: the probability that two independent root queries both return true is asymptotic to $q(1)^2\sim(2D)^{-1}$. A collision estimate that merely tends to zero would not control that second moment.

Throughout this section, $G$ is any graph satisfying Proposition <a href="#prop:prefix" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:prefix">[prop:prefix]</a>, and all constants and error estimates are uniform over such $G$. We work in the independent unfolding. The proof has two parts. First, the extra edge between the marked vertices makes their path union rare as a graph embedding. Second, reaching a long prescribed path requires many earlier candidate queries to fail. We retain the order of the selected priorities when combining these two bounds.

<a id="pm-h-25"></a>
#### Patterns and their embeddings

The paths in this section are paths in the *call forest*, not paths in the formal graph $\Gamma$. Consider the union of the ancestral paths to the birth calls of two marked formal vertices $a,b$. When the paths belong to the same rooted tree, let $h$ be their common trunk length and let $\ell_1,\ell_2$ be the two lengths after the trunk. Length counts non-root calls. When the paths belong to distinct trees, set $h=0$ and let $\ell_1,\ell_2$ be their individual lengths. In both cases put $$\ell=h+\ell_1+\ell_2,
 \qquad r=\text{the number of roots used}\in\{1,2\}.$$ Zero lengths are allowed. A mark born at a root specifies one of its two formal endpoints.

<a id="eq:pattern-count"></a>
A *pattern* records these rooted paths, their marks, and the choice of focus edge at each child step. The named focus edges from Section <a href="#sec:coupling" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:coupling">5</a> give at most two choices at a step. The choices of roots, the order of the two marked paths, and any marked root endpoints contribute only an absolute constant. Consequently, for each triple $(h,\ell_1,\ell_2)$ there are at most $$\label{eq:pattern-count}
 C\,2^\ell$$ patterns, for an absolute constant $C$. At a fork the two children each choose a focus edge; their third vertices will be determined by an embedding. Thus there is no further length-dependent multiplicity. Patterns that cannot occur under the query rule may be discarded.

The formal graph $J$ of a pattern starts with $r$ disjoint root edges and adds two edges and one vertex at each non-root call. In particular, $$v(J)=2r+\ell,\qquad e(J)=r+2\ell.$$ We consider only patterns whose marks $a,b$ are distinct and nonadjacent in $J$, and write $J^+=J+ab$. An embedding below is an injective map of the formal vertices into $V(G)$ that maps every edge of $J^+$ to an edge of $G$ and respects the named endpoints of each root.

<a id="stmt-lem-embeddings"></a>
<div id="lem:embeddings" class="lemma">

<a id="eq:embedding-bound"></a>
**Lemma 12** (Embedding an extra edge). *For every such pattern with $r\in\{1,2\}$ roots and $\ell$ non-root calls, the number of embeddings of $J^+$ into $G$ is at most $$\label{eq:embedding-bound}
 (2m)^r(2D)^\ell D^{-B}$$ for all sufficiently large $n$, uniformly over the pattern and $G$.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-embeddings"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-embeddings</summary>

*Proof.* Write $\kappa_n=(1+\log n)^{C_0}$ for the common factor in the two template bounds of Proposition <a href="#prop:prefix" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:prefix">[prop:prefix]</a>. Recall that $L=100$, $B=50$, $p\sim n^{-1/2+\epsilon}$, and $D=np^2\sim n^{2\epsilon}$, where $\epsilon=1/2000$.

<a id="pm-h-26"></a>
###### Fewer than $L$ calls.

Suppose $\ell<L$. Regard $J^+$ as a template with no distinguished vertices. Order all root vertices first, ordering the endpoints of each root edge, and then add the other vertices in any parent-first order of their birth calls. Before adding $ab$, each vertex has at most two neighbors preceding it. Moreover, the first vertex of any nonempty vertex subset has no preceding neighbor within that subset. An induced subgraph on $v>0$ vertices therefore has at most $2v-2$ edges from $J$, and at most $2v-1\leq 2v$ from $J^+$. Every subgraph satisfies the same weaker bound $e\leq 2v$. Since $p<1$ and $D>1$ for large $n$, its scaling is at least $$n^v p^{2v}=D^v\geq1.$$ The empty subgraph has scaling one. The first template bound gives $$\#\{J^+\hookrightarrow G\}
 \leq \kappa_n n^{2r+\ell}p^{r+2\ell+1}
 =\kappa_n(2m)^rD^\ell p.$$ Here the template has at most $4+(L-1)=103\leq H_0$ vertices. The inequality $$pD^B=n^{-1/2+(2B+1)\epsilon+o(1)}
      =n^{-899/2000+o(1)}$$ shows that $\kappa_np\leq D^{-B}$ for large $n$. This proves <a href="#eq:embedding-bound" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:embedding-bound">[eq:embedding-bound]</a> in the short case.

<a id="pm-h-27"></a>
###### The last $L$ births.

<a id="eq:preceding-labels"></a>
Suppose $\ell\geq L$. Choose any parent-first ordering of the non-root calls, with all roots placed first, and let $Z$ be the set of the last $L$ vertices born. The labels of the preceding vertices can be assigned in at most $$\label{eq:preceding-labels}
 (2m)^r(2D)^{\ell-L}$$ ways. Indeed, there are $2m$ choices for each oriented root, and at most $2D$ common neighbors of the two endpoints of each subsequent attachment edge. Discarding restrictions against repeated labels only increases this bound. We now fix an injective assignment of these preceding labels.

The birth call of every vertex in $Z$ is an ancestor of the birth call of a marked vertex in $Z$. To see this, every call in the pattern lies on a path to a marked birth call. Once that path enters the final suffix, all its subsequent calls lie in the suffix, by the parent-first order. In particular, at least one endpoint of $ab$ belongs to $Z$. Figure <a href="#fig:call-paths" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="fig:call-paths">2</a> illustrates how such a suffix can meet both arms.

Form a template $(H,I)$ as follows. Its free vertices are $Z$. Retain the two backward edges of each vertex of $Z$, meaning its edges to the endpoints of its attachment edge, and retain $ab$. The distinguished set $I$ consists of all preceding vertices needed as endpoints of these edges. Include no edges with both endpoints in $I$. We may discard such constraints because we seek an upper bound on extensions. Thus $I$ is independent in the template, whether or not its prescribed images have edges between them in $G$.

<a id="eq:terminal-scaling"></a>
The backward edges use at most $2L$ boundary vertices, and the extra edge uses at most one further boundary vertex. Hence $$|I|\leq2L+1,\qquad v(H)\leq3L+1\leq H_0.$$ The $2L$ backward edges are distinct, and $ab$ is not one of them. Every retained edge touches $Z$, so $$\label{eq:terminal-scaling}
 e(H)=2L+1,\qquad
 S(H,I)=n^Lp^{2L+1}=D^Lp
       =n^{-799/2000+o(1)}<1.$$

<a id="eq:proper-free-edges"></a>
We verify every completion condition needed for the second template bound. If $U\subsetneq Z$, then $$\label{eq:proper-free-edges}
 e\bigl(H[I\cup U]\bigr)\leq2|U|.$$ Indeed, assign each backward edge to its newer endpoint in $Z$. In $H[I\cup U]$ there are at most two such edges per vertex of $U$. If <a href="#eq:proper-free-edges" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:proper-free-edges">[eq:proper-free-edges]</a> failed, the induced graph would retain $ab$ and both backward edges of every vertex of $U$. Retaining $ab$ puts every free mark in $U$. Retaining both backward edges of a vertex also forces the vertex born at its immediate parent call into $U$, whenever that vertex belongs to $Z$. Indeed, both focus edges at a non-root parent contain its newly born vertex, so the child’s attachment edge contains that vertex. Repeating this argument backward along each marked path includes every vertex of $Z$, by the ancestor property just proved. This would give $U=Z$, a contradiction.

For a proper $U$, <a href="#eq:proper-free-edges" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:proper-free-edges">[eq:proper-free-edges]</a> implies $$S\bigl(H[I\cup U],I\bigr)
 =n^{|U|}p^{e(H[I\cup U])}\geq D^{|U|}\geq1.$$ Every intermediate set of distinguished vertices has the form $J_0=I\cup U$, with $U\subseteq Z$. Its completion scaling is $$n^{v(H)-|J_0|}p^{e(H)-e(H[J_0])}
 =\frac{S(H,I)}{S(H[J_0],I)}.$$ For proper $U$ the numerator is less than one by <a href="#eq:terminal-scaling" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:terminal-scaling">[eq:terminal-scaling]</a>, while the denominator is at least one. For $U=Z$ the ratio equals one. All the required completion scalings are therefore at most one.

The second template bound now gives at most $\kappa_n$ extensions for the labels of $Z$. In applying it we may ignore avoidance of previously assigned vertices outside $I$, again only enlarging the count. Since $L-B=50>0$ and $D\sim n^{1/1000}$, $$\kappa_n\leq(2D)^L D^{-B}$$ for all sufficiently large $n$. Multiplying this by <a href="#eq:preceding-labels" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:preceding-labels">[eq:preceding-labels]</a> proves <a href="#eq:embedding-bound" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:embedding-bound">[eq:embedding-bound]</a>. The threshold for $n$ is uniform: the template order and its polylogarithmic exponent are fixed independently of the path lengths. ◻

</details>

The embedding bound supplies a factor $D^{-B}$ even when the paths are arbitrarily long. It remains to sum over their lengths. Counting paths alone would lose this factor, since a call can have order $D$ children. The next estimate uses the failures required to reach a prescribed child, together with the decreasing order of priorities.

<a id="pm-h-28"></a>
#### The probability of visiting prescribed paths

Fix a pattern and an embedding of $J^+$ *before* exposing any priorities. The probability that its $r$ used roots have the prescribed ordered labels is $(2m)^{-r}$. Conditional on this root event, each step of the pattern specifies one candidate occurrence in a fixed potential call tree: its focus edge and third vertex determine its triangle. If these specifications cannot define distinct required children under the query rule, their visitation probability is zero and they can be discarded. Otherwise, write $u_y$ for the priority of the selected child occurrence corresponding to each of the $\ell$ non-root calls $y$. These are distinct independent uniform coordinates of the unfolding.

Set <a id="eq:visitation-weights"></a>$$\label{eq:visitation-weights}
 b(t)=q(t)^2=\frac{1}{1+2Dt},\qquad
 C_D=1+2D,\qquad
 \mu=\int_0^1b(t)\,dt=\frac{\log(1+2D)}{2D}.$$

<a id="stmt-lem-visitation"></a>
<div id="lem:visitation" class="lemma">

<a id="eq:visitation-bound"></a>
**Lemma 13** (Visiting an embedded pattern). *Conditional on the prescribed ordered root labels, the probability that all calls in the embedded pattern are visited is at most $$\label{eq:visitation-bound}
 C_D^3\frac{(2\mu)^\ell}{h!\,\ell_1!\,\ell_2!}.$$ This includes zero-length segments, with the convention $0!=1$.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-visitation"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-visitation</summary>

*Proof.* Condition only on the selected priority coordinates $(u_y)$, not on actual visitation, return values, or previously exposed lists. For all required calls to be visited, these priorities must decrease along every required path.

<a id="eq:necessary-offpattern"></a>
Consider a required non-root call $x$ that has one or two required children. Its focus is a pair $\{f,g\}$, and its omitted parent triangle is $T$. Let $t_x$ be the largest priority of its required children. Define $O_x$ prospectively to be the event that every off-pattern candidate at $x$ with priority below $t_x$ has a child query returning false, when that subtree is evaluated hypothetically. Reaching all the required children necessitates $O_x$. In particular, $$\label{eq:necessary-offpattern}
 \{\text{all required calls are visited}\}
 \subseteq
 \{\text{priorities decrease along the required paths}\}
 \cap\bigcap_x O_x.$$ If a required call has two required children, reaching the later child also requires the earlier selected child to return false. We discard this additional condition in <a href="#eq:necessary-offpattern" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:necessary-offpattern">[eq:necessary-offpattern]</a>.

Each $O_x$ depends only on subtrees rooted at off-pattern children of $x$. These subtrees contain none of the selected priority coordinates and are disjoint for different required calls. In particular, when one required call is an ancestor of another, the path between them starts through a selected child, so it does not enter an off-pattern subtree. The events $O_x$ are therefore independent conditional on $(u_y)$. This assertion concerns the prospective independent unfolding.

<a id="eq:offpattern-bound"></a>
For an off-pattern candidate $S$ attached through focus edge $e$, the probability that it is not a successful child before $t_x$ is exactly $1-I_{e,S}(t_x)$. By the product identities of Proposition <a href="#prop:products" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:products">7</a>, <a id="eq:offpattern-product"></a>$$\label{eq:offpattern-product}
 \mathbb P(O_x\mid (u_y))
 =\frac{q_{f,T}(t_x)q_{g,T}(t_x)}
 {\displaystyle\prod_{S\text{ selected at }x}
       (1-I_{e(S),S}(t_x))},$$ where $e(S)$ is the unique focus edge contained in $S$. The denominator removes the one or two selected candidate factors from the complete product. The product estimate <a href="#eq:off-pattern-bound" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:off-pattern-bound">[eq:off-pattern-bound]</a> therefore gives, uniformly in the types and in $t_x\in[0,1]$, $$\label{eq:offpattern-bound}
 \mathbb P(O_x\mid (u_y))\leq 2b(t_x)$$ for all sufficiently large $n$.

<a id="eq:conditional-visitation"></a>
We next distribute these factors among the selected child priorities. At a call with one required child of priority $u$, the bound is $2b(u)$. At a call with two required children of priorities $u,v$, we use $$\frac{b(\max\{u,v\})}{b(u)b(v)}
 =\frac{1}{b(\min\{u,v\})}\leq C_D.$$ There is at most one such non-root fork. We ignore the off-pattern requirements at roots entirely. There are at most two required child steps leaving roots in total, and inserting their missing factors $b(u)\geq C_D^{-1}$ costs at most $C_D^2$. The number of non-root calls with required children is at most $\ell$. Combining independence, <a href="#eq:necessary-offpattern" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:necessary-offpattern">[eq:necessary-offpattern]</a>, and <a href="#eq:offpattern-bound" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:offpattern-bound">[eq:offpattern-bound]</a>, we obtain the conditional bound $$\label{eq:conditional-visitation}
 C_D^3 2^\ell\prod_y b(u_y)$$ when the priorities decrease along the required paths, and zero otherwise. Every selected priority now carries one factor $b$. This also covers root marks and empty arms, which introduce no new priority coordinates.

The selected priorities have joint Lebesgue density one. In integrating <a href="#eq:conditional-visitation" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:conditional-visitation">[eq:conditional-visitation]</a>, retain the order constraints within the trunk and within each arm, but discard constraints joining different segments. For a segment of length $j$, symmetry of the product integrand gives $$\int_{1>u_1>\cdots>u_j>0}\prod_{i=1}^j b(u_i)\,du_1\cdots du_j
 =\frac{\mu^j}{j!}.$$ The three segments use disjoint coordinates. Their contributions multiply, proving <a href="#eq:visitation-bound" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:visitation-bound">[eq:visitation-bound]</a>. Priority ties have probability zero. ◻

</details>

<a id="pm-h-29"></a>
#### Summing all witnesses

<a id="stmt-prop-collision"></a>
<div id="prop:collision" class="proposition">

<a id="eq:collision-bound"></a>
**Proposition 14** (Uniform collision bound). *Fix $k\in\{1,2\}$ and sample $k$ independent uniform oriented edges of $G$, with replacement. Execute the independent root queries at threshold $1$, executing every root regardless of earlier outputs. Let $\mathcal C_k$ be the event that an actual triangle type is repeated among all exposed candidate lists, including candidates not traversed. Then $$\label{eq:collision-bound}
 \mathbb P_{\mathrm{ind}}(\mathcal C_k)
 =O(D^{15-B}+n^{-1})
 =O(D^{-35}+n^{-1})
 =o(D^{-1}).$$ The bound is uniform over the graphs in Proposition <a href="#prop:prefix" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:prefix">[prop:prefix]</a>. Consequently, by Lemma <a href="#lem:coupling" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:coupling">10</a>, the probabilities that all root queries return true in the finite and independent experiments differ by at most the same bound.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-prop-collision"><summary>Proof of stmt-prop-collision</summary>

*Proof.* Lemma <a href="#lem:witness" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:witness">11</a> reduces a collision to initial root overlap, which has probability $O(n^{-1})$, or to an injectively labeled marked path union with an extra edge. For each fixed length triple, multiply the pattern count <a href="#eq:pattern-count" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:pattern-count">[eq:pattern-count]</a>, the embedding bound <a href="#eq:embedding-bound" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:embedding-bound">[eq:embedding-bound]</a>, the root-matching probability $(2m)^{-r}$, and the visitation bound <a href="#eq:visitation-bound" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:visitation-bound">[eq:visitation-bound]</a>. The result is at most an absolute constant times $$D^{-B}C_D^3
 \frac{(8D\mu)^{h+\ell_1+\ell_2}}{h!\,\ell_1!\,\ell_2!}.$$ For two-tree patterns $h=0$; allowing all triples of nonnegative integers only enlarges the sum. A union bound over patterns and embeddings therefore bounds the witness probability by $$\begin{aligned}
 O\left(D^{-B}C_D^3
   \sum_{h,\ell_1,\ell_2\geq0}
   \frac{(8D\mu)^{h+\ell_1+\ell_2}}{h!\,\ell_1!\,\ell_2!}\right)
 &=O\left(D^{-B}C_D^3\exp(24D\mu)\right)\\
 &=O\left(D^{-B}C_D^{15}\right)
  =O(D^{15-B}).
\end{aligned}$$ All terms are nonnegative; the countable union bound applies to the almost surely finite evaluations established in Section <a href="#sec:queries" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:queries">3</a>. The identity $24D\mu=12\log C_D$ accounts for the exponent $15$. Finally, $B=50$ and $D\sim n^{1/1000}$ imply $D^{-35}+n^{-1}=o(D^{-1})$, proving <a href="#eq:collision-bound" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:collision-bound">[eq:collision-bound]</a>. ◻

</details>

We have thus compared the finite continuation with independent root queries at an error smaller than their two-root success probability. The first and second terminal moments can now be read directly from these root-query probabilities.

<a id="sec:moments"></a>
### Terminal moments and the sharp constant

We finish by converting the query estimates into moments and then removing the conditioning on the prefix. Both steps use the uniformity of all preceding bounds.

<a id="eq:finite-moments"></a>
Fix a good prefix, and choose $k\in\{1,2\}$ oriented root edges independently and uniformly with replacement from the $2m$ oriented edges of $G$. The orientations specify the formal roots and do not affect the query outputs. In the finite-priority model, each query at threshold one tests survival of its root edge in the same terminal graph. Conditional on that graph, its surviving edge fraction is $F_n/m$, and the sampled roots are independent. Consequently $$\label{eq:finite-moments}
 \mathbb P(\text{all $k$ finite root queries return true}\mid\text{prefix})
       =\mathbb E\left[\left(\frac{F_n}{m}\right)^k
                        \mathrel{\Big|}\text{prefix}\right].$$ This identity includes the possibility of repeated sampled edges. Removing such samples would replace the ordinary second moment by a different expression.

<a id="eq:unfolded-moments"></a>
In the independent unfolding, the trees of the $k$ roots are independent, even when their edge types coincide. Averaging over the roots gives $$\label{eq:unfolded-moments}
 \mathbb P(\text{all $k$ independent root queries return true}\mid\text{prefix})
       =\left(\frac1m\sum_{e\in\mathcal E}q_e(1)\right)^k
       =(1+o(1))q(1)^k,$$ by Proposition <a href="#prop:unfolding" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:unfolding">9</a>. Lemma <a href="#lem:coupling" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:coupling">10</a> and Proposition <a href="#prop:collision" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:collision">14</a> bound the difference between <a href="#eq:finite-moments" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:finite-moments">[eq:finite-moments]</a> and <a href="#eq:unfolded-moments" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:unfolded-moments">[eq:unfolded-moments]</a> by $$\Delta_n=O(D^{-35}+n^{-1}).$$ Since $q(1)^2=(1+2D)^{-1}$, <a id="eq:relative-error"></a>$$\label{eq:relative-error}
 \frac{\Delta_n}{q(1)^2}
    =O(D^{-34}+D/n)=o(1).$$ This also controls $\Delta_n/q(1)$, as $q(1)\le1$.

<a id="eq:conditional-l2"></a>
Set $Y_n=F_n/(mq(1))$, using the deterministic $m$ and $D$ from <a href="#eq:prefix-time" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:prefix-time">[eq:prefix-time]</a>. The two moment identities give, uniformly over all good prefixes, $$\mathbb E[Y_n\mid\text{prefix}]=1+o(1),\qquad
 \mathbb E[Y_n^2\mid\text{prefix}]=1+o(1).$$ Subtracting twice the first identity from the second and adding one yields $$\label{eq:conditional-l2}
 \mathbb E[(Y_n-1)^2\mid\text{prefix}]=o(1)
 \quad\text{uniformly on }\mathcal G_n.$$

The normalization has the exact form <a id="eq:constant"></a>$$\label{eq:constant}
 a_n:=\frac{mq(1)}{n^{3/2}}
     =\frac{\sqrt D}{2\sqrt{1+2D}}
     =\frac1{2\sqrt{2+1/D}}
     \longrightarrow a:=\frac1{2\sqrt2}.$$ In particular $a_n$ is bounded below by a positive constant for large $n$. Regardless of the prefix, $0\le F_n\le\binom n2\le n^2/2$. It follows that $(Y_n-1)^2\le Cn$ deterministically. The bad-prefix contribution therefore satisfies $$\mathbb E[(Y_n-1)^2\mathbf1_{\mathcal G_n^c}]
      \le Cn\mathbb P(\mathcal G_n^c)=o(1).$$ On $\mathcal G_n$, condition on the complete prefix and use <a href="#eq:conditional-l2" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:conditional-l2">[eq:conditional-l2]</a>. We conclude that $$\mathbb E[(Y_n-1)^2]\longrightarrow0.$$ Finally, $$\mathbb E\left[\left(\frac{F_n}{n^{3/2}}-a\right)^2\right]
 \le2a_n^2\mathbb E[(Y_n-1)^2]+2(a_n-a)^2\longrightarrow0.$$ Markov’s inequality gives the convergence in probability in Theorem <a href="#thm:main" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:main">1</a>; Cauchy–Schwarz gives the stated expectation limit. This completes the proof.
