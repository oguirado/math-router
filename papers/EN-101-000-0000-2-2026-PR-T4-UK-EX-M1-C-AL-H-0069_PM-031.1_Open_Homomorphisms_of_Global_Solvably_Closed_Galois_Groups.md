## 1. Abstract and result contract

> We prove Uchida's conjecture on open homomorphisms of Galois groups. Every continuous open homomorphism between Galois groups of solvably closed Galois extensions of number fields is induced by a unique equivariant field embedding in the opposite direction. No restriction on the kernel or additional compatibility hypothesis is required.

- **claim-thm-main** (theorem, [source](#stmt-thm-main)): Let $F_1,F_2$ be number fields, and let $E_i/F_i$ be possibly infinite solvably closed Galois extensions. Every continuous open homomorphism $$\alpha:\mathop{\mathrm{Gal}}(E_1/F_1)\longrightarrow\mathop{\mathrm{Gal}}(E_2/F_2)$$ is induced by a unique field embedding $j:E_2\hookrightarrow E_1$.
  - Explanation: The open Galois homomorphism determines a unique equivariant embedding in the opposite direction.
- **claim-lem-solvable-closure** (theorem, [source](#stmt-lem-solvable-closure)): Every solvably closed algebraic extension $E$ of $\mathbb Q$ contains $\mathbb Q^s$. The field $\mathbb Q^s$ is solvably closed. For every number field $M\subset\mathbb Q^s$, the extension $\mathbb Q^s/M$ is the maximal prosolvable extension of $M$.
  - Explanation: Solvable closure contains the maximal prosolvable extension and is compatible with intermediate number fields.
- **claim-thm-uchida-isomorphism** (theorem, [source](#stmt-thm-uchida-isomorphism)): Let $E_i/F_i$ be solvably closed Galois extensions of number fields. Every isomorphism $\theta:\mathop{\mathrm{Gal}}(E_1/F_1)\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathop{\mathrm{Gal}}(E_2/F_2)$ is induced by a unique field isomorphism $j:E_2\xrightarrow{\sim}E_1$, with $g j=j\theta(g)$ for all $g$.
  - Explanation: An isomorphism of the specified Galois groups determines a unique field isomorphism in the opposite direction.
- **claim-lem-centralizer** (theorem, [source](#stmt-lem-centralizer)): The centralizer in $P$ of any open subgroup of $P$ is trivial.
  - Explanation: Uniqueness of the implementing field automorphism forces an open subgroup to have trivial centralizer.
- **claim-thm-local-correspondence** (theorem, [source](#stmt-thm-local-correspondence)): Let $M$ be a number field, let $N\subset\mathbb Q^s$ be a number field, and let $\varphi:G_M\to P_N$ be open. Fix an odd prime $\ell$. Every prime $v\nmid\ell$ active at $\ell$ determines a unique prime $w\nmid\ell$ of $N$ for which, with suitable prolongations, $$\varphi(D_v)\subset D_w.$$ Moreover, $\varphi$ is injective on a pro-$\ell$ Sylow subgroup of $I_v$, and sends this subgroup into $I_w$. All but finitely many primes of $N$ arise from such active primes of $M$.
  - Explanation: Surviving auxiliary inertia determines a finite target prime and gives cofinite target coverage for a fixed auxiliary prime.
- **claim-thm-hoshi** (theorem, [source](#stmt-thm-hoshi)): Let $E_i/F_i$ be solvably closed Galois extensions of number fields, and let $\alpha:\mathop{\mathrm{Gal}}(E_1/F_1)\to\mathop{\mathrm{Gal}}(E_2/F_2)$ be open. There exists an equivariant field embedding $E_2\hookrightarrow E_1$ if and only if $$\chi_{E_2/F_2}\circ\alpha=\chi_{E_1/F_1} \quad\text{in }\widehat{\mathbb Z}^{\times}.$$
  - Explanation: Full cyclotomic compatibility characterizes existence of the equivariant field embedding.
- **claim-lem-norm-identity** (theorem, [source](#stmt-lem-norm-identity)): For an active pair $(v,w)$ at an odd prime $\ell$, $$\label{eq:norm-identity} (Nv)^{b(g)}=(Nw)^{a(g)}\quad\text{in }\mathbb Z_\ell^\times \qquad(g\in D_v).$$ Powers by profinite integers are interpreted by continuous powering in the compact group $\mathbb Z_\ell^\times$.
  - Explanation: Tame conjugation identifies the source and target norm powers at an active pair.
- **claim-lem-log-rank** (theorem, [source](#stmt-lem-log-rank)): Let $n,m\geq1$, let $E$ be a number field embedded in a finite extension $K/\mathbb Q_\ell$, and let $$u_i=(u_{i1},\ldots,u_{in})\in(E^\times)^n, \qquad 1\leq i\leq m,$$ have algebraic-integer coordinates that are units in $K$. Suppose that for every $a=(a_1,\ldots,a_m)\in\mathbb Z^m\setminus\{0\}$, the coordinates of $u^a:=\prod_{i=1}^m u_i^{a_i}$ are multiplicatively independent modulo roots of unity. If $m>n(n-1)$, then $$\operatorname{span}_K\{\log u_1,\ldots,\log u_m\}=K^n.$$
  - Explanation: Multiplicative independence forces full logarithmic rank once the displayed threshold is exceeded.
- **claim-prop-character-detection** (theorem, [source](#stmt-prop-character-detection)): Let $M$ be a number field, let $\ell$ be a rational prime, and let $x\in X_{\ell}(M)$.* 1. *If $x(\mathop{\mathrm{Fr}}_v)=0$ at infinitely many primes $v\nmid\ell$ of absolute residue degree one, then $x=0$.* 2. *Suppose that $M/\mathbb Q$ is Galois, and fix $\delta\in\mathop{\mathrm{Gal}}(M/\mathbb Q)$. If $x(\mathop{\mathrm{Fr}}_v)=0$ at infinitely many primes $v\nmid\ell$ lying over rational primes unramified in $M$ whose Frobenius element at $v$ in $\mathop{\mathrm{Gal}}(M/\mathbb Q)$ is $\delta$, then $E_{\langle\delta\rangle}x=0$.* 3. *If $\mathop{\mathrm{cor}}_{M/\mathbb Q}x\ne0$, then $x(\mathop{\mathrm{Fr}}_v)=0$ at only finitely many primes $v\nmid\ell$, with no restriction on their residue degrees.
  - Explanation: Infinite Frobenius vanishing detects additive characters, with averaging and corestriction variants.
- **claim-prop-signed-representation** (theorem, [source](#stmt-prop-signed-representation)): Let $M/\mathbb Q$ be a finite, totally imaginary Galois extension, put $R=\mathop{\mathrm{Gal}}(M/\mathbb Q)$, and let $c\in R$ be a complex conjugation. For every rational prime $\ell$, there are $R$-equivariant embeddings $$\label{eq:signed-representation} W_M:=\mathop{\mathrm{Ind}}_{\langle c\rangle}^{R}(\mathrm{sign}) \lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow X_{\ell}(M) \lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow\mathbb Q_\ell[R],$$ where $\mathrm{sign}(c)=-1$. In particular, the action of $R$ on $X_{\ell}(M)$ is faithful.
  - Explanation: The induced signed representation embeds in the character space and supplies a faithful group action.
- **claim-lem-normalizer-extension** (theorem, [source](#stmt-lem-normalizer-extension)): There is a unique continuous open homomorphism $$\beta:T\longrightarrow P$$ whose restriction to $U$ is $\alpha_0\|_U$.
  - Explanation: The restricted map extends uniquely to the normalizer of its kernel.
- **claim-lem-quotient-amplification** (construction, [source](#stmt-lem-quotient-amplification)): Fix an odd prime $\ell$ and a finite quotient $R_0$ of $P$. There is a finite Galois extension $L/\mathbb Q$ in $\mathbb Q^s$, totally imaginary, such that the quotient $R=\mathop{\mathrm{Gal}}(L/\mathbb Q)$ dominates $R_0$ and has the following property. For every $\gamma\in R$, with $C=\langle\gamma\rangle$, and every proper subgroup $D<C$, there is $x\in X_{\ell}(L)$ satisfying $$\label{eq:separating-character} x\in X_{\ell}(L)^{D},\qquad E_Cx=0,\qquad x\notin\sum_{i=1}^rX_{\ell}(L)^{H_i}.$$ Here each $H_i$ acts through its image in $R$.
  - Explanation: Regular Kummer modules enlarge a finite quotient so that characters separate proper cyclic subgroups while escaping forbidden invariant spaces.
- **claim-prop-frobenius-generation** (theorem, [source](#stmt-prop-frobenius-generation)): Fix an odd prime $\ell$, a finite quotient $R_0$ of $P$, and $\gamma_0\in R_0$. There are a quotient $$P\twoheadrightarrow R=\mathop{\mathrm{Gal}}(L/\mathbb Q)\twoheadrightarrow R_0$$ and a lift $\gamma\in R$ of $\gamma_0$ with the following property. There are infinitely many $\ell$-active primes $v$ of $k$, over distinct rational primes $p\ne\ell$, for which compatible prolongations can be chosen so that:* 1. *the corresponding target rational prime $q\ne\ell$ is unramified in $L$, and the target prolongation restricts to a prime $w'$ of $L$ whose Frobenius in $R$ is $\gamma$;* 2. *the image in $R$ of an arithmetic Frobenius lift at $v$ generates $\langle\gamma\rangle$.
  - Explanation: Infinitely many active source Frobenius images generate the specified target cyclic subgroup in an enlarged quotient.
- **claim-thm-normalized-map** (theorem, [source](#stmt-thm-normalized-map)): The extension $\beta:G_k\to P$ is surjective. Put $$m_0=[k:\mathbb Q]!,\qquad \mathcal L=\{\ell:\ell\text{ is an odd prime and } \ell\equiv1\pmod{m_0}\}.$$ The set $\mathcal L$ is infinite, and for every $\ell\in\mathcal L$, $$\label{eq:cyclotomic-logarithms} \log\chi_\ell\circ\beta=\log\chi_{k,\ell} \quad\text{as additive characters on }G_k.$$ Here $\chi_\ell$ is the $\ell$-adic cyclotomic character on $P$, and $\chi_{k,\ell}$ is the ordinary cyclotomic character on $G_k$.
  - Explanation: The normalized map is surjective and preserves cyclotomic logarithms for the specified infinite congruence class.
- **claim-lem-finite-fibers** (theorem, [source](#stmt-lem-finite-fibers)): For all but finitely many primes $w$ of $B$ above rational primes split in $B$, the set $\mathcal V_w$ is finite.
  - Explanation: Almost every split target prime has finitely many possible active source partners across the auxiliary primes.
- **claim-lem-kummer-unramified** (theorem, [source](#stmt-lem-kummer-unramified)): Let $F$ be a number field, let $\ell$ be a rational prime, and let $S$ be a finite set of places of $F$ containing all places above $\ell$ and all archimedean places. Then Kummer theory induces a natural isomorphism $$\label{eq:unramified-kummer} H^1_{\mathrm{cont}}(G_F,\mathbb Q_\ell(1)) _{\text{unramified outside }S} \simeq \mathcal O_{F,S}^{\times}\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathbb Q_\ell.$$ It is equivariant for every group of field automorphisms of $F$ preserving $S$, with the natural action on cohomology combining conjugation on $G_F$ and the action on the Tate twist.
  - Explanation: Unramified rational Kummer classes identify naturally and equivariantly with rationalized units outside the specified places.
- **claim-prop-uniform-missed-primes** (bound, [source](#stmt-prop-uniform-missed-primes)): For every $\ell\in\mathcal L$, $$\#\{w\in\mathcal M_\ell:w\nmid\ell\}\le 2[K:\mathbb Q], \qquad \#\mathcal M_\ell\le 2[K:\mathbb Q]+2.$$
  - Explanation: The number of target primes missed by the partial correspondence has a bound independent of the auxiliary prime.
- **claim-lem-power-separation** (construction, [source](#stmt-lem-power-separation)): Let $m,d\ge1$, let $q_1,\ldots,q_n$ be distinct rational primes, and for each $j$ let $\mathcal P_j$ be a finite set of rational primes not containing $q_j$. Given any finite set of rational primes to avoid, there are infinitely many odd primes $\ell\equiv1\pmod m$ outside that set and all the displayed prime sets such that $$p^f\bmod\ell\notin\langle q_j\bmod\ell\rangle \quad (1\le j\le n,\ p\in\mathcal P_j,\ 1\le f\le d).$$ The sets $\mathcal P_j$ may overlap, and may contain $q_i$ for $i\ne j$.
  - Explanation: Kummer extensions and Chebotarev provide simultaneous power-residue separation even when the prime lists overlap.
- **claim-prop-same-characteristic** (theorem, [source](#stmt-prop-same-characteristic)): For all but finitely many primes $w$ of $B$ above rational primes $q$ split in $B$, there exist a prime $v$ of $K$ above the same $q$ and an auxiliary prime $\ell\in\mathcal L$ for which $(v,w)$ is an active pair for $\beta_B$.
  - Explanation: Almost every split target prime has an active source partner of the same rational residue characteristic.
- **claim-prop-identify-normalized** (theorem, [source](#stmt-prop-identify-normalized)): There is an element $a\in P$ such that $\beta=\mathop{\mathrm{Ad}}(a)\circ\sigma$ on $T$.
  - Explanation: Character pullbacks and compactness identify the normalized map with restriction up to a single conjugation.
- **claim-thm-prosolvable-rigidity** (theorem, [source](#stmt-thm-prosolvable-rigidity)): Let $F$ be a number field in $\overline{\mathbb Q}$. Every continuous open homomorphism $\varphi:G_F\to P$ is conjugate in $P$ to ordinary restriction.
  - Explanation: Every continuous open map from the specified absolute Galois group to the maximal prosolvable Galois group is restriction up to conjugation.

## 2. Definitions, validity and bounds

- **solvably closed** ([source](#sec:introduction)): An algebraic extension of a number field is *solvably closed* if it has no nontrivial abelian algebraic extension.
- **induced Galois homomorphism** ([source](#sec:introduction)): A field embedding $j:E_2\hookrightarrow E_1$ *induces* a homomorphism $\alpha:G_1\to G_2$ if $$g\circ j=j\circ\alpha(g)\qquad(g\in G_1).
- **absolute and prosolvable Galois groups** ([source](#pm-h-6)): For a number field $M\subset\overline{\mathbb Q}$ put $G_M=\mathop{\mathrm{Gal}}(\overline{\mathbb Q}/M)$, and put $$\mathcal G=G_{\mathbb Q},\qquad P=\mathop{\mathrm{Gal}}(\mathbb Q^s/\mathbb Q),$$ where $\mathbb Q^s\subset\overline{\mathbb Q}$ is the maximal prosolvable extension of $\mathbb Q$.
- **intermediate prosolvable Galois group** ([source](#pm-h-6)): For a number field $M\subset\mathbb Q^s$ write $P_M=\mathop{\mathrm{Gal}}(\mathbb Q^s/M)$.
- **open homomorphism** ([source](#pm-h-6)): An open homomorphism is a homomorphism with open image: a continuous surjection between profinite groups is a quotient map and is open.
- **active prime** ([source](#pm-h-7)): For a homomorphism $\varphi:G_M\to P_N$, an odd prime $\ell$, and a prime $v\nmid\ell$ of $M$, say that $v$ is *active at $\ell$* if $\varphi$ is nontrivial on a pro-$\ell$ Sylow subgroup of $I_v$.
- **additive character space** ([source](#pm-h-11)): For a number field $M\subset\overline{\mathbb Q}$, write $$X_{\ell}(M):=\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathrm{cont}}(G_M,\mathbb Q_\ell), \qquad G_M=\mathop{\mathrm{Gal}}(\overline{\mathbb Q}/M),$$ where $\mathbb Q_\ell$ is an additive group with trivial Galois action.
- **invariant projector** ([source](#pm-h-11)): For a subgroup $J\subseteq R$, write $$E_J=\frac1{\|J\|}\sum_{g\in J}g$$ for the projector onto the $J$-invariants.
- **possible active partners** ([source](#sec:matching)): For a prime $w$ of $B$, define $$\mathcal V_w= \{v:\text{$(v,w)$ is an active pair at some $\ell\in\mathcal L$}\}.

- **step-prop-frobenius-generation** [claim](#stmt-prop-frobenius-generation) / [proof](#proof-stmt-prop-frobenius-generation): Chebotarev, separating characters, Frobenius detection, and equivariant projection. -> Infinitely many active source Frobenius images generate the specified target cyclic subgroup in an enlarged quotient.
- **step-thm-normalized-map** [claim](#stmt-thm-normalized-map) / [proof](#proof-stmt-thm-normalized-map): Finite quotient surjectivity and residue-degree forcing by the cyclotomic congruence. -> The normalized map is surjective and preserves cyclotomic logarithms for the specified infinite congruence class.
- **step-lem-finite-fibers** [claim](#stmt-lem-finite-fibers) / [proof](#proof-stmt-lem-finite-fibers): A signed character and nonzero corestriction bound the zero-prime set. -> Almost every split target prime has finitely many possible active source partners across the auxiliary primes.
- **step-lem-kummer-unramified** [claim](#stmt-lem-kummer-unramified) / [proof](#proof-stmt-lem-kummer-unramified): Integral Kummer completion and finite ideal class groups. -> Unramified rational Kummer classes identify naturally and equivariantly with rationalized units outside the specified places.
- **step-prop-uniform-missed-primes** [claim](#stmt-prop-uniform-missed-primes) / [proof](#proof-stmt-prop-uniform-missed-primes): Independent missed-prime classes in a single unit-character component. -> The number of target primes missed by the partial correspondence has a bound independent of the auxiliary prime.

- **step-prop-same-characteristic** [claim](#stmt-prop-same-characteristic) / [proof](#proof-stmt-prop-same-characteristic): Finite fibers, simultaneous separation, and the uniform missed-prime bound. -> Almost every split target prime has an active source partner of the same rational residue characteristic.
- **step-prop-identify-normalized** [claim](#stmt-prop-identify-normalized) / [proof](#proof-stmt-prop-identify-normalized): Matched Frobenius evaluations, faithful character action, and compactness. -> Character pullbacks and compactness identify the normalized map with restriction up to a single conjugation.

## 4. Engineering Triptych

### Panel 1 - epistemic contract

- **equivariant-reconstruction**: A continuous open Galois homomorphism determines a unique field embedding in the opposite direction.
- **fixed-auxiliary-local-coverage**: Active source primes determine unique finite target primes with decomposition containment, and the target coverage is cofinite for each fixed odd auxiliary prime.
- **normalized-cyclotomic-logarithms**: The normalized extension is surjective and preserves cyclotomic logarithms at the auxiliary primes in the stated congruence class.
- **uniform-missed-prime-bound**: The number of missed target primes is bounded in terms of the degree of the field over the rationals, independently of the auxiliary prime.

### Panel 2 - negative knowledge

- **arbitrary-group-maps**: Reconstruct a field embedding from an arbitrary abstract group map. -> The reconstruction conclusion has no stated guarantee outside the specified Galois setting.
- **finite-quotient-certificate**: Certify the global embedding from agreement on a chosen finite quotient. -> A finite quotient does not establish equality in the entire profinite target.
- **logarithm-versus-full-character**: Substitute logarithmic compatibility for the full cyclotomic criterion. -> The logarithm kills finite torsion and does not alone identify the full cyclotomic character.
- **uniform-exceptional-set**: Assume a fixed local correspondence immediately gives a common exceptional set for all auxiliary primes. -> Cofinite coverage at a fixed auxiliary prime does not supply uniform coverage as that prime varies.
- **software-correctness-transfer**: Use Galois reconstruction to certify orchestration, security, or network behavior. -> No qualifying arithmetic model for those software operations is supplied.

### Panel 3 - reference implementation and oracle

Applicability: `not_applicable`. The field-embedding reconstruction concerns possibly infinite solvably closed extensions. The proof detects characters through infinitely many primes and obtains a simultaneous conjugation by compactness of the profinite target. It supplies no finite constructive procedure for computing the embedding; finite quotient comparisons alone do not certify this global conclusion.

## 6. Applicability limits and search

- **field-premises** (hypothesis): The reconstruction requires solvably closed Galois extensions of number fields. The result supplies no conclusion for arbitrary algebraic extensions or unrelated groups. Blocked use: ['Reconstruction without the stated field and Galois hypotheses.']
- **open-image** (hypothesis): Continuity and open image are hypotheses. Openness concerns the image, rather than injectivity or an unrestricted map between abstract groups. Blocked use: ['Applying reconstruction to a discontinuous map or a map whose image is not open.']
- **kernel-unrestricted** (edge_case): No injectivity or restriction on the kernel is required. Such a restriction would narrow the stated theorem without source support. Blocked use: ['Rejecting an otherwise qualifying homomorphism solely because its kernel is nontrivial.']
- **local-activity** (hypothesis): The local correspondence requires an odd auxiliary prime and nontrivial action on the indicated inertia subgroup. It cannot be applied to every source prime without checking activity. Blocked use: ['Assuming decomposition containment at inactive source primes.']
- **fixed-auxiliary-coverage** (edge_case): Cofinite target coverage at a fixed auxiliary prime does not itself give a uniform exceptional set as that prime varies. Blocked use: ['Interchanging exceptional sets across auxiliary primes using only the partial local correspondence.']
- **logarithm-versus-character** (approximation): Equality of cyclotomic logarithms leaves a finite torsion twist. It cannot alone replace the full cyclotomic compatibility required for an embedding. Blocked use: ['Inferring full cyclotomic compatibility directly from logarithmic equality.']
- **rank-hypothesis** (hypothesis): The logarithm-rank conclusion depends on multiplicative independence of the coordinates of every nontrivial integral product, modulo roots of unity. Merely choosing many vectors is insufficient. Blocked use: ['Using vector count alone to certify logarithmic spanning.']
- **infinite-detection** (nonconstructive): Character detection uses vanishing on infinitely many primes of absolute residue degree one. A finite sample of Frobenius evaluations does not certify that conclusion. Blocked use: ['Treating finite sampled evaluations as a certificate that a character vanishes globally.']
- **simultaneous-conjugation** (nonconstructive): The global conjugating element is obtained by compactness after comparisons in finite quotients. Checking selected finite quotients does not certify the simultaneous global equality. Blocked use: ['Certifying global reconstruction from finitely many quotient comparisons.']
- **coefficient-auxiliary-distinction** (edge_case): The coefficient prime is fixed in the final comparison while the auxiliary prime witnessing activity may vary. The proof does not require these primes to coincide. Blocked use: ['Requiring an active correspondence at the fixed coefficient prime as an additional premise.']
- **actual-unit-image** (formal_scope): The character-space argument uses the actual logarithmic image of the units and makes no assertion about its dimension. A separate maximal-rank hypothesis is not supplied. Blocked use: ['Inserting an unproved unit-logarithm rank assumption into the character-space description.']
- **finite-exceptions** (edge_case): The same-characteristic matching conclusion allows finitely many exceptions and varies the auxiliary prime with the pair. It is not a universal prime-by-prime correspondence. Blocked use: ['Claiming matching for every split target prime at a prescribed auxiliary prime.']
- **engineering-scope** (formal_scope): A concrete software model satisfying the field-extension and continuous open homomorphism premises is ABSENT. The theorem alone provides no guarantee for program effects, security, concurrency, storage, or network behavior. Blocked use: ['Using field-embedding uniqueness as a software correctness or performance guarantee.']
- **finite-algorithm-absent** (unknown): A finite executable construction of the global embedding, with termination and complexity guarantees, is ABSENT. Finite constructive subarguments do not supply such an algorithm for the whole theorem. Blocked use: ['Presenting the global reconstruction theorem as a finite computational oracle.']

No restriction on the kernel or additional compatibility hypothesis is required.

<a id="sec:introduction"></a>
### Introduction

An algebraic extension of a number field is *solvably closed* if it has no nontrivial abelian algebraic extension. Algebraic closures and maximal prosolvable extensions are examples. The isomorphism theorems of Neukirch and Uchida show that Galois groups of such fields retain remarkably precise arithmetic information. The corresponding question for open homomorphisms asks whether this reconstruction also recovers field embeddings.

Let $E_i/F_i$ be Galois extensions of number fields, with each $E_i$ solvably closed, and write $G_i=\mathop{\mathrm{Gal}}(E_i/F_i)$. A field embedding $j:E_2\hookrightarrow E_1$ *induces* a homomorphism $\alpha:G_1\to G_2$ if $$g\circ j=j\circ\alpha(g)\qquad(g\in G_1).$$ In particular, such an embedding sends $F_2$ into $F_1$. Uchida conjectured that every continuous homomorphism with open image arises in this way <a id="pm-cite-1"></a>[[uchida1981](#ref-uchida1981), p. 595]. We prove the conjecture in its full form.

<a id="stmt-thm-main"></a>
<div id="thm:main" class="theorem">

**Theorem 1**. *Let $F_1,F_2$ be number fields, and let $E_i/F_i$ be possibly infinite solvably closed Galois extensions. Every continuous open homomorphism $$\alpha:\mathop{\mathrm{Gal}}(E_1/F_1)\longrightarrow\mathop{\mathrm{Gal}}(E_2/F_2)$$ is induced by a unique field embedding $j:E_2\hookrightarrow E_1$.*

</div>

The kernel is unrestricted. The proof establishes the necessary arithmetic compatibility from openness itself.

<a id="pm-h-3"></a>
#### Historical context

The reconstruction of number fields from their Galois groups begins with Neukirch’s work on normal number fields and decomposition groups <a id="pm-cite-2"></a>[[neukirch1969a](#ref-neukirch1969a), [neukirch1969b](#ref-neukirch1969b)]. The passage to arbitrary number fields and prescribed group isomorphisms involved Uchida, Iwasawa, and Ikeda; their contributions are recorded in the contemporary accounts <a id="pm-cite-3"></a>[[uchida1976](#ref-uchida1976), [neukirch1977](#ref-neukirch1977)]. Uchida subsequently established the isomorphism theorem for arbitrary solvably closed Galois extensions of number fields <a id="pm-cite-4"></a>[[uchida1979](#ref-uchida1979)].

In the paper formulating the homomorphism conjecture, Uchida proved it when the source number field is $\mathbb Q$, proved uniqueness in general, and established existence under a suitable local condition on decomposition groups <a id="pm-cite-5"></a>[[uchida1981](#ref-uchida1981), Theorem 1, Proposition 2, and Theorem 2]. His local analysis also associates primes to one another whenever a chosen pro-$\ell$ inertia group survives under the homomorphism, for an odd auxiliary prime $\ell$. For each fixed auxiliary prime $\ell$, this partial correspondence reaches all but finitely many target primes <a id="pm-cite-6"></a>[[uchida1981](#ref-uchida1981), Proposition 1].

A later reduction of Hoshi identifies an exact arithmetic condition for existence: an open homomorphism is induced by a field embedding if and only if it preserves the full cyclotomic character <a id="pm-cite-7"></a>[[hoshi2025](#ref-hoshi2025), Theorem 3.4]. Hoshi’s subsequent work reformulates the unrestricted conjecture in terms of radicals and normality of the field fixed by a kernel <a id="pm-cite-8"></a>[[hoshi2026](#ref-hoshi2026), Theorem B]. Related work on finite solvable quotients includes the isomorphism theorem of Saïdi and Tamagawa <a id="pm-cite-9"></a>[[saiditamagawa2022](#ref-saiditamagawa2022)] and the cyclotomic-compatible homomorphism theorem of Mao and Saïdi <a id="pm-cite-10"></a>[[maosaidi2025](#ref-maosaidi2025)]. Our proof uses the cyclotomic criterion at its endpoint. The main task is to derive that criterion from the partial local correspondences, even though their domains and exceptional sets initially depend on $\ell$.

<a id="pm-h-4"></a>
#### The main ideas

Two arguments used below are useful beyond the final reduction. First, an additive $\ell$-adic character of the absolute Galois group of a number field is determined by its values on any infinite set of primes of absolute residue degree one. More precisely, a character vanishing at all those primes is zero. The logarithmic-rank input is a special case of the $\ell$-adic Waldschmidt–Masser theorem <a id="pm-cite-11"></a>[[waldschmidt1981](#ref-waldschmidt1981), [masser1981](#ref-masser1981), [dasgupta2023](#ref-dasgupta2023)]. We give a complete proof using Laurent’s interpolation-determinant method <a id="pm-cite-12"></a>[[laurent1991](#ref-laurent1991)] and a translate-and-degree zero estimate on a torus. Its variants for Frobenius averages and corestriction permit us to treat primes of arbitrary residue degree as well. These statements concern the actual unit-logarithm image and require no hypothesis on its rank.

Second, we refine the Kummer argument in Uchida’s proof of cofinite target coverage <a id="pm-cite-13"></a>[[uchida1981](#ref-uchida1981), Proposition 1]. After logarithmic cyclotomic compatibility has been established, the number of target primes missed by the partial correspondence can be bounded independently of $\ell$. A missed prime supplies a Kummer class. On killing a finite cyclotomic twist, these classes lie in one character component of an $S$-unit representation. The full extension degree may grow with $\ell$, but the multiplicity of that character is bounded by the number of orbits of $S$, hence by the degree of the fixed source field. This uniformity allows us to choose $\ell$ by simultaneous Kummer conditions and force enough corresponding primes to have the same rational residue characteristic.

Here is the route from these ingredients to Theorem <a href="#thm:main" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:main">1</a>. Put $P=\mathop{\mathrm{Gal}}(\mathbb Q^s/\mathbb Q)$, where $\mathbb Q^s$ is the maximal prosolvable extension of $\mathbb Q$. For a number field $F$, let $G_F$ denote its absolute Galois group. It suffices to identify every continuous open homomorphism $G_F\to P$ with ordinary restriction up to conjugation. Indeed, every solvably closed field contains $\mathbb Q^s$, and this identification will give the full cyclotomic compatibility required by Hoshi’s theorem.

We first restrict to a normal open subgroup of $G_{\mathbb Q}$ and extend the map to the normalizer of its kernel. Write this extension as $\beta:G_k\to P$. Following the regular-module constructions used by Uchida <a id="pm-cite-14"></a>[[uchida1979](#ref-uchida1979)], we enlarge finite quotients of $P$ by elementary abelian kernels consisting of many copies of a regular representation. Each quotient $R=\mathop{\mathrm{Gal}}(L/\mathbb Q)$ acts on the additive character space of $G_L$. Suitable characters distinguish a cyclic subgroup generated by a target Frobenius element from each of its proper subgroups. Comparing Frobenius averages proves that $\beta$ is surjective and preserves logarithmic cyclotomic characters for an infinite congruence class of auxiliary primes.

Next, pass to the preimage of $P_{\mathbb Q(i)}=\mathop{\mathrm{Gal}}(\mathbb Q^s/\mathbb Q(i))$. A sign character shows that, for almost every split target prime, only finitely many source primes can correspond to it as $\ell$ varies. The uniform Kummer bound then forces a correspondence in the same residue characteristic for almost every such target prime. Finally, use rational primes split completely in suitable finite extensions. Their additive Frobenius evaluations identify the two character pullbacks, one from $\beta$ and one from ordinary restriction. A faithful representation in the character space identifies the group maps modulo every finite quotient; compactness supplies a single conjugation.

Section <a href="#sec:characters" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:characters">3</a> develops the character tests. Section <a href="#sec:generation" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:generation">4</a> obtains surjectivity and logarithmic cyclotomic compatibility. Section <a href="#sec:matching" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:matching">5</a> proves the uniform bound and matches residue characteristics. <a id="sec:preliminaries"></a>
### Galois groups and partial local correspondences

We collect the anabelian inputs and the local calculation that will connect Frobenius elements with cyclotomic characters. The substantial inputs from Uchida and Hoshi are stated explicitly; the remaining arguments use standard global and local class field theory, Kummer theory, and Chebotarev’s theorem.

<a id="pm-h-6"></a>
#### Conventions and solvably closed fields

Fix an algebraic closure $\overline{\mathbb Q}$ of $\mathbb Q$. For a number field $M\subset\overline{\mathbb Q}$ put $G_M=\mathop{\mathrm{Gal}}(\overline{\mathbb Q}/M)$, and put $$\mathcal G=G_{\mathbb Q},\qquad
 P=\mathop{\mathrm{Gal}}(\mathbb Q^s/\mathbb Q),$$ where $\mathbb Q^s\subset\overline{\mathbb Q}$ is the maximal prosolvable extension of $\mathbb Q$. For a number field $M\subset\mathbb Q^s$ write $P_M=\mathop{\mathrm{Gal}}(\mathbb Q^s/M)$. All homomorphisms between profinite groups and all characters in this paper are continuous. An open homomorphism is a homomorphism with open image: a continuous surjection between profinite groups is a quotient map and is open.

We use finite primes unless infinity is explicitly mentioned. For a finite prime $v$, its absolute norm is $Nv$, and its absolute residue degree is $[\kappa(v):\mathbb F_p]$, where $p$ is its rational residue characteristic. Frobenius is arithmetic. A chosen prolongation of $v$ determines a decomposition group $D_v$ and inertia group $I_v$; a different prolongation conjugates both. Expressions involving several such groups always use specified compatible prolongations.

<a id="stmt-lem-solvable-closure"></a>
<div id="lem:solvable-closure" class="lemma">

**Lemma 2**. *Every solvably closed algebraic extension $E$ of $\mathbb Q$ contains $\mathbb Q^s$. The field $\mathbb Q^s$ is solvably closed. For every number field $M\subset\mathbb Q^s$, the extension $\mathbb Q^s/M$ is the maximal prosolvable extension of $M$.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-solvable-closure"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-solvable-closure</summary>

*Proof.* Since $E(\mu_n)/E$ is abelian, $E$ contains every root of unity. Adjoining an $n$th root of an element of $E$ then gives a cyclic extension, so $E$ is closed under radicals. Finite solvable Galois extensions can be built by towers of radical extensions after adjoining roots of unity; hence $\mathbb Q^s\subset E$.

The same tower argument shows that $\mathbb Q^s$ contains all roots of unity and is closed under radicals. Any nontrivial finite abelian extension would have a cyclic subextension of prime degree, which Kummer theory excludes. Any nontrivial abelian algebraic extension would have a nontrivial finite abelian subextension. Thus $\mathbb Q^s$ is solvably closed.

Finally, a finite solvable Galois extension of $M$ lies in a finite solvable Galois extension of $\mathbb Q$: first take a solvable normal closure of $M/\mathbb Q$ inside $\mathbb Q^s$, and then the compositum of the finitely many conjugate extensions. The group of this compositum over that normal closure embeds in a product of solvable groups. This proves the last assertion. ◻

</details>

In particular, every additive $\ell$-adic character of $G_M$ factors through $P_M$ when $M\subset\mathbb Q^s$. For any solvably closed Galois extension $E/F$, the action on roots of unity defines its cyclotomic character $$\chi_{E/F}:\mathop{\mathrm{Gal}}(E/F)\longrightarrow\widehat{\mathbb Z}^{\times}.$$ We use $\chi_{M,\ell}$ for the usual $\ell$-adic character of $G_M$, and $\chi_\ell$ for that of $P$. The $\ell$-adic logarithm is normalized to vanish on the finite torsion subgroup of $\mathbb Z_\ell^\times$.

<a id="pm-h-7"></a>
#### The anabelian inputs

The first input permits a homomorphism to be extended from an open subgroup to the normalizer of its kernel.

<a id="stmt-thm-uchida-isomorphism"></a>
<div id="thm:uchida-isomorphism" class="theorem">

**Theorem 3** (Uchida). *Let $E_i/F_i$ be solvably closed Galois extensions of number fields. Every isomorphism $\theta:\mathop{\mathrm{Gal}}(E_1/F_1)\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathop{\mathrm{Gal}}(E_2/F_2)$ is induced by a unique field isomorphism $j:E_2\xrightarrow{\sim}E_1$, with $g j=j\theta(g)$ for all $g$.*

</div>

This is <a id="pm-cite-15"></a>[[uchida1979](#ref-uchida1979), Theorem]. One consequence we shall use twice is the following.

<a id="stmt-lem-centralizer"></a>
<div id="lem:centralizer" class="lemma">

**Lemma 4**. *The centralizer in $P$ of any open subgroup of $P$ is trivial.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-centralizer"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-centralizer</summary>

*Proof.* An open subgroup is $P_M$ for a number field $M\subset\mathbb Q^s$. An element centralizing $P_M$ is a field automorphism of $\mathbb Q^s$ implementing the identity automorphism of $P_M$. The identity field map also implements it, so uniqueness in Theorem <a href="#thm:uchida-isomorphism" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:uchida-isomorphism">3</a> gives the assertion. ◻

</details>

Here is the local input, in the precise setting in which we need it. For a homomorphism $\varphi:G_M\to P_N$, an odd prime $\ell$, and a prime $v\nmid\ell$ of $M$, say that $v$ is *active at $\ell$* if $\varphi$ is nontrivial on a pro-$\ell$ Sylow subgroup of $I_v$. The condition is independent of the chosen Sylow subgroup and prolongation, since such choices are conjugate.

<a id="stmt-thm-local-correspondence"></a>
<div id="thm:local-correspondence" class="theorem">

**Theorem 5** (Uchida’s partial local correspondence). *Let $M$ be a number field, let $N\subset\mathbb Q^s$ be a number field, and let $\varphi:G_M\to P_N$ be open. Fix an odd prime $\ell$. Every prime $v\nmid\ell$ active at $\ell$ determines a unique prime $w\nmid\ell$ of $N$ for which, with suitable prolongations, $$\varphi(D_v)\subset D_w.$$ Moreover, $\varphi$ is injective on a pro-$\ell$ Sylow subgroup of $I_v$, and sends this subgroup into $I_w$. All but finitely many primes of $N$ arise from such active primes of $M$.*

</div>

This is the odd-prime case of the trichotomy in <a id="pm-cite-16"></a>[[uchida1981](#ref-uchida1981), 1, pp. 595–596], together with its Proposition 1. In that trichotomy, nontrivial pro-$\ell$ inertia is case (ii); case (i) kills that inertia, and the torsion case (iii) requires $\ell=2$. The open-image hypothesis, rather than surjectivity onto $P_N$, suffices. We will apply the theorem after restricting to smaller open subgroups as well. The cofinite target coverage in this theorem is for a fixed $\ell$; its exceptional set is not asserted to be uniform in $\ell$.

The uniqueness assertion concerns primes of the finite target field $N$. When restricting a map to smaller open subgroups, we will intersect a containing target decomposition group with $P_{N'}$ for a finite extension $N'/N$ inside $\mathbb Q^s$, and then use uniqueness at the $N'$-level. Proposition <a href="#prop:frobenius-generation" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:frobenius-generation">13</a> carries out this comparison with explicit source and target fields; no uniqueness of infinite prolongations is required.

The final input converts cyclotomic compatibility into the required field embedding.

<a id="stmt-thm-hoshi"></a>
<div id="thm:hoshi" class="theorem">

**Theorem 6** (Hoshi). *Let $E_i/F_i$ be solvably closed Galois extensions of number fields, and let $\alpha:\mathop{\mathrm{Gal}}(E_1/F_1)\to\mathop{\mathrm{Gal}}(E_2/F_2)$ be open. There exists an equivariant field embedding $E_2\hookrightarrow E_1$ if and only if $$\chi_{E_2/F_2}\circ\alpha=\chi_{E_1/F_1}
 \quad\text{in }\widehat{\mathbb Z}^{\times}.$$*

</div>

We use <a id="pm-cite-17"></a>[[hoshi2025](#ref-hoshi2025), Theorem 3.4]. Uniqueness of the embedding, when it exists, is <a id="pm-cite-18"></a>[[uchida1981](#ref-uchida1981), Proposition 2].

#### Tame inertia and norms

Decomposition groups in $P_M$ are the full absolute Galois groups of the corresponding local fields. Here is a justification for using the usual local ramification structure. Fix a prime of $\overline{\mathbb Q}$ above a prime of $M$, and form inside a local algebraic closure the union of the completions of finite fields between $M$ and $\mathbb Q^s$. This union contains all roots of unity and is closed under radicals: for a fixed exponent, a nonzero local element can be approximated by a global element in the field defining that completion, and their ratio can be made a power. Thus the union has no cyclic extension. Every finite Galois group over a nonarchimedean local field is solvable, by its wild inertia, tame inertia, and unramified filtration. Every closed subgroup of its prosolvable absolute Galois group is prosolvable as well; a nontrivial such group has a nontrivial finite cyclic quotient after passing through a finite solvable quotient. The union is therefore the full local algebraic closure. The usual decomposition-group identification proves the claim.

For an active pair $(v,w)$ from Theorem <a href="#thm:local-correspondence" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:local-correspondence">5</a>, and $g\in D_v$, let $b(g)\in\widehat{\mathbb Z}$ be its source residue exponent and let $a(g)\in\widehat{\mathbb Z}$ be the target residue exponent of $\varphi(g)$. These are measured using $Nv$- and $Nw$-Frobenius, respectively. The target residue exponent is defined on $D_v$ by composition; we do not assume that the map sends all source inertia into target inertia.

<a id="stmt-lem-norm-identity"></a>
<div id="lem:norm-identity" class="lemma">

<a id="eq:norm-identity"></a>
**Lemma 7**. *For an active pair $(v,w)$ at an odd prime $\ell$, $$\label{eq:norm-identity}
 (Nv)^{b(g)}=(Nw)^{a(g)}\quad\text{in }\mathbb Z_\ell^\times
 \qquad(g\in D_v).$$ Powers by profinite integers are interpreted by continuous powering in the compact group $\mathbb Z_\ell^\times$.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-norm-identity"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-norm-identity</summary>

*Proof.* Let $p\ne\ell$ be the residue characteristic of $v$, and choose a topological generator $\tau$ of a pro-$\ell$ Sylow subgroup of $I_v$. Its image in source tame inertia generates the pro-$\ell$ factor. The tame conjugation relation shows that $$e=g\tau g^{-1}\tau^{-(Nv)^{b(g)}}$$ belongs to source wild inertia, a pro-$p$ group. In the target tame quotient, the image of $e$ also lies in the pro-$\ell$ factor of tame inertia: both $\varphi(\tau)$ and its conjugate do. Its image is therefore trivial, because it belongs to a pro-$p$ subgroup and to a pro-$\ell$ group. The nontrivial image of $\varphi(\tau)$ in the torsion-free target pro-$\ell$ tame factor then gives <a href="#eq:norm-identity" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:norm-identity">[eq:norm-identity]</a> by the target tame conjugation relation. The target residue characteristic is different from $\ell$ by Theorem <a href="#thm:local-correspondence" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:local-correspondence">5</a>. ◻

</details>

<a id="sec:characters"></a>
### Additive characters and Frobenius detection

We will compare homomorphisms of Galois groups by evaluating additive $\ell$-adic characters at Frobenius elements. The main fact needed for this comparison is that infinitely many primes of absolute residue degree one detect a character. We first prove the logarithm-rank statement behind this fact, then establish the versions involving averaging and corestriction. The section ends with a representation contained in the character space that will supply enough characters for the comparison.

<a id="pm-h-10"></a>
#### A logarithm-rank lemma

For a finite extension $K/\mathbb Q_\ell$, normalize its absolute value by $|\ell|_\ell=\ell^{-1}$. The $\ell$-adic logarithm on $\mathcal O_K^\times$ is the continuous homomorphism that agrees with the usual power series near $1$; its kernel is the finite group of roots of unity in $K$. Logarithms of points in a torus are taken coordinatewise.

For a number field $M$, class field theory expresses an additive character of $G_M$ as a linear form on $M\otimes_\mathbb Q\mathbb Q_\ell$. A zero Frobenius value at a prime $v\nmid\ell$ forces this form to annihilate $\log b$, where $(b)=v^h$ for some $h>0$. For primes of residue degree one chosen over distinct rational primes unramified in a Galois closure, the conjugate generators have disjoint ideal supports; the following lemma turns their multiplicative independence into full logarithmic rank.

This special rank estimate admits an interpolation-determinant proof. It is a special case of the $\ell$-adic Waldschmidt–Masser rank theorem <a id="pm-cite-19"></a>[[waldschmidt1981](#ref-waldschmidt1981), [masser1981](#ref-masser1981)]. We retain a proof using the translate estimates of that theory and Laurent’s interpolation determinants <a id="pm-cite-20"></a>[[laurent1991](#ref-laurent1991)]; see also <a id="pm-cite-21"></a>[[dasgupta2023](#ref-dasgupta2023), Theorems 5.1 and 5.8].

<a id="stmt-lem-log-rank"></a>
<div id="lem:log-rank" class="lemma">

**Lemma 8** (Logarithm rank). *Let $n,m\geq1$, let $E$ be a number field embedded in a finite extension $K/\mathbb Q_\ell$, and let $$u_i=(u_{i1},\ldots,u_{in})\in(E^\times)^n,
 \qquad 1\leq i\leq m,$$ have algebraic-integer coordinates that are units in $K$. Suppose that for every $a=(a_1,\ldots,a_m)\in\mathbb Z^m\setminus\{0\}$, the coordinates of $u^a:=\prod_{i=1}^m u_i^{a_i}$ are multiplicatively independent modulo roots of unity. If $m>n(n-1)$, then $$\operatorname{span}_K\{\log u_1,\ldots,\log u_m\}=K^n.$$*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-log-rank"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-log-rank</summary>

*Proof.* The multiplicative hypothesis implies that the cyclic subgroup generated by every $u^a$ with $a\ne0$ is Zariski dense in $\mathbb G_m^n$. Indeed, distinct Laurent monomials have distinct values at $u^a$. If a Laurent polynomial vanished at all nonnegative powers of $u^a$, a Vandermonde matrix applied to its finitely many monomials would force all its coefficients to vanish.

Suppose that the logarithms span a subspace of dimension $r<n$. The case $r=0$ contradicts the multiplicative hypothesis because the kernel of the logarithm on local units consists of roots of unity. Thus $1\leq r<n$, and in particular $n\geq2$. We will obtain an algebraic determinant whose small $\ell$-adic absolute value is incompatible with its archimedean size.

First make all choices needed for the analytic estimate. Replacing all $u_i$ by the same positive integer power preserves the hypotheses and the logarithmic rank. Such a power puts their coordinates in a neighborhood where exponential and logarithm are inverse. The $\mathcal O_K$-module generated by their logarithms is free of rank $r$; choose a basis $b_1,\ldots,b_r\in K^n$. After a further common $\ell$-power, these basis vectors have sufficiently small coordinates that $$\rho:=\max_{j,t}|b_{t,j}|_\ell
 \quad\hbox{satisfies}\quad
 \eta:=\rho\ell^{1/(\ell-1)}<1.$$ Here the basis is multiplied by the same power as the logarithms. Consequently there are $z_i\in\mathcal O_K^r$ such that $$\log u_i=\sum_{t=1}^r z_{i,t}b_t.$$ From now on these powered points, the number field $E$, the local field $K$, the basis, and $\eta$ are fixed. In particular, none depends on the degree parameter introduced below.

<a id="eq:log-taylor-bound"></a>
For an integer vector $\alpha=(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_n)$, the monomial $X^\alpha$ on these points is represented by $$f_\alpha(z)=
 \exp\!\left(\sum_{j=1}^n\alpha_j
                      \sum_{t=1}^r b_{t,j}z_t\right).$$ It is analytic on a polydisc of radius greater than $1$. If $f_\alpha(z)=\sum_{\nu\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}^r}c_{\alpha,\nu}z^\nu$, the exponential series and $|k!|_\ell^{-1}\leq\ell^{k/(\ell-1)}$ give the uniform bound $$\label{eq:log-taylor-bound}
 |c_{\alpha,\nu}|_\ell\leq\eta^{|\nu|},
 \qquad |\nu|=\nu_1+\cdots+\nu_r.$$ The bound is independent of $\alpha$, since every integer has $\ell$-adic absolute value at most $1$. Moreover, for every $a\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}^m$, the point $z(a):=\sum_i a_i z_i$ lies in $\mathcal O_K^r$, and $f_\alpha(z(a))=(u^a)^\alpha$.

<a id="eq:log-grid-size"></a>
We next show that sufficiently many of these evaluations have full algebraic rank. For an integer $D\geq1$, put $A=(D+1)^n$ and choose an integer $S\geq1$ such that $$\label{eq:log-grid-size}
 (S+1)^m>(nD)^n,
 \qquad S=O(D^{n/m}).$$ Consider the matrix with rows indexed by $\alpha\in\{0,\ldots,D\}^n$, columns indexed by $a\in\{0,\ldots,nS\}^m$, and entries $(u^a)^\alpha$. We claim that its rank is $A$.

Otherwise a nonzero polynomial $P$ of degree at most $D$ in each variable would vanish at every point $u^a$ in this grid. Over an algebraic closure of $E$, define closed subsets of the torus by $$Z_j=\{x\in\mathbb G_m^n:
          P(u^a x)=0\ \hbox{for all }a\in\{0,\ldots,jS\}^m\},
 \qquad 0\leq j\leq n.$$ These sets are nested and nonempty, since $1\in Z_n$, whereas $\dim Z_0\leq n-1$. Hence some adjacent pair $Z_j,Z_{j+1}$ has the same dimension $d$. Let $V$ be a $d$-dimensional irreducible component of $Z_{j+1}$. For every $b\in\{0,\ldots,S\}^m$, the translate $u^bV$ is contained in $Z_j$ and has dimension $d$, so it is an irreducible component of $Z_j$. All these translates are distinct: an equality $u^bV=u^{b'}V$ for $b\ne b'$ would make $V$ stable under every power of $u^{b-b'}$. The density proved above would then force $V=\mathbb G_m^n$, contrary to $V\subseteq Z_0$.

For completeness, a common zero set of polynomials of total degree at most $\delta\geq1$ in $\mathbb A^n$ has at most $\delta^n$ irreducible components. One can obtain this form of the affine Bézout bound by assigning a component $Y$ the weight $\delta^{\dim Y}\deg Y$. Start with $\mathbb A^n$, of weight $\delta^n$, and impose the equations successively. A component contained in the next hypersurface retains its weight; a component not contained in it is cut into components of dimension one less, whose total degree is at most $\delta\deg Y$ by Bézout. Thus the total weight does not increase, and removing redundant components can only decrease it. The same component bound holds after restriction to the torus. Applying it to $Z_j$, whose equations have total degree at most $nD$, contradicts <a href="#eq:log-grid-size" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:log-grid-size">[eq:log-grid-size]</a>. This proves the rank claim.

<a id="eq:log-determinant-small"></a>
Choose a nonzero $A\times A$ minor $\Delta_D$ of the evaluation matrix. It is an algebraic integer in the fixed field $E$. Expand its rows using the convergent power series for $f_\alpha$. A nonzero term must involve $A$ distinct monomials in the $r$ variables $z_1,\ldots,z_r$, since repeated monomials give repeated rows in the corresponding evaluation determinant. The sum of the degrees of any $A$ distinct such monomials is at least $c_rA^{1+1/r}$ for all sufficiently large $A$: the number of monomials of degree at most $t$ is $\binom{t+r}{r}=O_r(t^r)$, so at least half of the $A$ monomials have degree bounded below by a positive multiple of $A^{1/r}$. Their evaluations have absolute value at most $1$. The nonarchimedean triangle inequality and <a href="#eq:log-taylor-bound" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:log-taylor-bound">[eq:log-taylor-bound]</a>, first for truncated series and then by convergence, therefore give a constant $c>0$ such that $$\label{eq:log-determinant-small}
 \log|\Delta_D|_\ell\leq-cA^{1+1/r}.$$

<a id="eq:log-determinant-large"></a>
At every archimedean embedding $\tau:E\hookrightarrow\mathbb C$, an entry of this minor has absolute value at most $\exp(CDS)$, with $C$ independent of $D$. Expanding the determinant thus gives $$\label{eq:log-determinant-large}
 \log|\tau(\Delta_D)|\leq C'ADS+A\log A.$$ Let $\lambda$ be the place of $E$ induced by $E\hookrightarrow K$. Use absolute values at finite places extending the usual ones on $\mathbb Q$, and sum over all embeddings $E\hookrightarrow\mathbb C$, so that each complex place occurs twice. Writing $p_v$ for the rational prime below a finite place $v$, the product formula reads $$0=\sum_{\tau:E\hookrightarrow\mathbb C}\log|\tau(\Delta_D)|
     +\sum_{v\text{ finite}}[E_v:\mathbb Q_{p_v}]
                            \log|\Delta_D|_v.$$ Every term in the second sum is nonpositive because $\Delta_D$ is an algebraic integer. Retaining its $\lambda$-term and using <a href="#eq:log-determinant-small" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:log-determinant-small">[eq:log-determinant-small]</a>–<a href="#eq:log-determinant-large" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:log-determinant-large">[eq:log-determinant-large]</a> would imply $$c[E_\lambda:\mathbb Q_\ell]A^{1+1/r}
 \leq [E:\mathbb Q](C'ADS+A\log A).$$ This is impossible as $D\to\infty$: the left side has order $D^{n+n/r}$, whereas the right side is $O(D^{n+1+n/m}+D^n\log D)$, and $$\frac nr\geq\frac n{n-1}>1+\frac nm$$ by $m>n(n-1)$. This contradiction proves the lemma. ◻

</details>

#### Detection, averaging, and corestriction

For a number field $M\subset\overline{\mathbb Q}$, write $$X_{\ell}(M):=\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathrm{cont}}(G_M,\mathbb Q_\ell),
 \qquad G_M=\mathop{\mathrm{Gal}}(\overline{\mathbb Q}/M),$$ where $\mathbb Q_\ell$ is an additive group with trivial Galois action. Local class field theory shows that these characters are unramified at every finite prime $v\nmid\ell$: the local unit group has a pro-$p$ subgroup of finite index for $p\ne\ell$, and has no nonzero continuous homomorphism to $\mathbb Q_\ell$. Thus $x(\mathop{\mathrm{Fr}}_v)$ is defined for $x\in X_{\ell}(M)$ and $v\nmid\ell$.

If $M/\mathbb Q$ is Galois, $R=\mathop{\mathrm{Gal}}(M/\mathbb Q)$ acts by $(g x)(h)=x(\widetilde g^{-1}h\widetilde g)$, where $\widetilde g\in G_\mathbb Q$ lifts $g$. The definition is independent of the lift. For a subgroup $J\subseteq R$, write $$E_J=\frac1{|J|}\sum_{g\in J}g$$ for the projector onto the $J$-invariants. We use $\mathop{\mathrm{res}}_{L/M}$ and $\mathop{\mathrm{cor}}_{L/M}$ for restriction and corestriction on additive characters for a finite extension $L/M$.

<a id="stmt-prop-character-detection"></a>
<div id="prop:character-detection" class="proposition">

**Proposition 9** (Frobenius detection). *Let $M$ be a number field, let $\ell$ be a rational prime, and let $x\in X_{\ell}(M)$.*

1.  *If $x(\mathop{\mathrm{Fr}}_v)=0$ at infinitely many primes $v\nmid\ell$ of absolute residue degree one, then $x=0$.*

2.  *Suppose that $M/\mathbb Q$ is Galois, and fix $\delta\in\mathop{\mathrm{Gal}}(M/\mathbb Q)$. If $x(\mathop{\mathrm{Fr}}_v)=0$ at infinitely many primes $v\nmid\ell$ lying over rational primes unramified in $M$ whose Frobenius element at $v$ in $\mathop{\mathrm{Gal}}(M/\mathbb Q)$ is $\delta$, then $E_{\langle\delta\rangle}x=0$.*

3.  *If $\mathop{\mathrm{cor}}_{M/\mathbb Q}x\ne0$, then $x(\mathop{\mathrm{Fr}}_v)=0$ at only finitely many primes $v\nmid\ell$, with no restriction on their residue degrees.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-prop-character-detection"><summary>Proof of stmt-prop-character-detection</summary>

<a id="eq:character-class-field"></a>
*Proof.* We first recall the class-field-theoretic description of the character space. Let $V_M=M\otimes_\mathbb Q\mathbb Q_\ell$, and let $U_M\subseteq V_M$ be the $\mathbb Q_\ell$-linear span of the logarithms of the global units, with logarithms taken at all places above $\ell$. Then $$\label{eq:character-class-field}
 X_{\ell}(M)\simeq
 \{\lambda\in\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathbb Q_\ell}(V_M,\mathbb Q_\ell):
                         \lambda(U_M)=0\}.$$ Indeed, pass to the inverse limit of the ray class sequences with moduli supported above $\ell$. Local logarithms identify the rationalized local unit groups with $V_M$; the global units impose exactly the subspace $U_M$. The ordinary class group and the factors at real places are finite, so contribute no additive $\mathbb Q_\ell$-characters. This description uses the actual logarithmic image of the units and makes no assertion about its dimension.

<a id="eq:character-principal-reciprocity"></a>
For $v\nmid\ell$, choose $h_v>0$ and $b_v\in\mathcal O_M$ with $(b_v)=v^{h_v}$. Such a choice is possible because the ideal class group is finite. Principal reciprocity, with arithmetic Frobenius, gives for the linear form corresponding to $x$ the identity $$\label{eq:character-principal-reciprocity}
 \lambda(\log b_v)=-h_vx(\mathop{\mathrm{Fr}}_v).$$ Here $b_v$ is a unit at every place above $\ell$; all other local unit contributions are killed by $x$.

For (i), set $n=[M:\mathbb Q]$ and choose $m>n(n-1)$ of the zero primes, over distinct rational primes unramified in a Galois closure $E$ of $M$. Let $b_i$ be the elements just chosen for these primes, and let $$u_i=(\sigma(b_i))_{\sigma:M\hookrightarrow E}\in(E^\times)^n.$$ All coordinates are algebraic integers and are units at every place of $E$ above $\ell$. We verify the multiplicative hypothesis of Lemma <a href="#lem:log-rank" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:log-rank">8</a> by valuations.

Put $G=\mathop{\mathrm{Gal}}(E/\mathbb Q)$ and $H=\mathop{\mathrm{Gal}}(E/M)$. For a selected degree-one prime $v_i$ and a prime $\mathfrak p_i$ of $E$ above it, the decomposition group $D_i$ is contained in $H$. In fact, the rational prime is unramified in $E$, and the degree-one condition says that $[D_i:D_i\cap H]=1$. The support of $v_i\mathcal O_E$ is indexed by $H/D_i$. Its translates corresponding to the embeddings $M\hookrightarrow E$, indexed by $G/H$, have supports indexed by the disjoint sets $gH/D_i$. Distinct indices $i$ involve distinct rational primes. It follows that the $mn$ numbers $\sigma(b_i)$ have pairwise disjoint nonempty supports of their principal ideals. They are therefore multiplicatively independent modulo roots of unity. In particular, for any nonzero $a\in\mathbb Z^m$, the $n$ coordinates of $u^a$ are multiplicatively independent modulo roots of unity.

Embed $E$ in a finite extension $K/\mathbb Q_\ell$. Under the splitting $$(M\otimes_\mathbb Q\mathbb Q_\ell)\otimes_{\mathbb Q_\ell}K
   \simeq\prod_{\sigma:M\hookrightarrow E}K,$$ the vector $\log b_i$ becomes $\log u_i$. Lemma <a href="#lem:log-rank" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:log-rank">8</a> says that these vectors span the right side. Equations <a href="#eq:character-class-field" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:character-class-field">[eq:character-class-field]</a> and <a href="#eq:character-principal-reciprocity" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:character-principal-reciprocity">[eq:character-principal-reciprocity]</a> therefore force $\lambda=0$, and hence $x=0$.

For (ii), put $C=\langle\delta\rangle$, $F=M^C$, and $y=E_Cx$. Since $\delta$ fixes every indicated prime $v$, averaging preserves its Frobenius value, so $y(\mathop{\mathrm{Fr}}_v)=0$. The restriction–corestriction identity gives $$\mathop{\mathrm{res}}_{M/F}\mathop{\mathrm{cor}}_{M/F}y=\sum_{g\in C}g y=|C|y.$$ Thus $y$ is the restriction of $z=|C|^{-1}\mathop{\mathrm{cor}}_{M/F}y\in X_{\ell}(F)$. At an indicated prime, the decomposition group of $M/\mathbb Q$ is exactly $C$. The prime $u=v\cap F$ consequently has absolute residue degree one, and $$0=y(\mathop{\mathrm{Fr}}_v)=[\kappa(v):\kappa(u)]z(\mathop{\mathrm{Fr}}_u).$$ Infinitely many distinct $u$ occur because $M/F$ is finite. Part (i) gives $z=0$, and therefore $y=0$.

For (iii), suppose that infinitely many zero primes exist. Let $E/\mathbb Q$ be a finite Galois closure of $M$, put $R=\mathop{\mathrm{Gal}}(E/\mathbb Q)$, and let $y=\mathop{\mathrm{res}}_{E/M}x$. Discarding finitely many primes, choose primes of $E$ above these zero primes whose rational primes are unramified in $E$. Restriction multiplies a Frobenius value by the relative residue degree, so they are zero primes for $y$. An infinite subset has one fixed Frobenius element $\delta\in R$. By (ii), $E_{\langle\delta\rangle}y=0$, and hence $E_Ry=0$. It follows that $$\mathop{\mathrm{res}}_{E/\mathbb Q}\mathop{\mathrm{cor}}_{E/\mathbb Q}y=|R|E_Ry=0.$$ Restriction on additive characters across a finite extension is injective: a character that vanishes on a subgroup of finite index has finite image in the torsion-free group $\mathbb Q_\ell$, and so is zero. On the other hand, transitivity and the corestriction–restriction identity give $$\mathop{\mathrm{cor}}_{E/\mathbb Q}y
 =\mathop{\mathrm{cor}}_{M/\mathbb Q}\mathop{\mathrm{cor}}_{E/M}\mathop{\mathrm{res}}_{E/M}x
 =[E:M]\mathop{\mathrm{cor}}_{M/\mathbb Q}x\ne0,$$ a contradiction. ◻

</details>

<a id="pm-h-12"></a>
#### A signed representation in the character space

The class field description also controls the supply of characters when the number field varies. The regular representation gives upper bounds for dimensions of subgroup invariants, while the signed representation below gives lower bounds and ensures a faithful action on the character space.

<a id="stmt-prop-signed-representation"></a>
<div id="prop:signed-representation" class="proposition">

<a id="eq:signed-representation"></a>
**Proposition 10**. *Let $M/\mathbb Q$ be a finite, totally imaginary Galois extension, put $R=\mathop{\mathrm{Gal}}(M/\mathbb Q)$, and let $c\in R$ be a complex conjugation. For every rational prime $\ell$, there are $R$-equivariant embeddings $$\label{eq:signed-representation}
 W_M:=\mathop{\mathrm{Ind}}_{\langle c\rangle}^{R}(\mathrm{sign})
 \lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow X_{\ell}(M)
 \lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow\mathbb Q_\ell[R],$$ where $\mathrm{sign}(c)=-1$. In particular, the action of $R$ on $X_{\ell}(M)$ is faithful.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-prop-signed-representation"><summary>Proof of stmt-prop-signed-representation</summary>

*Proof.* By the normal basis theorem, $V_M=M\otimes_\mathbb Q\mathbb Q_\ell$ is the regular representation of $R$, as is its dual. The naturality of <a href="#eq:character-class-field" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:character-class-field">[eq:character-class-field]</a> gives the second embedding.

The equivariant Dirichlet unit theorem <a id="pm-cite-22"></a>[[bass1966](#ref-bass1966), Section 3] identifies the representation $\mathcal O_M^\times\otimes_\mathbb Z\mathbb Q_\ell$ with the permutation representation on the archimedean places minus its trivial summand. Since these places are indexed by $R/\langle c\rangle$, writing $U^{\mathrm{alg}}=\mathcal O_M^\times\otimes_\mathbb Z\mathbb Q_\ell$ gives $$\mathbb Q_\ell\oplus U^{\mathrm{alg}}
 \simeq\mathop{\mathrm{Ind}}_{\langle c\rangle}^{R}\mathbf1.$$ The character identity follows first over the real numbers from the usual logarithmic unit embedding, and hence over $\mathbb Q_\ell$ by semisimplicity in characteristic zero. In particular, $U^{\mathrm{alg}}$ is self-dual. Its $\ell$-adic logarithmic image $U_M$ is a quotient of $U^{\mathrm{alg}}$; no injectivity of the logarithm on this tensor product is needed.

The decomposition of the regular representation induced from the two characters of $\langle c\rangle$ is $$\mathbb Q_\ell[R]
 \simeq\mathop{\mathrm{Ind}}_{\langle c\rangle}^{R}\mathbf1\oplus W_M
 \simeq\mathbb Q_\ell\oplus U^{\mathrm{alg}}\oplus W_M.$$ Since $U_M^\vee$ is a subrepresentation of $(U^{\mathrm{alg}})^\vee\simeq U^{\mathrm{alg}}$, semisimplicity and $X_{\ell}(M)\simeq(V_M/U_M)^\vee$ show that $X_{\ell}(M)$ contains $\mathbb Q_\ell\oplus W_M$. This proves the first embedding in <a href="#eq:signed-representation" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:signed-representation">[eq:signed-representation]</a>.

Finally, realize $W_M$ as the direct sum of the signed lines indexed by the left cosets of $\langle c\rangle$. An element acting trivially must fix the line of the identity coset, and therefore belongs to $\langle c\rangle$. The nonidentity element $c$ acts by $-1$ on that line. Thus only the identity acts trivially on $W_M$, proving faithfulness. ◻

</details>

### Normalizer extension and Frobenius generation

Let $F$ be a number field and let $$\alpha_0:G_F\longrightarrow P$$ be a continuous open homomorphism. Our eventual aim is to identify this map with ordinary restriction, up to conjugation in $P$. We first enlarge an open part of its domain in a canonical way. For the enlarged map we will prove surjectivity and equality of cyclotomic logarithms at an infinite set of auxiliary primes. The main step is to show that images of local Frobenius elements generate prescribed cyclic subgroups in suitable finite quotients of $P$.

Choose an open normal subgroup $U\trianglelefteq\mathcal G$ contained in $G_F$, and put $$A=\alpha_0(U),\qquad N=\ker(\alpha_0|_U),\qquad
 T=N_{\mathcal G}(N).$$ The subgroup $A$ is open in $P$. Since $U\subset T$, the normalizer $T$ is open and closed in $\mathcal G$; write $T=G_k$.

<a id="stmt-lem-normalizer-extension"></a>
<div id="lem:normalizer-extension" class="lemma">

**Lemma 11** (Extension to the normalizer). *There is a unique continuous open homomorphism $$\beta:T\longrightarrow P$$ whose restriction to $U$ is $\alpha_0|_U$.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-normalizer-extension"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-normalizer-extension</summary>

*Proof.* Conjugation by $t\in T$ induces a continuous automorphism of $U/N=A$. By Theorem <a href="#thm:uchida-isomorphism" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:uchida-isomorphism">3</a>, applied to $\mathbb Q^s/(\mathbb Q^s)^A$, this automorphism is conjugation by a unique element $\beta(t)$ of $P$. Indeed, the implementing field automorphism of $\mathbb Q^s$ fixes $\mathbb Q$. Uniqueness shows both that $\beta$ is a homomorphism and that $\beta(u)=\alpha_0(u)$ for $u\in U$. It also shows that any extension of $\alpha_0|_U$ to $T$ must equal $\beta$: its values must induce these same conjugations on $A$.

Continuity on the open subgroup $U$ implies continuity on $T$. Moreover, $\beta(T)$ contains $A$ and is therefore open in $P$. A continuous homomorphism of profinite groups is open onto its image, so $\beta$ is open. ◻

</details>

We keep $U,A,N,T,k$, and $\beta$ fixed for the rest of the argument. List the distinct conjugates of $N$ other than $N$ itself as $$N_i=t_iNt_i^{-1}\quad(1\leq i\leq r),\qquad t_i\notin T,
 \qquad H_i=\beta(N_i).$$ There are finitely many because $U$ normalizes $N$. Normality of $U$ in $\mathcal G$ implies that each $N_i$ lies in $U$ and is normal in $U$, so $H_i$ is normal in $A$. Each $H_i$ is nontrivial. Otherwise $t_iNt_i^{-1}\subset N$, and iterating this inclusion would give a descending chain returning to $N$: some power of $t_i$ lies in $U$ and hence normalizes $N$. All inclusions would be equalities, contrary to $t_i\notin T$. We allow $r=0$, with sums indexed by $i$ then equal to zero.

#### A finite quotient with enough characters

The next lemma constructs characters that distinguish a proper subgroup of a cyclic group while avoiding the invariant spaces attached to the $H_i$. The latter condition will prevent cancellation by conjugates of $N$ when we average a pulled-back character on the source. For a finite subgroup $J$ acting on a characteristic-zero vector space, we use the averaging operator $E_J=|J|^{-1}\sum_{g\in J}g$.

<a id="stmt-lem-quotient-amplification"></a>
<div id="lem:quotient-amplification" class="lemma">

<a id="eq:separating-character"></a>
**Lemma 12** (Quotient amplification). *Fix an odd prime $\ell$ and a finite quotient $R_0$ of $P$. There is a finite Galois extension $L/\mathbb Q$ in $\mathbb Q^s$, totally imaginary, such that the quotient $R=\mathop{\mathrm{Gal}}(L/\mathbb Q)$ dominates $R_0$ and has the following property. For every $\gamma\in R$, with $C=\langle\gamma\rangle$, and every proper subgroup $D<C$, there is $x\in X_{\ell}(L)$ satisfying $$\label{eq:separating-character}
 x\in X_{\ell}(L)^{D},\qquad E_Cx=0,\qquad
 x\notin\sum_{i=1}^rX_{\ell}(L)^{H_i}.$$ Here each $H_i$ acts through its image in $R$.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-quotient-amplification"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-quotient-amplification</summary>

*Proof.* Choose a quotient $R_1=\mathop{\mathrm{Gal}}(L_1/\mathbb Q)$ dominating $R_0$ such that every $H_i$ has nontrivial image in $R_1$, the kernel of $P\to R_1$ is contained in $A$, and $L_1$ contains $\mathbb Q(i)$. These are finitely many compatible conditions on an open normal subgroup of $P$. Put $n=|R_1|$.

We will enlarge $R_1$ while keeping element orders bounded by $2n$. At the same time, the images of the $H_i$ and the conjugacy class of complex conjugation will grow. Growth of the $H_i$ makes the sum of their invariant spaces small. Growth of the conjugacy class makes the signed representation supply enough $D$-invariant vectors killed by $E_C$ to escape that sum.

<a id="eq:regular-kummer-kernel"></a>
We use the regular-module auxiliary extension construction appearing in Uchida’s proof <a id="pm-cite-23"></a>[[uchida1979](#ref-uchida1979), pp. 360–362], with quantitative bounds on its invariant spaces. More precisely, we construct a further quotient with kernel consisting of $m$ copies of the regular $\mathbb F_2[R_1]$-module, where $m$ will be chosen below. Choose $m$ distinct rational primes that split completely in $L_1$. For each of them, select one prime of $L_1$ above it. Approximation gives elements $a_1,\ldots,a_m\in L_1^\times$ such that $a_j$ has valuation one at its selected prime and valuation zero at every other prime in these $m$ sets. The $mn$ elements $$g(a_j),\qquad g\in R_1,\quad 1\leq j\leq m,$$ have independent square classes: their valuations at the selected sets of primes form a permutation of the identity matrix modulo two. Adjoin their square roots and call the resulting field $L$. Kummer theory gives $$\label{eq:regular-kummer-kernel}
  1\longrightarrow V\longrightarrow R=\mathop{\mathrm{Gal}}(L/\mathbb Q)
   \longrightarrow R_1\longrightarrow1,
  \qquad V\simeq\mathbb F_2[R_1]^m$$ as $R_1$-modules. The dual module supplied by Kummer theory is again the regular permutation module. The field $L$ is Galois over $\mathbb Q$ because all conjugates of the $a_j$ were included; its group is solvable, so $L\subset\mathbb Q^s$. No splitting of <a href="#eq:regular-kummer-kernel" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:regular-kummer-kernel">[eq:regular-kummer-kernel]</a> is required.

<a id="eq:large-subgroups"></a>
Every element of $R$ has order at most $2n$, since its $n$th power lies in the exponent-two group $V$. Write $A_R$ and $H_{i,R}$ for the images of $A$ and $H_i$ in $R$. The choice of $R_1$ gives $V\subset A_R$. Choose $h_i\in H_{i,R}$ with nonidentity image $g_i\in R_1$, of order $d_i>1$. As $H_{i,R}$ is normal in $A_R$, $$(g_i-1)V=[h_i,V]\subset H_{i,R}.$$ On $m$ regular modules the rank of $g_i-1$ is $mn(1-1/d_i)\geq mn/2$. Consequently $$\label{eq:large-subgroups}
  |H_{i,R}|\geq 2^{mn/2}.$$ If $c\in R$ is a complex conjugation, its image in $R_1$ is a nontrivial involution because $L_1$ is totally imaginary. Conjugation by $V$ alone therefore gives at least $2^{mn/2}$ distinct conjugates of $c$; thus <a id="eq:large-conjugacy-class"></a>$$\label{eq:large-conjugacy-class}
  |c^R|\geq 2^{mn/2}.$$

<a id="eq:forbidden-dimension"></a>
<a id="eq:signed-invariants"></a>
Set $\eta=2^{-mn/2}$ and write $X=X_{\ell}(L)$. By Proposition <a href="#prop:signed-representation" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:signed-representation">10</a>, $X$ embeds in the regular $\mathbb Q_\ell[R]$-representation and contains $$W=\mathop{\mathrm{Ind}}_{\langle c\rangle}^{R}(\operatorname{sign}).$$ The regular-representation bound and <a href="#eq:large-subgroups" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:large-subgroups">[eq:large-subgroups]</a> give $$\label{eq:forbidden-dimension}
 \dim\left(\sum_{i=1}^rX^{H_i}\right)
 \leq\sum_{i=1}^r\frac{|R|}{|H_{i,R}|}
 \leq r\eta|R|.$$ The character of $W$ takes the value $|R|/2$ at the identity, the value $-|R|/(2|c^R|)$ on $c^R$, and zero elsewhere. Hence, for every subgroup $J\subset R$, $$\label{eq:signed-invariants}
 \left|\dim W^J-\frac{|R|}{2|J|}\right|
 \leq\frac{\eta|R|}{2}.$$ For $D<C=\langle\gamma\rangle$, the map $E_C:W^D\to W^C$ is surjective. Since $|D|\leq |C|/2$ and $|C|\leq2n$, <a href="#eq:signed-invariants" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:signed-invariants">[eq:signed-invariants]</a> implies $$\begin{aligned}
 \dim\ker(E_C|_{W^D})
 &=\dim W^D-\dim W^C\\
 &\geq\frac{|R|}{2}\left(\frac1{|D|}-\frac1{|C|}\right)
       -\eta|R|\\
 &\geq |R|\left(\frac1{4n}-\eta\right).
\end{aligned}$$ Choose the integer $m\geq1$ so large that $$(r+1)2^{-mn/2}<\frac1{4n}.$$ This single choice works for all $\gamma$ and all proper $D<C$. The last dimension is then greater than <a href="#eq:forbidden-dimension" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:forbidden-dimension">[eq:forbidden-dimension]</a>, so its kernel contains a vector outside the indicated sum. This vector gives <a href="#eq:separating-character" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:separating-character">[eq:separating-character]</a>. If $C$ is trivial, there is no proper subgroup to consider. ◻

</details>

#### Generation by local Frobenius images

We now use these characters to strengthen the local correspondence. Containment of a source decomposition group in a target decomposition group places its Frobenius image in a cyclic subgroup of a finite quotient. The next proposition shows that, after enlarging the quotient, infinitely many such images generate the entire cyclic subgroup.

<a id="stmt-prop-frobenius-generation"></a>
<div id="prop:frobenius-generation" class="proposition">

**Proposition 13** (Frobenius generation). *Fix an odd prime $\ell$, a finite quotient $R_0$ of $P$, and $\gamma_0\in R_0$. There are a quotient $$P\twoheadrightarrow R=\mathop{\mathrm{Gal}}(L/\mathbb Q)\twoheadrightarrow R_0$$ and a lift $\gamma\in R$ of $\gamma_0$ with the following property. There are infinitely many $\ell$-active primes $v$ of $k$, over distinct rational primes $p\ne\ell$, for which compatible prolongations can be chosen so that:*

1.  *the corresponding target rational prime $q\ne\ell$ is unramified in $L$, and the target prolongation restricts to a prime $w'$ of $L$ whose Frobenius in $R$ is $\gamma$;*

2.  *the image in $R$ of an arithmetic Frobenius lift at $v$ generates $\langle\gamma\rangle$.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-prop-frobenius-generation"><summary>Proof of stmt-prop-frobenius-generation</summary>

*Proof.* Choose $R$ by Lemma <a href="#lem:quotient-amplification" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:quotient-amplification">12</a> and choose any lift $\gamma$ of $\gamma_0$. Put $C=\langle\gamma\rangle$. Take an open normal subgroup $U'\trianglelefteq\mathcal G$ contained in $U\cap\beta^{-1}(P_L)$; for example, take the core of this open subgroup in $\mathcal G$. Let $F'/\mathbb Q$ be the finite Galois extension with $G_{F'}=U'$. The map $$\beta|_{U'}:G_{F'}\longrightarrow P_L$$ is open.

Chebotarev’s theorem supplies infinitely many primes $w'$ of $L$, over distinct rational primes $q\ne\ell$ unramified in $L$, with Frobenius exactly $\gamma$. Theorem <a href="#thm:local-correspondence" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:local-correspondence">5</a> applied to $\beta|_{U'}$ pairs all but finitely many of them with $\ell$-active primes $v'$ of $F'$, away from $\ell$. Uniqueness of the target prime for an active source prime implies that infinitely many $v'$ occur. Since only finitely many primes of $F'$ lie over a given rational prime, we may pass to a subset with distinct source rational primes $p$, all unramified in $F'$. Passing to another infinite subset, we may also assume that their Frobenius elements in $\mathop{\mathrm{Gal}}(F'/\mathbb Q)$ are a fixed element $\delta$.

Fix a source prolongation above each $v'$, and use it also for the prime $v=v'|_k$. The original correspondence at $v'$ can be made with this prolongation: changing the source prolongation conjugates the target group by an element of $\beta(U')\subset P_L$, which preserves the finite prime $w'$. Activity passes from $v'$ to $v$, since the relevant inertia subgroup for $U'$ is contained in that for $T$. Apply Theorem <a href="#thm:local-correspondence" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:local-correspondence">5</a> at $v$ using this same source prolongation. If $w_1$ is the restriction to $L$ of its target prolongation, then $$\beta(D_{v'})\subset\beta(D_v)\cap P_L\subset D_{w_1}.$$ Uniqueness of the target prime for $v'$ at the level of $L$ gives $w_1=w'$. Consequently the corresponding target rational prime is $q$, and the image of $D_v$ in $R$ lies in $C$. These changes of prolongations preserve $v'$ and $w'$, so they preserve the previously selected finite Frobenius elements $\delta$ and $\gamma$.

Put $H=T/U'\subset\mathcal G/U'$ and let $f$ be the residue degree of $v$ over $p$. Figure <a href="#fig:generation-primes" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="fig:generation-primes">1</a> records the primes involved. Here $\delta=\mathop{\mathrm{Fr}}(v'/p)$ and $\gamma=\mathop{\mathrm{Fr}}(w'/q)$ belong to the finite Galois groups over $\mathbb Q$. The symbols $\mathop{\mathrm{Fr}}_{v'}$ and $\mathop{\mathrm{Fr}}_{w'}$ used below instead denote local arithmetic Frobenius over $F'$ and $L$, respectively.

<a id="eq:residue-degree-intersection"></a>
In the decomposition group $\langle\delta\rangle\subset\mathop{\mathrm{Gal}}(F'/\mathbb Q)$ we have $$\label{eq:residue-degree-intersection}
 \langle\delta\rangle\cap H=\langle\delta^f\rangle,
 \qquad
 f=\min\{j\geq1:\delta^j\in H\}.$$ Indeed, the residue degree is the orbit size of the distinguished coset in the action of $\langle\delta\rangle$ on $\mathop{\mathrm{Gal}}(F'/\mathbb Q)/H$. This description does not require $H$ to be normal. In particular $f$ is constant on the chosen infinite set. The homomorphism $\beta$ induces $H\to R$, since $\beta(U')\subset P_L$; define $$d=\beta(\delta^f)\in R.$$ This notation is independent of the lift of $\delta^f$ to $T$. A local lift of rational Frobenius to the $f$th power is a Frobenius lift at $v$, so the preceding containment gives $d\in C$.

<a id="eq:source-average-zero"></a>
We claim that $D=\langle d\rangle$ equals $C$. Suppose that $D<C$, and choose $x$ as in <a href="#eq:separating-character" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:separating-character">[eq:separating-character]</a>. Pullback along $\beta|_{U'}$ gives a linear map $$j:X_{\ell}(L)\longrightarrow X_{\ell}(F'),\qquad y=j(x).$$ The map $j$ is injective: its defining homomorphism has open image in $P_L$, and an additive $\mathbb Q_\ell$-valued character vanishing on an open subgroup vanishes everywhere. Frobenius evaluation at $w'$ is invariant under $\gamma$, because $\gamma$ fixes $w'$ and acts trivially by conjugation on its unramified local quotient. Therefore $$x(\mathop{\mathrm{Fr}}_{w'})=(E_Cx)(\mathop{\mathrm{Fr}}_{w'})=0.$$ The character $x$ is unramified at $w'$, so this equality says that it vanishes on the entire decomposition group there. It follows that $y(\mathop{\mathrm{Fr}}_{v'})=0$ for every prime in our infinite set. Proposition <a href="#prop:character-detection" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:character-detection">9</a>(ii) now gives $$\label{eq:source-average-zero}
 E_{\langle\delta\rangle}y=0.$$

To see why this contradicts the choice of $x$, write $a=|\langle\delta\rangle|$ and $Y=j(X_{\ell}(L))$. Pullback is $T$-equivariant, where the action on the target character space is through $\beta$. In particular $y$ is fixed by $N$. In the sum defining <a href="#eq:source-average-zero" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:source-average-zero">[eq:source-average-zero]</a>, the powers $\delta^j$ belonging to $H$ are exactly those for which $f\mid j$, by <a href="#eq:residue-degree-intersection" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:residue-degree-intersection">[eq:residue-degree-intersection]</a>. Each such power acts as the identity on $y$, because $x$ is $D$-invariant. If $f\nmid j$, choose a lift $t$ of $\delta^j$ to $\mathcal G$. Then $t\notin T$, and $\delta^jy$ is fixed by $tNt^{-1}$. Multiplying <a href="#eq:source-average-zero" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:source-average-zero">[eq:source-average-zero]</a> by $a$ thus yields $$0=\frac af\,y+\sum_{\substack{0\leq j<a\\f\nmid j}}\delta^jy,
  \qquad
  y\in\sum_{i=1}^rX_{\ell}(F')^{N_i}.$$

It remains to return this membership to the target character space. The finite group $U/U'$ acts on $X_{\ell}(F')$, and its subspace $Y$ is stable. Semisimplicity supplies a $U$-equivariant projection $$\pi:X_{\ell}(F')\longrightarrow Y.$$ Every $N_i$ lies in $U$, so $\pi$ carries $N_i$-invariants into $Y^{N_i}$. Equivariance and injectivity of $j$ give $$Y^{N_i}=j\bigl(X_{\ell}(L)^{H_i}\bigr).$$ Applying $\pi$ to the displayed membership and then applying $j^{-1}$ contradicts the last condition in <a href="#eq:separating-character" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:separating-character">[eq:separating-character]</a>. Only $U$-equivariance of the projection is used; no normality of $T$ in $\mathcal G$ is needed. Thus $D=C$, proving the proposition. ◻

</details>

#### Surjectivity and cyclotomic logarithms

The generation statement now gives the two properties needed in the next stage. The first removes any restriction on the target image. For the second, a congruence condition on $\ell$ forces the source primes supplied by the proposition to have residue degree one, so the character-detection theorem applies.

<a id="stmt-thm-normalized-map"></a>
<div id="thm:normalized-map" class="theorem">

<a id="eq:cyclotomic-logarithms"></a>
**Theorem 14**. *The extension $\beta:G_k\to P$ is surjective. Put $$m_0=[k:\mathbb Q]!,\qquad
 \mathcal L=\{\ell:\ell\text{ is an odd prime and }
                    \ell\equiv1\pmod{m_0}\}.$$ The set $\mathcal L$ is infinite, and for every $\ell\in\mathcal L$, $$\label{eq:cyclotomic-logarithms}
 \log\chi_\ell\circ\beta=\log\chi_{k,\ell}
 \quad\text{as additive characters on }G_k.$$ Here $\chi_\ell$ is the $\ell$-adic cyclotomic character on $P$, and $\chi_{k,\ell}$ is the ordinary cyclotomic character on $G_k$.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-thm-normalized-map"><summary>Proof of stmt-thm-normalized-map</summary>

*Proof.* Let $R_0$ be any finite quotient of $P$ and let $\gamma_0\in R_0$. Proposition <a href="#prop:frobenius-generation" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:frobenius-generation">13</a> produces an element $d$ in the image of $\beta$ in a further quotient $R$ such that $\langle d\rangle=\langle\gamma\rangle$, where $\gamma$ lifts $\gamma_0$. Thus $\gamma$, and hence $\gamma_0$, belongs to the image. Since $\beta(T)$ is compact, surjectivity onto every finite quotient implies $\beta(T)=P$.

<a id="eq:generating-norm"></a>
Dirichlet’s theorem on primes in arithmetic progressions shows that $\mathcal L$ is infinite. Fix $\ell\in\mathcal L$, take $$R_0=\mathop{\mathrm{Gal}}(\mathbb Q(\mu_\ell)/\mathbb Q)\simeq\mathbb F_\ell^\times,$$ and choose $\gamma_0$ to generate this cyclic group. The proposition gives infinitely many active primes $v$ of $k$ over distinct rational primes $p\ne\ell$, for which the image of a Frobenius lift $\sigma_v$ acts on $\mu_\ell$ by a generator. Let $q$ be the corresponding target rational prime and let $f=[\kappa(v):\mathbb F_p]$, so $Nv=p^f$. If $a(\sigma_v)\in\widehat{\mathbb Z}$ is the target residue exponent, Lemma <a href="#lem:norm-identity" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:norm-identity">7</a> gives $$\label{eq:generating-norm}
  p^f=q^{a(\sigma_v)}\quad\text{in }\mathbb Z_\ell^\times.$$ Its reduction modulo $\ell$ says that $p^f$ generates $\mathbb F_\ell^\times$. In particular $\gcd(f,\ell-1)=1$. But $1\leq f\leq[k:\mathbb Q]$, and every prime divisor of any $f>1$ in this range divides $m_0$, hence divides $\ell-1$. Consequently $f=1$.

Taking logarithms in <a href="#eq:generating-norm" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:generating-norm">[eq:generating-norm]</a> gives $$(\log\chi_\ell\circ\beta)(\sigma_v)
   =\log p
   =\log\chi_{k,\ell}(\sigma_v).$$ The difference is an element of $X_{\ell}(k)$ vanishing at infinitely many primes of absolute residue degree one. Proposition <a href="#prop:character-detection" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:character-detection">9</a>(i) proves <a href="#eq:cyclotomic-logarithms" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:cyclotomic-logarithms">[eq:cyclotomic-logarithms]</a>. ◻

</details>

<a id="sec:matching"></a>
### Matching residue characteristics

We now show that almost every prime of $\mathbb Q(i)$ above a split rational prime has an active partner of the same residue characteristic. The auxiliary prime defining activity may vary with the pair. Two finiteness statements make this possible: almost every such target prime has only finitely many possible partners, and the number of target primes missed at a fixed auxiliary prime admits a uniform bound.

Keep the surjection $\beta:G_k\to P$ and the set $\mathcal L$ from Theorem <a href="#thm:normalized-map" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:normalized-map">14</a>, and put $$B=\mathbb Q(i),\qquad G_K=\beta^{-1}(P_B),\qquad
 \beta_B=\beta|_{G_K}:G_K\twoheadrightarrow P_B.$$ Thus $K/k$ is quadratic. An *active pair at $\ell$* in this section means a source prime $v$ of $K$ active at $\ell$ for $\beta_B$, together with its corresponding prime $w$ of $B$ from Theorem <a href="#thm:local-correspondence" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:local-correspondence">5</a>. In particular, both primes lie away from $\ell$. For a prime $w$ of $B$, define $$\mathcal V_w=
 \{v:\text{$(v,w)$ is an active pair at some $\ell\in\mathcal L$}\}.$$

<a id="pm-h-18"></a>
#### Finite fibers and a uniform bound on missed primes

<a id="stmt-lem-finite-fibers"></a>
<div id="lem:finite-fibers" class="lemma">

**Lemma 15**. *For all but finitely many primes $w$ of $B$ above rational primes split in $B$, the set $\mathcal V_w$ is finite.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-finite-fibers"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-finite-fibers</summary>

*Proof.* Fix $r\in\mathcal L$. Proposition <a href="#prop:signed-representation" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:signed-representation">10</a> supplies a nonzero character $\psi\in X_{r}(B)$ on which $\mathop{\mathrm{Gal}}(B/\mathbb Q)$ acts by its nontrivial character. By Proposition <a href="#prop:character-detection" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:character-detection">9</a>(i), $\psi(\mathop{\mathrm{Fr}}_w)$ is nonzero at all but finitely many degree-one primes $w\nmid r$. Fix such a $w$ above a split rational prime $q\ne r$, and put $$x_0=(\log\chi_r)|_{P_B},\qquad
 z=\frac{\psi(\mathop{\mathrm{Fr}}_w)}{\log_r q}.$$ The denominator is nonzero: the kernel of the logarithm on $\mathbb Z_r^\times$ consists of roots of unity, and the positive rational integer $q$ is not a root of unity. Thus $z\ne0$, and $\psi-zx_0$ vanishes on the decomposition group at $w$, since it is unramified there and vanishes on Frobenius.

Consider its pullback $$y=\beta_B^*(\psi-zx_0)\in X_{r}(K).$$ The pullback of $\psi$ transforms by sign under $G_k/G_K$. Its corestriction to $k$ is therefore zero: restricting that corestriction back to $K$ gives the sum of the two translates, and restriction is injective for additive characters with characteristic-zero coefficients. Theorem <a href="#thm:normalized-map" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:normalized-map">14</a> identifies $\beta_B^*x_0$ with $\log\chi_{K,r}$. Consequently $$\mathop{\mathrm{cor}}_{K/\mathbb Q}(y)
 =-z[K:\mathbb Q]\log\chi_{\mathbb Q,r}\ne0.$$ For every $v\in\mathcal V_w$ away from $r$, decomposition containment gives $y(\mathop{\mathrm{Fr}}_v)=0$, regardless of which auxiliary prime makes the pair active. Proposition <a href="#prop:character-detection" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:character-detection">9</a>(iii) permits only finitely many such $v$. There are also only finitely many primes of $K$ above $r$, proving the assertion. ◻

</details>

For $\ell\in\mathcal L$, let $\mathcal M_\ell$ be the set of primes of $B$ which occur in no active pair at $\ell$. This includes the primes above $\ell$.

We seek a bound for $\#\mathcal M_\ell$ depending only on $[K:\mathbb Q]$. The proof will place independent classes attached to missed primes in one character space of an $S$-unit group. We first record the standard cohomological identification of that group, including the completion step; see also <a id="pm-cite-24"></a>[[neukirch1999](#ref-neukirch1999)].

<a id="stmt-lem-kummer-unramified"></a>
<div id="lem:kummer-unramified" class="lemma">

<a id="eq:unramified-kummer"></a>
**Lemma 16** (Unramified Kummer classes). *Let $F$ be a number field, let $\ell$ be a rational prime, and let $S$ be a finite set of places of $F$ containing all places above $\ell$ and all archimedean places. Then Kummer theory induces a natural isomorphism $$\label{eq:unramified-kummer}
 H^1_{\mathrm{cont}}(G_F,\mathbb Q_\ell(1))
 _{\text{unramified outside }S}
 \simeq \mathcal O_{F,S}^{\times}\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathbb Q_\ell.$$ It is equivariant for every group of field automorphisms of $F$ preserving $S$, with the natural action on cohomology combining conjugation on $G_F$ and the action on the Tate twist.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-kummer-unramified"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-kummer-unramified</summary>

*Proof.* Integral Kummer theory gives $$H^1_{\mathrm{cont}}(G_F,\mathbb Z_\ell(1))
 \simeq\varprojlim_n F^\times/(F^\times)^{\ell^n}.$$ The inverse-limit assertion follows from the finite-level Kummer isomorphisms; the degree-zero groups are finite and satisfy the Mittag–Leffler condition. Every continuous rational cocycle has bounded image, since $G_F$ is compact, and hence becomes integral after multiplication by one power of $\ell$. The same observation for coboundaries gives rationalization of this integral cohomology. At a prime outside $S$, inertia acts trivially on $\mathbb Z_\ell(1)$, and its Kummer evaluation is the valuation times the tame $\ell$-adic character. These integral inertia homomorphisms are torsion-free. Thus unramifiedness after rationalization is exactly the vanishing of all integral valuation coordinates outside $S$.

For completeness, put $U_S=\mathcal O_{F,S}^\times$ and let $$H_n=\{[a]\in F^\times/(F^\times)^{\ell^n}:
       \operatorname{ord}_v(a)\equiv0\pmod{\ell^n}
       \text{ for every }v\notin S\}.$$ Writing $\mathop{\mathrm{Cl}}_S(F)$ for the $S$-ideal class group, there is an exact sequence <a id="eq:s-unit-completion"></a>$$\label{eq:s-unit-completion}
 0\longrightarrow U_S/U_S^{\ell^n}
 \longrightarrow H_n
 \longrightarrow \mathop{\mathrm{Cl}}_S(F)[\ell^n]
 \longrightarrow0.$$ The last map sends $[a]$ to the ideal class of $\operatorname{div}_S(a)/\ell^n$, where $\operatorname{div}_S$ records the valuations outside $S$. The transition from level $n+1$ to level $n$ induces multiplication by $\ell$ on these obstruction classes. Since $\mathop{\mathrm{Cl}}_S(F)$ is finite, their inverse limit is zero. It follows that the kernel of all outside-$S$ valuations in the completed multiplicative group is precisely $\varprojlim_n U_S/U_S^{\ell^n}$. The argument takes place in the full completed multiplicative group; it does not assume finite valuation support for its elements. Finally $U_S$ is finitely generated, so rationalizing its completion gives $U_S\otimes\mathbb Q_\ell$. This proves <a href="#eq:unramified-kummer" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:unramified-kummer">[eq:unramified-kummer]</a>. All the constructions commute with field automorphisms preserving $S$, giving the asserted equivariance. ◻

</details>

Uchida’s proof of target-side finiteness associates Kummer classes to missed primes and bounds them by a unit rank <a id="pm-cite-25"></a>[[uchida1981](#ref-uchida1981), Proposition 1]. We refine that argument by keeping the classes in one character space of a cyclic extension. The multiplicity of this character has a bound independent of $\ell$, even when the extension degree grows.

<a id="stmt-prop-uniform-missed-primes"></a>
<div id="prop:uniform-missed-primes" class="proposition">

**Proposition 17**. *For every $\ell\in\mathcal L$, $$\#\{w\in\mathcal M_\ell:w\nmid\ell\}\le 2[K:\mathbb Q],
 \qquad
 \#\mathcal M_\ell\le 2[K:\mathbb Q]+2.$$*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-prop-uniform-missed-primes"><summary>Proof of stmt-prop-uniform-missed-primes</summary>

*Proof.* Fix $\ell\in\mathcal L$ and any finite set $J\subset\mathcal M_\ell$ of primes away from $\ell$. For each $w\in J$, choose $a_w\in B^\times$ and $h_w>0$ with $$(a_w)=w^{h_w}.$$ Its compatible Kummer cocycles define a class $$\kappa_w\in H^1_{\mathrm{cont}}(P_B,\mathbb Q_\ell(1)).$$ They factor through $P_B$ because $\mathbb Q^s$ contains the roots of unity and all the required radicals. Valuations at the distinct primes $w$ show that these classes are linearly independent. Each is unramified at every finite prime other than $w$ and the primes above $\ell$.

Write $$\rho=\chi_\ell\circ\beta_B,\qquad
 \epsilon=\rho\chi_{K,\ell}^{-1}.$$ By Theorem <a href="#thm:normalized-map" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:normalized-map">14</a>, $\epsilon$ takes values in $\mu_{\ell-1}\subset\mathbb Q_\ell^\times$. Let $K'/K$ be the cyclic extension fixed by its kernel, and let $\Delta=\mathop{\mathrm{Gal}}(K'/K)$. Pullback along the surjection $\beta_B$ is injective on $H^1$, with pulled-back coefficient module $\mathbb Q_\ell(\rho)$. Restriction to $G_{K'}$ remains injective: its kernel is a first cohomology group of the finite group $\Delta$, which vanishes in characteristic zero. We therefore obtain independent classes $$c_w\in H^1_{\mathrm{cont}}(G_{K'},\mathbb Q_\ell(1))
 \qquad(w\in J).$$ Under the natural $\Delta$-action on this cohomology, they all lie in the $\epsilon^{-1}$ eigenspace. Indeed the original coefficient module is $\mathbb Q_\ell(1)\otimes\epsilon$, so invariance for its twisted action becomes the eigenvalue $\epsilon^{-1}$ after restriction.

We claim that every $c_w$ is unramified away from $\ell$. Suppose that it is ramified at a prime $u\nmid\ell$ of $K'$, and let $v$ be the prime below it in $K$. The cyclotomic module is trivial on $I_u$, so the restricted cocycle is a nonzero continuous homomorphism from $I_u$ to the additive group $\mathbb Q_\ell(1)$. Choose a pro-$\ell$ Sylow subgroup $J_u\subset I_u$ on which it is nonzero, and a pro-$\ell$ Sylow subgroup $J_v\subset I_v$ containing $J_u$. Then $\beta_B(J_v)\ne1$, so $v$ is active at $\ell$. Theorem <a href="#thm:local-correspondence" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:local-correspondence">5</a> sends $J_v$ into inertia at a corresponding prime $t\nmid\ell$ of $B$. Since $t$ occurs in an active pair, $t\notin J$, whereas $\kappa_w$ is unramified at $t$. Inertia at $t$ acts trivially on the coefficient module, so the Kummer cocycle itself vanishes on that inertia group. Its pullback therefore vanishes on $J_u$, a contradiction. This proves the claim.

Let $S$ consist of the archimedean places of $K'$ and its primes above $\ell$. By Lemma <a href="#lem:kummer-unramified" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:kummer-unramified">16</a>, the classes $c_w$ give independent elements of the $\epsilon^{-1}$ eigenspace in $\mathcal O_{K',S}^{\times}\otimes\mathbb Q_\ell$. It remains to bound this eigenspace. The equivariant Dirichlet $S$-unit theorem <a id="pm-cite-26"></a>[[bass1966](#ref-bass1966), Section 3] gives the character of the full $S$-unit module as $$[\mathcal O_{K',S}^{\times}\otimes\mathbb Q_\ell]
   =[\mathbb Q_\ell[S]]-[\mathbb Q_\ell].$$ Each $\Delta$-orbit in $S$ contributes at most one copy of the one-dimensional character $\epsilon^{-1}$ to the permutation representation: for a stabilizer $H\subset\Delta$, its multiplicity in $\mathbb Q_\ell[\Delta/H]$ is one if it is trivial on $H$, and zero otherwise. There are $$\#(S/\Delta)
 =r_1(K)+r_2(K)+\#\{v\text{ of }K:v\mid\ell\}
 \le2[K:\mathbb Q]$$ such orbits. Subtracting the trivial representation cannot increase this multiplicity. Hence the independent classes $c_w$ lie in a space of dimension at most $2[K:\mathbb Q]$, and $\#J\le2[K:\mathbb Q]$. Since $J$ was arbitrary, this bounds all missed primes away from $\ell$. There are at most two primes of $B$ above $\ell$, giving the second bound. ◻

</details>

The bound depends on $K$, but neither on $\ell$ nor on the degree of the cyclic extension $K'/K$. We can therefore choose one auxiliary prime that excludes every possible partner of several specified target primes, and contradict this bound. The following elementary Kummer construction provides that choice.

<a id="pm-h-19"></a>
#### Simultaneous separation by power residues

<a id="stmt-lem-power-separation"></a>
<div id="lem:power-separation" class="lemma">

**Lemma 18**. *Let $m,d\ge1$, let $q_1,\ldots,q_n$ be distinct rational primes, and for each $j$ let $\mathcal P_j$ be a finite set of rational primes not containing $q_j$. Given any finite set of rational primes to avoid, there are infinitely many odd primes $\ell\equiv1\pmod m$ outside that set and all the displayed prime sets such that $$p^f\bmod\ell\notin\langle q_j\bmod\ell\rangle
 \quad
 (1\le j\le n,\ p\in\mathcal P_j,\ 1\le f\le d).$$ The sets $\mathcal P_j$ may overlap, and may contain $q_i$ for $i\ne j$.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-power-separation"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-power-separation</summary>

*Proof.* Choose distinct auxiliary primes $s_j$ larger than $m$, $d$, and every prime in the lists, and put $$C=\mathbb Q(\zeta_{m\prod_j s_j}).$$ For each $j$, the classes of the distinct rational primes in $\{q_j\}\cup\mathcal P_j$ are independent in $C^\times/(C^\times)^{s_j}$. Indeed, at any prime in those lists, the ramification index in $C/\mathbb Q$ divides $[\mathbb Q(\zeta_m):\mathbb Q]$: the other cyclotomic conductors are prime to that prime. This index is prime to $s_j$, and valuations at the separate rational primes give the asserted independence.

Kummer theory therefore identifies the Galois group over $C$ of $$E_j=C\bigl(q_j^{1/s_j},\ p^{1/s_j}\ (p\in\mathcal P_j)\bigr)$$ with an elementary abelian $s_j$-group having one independent coordinate for each listed prime. Choose $\sigma_j\in\mathop{\mathrm{Gal}}(E_j/C)$ which fixes $q_j^{1/s_j}$ and acts nontrivially on every $p^{1/s_j}$ with $p\in\mathcal P_j$. The extensions $E_j/C$ are linearly disjoint, since their degrees are powers of distinct primes. Thus these automorphisms combine to an element $\sigma\in\mathop{\mathrm{Gal}}(E/C)$, where $E$ is their compositum.

The extension $E/\mathbb Q$ is Galois. Moreover, conjugation in $\mathop{\mathrm{Gal}}(E/\mathbb Q)$ preserves whether each of the specified radical coordinates of $\sigma$ is trivial: a conjugation only raises its root-of-unity multiplier to a power prime to the corresponding $s_j$. Chebotarev’s theorem supplies infinitely many rational primes $\ell$ with Frobenius in the conjugacy class of $\sigma$, avoiding the prescribed finite set, all listed primes, and $2$. They split completely in $C$, so $\ell\equiv1\pmod m$ and $\ell\equiv1\pmod{s_j}$. At these primes, $q_j$ is an $s_j$th power modulo $\ell$ and every $p\in\mathcal P_j$ is not.

The quotient of $\mathbb F_\ell^\times$ by its $s_j$th powers has prime order $s_j$. A nontrivial class stays nontrivial on raising it to any exponent $1\le f\le d<s_j$. Every power of $q_j$, however, is an $s_j$th power. This proves the displayed separation. In particular, overlaps between different lists impose no conflict: their conditions belong to linearly disjoint Kummer extensions with different prime exponents. ◻

</details>

<a id="stmt-prop-same-characteristic"></a>
<div id="prop:same-characteristic" class="proposition">

**Proposition 19**. *For all but finitely many primes $w$ of $B$ above rational primes $q$ split in $B$, there exist a prime $v$ of $K$ above the same $q$ and an auxiliary prime $\ell\in\mathcal L$ for which $(v,w)$ is an active pair for $\beta_B$.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-prop-same-characteristic"><summary>Proof of stmt-prop-same-characteristic</summary>

*Proof.* Suppose that there are infinitely many exceptions. Lemma <a href="#lem:finite-fibers" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:finite-fibers">15</a> allows us to choose $$w_1,\ldots,w_n,\qquad n>2[K:\mathbb Q]+2,$$ among them, above distinct split rational primes $q_1,\ldots,q_n$, with every $\mathcal V_{w_j}$ finite. Let $\mathcal P_j$ be the set of rational residue characteristics of the primes in $\mathcal V_{w_j}$. The failure of the asserted conclusion means $q_j\notin\mathcal P_j$.

<a id="eq:norm-separation"></a>
Apply Lemma <a href="#lem:power-separation" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:power-separation">18</a> with $m=m_0$ and $d=[K:\mathbb Q]$. It gives $\ell\in\mathcal L$, away from all the primes involved, such that $$\label{eq:norm-separation}
 Nv\bmod\ell\notin\langle q_j\bmod\ell\rangle
 \qquad(v\in\mathcal V_{w_j},\ 1\le j\le n),$$ because $Nv=p^f$ with $p\in\mathcal P_j$ and $1\le f\le[K:\mathbb Q]$. If $w_j$ occurred in an active pair at this $\ell$, its source prime would lie in $\mathcal V_{w_j}$. Applying Lemma <a href="#lem:norm-identity" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:norm-identity">7</a> to a lift of source Frobenius and reducing modulo $\ell$ would give $$Nv\bmod\ell\in\langle Nw_j\bmod\ell\rangle
 =\langle q_j\bmod\ell\rangle,$$ contrary to <a href="#eq:norm-separation" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:norm-separation">[eq:norm-separation]</a>. All $n$ primes $w_j$ therefore belong to $\mathcal M_\ell$, contradicting Proposition <a href="#prop:uniform-missed-primes" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:uniform-missed-primes">17</a>. ◻

</details>

<a id="sec:identification"></a>
### Identifying the homomorphism

We now turn the prime correspondence into equality of group homomorphisms. The argument uses one fixed coefficient prime $r$, although the auxiliary prime witnessing activity is allowed to vary. This distinction is what makes the correspondences constructed in Section <a href="#sec:matching" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:matching">5</a> sufficient.

<a id="eq:fixed-r-log"></a>
Retain the map $\beta:T=G_k\to P$, the infinite set $\mathcal L$, and the fields $B=\mathbb Q(i)$ and $K$ from Sections <a href="#sec:generation" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:generation">4</a>–<a href="#sec:matching" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:matching">5</a>. Thus $G_K=\beta^{-1}(P_B)$, and Theorem <a href="#thm:normalized-map" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:normalized-map">14</a> gives $$\label{eq:fixed-r-log}
 \log\chi_r\circ\beta=\log\chi_{k,r}\qquad(r\in\mathcal L).$$ Write $\sigma:T\to P$ for ordinary restriction.

<a id="stmt-prop-identify-normalized"></a>
<div id="prop:identify-normalized" class="proposition">

**Proposition 20**. *There is an element $a\in P$ such that $\beta=\mathop{\mathrm{Ad}}(a)\circ\sigma$ on $T$.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-prop-identify-normalized"><summary>Proof of stmt-prop-identify-normalized</summary>

*Proof.* Fix a finite Galois extension $L/\mathbb Q$ inside $\mathbb Q^s$ containing $B$, and put $R=\mathop{\mathrm{Gal}}(L/\mathbb Q)$. Choose a finite Galois extension $F'/\mathbb Q$ containing $K$ and $L$ such that $\beta(G_{F'})\subset P_L$. This is possible by taking the core in $\mathcal G$ of the appropriate open subgroup of $T$. Fix $r\in\mathcal L$.

The maps $\beta$ and $\sigma$, restricted to $G_{F'}$, give two pullbacks <a id="eq:two-pullbacks"></a>$$\label{eq:two-pullbacks}
 i_\beta,i_\sigma:X_{r}(L)\longrightarrow X_{r}(F').$$ Both are injective: their underlying homomorphisms have open image, and an additive $\mathbb Q_r$-valued character vanishing on a finite-index subgroup is zero. For $a\in P$ let $\sigma_a=\mathop{\mathrm{Ad}}(a)\circ\sigma$ and let $i_a$ be its pullback. Since $L/\mathbb Q$ is Galois, $\sigma_a(G_{F'})\subset P_L$. The map $i_a$ depends only on $a$ modulo $P_L$, because inner automorphisms of $P_L$ act trivially on additive characters.

*Frobenius evaluations.* Let $q\ne r$ range over rational primes split completely in $F'$. By Proposition <a href="#prop:same-characteristic" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:same-characteristic">19</a>, for all but finitely many such $q$ there is a prime $v$ of $K$ above $q$ whose decomposition group maps into a decomposition group at a prime $w$ of $B$ above the same $q$. Choose a prime $v'$ of $F'$ above $v$ and compatible prolongations. Then $$\beta(D_{v'})\subset D_u\subset P_L$$ for a prime $u$ of $L$ above $w$. Every prime here has absolute residue degree one, because $q$ splits completely in $F'$.

Choose a lift $h$ of arithmetic Frobenius in $D_{v'}$. Let $a_q\in\widehat{\mathbb Z}$ be the residue exponent of $\beta(h)$ at $u$. Equation <a href="#eq:fixed-r-log" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:fixed-r-log">[eq:fixed-r-log]</a> gives $$(a_q)_r\log_r q=\log_r q.$$ The number $\log_r q$ is nonzero: $q\in\mathbb Z_r^\times$ is a positive rational integer larger than one and is not a root of unity. Consequently $(a_q)_r=1$. Every $x\in X_{r}(L)$ is unramified at $u$, so <a id="eq:matched-evaluation"></a>$$\label{eq:matched-evaluation}
 i_\beta(x)(\mathop{\mathrm{Fr}}_{v'})=x(\mathop{\mathrm{Fr}}_u).$$ This calculation uses only decomposition containment from the active correspondence. It does not require that its auxiliary prime be $r$.

<a id="eq:equal-pullbacks"></a>
Under ordinary restriction, the same Frobenius maps to Frobenius at another prime of $L$ above $q$. An element of $R$ carries this prime to $u$. Choose a lift $a\in P$ of that element. Then $$i_\beta(x)(\mathop{\mathrm{Fr}}_{v'})=i_a(x)(\mathop{\mathrm{Fr}}_{v'})
 \qquad\text{for every }x\in X_{r}(L).$$ The choice of $a$ modulo $P_L$ may initially depend on $q$. Since $R$ is finite, an infinite set of distinct $q$ uses one fixed choice. For each $x$, the difference $i_\beta(x)-i_a(x)$ therefore vanishes at infinitely many degree-one primes of $F'$. Proposition <a href="#prop:character-detection" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:character-detection">9</a> gives $$\label{eq:equal-pullbacks}
 i_\beta=i_a.$$

*From characters to the finite group.* The field $F'/\mathbb Q$ is Galois, so $T$ acts on $X_{r}(F')$ by conjugation. The two pullbacks intertwine this action with the respective actions on $X_{r}(L)$ induced by $\beta$ and $\sigma_a$. If $i$ denotes their common injective value in <a href="#eq:equal-pullbacks" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:equal-pullbacks">[eq:equal-pullbacks]</a>, then for $t\in T$ and $x\in X_{r}(L)$, $$i\bigl(\beta(t)\cdot x\bigr)
   =t\cdot i(x)
   =i\bigl(\sigma_a(t)\cdot x\bigr).$$ The field $L$ is totally imaginary because it contains $B$. By Proposition <a href="#prop:signed-representation" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:signed-representation">10</a>, $R$ acts faithfully on $X_{r}(L)$. Hence $$\beta(t)\bmod P_L=\sigma_a(t)\bmod P_L\qquad(t\in T).$$

*One conjugation for all finite quotients.* For each such $L$, let $$C_L=\{a\in P:\ \beta(t)\equiv\mathop{\mathrm{Ad}}(a)(\sigma(t))\pmod{P_L}
                    \text{ for every }t\in T\}.$$ We have proved that $C_L$ is nonempty. It is closed, and indeed clopen, since the defining condition depends only on $a$ modulo $P_L$. If $L$ contains finitely many fields $L_1,\ldots,L_s$, then $C_L\subset\bigcap_j C_{L_j}$. Composita thus give the finite-intersection property. Compactness of $P$ supplies an element in every $C_L$. The extensions $L$ containing $B$ are cofinal among finite Galois subextensions of $\mathbb Q^s/\mathbb Q$, so this element gives the asserted equality in $P$. ◻

</details>

We remove the normalizer extension and return to the original source.

<a id="stmt-thm-prosolvable-rigidity"></a>
<div id="thm:prosolvable-rigidity" class="theorem">

**Theorem 21**. *Let $F$ be a number field in $\overline{\mathbb Q}$. Every continuous open homomorphism $\varphi:G_F\to P$ is conjugate in $P$ to ordinary restriction.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-thm-prosolvable-rigidity"><summary>Proof of stmt-thm-prosolvable-rigidity</summary>

*Proof.* As in Lemma <a href="#lem:normalizer-extension" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:normalizer-extension">11</a>, choose an open normal subgroup $U$ of $\mathcal G$ contained in $G_F$, and extend $\varphi|_U$ to the normalizer of its kernel. Proposition <a href="#prop:identify-normalized" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:identify-normalized">20</a> shows that, after one conjugation of $\varphi$, it agrees on $U$ with ordinary restriction $\sigma:G_F\to P$.

For $g\in G_F$ and $u\in U$, normality of $U$ gives $$\varphi(g)\sigma(u)\varphi(g)^{-1}
 =\varphi(gug^{-1})
 =\sigma(gug^{-1})
 =\sigma(g)\sigma(u)\sigma(g)^{-1}.$$ Thus $\sigma(g)^{-1}\varphi(g)$ centralizes $\sigma(U)=\varphi(U)$, an open subgroup of $P$. Lemma <a href="#lem:centralizer" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:centralizer">4</a> makes this element trivial. The equality holds for every $g\in G_F$. ◻

</details>

<details id="proof-stmt-thm-prosolvable-rigidity-2"><summary>Proof of stmt-thm-prosolvable-rigidity</summary>

<a id="eq:final-composite"></a>
*Proof of Theorem <a href="#thm:main" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:main">1</a>.* Choose copies of the given fields inside $\overline{\mathbb Q}$. By Lemma <a href="#lem:solvable-closure" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:solvable-closure">2</a>, each $E_i$ contains $\mathbb Q^s$. Form the composite $$\label{eq:final-composite}
 G_{F_1}\longrightarrow G_1\xrightarrow{\alpha}G_2
     \longrightarrow P,$$ where the outer arrows are restrictions. The first is surjective. The last has open image, since its composite with $G_{F_2}\twoheadrightarrow G_2$ is ordinary restriction from an open subgroup of $\mathcal G$ to $P$. Continuous surjective homomorphisms between profinite groups are open, so <a href="#eq:final-composite" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:final-composite">[eq:final-composite]</a> is open.

By Theorem <a href="#thm:prosolvable-rigidity" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:prosolvable-rigidity">21</a>, this composite is conjugate to ordinary restriction. Cyclotomic characters are unchanged by conjugation. All roots of unity lie in $\mathbb Q^s$, so the full cyclotomic characters factor through the groups in <a href="#eq:final-composite" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:final-composite">[eq:final-composite]</a>. Surjectivity of the first arrow therefore yields $$\chi_{E_2/F_2}\circ\alpha=\chi_{E_1/F_1}
 \quad\text{in }\widehat{\mathbb Z}^{\times}.$$ Theorem <a href="#thm:hoshi" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:hoshi">6</a> gives an equivariant embedding $E_2\hookrightarrow E_1$. Its uniqueness is Uchida’s theorem cited after Theorem <a href="#thm:hoshi" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:hoshi">6</a>. ◻

</details>
