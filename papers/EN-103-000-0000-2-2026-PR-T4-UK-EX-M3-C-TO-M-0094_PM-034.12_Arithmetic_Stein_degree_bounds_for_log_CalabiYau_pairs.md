## 1. Abstract and result contract

> Fix <i>d</i> ≥ 1 and <i>t</i> &gt; 0. Let $`(X,B)`$ be an ordinary projective log canonical ℚ-pair of dimension <i>d</i> over a characteristic-zero field <i>k</i>, with <i>X</i> normal and integral, $`H^0(X,\mathcal O_X)=k`$, <i>B</i> effective, and $`K_X+B\sim_{\mathbb Q}0`$. We prove that every prime component <i>S</i> of <i>B</i> with coefficient at least <i>t</i> satisfies $`[k_S:k]\leq N(d,t)`$, where <i>k</i><sub><i>S</i></sub> is the relative algebraic closure of <i>k</i> in $`k(S)`$. This also bounds the Stein degree of <i>S</i> over <i>k</i>, proving the contraction-to-a-point formulation of Birkar's Stein-degree conjecture for ordinary ℚ-pairs.

- **C-01** (theorem, [source](#stmt-thm-main)): For every integer $d\geq 1$ and real number $t>0$, there is an integer $N(d,t)\geq1$ with the following property. Let $k$ be any field of characteristic zero, and let $(X,B)$ be a projective log canonical $\mathbb Q$-pair such that $$\dim X=d,\qquad H^0(X,\mathcal O_X)=k,\qquad K_X+B\sim_{\mathbb Q}0.$$ Assume that $X$ is normal and integral and that $B$ is effective. If $S$ is a prime component of $B$ with coefficient at least $t$, then $$c(S/k)\leq N(d,t).$$ Consequently $$\operatorname{sdeg}(S/\operatorname{Spec}k)\leq \dim_k H^0(S^\nu,\mathcal O_{S^\nu}) =c(S/k)\leq N(d,t),$$ where $S^\nu$ is the normalization of $S$.
  - Explanation: Establishes a uniform integer upper bound N(d,t) on both the constant extension degree c(S/k) and the Stein degree over k for any prime boundary component S of coefficient at least t in a normal projective log canonical Q-pair (X,B) of dimension d with trivial log canonical class over a characteristic-zero field k.
- **C-02** (theorem, [source](#stmt-lem-constants)): Let $V$ be an integral $k$-variety.* 1. *The number $c(V/k)$ is the number of irreducible components of $V_{\overline{k}}$; these components form one Galois orbit. It is a birational invariant. If $V$ is proper, then $H^0(V,\mathcal O_V)\subseteq k_V$, with equality when $V$ is normal.* 2. *If $V\to Z$ is dominant and $Z$ is geometrically integral over $k$, then, for its generic fibre, $$\label{eq:generic-constants} c(V/k)\leq c(V_{\eta_Z}/k(Z)).$$* 3. *If $X$ is normal projective and $H^0(X,\mathcal O_X)=k$, then $X$ is geometrically integral. The normal base of a contraction from $X$ is also geometrically integral. Its generic fibre is normal, projective, and has global functions $k(Z)$.
  - Explanation: Characterizes the constant field degree c(V/k) as the number of geometric irreducible components forming a single Galois orbit, establishes its birational invariance and monotonicity under dominant morphisms to geometrically integral bases, and shows that contractions from normal projective varieties with global functions k have geometrically integral bases and normal generic fibres.
- **C-03** (theorem, [source](#stmt-lem-crepant)): Suppose $(V,D)$ is lc and $K_V+D\sim_{\mathbb Q}0$. Under a birational contraction or flip, push $D$ forward and keep compatible canonical divisors. Then the resulting pair is crepant to $(V,D)$, remains lc with effective boundary, and retains every specified relation $n(K_V+D)\sim0$.
  - Explanation: Shows that pushing forward the boundary divisor of a log canonical log Calabi-Yau pair under a birational contraction or flip yields a crepant log canonical pair with effective boundary that preserves every principal multiple relation n(K_V+D) ~ 0.
- **C-04** (construction, [source](#stmt-lem-complement)): Fix $d$ and a rational number $b\in(0,1)$. There is an integer $n=n(d,b)$ such that the following holds over every characteristic-zero field $k$. Suppose $V$ is a normal geometrically integral projective klt Fano variety of dimension $d$, $S$ is a $\mathbb Q$-Cartier prime divisor, $(V,bS)$ is lc, and $-K_V-bS$ is nef. Then there is an effective rational boundary $C\geq bS$ over $k$ with $$(V,C)\text{ lc},\qquad n(K_V+C)\sim0.$$ We may assume $nb\in\mathbb Z$.
  - Explanation: Descends bounded complements from the algebraic closure to an arbitrary characteristic-zero field k for a klt Fano variety V of dimension d with a Q-Cartier prime divisor S such that (V,bS) is lc and -K_V-bS is nef, producing an effective lc complement C >= bS over k of uniformly bounded index n(d,b).
- **C-05** (classification, [source](#stmt-lem-weights)): Let $W$ be smooth over an algebraically closed field, and let $H$ be a reduced simple normal crossings divisor. If a divisorial valuation $v$ satisfies $a(v,W,H)=0$, then its centre is a stratum. Moreover, $v$ is uniquely determined by that stratum and its positive integral values on the components of $H$ containing the stratum.
  - Explanation: Proves that any divisorial valuation of log discrepancy zero on a smooth variety with a reduced simple normal crossings divisor H over an algebraically closed field has a stratum of H as its centre and is uniquely determined by that stratum together with its positive integral values on the components containing the stratum.
- **C-06** (bound, [source](#stmt-cor-finite-low)): A klt rational pair has only finitely many divisorial valuations of log discrepancy less than one.
  - Explanation: Deduces from the stratum-and-weight characterization of log canonical places on a log resolution that any klt rational pair admits only finitely many divisorial valuations of log discrepancy strictly less than one.
- **C-07** (bound, [source](#stmt-lem-embedded)): Fix bounds for the ambient projective dimension, the degree of a normal geometrically integral projective variety $V$, and the degree of a reduced divisor $D\subset V$, where $D$ may be empty. There are constants $R,M$ depending only on these bounds such that, over the field of the embedded pair, one can find a projective resolution $p:W\to V$ and a geometrically simple normal crossings reduced divisor $H$ containing the exceptional locus and the strict transform of $D$, with $$b_2(W_{\bar k})\leq R, \qquad \#\{\text{geometric components and strata of }H\}\leq M.$$ One may use an $\ell$-adic second Betti number for any $\ell$.
  - Explanation: Establishes that for a projective pair (V,D) of bounded ambient dimension and degree, there exists a projective log resolution over the ground field of the pair whose geometric second Betti number and total number of geometric boundary components and strata are uniformly bounded by constants R and M.
- **C-08** (bound, [source](#stmt-prop-bab-orbits)): Fix an integer $q\geq1$, a real $\epsilon>0$, and an integer $n\geq1$. There is a finite number $A(q,\epsilon,n)$ with the following property. Let $Q/k$ be a normal geometrically integral projective $\epsilon$-lc Fano variety of dimension $q$, and suppose $$(Q,\Lambda)\text{ is lc},\qquad \Lambda\geq0, \qquad n(K_Q+\Lambda)\sim0.$$ Then every divisorial valuation $v$ with $a(v,Q,\Lambda)<1$ satisfies $$c(v/k)\leq A(q,\epsilon,n).$$
  - Explanation: Proves a uniform upper bound A(q,epsilon,n) on the constant extension degree c(v/k) of any divisorial valuation v with log discrepancy less than one on a q-dimensional geometrically integral epsilon-lc Fano variety Q carrying an effective complement of index n.
- **C-09** (construction, [source](#stmt-lem-klt-model)): For $(X,B)$ as in <a href="#thm:main" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:main">1</a>, there is a projective birational morphism $h:Y\to X$ with $Y$ $\mathbb Q$-factorial and klt such that the crepant boundary $B_Y$ is effective. The strict transform of every component of $B$ has its original coefficient.
  - Explanation: Constructs a projective birational morphism h: Y -> X from a Q-factorial klt variety Y such that the crepant pullback boundary B_Y is effective and preserves the coefficients of all strict transforms of components of B.
- **C-10** (construction, [source](#stmt-lem-positive-component)): Let $X$ be a $\mathbb Q$-factorial klt projective variety, $(X,B)$ an effective lc pair with $K_X+B\sim_{\mathbb Q}0$, and $S$ a component of coefficient at least $t>0$. Fix a rational $b$ with $0<b<t$. There is a sequence of birational MMP steps preserving $S$, followed by a Mori fibre contraction $X_1\to Z_1$, such that $S_1$ is ample over $Z_1$ and $(X_1,B_1)$ is an effective lc log Calabi–Yau pair. If $Z_1$ is a point and $H^0(X,\mathcal O_X)=k$, then $$Z_1=\operatorname{Spec}k,\qquad -K_{X_1}\text{ ample},\qquad -K_{X_1}-bS_1\text{ nef}.$$
  - Explanation: Runs a birational minimal model program positive on S to reach a Mori fibre contraction X_1 -> Z_1 where the strict transform S_1 is relatively ample, and shows that when Z_1 is a point, X_1 is Fano with -K_{X_1}-bS_1 nef.
- **C-11** (construction, [source](#stmt-lem-vertical)): Let $U\to Z$ be a contraction with $U$ projective and $\mathbb Q$-factorial of Fano type over $Z$. Let $P$ be a vertical prime divisor. There is a sequence of $(-P)$-negative MMP steps over $Z$ such that the final transform $P'$ survives on a $\mathbb Q$-factorial klt variety $U'$ and $-P'$ is semiample over $Z$. If $f:U'\to Z'$ is its semiample contraction, then $Z'\to Z$ is birational and for some integer $m>0$ and a nonzero effective Cartier divisor $T$ on $Z'$, $$\label{eq:pullback} mP'=f^*T.$$ The MMP is an isomorphism over the generic point of $Z$. Every horizontal prime $E$ therefore survives and meets $P'$; its normalization has a nonzero effective restricted divisor $P'\|_{E^\nu}$.
  - Explanation: Applies a relative (-P)-negative minimal model program over Z on a Fano-type contraction U -> Z so that a vertical prime divisor P becomes a rational multiple of the pullback of a nonzero effective Cartier divisor T on a birational base Z', forcing every horizontal prime E to intersect P'.
- **C-12** (theorem, [source](#stmt-lem-adjunction)): Let $(U,C)$ be an effective lc $\mathbb Q$-pair over $k$, let $E$ be a prime component of coefficient one, and suppose $n(K_U+C)\sim0$. On the normalization $\nu:E^\nu\to E$ there is an effective different $C_{E^\nu}$ such that $$(E^\nu,C_{E^\nu})\text{ is lc},\qquad n(K_{E^\nu}+C_{E^\nu})\sim0.$$ In particular, $nC_{E^\nu}$ is integral and every positive coefficient of $C_{E^\nu}$ is at least $1/n$. If $P\ne E$ is a $\mathbb Q$-Cartier prime component of $C$ of coefficient $\beta>0$, then $$\label{eq:diff-restriction} C_{E^\nu}\geq\beta P\|_{E^\nu}.$$
  - Explanation: Shows that divisorial adjunction on the normalization of a prime boundary component E of coefficient one in an effective lc Q-pair (U,C) with n(K_U+C) ~ 0 preserves the exact index n over k, guarantees that nC_{E^nu} is integral with positive coefficients at least 1/n, and bounds the different below by the restriction of any other Q-Cartier boundary component.
- **C-13** (bound, [source](#stmt-lem-incidence)): Let $U$ be a projective $\mathbb Q$-Gorenstein klt variety over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero, and let $(U,C)$ be lc with $C\geq0$. Given $b>0$, at most $2/b$ prime components of $C$ with coefficient at least $b$ can contain a fixed irreducible codimension-two subvariety of $U$.
  - Explanation: Bounds by 2/b the number of prime components of an effective lc boundary C with coefficient at least b that can contain a given irreducible codimension-two subvariety of a projective Q-Gorenstein klt variety U.

## 2. Definitions, validity and bounds

- **Stein degree sdeg(S/Spec k)** ([source](#pm-h-2)): For a proper integral variety $S$ over a field $k$, the Stein degree over $k$ is $$\operatorname{sdeg}(S/\operatorname{Spec}k)=\dim_k H^0(S,\mathcal O_S).$$
- **Constant field extension degree c(V/k)** ([source](#pm-h-2)): For any integral $k$-variety $V$, let $k_V$ be the relative algebraic closure of $k$ in its function field and put $$\label{eq:constants} c(V/k)=[k_V:k].$$
- **Contraction** ([source](#pm-h-3)): A contraction is a projective surjective morphism $f:V\to Z$ between normal varieties with $f_*\mathcal O_V=\mathcal O_Z$.
- **Log discrepancy a(P,X,B)** ([source](#pm-h-3)): We use log discrepancies: $$a(P,X,B)=1-\operatorname{coeff}_P B_W, \qquad K_W+B_W=p^*(K_X+B).$$
- **Log canonical (lc) and Kawamata log terminal (klt) pair** ([source](#pm-h-3)): The pair is log canonical (lc) if these numbers are nonnegative and Kawamata log terminal (klt) if they are positive.
- **Log canonical place (lc place)** ([source](#pm-h-3)): A divisorial valuation of log discrepancy zero is an *lc place*.
- **Epsilon-log canonical variety** ([source](#pm-h-3)): A variety is $\epsilon$-lc if the pair with zero boundary has all log discrepancies at least $\epsilon$.
- **Q-linear equivalence to zero** ([source](#pm-h-3)): The relation $\sim_{\mathbb Q}0$ means that a positive integral multiple is a principal Cartier divisor.
- **Variety of Fano type over Z** ([source](#pm-h-5)): Here Fano type over $Z$ means the existence of an effective rational boundary $\Delta$ with $(V,\Delta)$ klt and $-(K_V+\Delta)$ ample over $Z$.
- **Stratum of a simple normal crossings divisor** ([source](#pm-h-8)): A stratum of a reduced simple normal crossings divisor will mean an irreducible component of a nonempty intersection of its components, including a single component.
- **Inductive bound maximum M_{<d}(u)** ([source](#pm-h-15)): Write $$\label{eq:Mnotation} M_{<d}(u)=\max_{1\leq r<d}N(r,u),\qquad u>0.$$

## 4. Engineering Triptych

### Panel 1 - epistemic contract

- **I-01**: For every integer $d\geq 1$ and real number $t>0$, there is an integer $N(d,t)\geq1$ such that for any projective log canonical $\mathbb Q$-pair $(X,B)$ of dimension $d$ over a characteristic-zero field $k$ with $X$ normal and integral, $H^0(X,\mathcal O_X)=k$, $B$ effective, and $K_X+B\sim_{\mathbb Q}0$, every prime component $S$ of $B$ with coefficient at least $t$ has constant field degree and Stein degree over $k$ bounded above by that uniform integer.
- **I-02**: In dimension $d=1$ on a smooth projective geometrically integral curve $X$ with $K_X+B\sim_{\mathbb Q}0$ and effective boundary $B$ having a positive component, the genus is $g(X)=0$, the boundary degree is $\deg B=2$, and any closed point component $S$ of coefficient at least $t$ satisfies the explicit upper bound $N(1,t)=\lceil2/t\rceil$ for $0<t\leq1$.
- **I-03**: On a variety $W$ smooth over an algebraically closed field with a reduced simple normal crossings divisor $H$, any divisorial valuation $v$ satisfying $a(v,W,H)=0$ has a stratum $T$ as its centre and is uniquely determined by $T$ and its positive integral values on the components of $H$ containing $T$ via iterated codimension-two blowups; for a klt rational pair, the inequality $\sum_i(1-b_i)v(H_i)<1$ allows only finitely many positive integral weight choices at each stratum.
- **I-04**: For fixed integer $q\geq1$, real $\epsilon>0$, and integer $n\geq1$, on any normal geometrically integral projective $\epsilon$-lc Fano variety $Q/k$ of dimension $q$ with an effective log canonical pair $(Q,\Lambda)$ whose log canonical divisor has principal multiple $n$, every divisorial valuation $v$ with $a(v,Q,\Lambda)<1$ has its constant extension degree bounded above by a uniform finite number $A(q,\epsilon,n)$.
- **I-05**: For an effective lc $\mathbb Q$-pair $(U,C)$ over $k$ with a prime component $E$ of coefficient one and $n(K_U+C)\sim0$, divisorial adjunction on the normalization $\nu:E^\nu\to E$ yields an effective different $C_{E^\nu}$ on $E^\nu$ retaining the same principal index $n$ so that $nC_{E^\nu}$ is integral and every positive coefficient of $C_{E^\nu}$ is at least $1/n$, and any $\mathbb Q$-Cartier prime component $P\ne E$ of coefficient $\beta>0$ contributes at least $\beta P\|_{E^\nu}$ to $C_{E^\nu}$.
- **I-06**: On a projective $\mathbb Q$-Gorenstein klt variety $U$ over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero with an lc pair $(U,C)$ and $C\geq0$, at most $2/b$ prime components of $C$ with coefficient at least $b>0$ can contain a fixed irreducible codimension-two subvariety of $U$, combining with adjunction on $E^\nu$ of dimension $d-1$ at threshold $1/n$ to close the four-branch inductive recurrence for $d\geq2$.

### Panel 2 - negative knowledge

- **AU-01**: Applying the uniform Stein-degree bound to prime divisors on $X$ whose boundary coefficient in $B$ is zero or not bounded away from zero by a fixed positive threshold $t>0$. -> As the threshold $t$ approaches zero, the bound $N(1,t)=\lceil2/t\rceil$ diverges to infinity already in dimension $d=1$, and prime divisors outside the support of $B$ can have arbitrarily large constant field extension degrees over $k$.
- **AU-02**: Invoking the uniform constant-field bound on projective pairs $(X,B)$ where $K_X+B$ is not $\mathbb Q$-linearly trivial or where $H^0(X,\mathcal O_X)=k$ fails to hold. -> If $H^0(X,\mathcal O_X)=k$ fails, the ambient variety $X$ is not geometrically integral over $k$ or already carries a finite constant field extension of unbounded degree, and without $K_X+B\sim_{\mathbb Q}0$ the bounded complement construction and crepant MMP steps do not apply.
- **AU-03**: Treating the higher-dimensional bound $N(d,t)\geq1$ for $d\geq2$ as an explicit closed-form numerical formula or applying the theorem over ground fields of positive characteristic. -> In dimension $d\geq2$, the inductive recurrence depends on the bounded complement index $n=n(d,b)$ and the Fano orbit bound $A(q,\epsilon,n)$ derived from non-constructive boundedness theorems, while in positive characteristic resolution of singularities, Kawamata-Viehweg vanishing, and separability of $k_V/k$ can fail.
- **AU-04**: Using the constant-field degree bound $N(d,t)\geq1$ or the codimension-two incidence bound $2/b$ to size concurrency pools, task queues, or worker limits in software systems. -> The invariant $c(V/k)$ measures the field extension degree of the relative algebraic closure of $k$ in the function field of an algebraic variety, which has no valid mapping to software queue capacities or concurrent execution limits.

### Panel 3 - reference implementation and oracle

Applicability: `constructive`. The curve base case $N(1,t)=\lceil2/t\rceil$, the iterated blowup weight reduction of Lemma 5, the finite enumeration of low-discrepancy weight tuples in Corollary 6, the index-preserving adjunction lower bound $1/n$ of Lemma 12, the codimension-two incidence bound $2/b$ of Lemma 13, and the four-branch inductive recurrence for $N(d,t)\geq1$ are finite constructive procedures verified in exact rational arithmetic, with non-constructive higher-dimensional boundedness inputs $n=n(d,b)$ and $A(q,\epsilon,n)$ supplied as explicit parameters.

# INPUTS: JSON object on stdin with key "mode" in {"curve_bound", "snc_weights", "incidence_and_recurrence"} and exact rational/integer parameters.
# PRECONDITIONS: d >= 1 integer; 0 < b < t <= 1 rationals; n >= 1 integer with n*b integral; weights positive integers; klt boundary_coeffs < 1.
# OUTPUTS: Deterministic JSON object on stdout reporting exact certified bounds, blowup weight reductions, and recurrence evaluations.
# TIME: O(W * r log(W) + C_adm) where r = len(weights), W = sum(weights), and C_adm is the finite count of low-discrepancy weight tuples.
# SPACE: O(r + C_adm) for active weight tuples and enumerated admissible valuations.
# ARITHMETIC: Exact arbitrary-precision integers and rationals via python-flint (fmpz, fmpq); zero floating-point operations.
# FAILURE: Exits with non-zero status on malformed JSON, unknown mode, or violated mathematical preconditions.

import json
import sys
from flint import fmpz, fmpq

def parse_q(val):
    if isinstance(val, int):
        return fmpq(val, 1)
    s = str(val).strip()
    if "/" in s:
        num, den = s.split("/", 1)
        return fmpq(int(num), int(den))
    return fmpq(int(s), 1)

def q_ceil(x):
    p = int(x.p)
    q = int(x.q)
    return (p + q - 1) // q

def q_floor(x):
    p = int(x.p)
    q = int(x.q)
    return p // q

def run_curve_bound(payload):
    t_q = parse_q(payload["t"])
    if t_q <= 0 or t_q > 1:
        raise ValueError("t out of (0, 1] range")
    deg_b = fmpq(2, 1)
    n1_t = q_ceil(deg_b / t_q)
    max_feasible_c = q_floor(deg_b / t_q)
    coeffs = [parse_q(c) for c in payload.get("coeffs", [])]
    degrees = [int(d) for d in payload.get("degrees", [])]
    if len(coeffs) != len(degrees):
        raise ValueError("coeffs/degrees length mismatch")
    total_deg = sum((coeffs[i] * fmpq(degrees[i], 1) for i in range(len(coeffs))), fmpq(0, 1))
    valid_pair = (total_deg == deg_b) and all(fmpq(0, 1) < c <= fmpq(1, 1) for c in coeffs) and all(d >= 1 for d in degrees)
    observed_max = 0
    for c_val, d_val in zip(coeffs, degrees):
        if c_val >= t_q and d_val > observed_max:
            observed_max = d_val
    return {
        "mode": "curve_bound",
        "deg_B": int(deg_b.p),
        "N_1_t": n1_t,
        "max_achievable_c": max_feasible_c,
        "valid_boundary": bool(valid_pair),
        "observed_max_c": observed_max,
        "bound_satisfied": bool(observed_max <= n1_t),
    }

    raw_w = [int(x) for x in payload["weights"]]
    if not raw_w or any(x <= 0 for x in raw_w):
        raise ValueError("weights must be positive integers")
    active = [fmpz(x) for x in raw_w]
    steps = 0
    total_drop = fmpz(0)
    while len(active) >= 2:
        active.sort()

We prove that every prime component <i>S</i> of <i>B</i> with coefficient at least <i>t</i> satisfies $`[k_S:k]\leq N(d,t)`$, where <i>k</i><sub><i>S</i></sub> is the relative algebraic closure of <i>k</i> in $`k(S)`$. This also bounds the Stein degree of <i>S</i> over <i>k</i>, proving the contraction-to-a-point formulation of Birkar's Stein-degree conjecture for ordinary ℚ-pairs.

### Introduction

For a proper integral variety $S$ over a field $k$, the Stein degree over $k$ is $$\operatorname{sdeg}(S/\operatorname{Spec}k)=\dim_k H^0(S,\mathcal O_S).$$ The field of global functions measures the finite part of the structural morphism. Its degree can be large even when the dimension of $S$ is fixed. The question here is whether a boundary component of a log Calabi–Yau pair has uniformly bounded Stein degree once its coefficient is bounded away from zero.

Birkar formulates this question for log Calabi–Yau fibrations in <a id="pm-cite-1"></a>[[Birkar2026](#ref-Birkar2026), Conjecture 11.1]. Birkar–Qu prove normalized Stein-degree bounds over algebraically closed characteristic-zero fields <a id="pm-cite-2"></a>[[BirkarQu2025](#ref-BirkarQu2025), Theorem 1.3], as summarized in <a id="pm-cite-3"></a>[[Birkar2026](#ref-Birkar2026), Theorem 12.1]. Over an arbitrary characteristic-zero field, the finite arithmetic extensions carried by boundary components must also be controlled.

For any integral $k$-variety $V$, let $k_V$ be the relative algebraic closure of $k$ in its function field and put <a id="eq:constants"></a>$$\label{eq:constants}
 c(V/k)=[k_V:k].$$ Our result bounds this stronger invariant.

<a id="stmt-thm-main"></a>
<div id="thm:main" class="theorem">

**Theorem 1**. *For every integer $d\geq 1$ and real number $t>0$, there is an integer $N(d,t)\geq1$ with the following property. Let $k$ be any field of characteristic zero, and let $(X,B)$ be a projective log canonical $\mathbb Q$-pair such that $$\dim X=d,\qquad H^0(X,\mathcal O_X)=k,\qquad K_X+B\sim_{\mathbb Q}0.$$ Assume that $X$ is normal and integral and that $B$ is effective. If $S$ is a prime component of $B$ with coefficient at least $t$, then $$c(S/k)\leq N(d,t).$$ Consequently $$\operatorname{sdeg}(S/\operatorname{Spec}k)\leq
 \dim_k H^0(S^\nu,\mathcal O_{S^\nu})
 =c(S/k)\leq N(d,t),$$ where $S^\nu$ is the normalization of $S$.*

</div>

thus proves the contraction-to-$\operatorname{Spec}k$ formulation of Birkar’s Stein-degree conjecture for ordinary $\mathbb Q$-pairs.

The birational strategy follows Birkar–Qu <a id="pm-cite-4"></a>[[BirkarQu2025](#ref-BirkarQu2025)], using the minimal model program (MMP), bounded complements, and boundedness of Fano varieties. Two additional arguments address the arithmetic and inductive difficulties. First, <a href="#sec:arithmetic" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:arithmetic">3</a> turns geometric boundedness into a bound on Galois orbits of divisorial valuations. A bounded extension fixes a polarization class; a determinant construction descends a bounded power of that class. On a resolution of the resulting bounded pair, a log canonical place is determined by a stratum and integral weights. The finite permutation action on strata therefore bounds its arithmetic orbit, even though there may be infinitely many such places. Second, when the distinguished divisor becomes vertical on a Mori fibre space, the earlier bigness of that divisor forces a horizontal boundary component of coefficient one. A relative MMP makes the two components intersect, so ordinary divisorial adjunction reduces the dimension ([sec:preparation,sec:proof] [sec:preparation](#sec:preparation), [sec:proof](#sec:proof)).

<a id="pm-h-3"></a>
### Conventions and foundational inputs

All fields have characteristic zero. A variety is integral and of finite type over its ground field. A contraction is a projective surjective morphism $f:V\to Z$ between normal varieties with $f_*\mathcal O_V=\mathcal O_Z$. Divisors over a variety are identified with divisorial valuations, normalized to have value group $\mathbb Z$. We use log discrepancies: $$a(P,X,B)=1-\operatorname{coeff}_P B_W,
 \qquad K_W+B_W=p^*(K_X+B).$$ The pair is log canonical (lc) if these numbers are nonnegative and Kawamata log terminal (klt) if they are positive. A divisorial valuation of log discrepancy zero is an *lc place*. A variety is $\epsilon$-lc if the pair with zero boundary has all log discrepancies at least $\epsilon$. Canonical divisors in birational comparisons are chosen compatibly. The relation $\sim_{\mathbb Q}0$ means that a positive integral multiple is a principal Cartier divisor.

We require $\mathbb Q$-factoriality only over the working field. It need not hold over an algebraic closure. For normal geometrically integral varieties, the singularity conditions just stated are preserved by ground-field extension: resolve in characteristic zero, base change, and apply the log smooth discrepancy criterion. Coefficients on geometric components are unchanged.

#### Constants and generic fibres

<a id="stmt-lem-constants"></a>
<div id="lem:constants" class="lemma">

**Lemma 2**. *Let $V$ be an integral $k$-variety.*

1.  *The number $c(V/k)$ is the number of irreducible components of $V_{\overline{k}}$; these components form one Galois orbit. It is a birational invariant. If $V$ is proper, then $H^0(V,\mathcal O_V)\subseteq k_V$, with equality when $V$ is normal.*

<a id="eq:generic-constants"></a>
2.  *If $V\to Z$ is dominant and $Z$ is geometrically integral over $k$, then, for its generic fibre, $$\label{eq:generic-constants}
     c(V/k)\leq c(V_{\eta_Z}/k(Z)).$$*

3.  *If $X$ is normal projective and $H^0(X,\mathcal O_X)=k$, then $X$ is geometrically integral. The normal base of a contraction from $X$ is also geometrically integral. Its generic fibre is normal, projective, and has global functions $k(Z)$.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-constants"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-constants</summary>

*Proof.* The extension $k_V/k$ is finite and separable, and $k(V)/k_V$ is regular. Its embeddings in $\overline{k}$ give precisely the geometric components. Properness makes global functions finite over $k$. On a normal variety every element of $k(V)$ algebraic over $k$ is integral over every local ring and hence regular, proving (i).

The regular extension $k(Z)/k$ is linearly disjoint from $k_V/k$. Thus $k_Vk(Z)$ is a degree-$[k_V:k]$ extension of $k(Z)$ inside $k(V)$ and is contained in the relative algebraic closure of $k(Z)$ there. This proves (ii). The first assertion of (iii) follows from (i). For a contraction, global functions on the base are $k$ and localizing $f_*\mathcal O_X=\mathcal O_Z$ gives the asserted generic-fibre function field. Normality is preserved by localization, and a normal variety over a perfect field is geometrically normal; the assertion follows over $k(Z)$ as well. ◻

</details>

We use $c(P/k)$ for a divisorial valuation by using its residue function field. Its geometric extensions on a model where it is a divisor are distinct valuations forming a single Galois orbit.

If $(X,B)$ is as in <a href="#thm:main" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:main">1</a> and $X\to Z$ is a contraction, restriction to the generic fibre gives a projective lc log Calabi–Yau pair over $k(Z)$. Indeed, work over the smooth locus of $Z$, restrict a log resolution, and use generic smoothness and adjunction. The same argument restricts a specified principal multiple of $K_X+B$. Hence a horizontal boundary component can be treated by induction in the dimension of this generic fibre, using <a href="#eq:generic-constants" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:generic-constants">[eq:generic-constants]</a>.

#### Established theorems used in the proof

We state the versions needed, so that no arithmetic strengthening of a geometric boundedness theorem is implicit.

- *Klt MMP and extraction.* For a projective morphism of normal varieties over a characteristic-zero field and a $\mathbb Q$-factorial klt pair $(V,\Delta)$ with effective rational boundary, an MMP with suitable ample scaling terminates in a Mori fibre space if $K_V+\Delta$ is not relatively pseudo-effective. If $\Delta$ is relatively big and $K_V+\Delta$ is relatively pseudo-effective, it terminates in a minimal model. Outputs are $\mathbb Q$-factorial and klt. A finite set of exceptional valuations of log discrepancy at most one for a klt $\mathbb Q$-pair can be extracted by a projective birational morphism with $\mathbb Q$-factorial source and no other exceptional prime divisors. These are consequences of <a id="pm-cite-5"></a>[[BCHM2010](#ref-BCHM2010)]; versions over the fields used here are <a id="pm-cite-6"></a>[[LyuMurayama](#ref-LyuMurayama), Theorem 21.7, Corollaries 21.9–21.10, Theorem 22.1]. We also use the negativity lemma, monotonicity of discrepancies along a klt MMP, and klt base point freeness.

- *Bounded complements.* In fixed dimension, for an lc pair over an algebraically closed characteristic-zero field, with coefficients in a fixed finite rational set, underlying variety of Fano type, and nef anti-log canonical divisor, there is an enlarged lc boundary $B^+\geq B$ such that $n(K+B^+)\sim0$, where $n$ depends only on the dimension and coefficient set. This is the relevant case of <a id="pm-cite-7"></a>[[Birkar2019](#ref-Birkar2019), Theorem 1.7]; a finite rational set is contained in an appropriate hyperstandard set. We may replace $n$ by a prescribed fixed multiple.

- *BAB.* For fixed $q$ and $\epsilon>0$, geometrically $\epsilon$-lc projective Fano varieties of dimension $q$ over an algebraically closed characteristic-zero field form a bounded family <a id="pm-cite-8"></a>[[Birkar2021](#ref-Birkar2021), Theorem 1.1]. In particular, they admit very ample line bundles of bounded degree.

Here Fano type over $Z$ means the existence of an effective rational boundary $\Delta$ with $(V,\Delta)$ klt and $-(K_V+\Delta)$ ample over $Z$. The absolute term means $Z=\operatorname{Spec}k$. The other standard inputs are resolution and principalization in characteristic zero, klt vanishing and rational singularities, and divisorial adjunction; see <a id="pm-cite-9"></a>[[BMT2011](#ref-BMT2011), [KM1998](#ref-KM1998), [Kollar2013](#ref-Kollar2013)]. We give the index and field arguments for adjunction in <a href="#lem:adjunction" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:adjunction">12</a>.

The algebraically closed bounds above are uniform in the ground field. One may also deduce this from their complex versions: descend all finite data, including resolutions and positivity witnesses, to a finitely generated field over $\mathbb Q$, embed it into $\mathbb C$, and spread and specialize an embedding or a complement. For an embedding, very ampleness and its degree persist after shrinking the parameter space. For the complement used below, the general-member argument in <a href="#lem:complement" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:complement">4</a> gives the same transfer in its specified linear system.

<a id="stmt-lem-crepant"></a>
<div id="lem:crepant" class="lemma">

**Lemma 3**. *Suppose $(V,D)$ is lc and $K_V+D\sim_{\mathbb Q}0$. Under a birational contraction or flip, push $D$ forward and keep compatible canonical divisors. Then the resulting pair is crepant to $(V,D)$, remains lc with effective boundary, and retains every specified relation $n(K_V+D)\sim0$.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-crepant"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-crepant</summary>

*Proof.* Write the specified multiple as the divisor of a rational function. Since the map extracts no divisors, pushing forward gives the same principal-divisor identity on the new model. Its two pullbacks to a common resolution are that same principal divisor. Thus the pullbacks agree, which proves crepancy and the discrepancy assertions. Without a specified multiple, choose one first. ◻

</details>

### Arithmetic consequences of geometric boundedness

<a id="pm-h-7"></a>
#### Descending a complement

<a id="stmt-lem-complement"></a>
<div id="lem:complement" class="lemma">

**Lemma 4**. *Fix $d$ and a rational number $b\in(0,1)$. There is an integer $n=n(d,b)$ such that the following holds over every characteristic-zero field $k$. Suppose $V$ is a normal geometrically integral projective klt Fano variety of dimension $d$, $S$ is a $\mathbb Q$-Cartier prime divisor, $(V,bS)$ is lc, and $-K_V-bS$ is nef. Then there is an effective rational boundary $C\geq bS$ over $k$ with $$(V,C)\text{ lc},\qquad n(K_V+C)\sim0.$$ We may assume $nb\in\mathbb Z$.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-complement"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-complement</summary>

<a id="eq:weil-system"></a>
*Proof.* Apply bounded complements to $(V_{\overline{k}},bS_{\overline{k}})$ and multiply the index by the denominator of $b$. The geometric complement has the form $bS_{\overline{k}}+D/n$, where $$\label{eq:weil-system}
 D\in|-nK_V-nbS|_{\overline{k}}.$$ This linear system is defined over $k$. For clarity, its underlying space is the space of rational sections of the integral Weil divisor $-nK_V-nbS$; it can be computed on the smooth locus and extended across codimension two. Sections commute with field extension. The divisor is $\mathbb Q$-Cartier, although it need not be Cartier.

A general geometric member of <a href="#eq:weil-system" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:weil-system">[eq:weil-system]</a> gives an lc pair. To see this, resolve both $(V,bS)$ and the rational map of this linear system. On a further resolution the pullbacks of its members have the form $F+M$, with $F$ fixed and rational and $M$ varying in a base point free Cartier system. Indeed, differences of pullbacks are divisors of ratios of sections, so eliminating the base ideal gives precisely this decomposition. Arrange that the support of $F$ and the crepant boundary $B_W$ of $(V,bS)$ has simple normal crossings. The existence of one lc member implies that every coefficient of $B_W+F/n$ is at most one: adding the effective moving part could not remove an excessive coefficient. By Bertini, a general $M$ is smooth and transverse to this support, and its coefficient $1/n$ is at most one. The resulting log smooth pair is lc.

The argument describes a nonempty geometric open subset of the projective space of sections. Since $k$ is infinite, its $k$-rational points are Zariski dense even after extension to $\overline{k}$. Choose a member $D$ over $k$ in this open subset and set $C=bS+D/n$. Then $n(K_V+C)=nK_V+nbS+D$ is principal over $k$, as required. ◻

</details>

#### Log canonical places of a simple normal crossings pair

A stratum of a reduced simple normal crossings divisor will mean an irreducible component of a nonempty intersection of its components, including a single component.

<a id="stmt-lem-weights"></a>
<div id="lem:weights" class="lemma">

**Lemma 5**. *Let $W$ be smooth over an algebraically closed field, and let $H$ be a reduced simple normal crossings divisor. If a divisorial valuation $v$ satisfies $a(v,W,H)=0$, then its centre is a stratum. Moreover, $v$ is uniquely determined by that stratum and its positive integral values on the components of $H$ containing the stratum.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-weights"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-weights</summary>

*Proof.* Let $T$ be the centre. If fewer than $\operatorname{codim}T$ components of $H$ pass through its generic point, complete their local equations by a smooth hypersurface containing $T$, keeping simple normal crossings. The enlarged reduced divisor is lc, and the additional hypersurface has positive value under $v$. The discrepancy for $H$ would therefore be positive. Thus the components through the generic point of $T$ have number $\operatorname{codim}T$, and $T$ is a stratum.

We prove uniqueness by a sequence of blowups that is determined by the weights. If $T$ is already a divisor, the normalized valuation centred at its generic point is its order of vanishing. Otherwise choose two components through $T$, with values $p_1,p_2>0$, and blow up their codimension-two intersection near the generic point of $T$. The new reduced total divisor is simple normal crossings and the blowup is log crepant. The exceptional divisor has value $\min(p_1,p_2)$; the values of the two strict transforms are $$p_1-\min(p_1,p_2),\qquad p_2-\min(p_1,p_2),$$ with a zero value indicating that the centre is not on that strict transform. The other values are unchanged.

The new centre is again a stratum. These data specify it uniquely over the generic point of $T$: in the exceptional $\mathbb P^1$-bundle, the two strict transforms give the two distinguished sections. Unequal weights choose one section; equal weights choose the full fibre, since a different proper subvariety would not be a stratum. Intersecting with the remaining components through $T$ imposes the remaining prescribed conditions. This is also immediate in the two blowup charts. The sum of the positive weights decreases by $\min(p_1,p_2)$. Repeating therefore reaches a divisorial centre, where the valuation is unique. Tracing back proves the assertion. ◻

</details>

<a id="stmt-cor-finite-low"></a>
<div id="cor:finite-low" class="corollary">

**Corollary 6**. *A klt rational pair has only finitely many divisorial valuations of log discrepancy less than one.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-cor-finite-low"><summary>Proof of stmt-cor-finite-low</summary>

*Proof.* First work over an algebraic closure and take a log resolution with crepant boundary $B_W$. Let $H=\sum H_i$ be a reduced simple normal crossings divisor containing its support, and write $B_W=\sum b_iH_i$, allowing zero and negative coefficients. Since the pair is klt, $b_i<1$. For a valuation $v$ with discrepancy less than one, <a id="eq:weight-discrepancy"></a>$$\label{eq:weight-discrepancy}
 a(v,W,B_W)=a(v,W,H)+\sum_i(1-b_i)v(H_i)<1.$$ The first term on the right is a nonnegative integer, so it is zero. The centre is therefore a stratum by <a href="#lem:weights" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:weights">5</a>. At each stratum, the positive integral weights satisfy $\sum_i(1-b_i)v(H_i)<1$, which allows only finitely many choices. There are finitely many strata, and <a href="#lem:weights" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:weights">5</a> gives uniqueness. Over the original field, each divisorial valuation extends to a geometric one with the same discrepancy, so finiteness follows there as well. ◻

</details>

#### Bounded resolutions and Galois orbits

We record the bounded-family fact needed to keep resolutions over the field of the data.

<a id="stmt-lem-embedded"></a>
<div id="lem:embedded" class="lemma">

**Lemma 7**. *Fix bounds for the ambient projective dimension, the degree of a normal geometrically integral projective variety $V$, and the degree of a reduced divisor $D\subset V$, where $D$ may be empty. There are constants $R,M$ depending only on these bounds such that, over the field of the embedded pair, one can find a projective resolution $p:W\to V$ and a geometrically simple normal crossings reduced divisor $H$ containing the exceptional locus and the strict transform of $D$, with $$b_2(W_{\bar k})\leq R,
 \qquad
 \#\{\text{geometric components and strata of }H\}\leq M.$$ One may use an $\ell$-adic second Betti number for any $\ell$.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-embedded"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-embedded</summary>

*Proof.* We explain both the finite-type parametrization and the field assertion. A reduced pure-dimensional projective locus of dimension $j$ and degree at most $D_0$ in a fixed $\mathbb P^N$ is cut out set-theoretically by equations of bounded degree. Indeed, given a geometric point outside the locus, choose a linear projection to $\mathbb P^{j+1}$ whose centre misses the locus and whose fibre through that point misses it. Such a choice exists by the usual dimension count. The projection is finite on the locus; its image is a hypersurface of degree at most $D_0$. Pulling back its equation separates the point. Multiplying by forms nonvanishing at that point puts these equations in one fixed degree. The codimension-one case uses the identity projection; the empty and full loci require no argument. A basis of the space of equations in this fixed degree has bounded size. These spaces of equations descend to the field of the locus, because extension of the ground field commutes with their defining linear conditions.

Apply this to $V$ and $D$. Tuples of equations of bounded size and degree give parameter spaces of finite type over $\mathbb Q$ containing all the embedded pairs over their original fields. Stratify to take fibrewise reductions and to impose geometric normality, integrality, dimension, and the divisor condition. This can be done by spreading the reductions at generic points and shrinking, using characteristic zero and generic flatness, and then proceeding by Noetherian induction.

At the generic point of each stratum, resolve the pair over that point’s own function field. Resolution and principalization in characteristic zero may be chosen to give geometric simple normal crossings, with no extension of that function field <a id="pm-cite-10"></a>[[BMT2011](#ref-BMT2011), Theorems 1.3 and 6.1, including the Addendum]. Spread the projective morphism, the reduced divisor, and a dense open on which the morphism is an isomorphism. After shrinking, the resolving variety is smooth and projective over the stratum, the morphism is fibrewise birational, and the divisor has geometric simple normal crossings in every fibre and contains the required exceptional and marked loci. To verify the last condition, spread the inverse image of the closed bad locus and the smoothness and expected codimensions of intersections of the divisor components; the components can be considered after an auxiliary finite étale cover of the parameter stratum for this verification. The resolution itself remains defined on the original stratum. Noetherian induction on its complement yields finitely many families.

Every embedded pair over a field is a point of one of these strata, so pulling back the corresponding family gives a resolution over that same field. Smooth proper base change bounds the second Betti numbers. Applying it also to intersections of components, after the finite étale covers just used, bounds the numbers of their geometric connected components and hence the numbers of strata. Taking maxima over the finitely many families gives $R$ and $M$. ◻

</details>

<a id="stmt-prop-bab-orbits"></a>
<div id="prop:bab-orbits" class="proposition">

**Proposition 8**. *Fix an integer $q\geq1$, a real $\epsilon>0$, and an integer $n\geq1$. There is a finite number $A(q,\epsilon,n)$ with the following property. Let $Q/k$ be a normal geometrically integral projective $\epsilon$-lc Fano variety of dimension $q$, and suppose $$(Q,\Lambda)\text{ is lc},\qquad \Lambda\geq0,
 \qquad n(K_Q+\Lambda)\sim0.$$ Then every divisorial valuation $v$ with $a(v,Q,\Lambda)<1$ satisfies $$c(v/k)\leq A(q,\epsilon,n).$$*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-prop-bab-orbits"><summary>Proof of stmt-prop-bab-orbits</summary>

*Proof.* *A bounded polarization after a bounded extension.* By BAB, $Q_{\overline{k}}$ has a very ample line bundle $L$ of bounded degree. Its space of sections also has bounded dimension: for a nondegenerate $q$-dimensional projective variety, degree is at least codimension plus one, so $h^0(L)\leq L^q+q$.

We claim that $\operatorname{Pic}(Q_{\overline{k}})$ is free abelian of bounded rank. First a numerically trivial line bundle $N$ on $Q_{\overline{k}}$ is trivial. Indeed, klt singularities are rational, so Euler characteristics can be computed on a resolution; Riemann–Roch there gives $\chi(N)=\chi(\mathcal O_{Q_{\overline{k}}})$. Klt Kawamata–Viehweg vanishing applies to both bundles, since $N-K_Q$ and $-K_Q$ are ample. Hence $h^0(N)=h^0(\mathcal O_{Q_{\overline{k}}})=1$. The divisor of a nonzero section of $N$ is effective and numerically trivial. Its intersection with a power of an ample divisor forces it to be zero, so $N$ is trivial.

Pullback to a resolution therefore embeds $\operatorname{Pic}(Q_{\overline{k}})$ into the Néron–Severi group modulo numerical equivalence of that resolution: numerical triviality of a pullback implies numerical triviality downstairs by lifting curves and the projection formula. This is a free abelian group of rank at most the second Betti number. [lem:embedded](#lem:embedded), applied to the bounded embedding with empty marking, bounds that number. This proves the claim.

The Galois action on this lattice has finite image, because its finitely many generators are defined over finite extensions. Finite subgroups of $\operatorname{GL}_r(\mathbb Z)$ have bounded order for bounded $r$: reduction modulo $3$ is injective on each such subgroup. To recall why, the kernel has no nontrivial finite-order element. If $I+3^aM$ with $a\geq1$ and $M\not\equiv0\pmod3$ had prime order, the binomial expansion at that prime would have a nonzero lowest $3$-adic term; every finite-order element has a power of prime order. Thus an extension $F/k$ of bounded degree fixes the class of $L$.

<a id="eq:determinant-descent"></a>
Invariance of a class need not descend a line bundle, so one more step is necessary. Put $r=h^0(L)$. Choose a finite Galois field of definition of $L$ over $F$, and isomorphisms between its conjugates. Their failure to satisfy the cocycle condition consists of scalar factors. On $$\label{eq:determinant-descent}
 L^{\otimes r}\otimes\bigl(\det H^0(L)\bigr)^{-1}$$ these factors cancel: a scalar acts to the $r$th power on both factors. This gives effective descent data. The required isomorphisms exist over the chosen field of definition because two line bundles that become isomorphic over an algebraic closure are already isomorphic over that field on a proper geometrically integral variety: apply base change to the sections of their quotient and its inverse. Consequently <a href="#eq:determinant-descent" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:determinant-descent">[eq:determinant-descent]</a> descends to a line bundle $A$ on $Q_F$ whose geometric class is $L^{\otimes r}$. It is very ample and has bounded degree and bounded $h^0(A)$.

*A bounded marked support.* The principal-divisor identity implies that $n\Lambda$ is integral, since $K_Q$ is an integral Weil divisor. Thus every positive coefficient of $\Lambda$ is at least $1/n$. On the geometric variety, <a id="eq:marked-degree"></a>$$\label{eq:marked-degree}
 (\operatorname{Supp}\Lambda)_{\mathrm{red}}\cdot A^{q-1}
 \leq n(-K_Q)\cdot A^{q-1}
 \leq n\bigl((q-1)A^q+2\bigr).$$ For the last inequality, intersect $q-1$ general members of $|A|$. When $q>1$ they give a smooth curve avoiding the singular locus of the normal variety, and adjunction says $$(-K_Q)\cdot A^{q-1}=(q-1)A^q+2-2g\leq(q-1)A^q+2.$$ For $q=1$ the same formula is the usual degree formula on a smooth curve. Hence the embedded pair $(Q_F,(\operatorname{Supp}\Lambda)_\mathrm{red})$ satisfies the hypotheses of <a href="#lem:embedded" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:embedded">7</a> with uniform bounds.

*The orbit of a valuation.* Choose the resolution supplied there over $F$, and write $K_W+B_W=p^*(K_Q+\Lambda)$. Its geometric reduced divisor $H$ contains the support of $B_W$. Since $(Q,\Lambda)$ is lc, $B_W\leq H$. For every geometric extension $\bar v$ of $v$, $$0\leq a(\bar v,W_{\overline{k}},H)
 \leq a(\bar v,W_{\overline{k}},B_W)<1.$$ The left-hand discrepancy is integral, hence zero. By <a href="#lem:weights" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:weights">5</a>, $\bar v$ is determined by its centre stratum and its weights on the components of $H$. The Galois subgroup over $F$ that fixes all the geometric components and strata fixes each such valuation: its centre and weights cannot change. There are at most $M$ objects in this finite set, so each orbit over $F$ has size at most $M!$. Its full orbit over $k$ has size at most $[F:k]M!$. These are uniformly bounded, proving the proposition. ◻

</details>

### Birational preparation and adjunction

<a id="pm-h-11"></a>
#### Making the distinguished component positive

<a id="stmt-lem-klt-model"></a>
<div id="lem:klt-model" class="lemma">

**Lemma 9**. *For $(X,B)$ as in <a href="#thm:main" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:main">1</a>, there is a projective birational morphism $h:Y\to X$ with $Y$ $\mathbb Q$-factorial and klt such that the crepant boundary $B_Y$ is effective. The strict transform of every component of $B$ has its original coefficient.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-klt-model"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-klt-model</summary>

*Proof.* Take a projective resolution and run a relative $K$-MMP with ample scaling over $X$. In this birational setting every divisor class, including the zero boundary, is big over $X$: the generic fibre is a point. The big-boundary MMP applies. A Mori fibre space over $X$ cannot be an output, since its total space is birational to $X$, whereas its base would have smaller dimension and still dominate $X$. We therefore obtain a $\mathbb Q$-factorial klt minimal model $h:Y\to X$. Write $$K_Y+B_Y=h^*(K_X+B).$$ Minimality says that $-B_Y$ is nef over $X$, and $h_*B_Y=B\geq0$. The negativity lemma gives $B_Y\geq0$. Crepancy proves the remaining assertions. ◻

</details>

<a id="stmt-lem-positive-component"></a>
<div id="lem:positive-component" class="lemma">

**Lemma 10**. *Let $X$ be a $\mathbb Q$-factorial klt projective variety, $(X,B)$ an effective lc pair with $K_X+B\sim_{\mathbb Q}0$, and $S$ a component of coefficient at least $t>0$. Fix a rational $b$ with $0<b<t$. There is a sequence of birational MMP steps preserving $S$, followed by a Mori fibre contraction $X_1\to Z_1$, such that $S_1$ is ample over $Z_1$ and $(X_1,B_1)$ is an effective lc log Calabi–Yau pair. If $Z_1$ is a point and $H^0(X,\mathcal O_X)=k$, then $$Z_1=\operatorname{Spec}k,\qquad -K_{X_1}\text{ ample},\qquad
 -K_{X_1}-bS_1\text{ nef}.$$*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-positive-component"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-positive-component</summary>

*Proof.* Set $D=K_X+B-bS\sim_{\mathbb Q}-bS$. It is not pseudo-effective, since its intersection with a fixed ample divisor to the power $d-1$ is negative. Choose an ample rational divisor $H$ and $\delta>0$ rational and small enough that $D+\delta H$ remains outside the pseudo-effective cone.

There is an effective klt boundary $\Delta$ with $K_X+\Delta\sim_{\mathbb Q}D+\delta H$. Indeed, $(X,B-bS)$ is lc and $X$ is klt, so $(X,(1-\eta)(B-bS))$ is klt for $0<\eta\ll1$. The class $\delta H+\eta(B-bS)$ is ample for sufficiently small $\eta$. Represent it by a general effective rational divisor with small coefficients, preserving klt, and add this divisor to $(1-\eta)(B-bS)$.

Run the MMP for $K_X+\Delta$ with scaling by a sufficiently large multiple $mH$. At a ray $R$ used by the program, including the final Mori fibre ray, write $L_i=D_i+\delta H_i$ for the transformed class. The scaling condition gives $$L_i\cdot R<0,\qquad (L_i+\lambda_i mH_i)\cdot R=0,
 \qquad \lambda_i\geq0.$$ It follows that $\lambda_i>0$, $H_i\cdot R>0$, and hence $D_i\cdot R<0$. Thus every step is positive on $S_i$. The divisor $S_i$ cannot be contracted by a divisorial step: if it were, it would be an effective exceptional divisor nef over that contraction, in contradiction with the negativity lemma. Flips do not contract divisors.

The resulting Mori fibre contraction has relative Picard number one, so positivity on its extremal ray makes $S_1$ relatively ample. [lem:crepant](#lem:crepant) gives the assertions about $(X_1,B_1)$. If the base is a point, the function-field condition makes it $\operatorname{Spec}k$. Now $\rho(X_1/k)=1$. The nonzero effective divisor $B_1\sim_{\mathbb Q}-K_{X_1}$ is ample, and the effective divisor $B_1-bS_1\sim_{\mathbb Q}-K_{X_1}-bS_1$ is nef, as asserted. ◻

</details>

<a id="pm-h-12"></a>
#### Making a vertical prime a pullback

<a id="stmt-lem-vertical"></a>
<div id="lem:vertical" class="lemma">

<a id="eq:pullback"></a>
**Lemma 11**. *Let $U\to Z$ be a contraction with $U$ projective and $\mathbb Q$-factorial of Fano type over $Z$. Let $P$ be a vertical prime divisor. There is a sequence of $(-P)$-negative MMP steps over $Z$ such that the final transform $P'$ survives on a $\mathbb Q$-factorial klt variety $U'$ and $-P'$ is semiample over $Z$. If $f:U'\to Z'$ is its semiample contraction, then $Z'\to Z$ is birational and for some integer $m>0$ and a nonzero effective Cartier divisor $T$ on $Z'$, $$\label{eq:pullback}
 mP'=f^*T.$$ The MMP is an isomorphism over the generic point of $Z$. Every horizontal prime $E$ therefore survives and meets $P'$; its normalization has a nonzero effective restricted divisor $P'|_{E^\nu}$.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-vertical"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-vertical</summary>

*Proof.* First $-P$ is pseudo-effective over $Z$. Choose an effective Cartier divisor on $Z$ containing the proper closed image of $P$. A sufficiently large multiple of its pullback has coefficient at least one along $P$, so subtracting $P$ leaves an effective divisor.

Choose an effective klt boundary $\Delta$ for which $L=-(K_U+\Delta)$ is ample over $Z$. For a sufficiently small rational $a>0$, the class $L-aP$ is also relatively ample. After twisting a sufficiently divisible multiple by a divisor from $Z$, choose a general member and divide by that multiple to obtain an effective rational divisor $D_a\sim_{\mathbb Q}L-aP$ over $Z$ with $(U,\Delta+D_a)$ klt. Then $\Psi=\Delta+D_a$ is big over $Z$, and $$K_U+\Psi\sim_{\mathbb Q}-aP\quad\text{over }Z.$$ The big-boundary MMP with ample scaling terminates in a minimal model $U'$, since this class is relatively pseudo-effective. Its steps are $(-P)$-negative. As in <a href="#lem:positive-component" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:positive-component">10</a>, the negativity lemma prevents the contraction of $P$. The steps are isomorphisms over a nonempty open subset of $Z$: there $P$ is absent and its negative has zero intersection with every curve in a fibre. In particular, every horizontal prime survives.

Put $L'=K_{U'}+\Psi'$, which is nef over $Z$. The boundary $\Psi'$ is still big over $Z$, so write $\Psi'\sim_{\mathbb Q}A'+D'$ over $Z$ with $A'$ relatively ample and $D'\geq0$. For sufficiently small rational $\eta>0$, the effective boundary $$\Theta'=(1-\eta)\Psi'+\eta D'$$ is klt and satisfies $$L'-(K_{U'}+\Theta')\sim_{\mathbb Q}\eta A'\quad\text{over }Z.$$ Klt base point freeness applies to a sufficiently divisible nef Cartier multiple of $L'$ <a id="pm-cite-11"></a>[[LyuMurayama](#ref-LyuMurayama), Theorem 11.1]. Thus $L'$, and consequently $-P'$, is semiample over $Z$. The relative theorem can also be applied geometrically and descended: relative generation of sections is detected by faithfully flat base change.

Let $f:U'\to Z'$ be the associated contraction. On the generic fibre over $Z$, the line bundle of a multiple of $P'$ is trivial and the field of global functions is $k(Z)$. Hence $Z'\to Z$ is birational. Choose $m>0$ such that $mP'$ is Cartier and $\mathcal O_{U'}(-mP')=f^*\mathcal A$ for a line bundle $\mathcal A$ on $Z'$. The canonical section of $\mathcal O_{U'}(mP')=f^*\mathcal A^{-1}$ descends, by $f_*\mathcal O_{U'}=\mathcal O_{Z'}$ and the projection formula, to a section of $\mathcal A^{-1}$. Its divisor is a nonzero effective Cartier divisor $T$, and its pullback is exactly $mP'$, proving <a href="#eq:pullback" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:pullback">[eq:pullback]</a>.

A horizontal prime $E$ dominates $Z'$ and, being proper, maps onto $Z'$. Thus it meets $f^{-1}(T)$. It is not contained there, so pulling back $T$ to its normalization gives a nonzero effective Cartier divisor. Dividing by $m$ proves the final assertion. ◻

</details>

<a id="pm-h-13"></a>
#### Adjunction with a specified index

<a id="stmt-lem-adjunction"></a>
<div id="lem:adjunction" class="lemma">

<a id="eq:diff-restriction"></a>
**Lemma 12**. *Let $(U,C)$ be an effective lc $\mathbb Q$-pair over $k$, let $E$ be a prime component of coefficient one, and suppose $n(K_U+C)\sim0$. On the normalization $\nu:E^\nu\to E$ there is an effective different $C_{E^\nu}$ such that $$(E^\nu,C_{E^\nu})\text{ is lc},\qquad
 n(K_{E^\nu}+C_{E^\nu})\sim0.$$ In particular, $nC_{E^\nu}$ is integral and every positive coefficient of $C_{E^\nu}$ is at least $1/n$. If $P\ne E$ is a $\mathbb Q$-Cartier prime component of $C$ of coefficient $\beta>0$, then $$\label{eq:diff-restriction}
 C_{E^\nu}\geq\beta P|_{E^\nu}.$$*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-adjunction"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-adjunction</summary>

*Proof.* Divisorial adjunction gives an effective lc different on the normalization, geometrically and over $k$; its residue construction is compatible with field extension. We verify that it retains the *specified* index $n$, rather than an unspecified multiple. For the construction see <a id="pm-cite-12"></a>[[KollarDraft](#ref-KollarDraft), Definition 122 and equations (122.7)–(122.10)] and <a id="pm-cite-13"></a>[[Kollar2013](#ref-Kollar2013)].

Choose a nonzero rational top differential $\omega$ on $U$, and write $K_U=\operatorname{div}(\omega)$. The relation in the hypothesis provides a rational $n$-canonical form $\tau$ with $$\operatorname{div}(\tau)=-nC.$$ At the generic point of $E$, the variety is regular and $E$ has coefficient one. The logarithmic $n$-fold residue of $\tau$ along $E$ therefore defines a nonzero rational $n$-canonical form $\rho$ on $E^\nu$, over $k$. Residue commutes with powers. After passing to $\overline{k}$ and taking any sufficiently divisible power, the usual pluri-residue definition of the different gives $$\operatorname{div}(\rho)+nC_{E^\nu}=0.$$ Indeed, the identity for that power is the positive integral multiple of this identity. It consequently holds already for $\rho$ over $k$. This proves the principal-divisor assertion at index $n$, and also that $nC_{E^\nu}$ is integral. There is no gluing across different normalizations here; the construction is on the single normalized prime $E^\nu$.

For <a href="#eq:diff-restriction" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:diff-restriction">[eq:diff-restriction]</a>, remove $\beta P$ from $C$. The remaining pair is effective and lc and still has coefficient one along $E$, so its different is effective. After a common Cartier multiple, the two log pluricanonical sheaves differ by the tensor factor $\mathcal O_U(m\beta P)$, and their residues differ by its restriction to $E^\nu$. Dividing by $m$ shows that adding $\beta P$ adds exactly $\beta P|_{E^\nu}$ to the different. This comparison does not require $K_U+E$ to be $\mathbb Q$-Cartier. Distinct primes over $k$ have disjoint sets of geometric prime components, so this restriction is defined on every geometric component of $E^\nu$. This proves the inequality. ◻

</details>

<a id="stmt-lem-incidence"></a>
<div id="lem:incidence" class="lemma">

**Lemma 13**. *Let $U$ be a projective $\mathbb Q$-Gorenstein klt variety over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero, and let $(U,C)$ be lc with $C\geq0$. Given $b>0$, at most $2/b$ prime components of $C$ with coefficient at least $b$ can contain a fixed irreducible codimension-two subvariety of $U$.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-incidence"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-incidence</summary>

*Proof.* Take general very ample hyperplane sections down to a surface, and choose a point where the surface meets a suitable dense open subset of the given codimension-two locus. The surface is normal and klt, and its restricted boundary is lc. These assertions can be checked on a log resolution: general hyperplanes meet its simple normal crossings divisors transversely, adjunction preserves the discrepancy coefficients, and the images of exceptional loci and boundary intersections are met in the expected dimensions. Each prime being counted restricts to a curve germ through the chosen point, with its original coefficient, and the germs are distinct.

It remains to bound the number of these germs at a klt surface singularity. Descend the finite data and a log resolution to an embeddable characteristic-zero field and work over $\mathbb C$. A klt complex surface singularity is a quotient singularity <a id="pm-cite-14"></a>[[KM1998](#ref-KM1998), Proposition 4.18]; it has a finite smooth analytic uniformizing cover étale in codimension one. The crepant pullback of the pair is lc by the finite-cover discrepancy formula. Each curve germ pulls back to at least one curve through the point upstairs, with at least its original coefficient, and different germs have no common curve component. Blowing up this smooth surface point gives the log discrepancy $$2-\operatorname{mult}_{0}(C_{\mathrm{up}})\geq0.$$ If there are $r$ original germs, their contributions give $rb\leq\operatorname{mult}_{0}(C_{\mathrm{up}})\leq2$. Thus $r\leq2/b$, as required. ◻

</details>

<a id="sec:proof"></a>
### Proof of the arithmetic bound

We prove <a href="#thm:main" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:main">1</a> by induction on $d$, proving the assertion for *all* positive thresholds simultaneously in each dimension. For $t>1$ the assertion is vacuous. Henceforth $0<t\leq1$.

<a id="pm-h-15"></a>
#### The curve case

For $d=1$, $X$ is a smooth projective geometrically integral curve. Since $B$ has a positive component and $K_X+B\sim_{\mathbb Q}0$, $$0<\deg B=2-2g(X).$$ Thus $g(X)=0$ and $\deg B=2$. If $S$ is a component with coefficient at least $t$, then $S$ is a closed point and $$t\,c(S/k)=t[k(S):k]\leq\deg B=2.$$ We may take $N(1,t)=\lceil2/t\rceil$ in the substantive range.

Fix $d\geq2$ and assume the theorem in every smaller dimension and for every positive threshold. Write <a id="eq:Mnotation"></a>$$\label{eq:Mnotation}
 M_{<d}(u)=\max_{1\leq r<d}N(r,u),\qquad u>0.$$ These are finite numbers by the induction hypothesis.

<a id="pm-h-16"></a>
#### An ample component and a bounded complement

Use <a href="#lem:klt-model" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:klt-model">9</a> to replace $X$ by a $\mathbb Q$-factorial klt variety, with effective crepant lc boundary and the same divisorial valuation $S$. Its constant degree is unchanged. Choose once and for all a rational number <a id="eq:bchoice"></a>$$\label{eq:bchoice}
 0<b=b(t)<t.$$ Apply <a href="#lem:positive-component" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:positive-component">10</a> to obtain a Mori fibre contraction $X_1\to Z_1$ on which $S_1$ is relatively ample.

<a id="eq:first-horizontal"></a>
<a id="eq:C1"></a>
If $\dim Z_1>0$, the divisor $S_1$ is horizontal: an effective vertical divisor is trivial on the generic fibre and cannot be relatively ample on a positive-dimensional fibre. Its coefficient in $B_1$ is at least $t$, so the generic-fibre induction gives $$\label{eq:first-horizontal}
 c(S/k)\leq M_{<d}(t).$$ We may therefore assume $Z_1=\operatorname{Spec}k$. Now $S_1$ and $-K_{X_1}$ are ample, and $-K_{X_1}-bS_1$ is nef. [lem:complement](#lem:complement) supplies an integer $n=n(d,b)$ and a boundary $C_1$ over $k$ satisfying $$\label{eq:C1}
 (X_1,C_1)\text{ lc},\qquad C_1\geq bS_1,
 \qquad n(K_{X_1}+C_1)\sim0.$$ The index $n$ is independent of the field, the original boundary, and its denominators.

<a id="pm-h-17"></a>
#### A Mori fibre space with controlled singularities

Set $\epsilon=1/n$. By <a href="#cor:finite-low" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="cor:finite-low">6</a>, there are only finitely many valuations with $a(v,X_1,0)<\epsilon$. They are exceptional, since a divisor on $X_1$ has log discrepancy one for the zero boundary. Every such valuation is an lc place of $(X_1,C_1)$. Indeed, the principal-divisor identity in <a href="#eq:C1" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:C1">[eq:C1]</a> implies $$a(v,X_1,C_1)\in\tfrac1n\mathbb Z,
 \qquad 0\leq a(v,X_1,C_1)\leq a(v,X_1,0)<\tfrac1n,$$ so the first discrepancy is zero. The middle inequality uses that $C_1$ is effective and $\mathbb Q$-Cartier.

Extract exactly these exceptional divisors by $\pi:X_2\to X_1$, using the extraction theorem for the klt pair $(X_1,0)$. The variety $X_2$ is $\mathbb Q$-factorial, and the crepant pair $(X_2,C_2)$ is effective and lc, with coefficient one on each $\pi$-exceptional divisor. Moreover $X_2$ is $\epsilon$-lc. To see this, write $$K_{X_2}+G=\pi^*K_{X_1},
 \qquad
 G=\sum_{F\text{ exceptional}}(1-a(F,X_1,0))F\geq0.$$ For valuations not extracted, effectivity and $\mathbb Q$-Cartierness of $G$ give $a(v,X_2,0)\geq a(v,X_1,0)\geq\epsilon$. Each extracted valuation now has log discrepancy one on $X_2$ with zero boundary.

<a id="eq:second-mori"></a>
<a id="eq:Sdiscrepancy"></a>
<a id="eq:bounded-fano-case"></a>
Since $K_{X_2}\sim_{\mathbb Q}-C_2$ and $C_2$ is nonzero and effective, $K_{X_2}$ is not pseudo-effective. Run a $K_{X_2}$-MMP with ample scaling to a Mori fibre contraction $$\label{eq:second-mori}
 X_2\dashrightarrow X_3\xrightarrow{g}Z.$$ The variety $X_3$ is $\mathbb Q$-factorial and $\epsilon$-lc, since log discrepancies do not decrease along this MMP. By <a href="#lem:crepant" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:crepant">3</a>, its transformed boundary satisfies $$(X_3,C_3)\text{ lc},\qquad C_3\geq0,
 \qquad n(K_{X_3}+C_3)\sim0.$$ Let $v_S$ denote the original valuation of $S$, whether or not it survives as a divisor on $X_3$. Crepancy gives $$\label{eq:Sdiscrepancy}
 a(v_S,X_3,C_3)\leq1-b<1.$$ If $\dim Z=0$, then $Z=\operatorname{Spec}k$ and $X_3$ is an $\epsilon$-lc Fano variety. [prop:bab-orbits](#prop:bab-orbits) proves $$\label{eq:bounded-fano-case}
 c(S/k)\leq A(d,1/n,n).$$ We henceforth assume $0<\dim Z<d$.

<a id="pm-h-18"></a>
#### Recovering the divisor over a positive-dimensional base

We first realize $v_S$ as a prime $P$ on a model of Fano type over $Z$. For a sufficiently small rational $\lambda>0$, set $\Gamma=(1-\lambda)C_3$. The pair $(X_3,\Gamma)$ is klt: its log discrepancies are the corresponding convex combinations for the klt pair $(X_3,0)$ and the lc pair $(X_3,C_3)$. Also $$-(K_{X_3}+\Gamma)\sim_{\mathbb Q}-\lambda K_{X_3}$$ is ample over $Z$. By <a href="#eq:Sdiscrepancy" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:Sdiscrepancy">[eq:Sdiscrepancy]</a>, $\lambda$ can be chosen so that $a(v_S,X_3,\Gamma)<1$.

If $v_S$ is exceptional over $X_3$, extract it alone by a projective birational morphism $h:U\to X_3$ with $U$ $\mathbb Q$-factorial. Otherwise set $U=X_3$. The crepant boundaries for $C_3$ and $\Gamma$ are both effective: on the possible new exceptional prime their coefficients are respectively $1-a(v_S,X_3,C_3)\geq b$ and $1-a(v_S,X_3,\Gamma)>0$. The first gives an lc log Calabi–Yau pair $(U,C_U)$ with the same principal multiple $n$; the second gives a klt pair $(U,\Gamma_U)$ whose anti-log canonical divisor $L$ is nef and big over $Z$.

This makes $U$ of Fano type over $Z$ in the ample sense used above. Indeed, write $L\sim_{\mathbb Q}A+D$ over $Z$, with $A$ relatively ample and $D\geq0$. For sufficiently small $\eta>0$, the boundary $\Gamma_U+\eta D$ is effective and klt, and $$-(K_U+\Gamma_U+\eta D)
 \sim_{\mathbb Q}L-\eta D\sim_{\mathbb Q}(1-\eta)L+\eta A$$ is ample over $Z$.

<a id="eq:second-horizontal"></a>
Let $P$ denote the prime realizing $v_S$ on $U$. If $P$ is horizontal over $Z$, its coefficient in $C_U$ is at least $b$ and the generic-fibre induction gives $$\label{eq:second-horizontal}
 c(S/k)=c(P/k)\leq M_{<d}(b).$$ It remains to treat the case where $P$ is vertical.

<a id="pm-h-19"></a>
#### A horizontal coefficient-one component

The bigness established before the second MMP now supplies the component needed for adjunction. On $X_2$ the divisor $\pi^*S_1$ is effective and big. Its support is contained in the strict transform of $S_1$ and the exceptional divisors of $\pi$. Its pushforward to $X_3$ under <a href="#eq:second-mori" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:second-mori">[eq:second-mori]</a> is also effective and big. For completeness, bigness survives this pushforward because every section of a divisible multiple upstairs is a rational function with nonnegative divisor on every prime that remains downstairs. The birational map extracts no divisors, so these sections inject into the corresponding spaces downstairs; the growth of order $m^d$ is retained.

An effective big divisor on $X_3$ cannot have only vertical components over $Z$. Such a divisor restricts to zero on the generic fibre, whereas a big divisor restricts to a big class: restrict a Kodaira decomposition into an ample rational divisor and an effective divisor. The generic fibre has positive dimension, so its zero class is not big.

The transform of $S_1$, if present on $X_3$, is vertical because $v_S$ has vertical centre. Hence some horizontal component $E_3$ of the pushforward of $\pi^*S_1$ comes from a $\pi$-exceptional divisor. All of those have coefficient one in $C_2$, and their uncontracted transforms have coefficient one in $C_3$. We have therefore found <a id="eq:horizontal-floor"></a>$$\label{eq:horizontal-floor}
 E_3\subset\lfloor C_3\rfloor,
 \qquad E_3\text{ horizontal over }Z.$$ The generic-fibre induction at threshold one gives <a id="eq:Ebound"></a>$$\label{eq:Ebound}
 c(E_3/k)\leq M_{<d}(1).$$

<a id="pm-h-20"></a>
#### Intersection, adjunction, and counting conjugates

Apply <a href="#lem:vertical" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:vertical">11</a> to $U/Z$ and $P$. We obtain a model $U'$ with a contraction $f:U'\to Z'$, birational $Z'\to Z$, and $mP'=f^*T$ for a nonzero effective Cartier divisor $T$ on $Z'$. The transform $E$ of $E_3$ survives and is horizontal. Its coefficient in the crepant boundary $C'$ remains one, the coefficient of $P'$ is at least $b$, and $$(U',C')\text{ lc},\qquad C'\geq0,
 \qquad n(K_{U'}+C')\sim0.$$ In particular, $P'\ne E$. Since $E\to Z'$ is surjective, $P'|_{E^\nu}$ is a nonzero effective rational divisor.

- Horizontal arrow from $E^\nu$ to $U'$ labeled "$\iota \text{ (finite)}$"
- Vertical downward arrow from $U'$ to $Z'$ labeled "$f, \quad mP' = f^*T$"
- Diagonal arrow pointing from $E^\nu$ down-right to $Z'$ labeled "$f \circ \iota$" above the arrow and "surjective" below the arrow.
```


Take a prime component $J$ of $P'|_{E^\nu}$. By <a href="#lem:adjunction" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:adjunction">12</a>, its coefficient in the different $C_{E^\nu}$ is positive and hence at least $1/n$. View $E^\nu$ over its own field of constants $k_E$. By <a href="#lem:constants" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:constants">2</a>, this is a normal geometrically integral projective variety of dimension $d-1$ over $k_E$, with $$[k_E:k]=c(E/k)=c(E_3/k)\leq M_{<d}(1).$$ Since $k_E/k$ is finite separable, changing the ground field from $k$ to $k_E$ does not alter its canonical divisor. The adjunction pair is therefore a log Calabi–Yau pair over $k_E$. Induction in dimension $d-1$ at threshold $1/n$ gives $$c(J/k_E)\leq N(d-1,1/n).$$ As $k_E\subset k(J)$, degrees in the tower of constant fields multiply, and hence <a id="eq:Jbound"></a>$$\label{eq:Jbound}
 c(J/k)=[k_E:k]c(J/k_E)
 \leq M_{<d}(1)N(d-1,1/n).$$

Finally work over $\overline{k}$. Choose a geometric component $J_0$ of $J$ and let $W_0$ be its image on $U'_{\overline{k}}$. The normalization map is finite, so $W_0$ has dimension $d-2$ and is contained in a geometric prime component of $P'$. The geometric components of $P'$ form a single Galois orbit. Thus every one of them contains an image of a conjugate of $J_0$: conjugate both the chosen containment and $J_0$.

<a id="eq:final-incidence"></a>
There are at most $c(J/k)$ such images. For each image, <a href="#lem:incidence" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:incidence">13</a> bounds by $2/b$ the number of components of $P'_{\overline{k}}$ that contain it, since all these components have coefficient at least $b$ in $C'_{\overline{k}}$ and $U'_{\overline{k}}$ is $\mathbb Q$-Gorenstein klt. Counting the incidences, with repeated images only increasing the upper bound, yields $$\label{eq:final-incidence}
 c(S/k)=c(P'/k)
 \leq\frac2bc(J/k)
 \leq\frac2b M_{<d}(1)N(d-1,1/n).$$

<a id="pm-h-21"></a>
#### Uniformity and conclusion

The four cases <a href="#eq:first-horizontal" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:first-horizontal">[eq:first-horizontal]</a>, <a href="#eq:bounded-fano-case" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:bounded-fano-case">[eq:bounded-fano-case]</a>, <a href="#eq:second-horizontal" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:second-horizontal">[eq:second-horizontal]</a>, and <a href="#eq:final-incidence" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:final-incidence">[eq:final-incidence]</a> exhaust the proof. In particular, after choosing $b=b(t)$ and $n=n(d,b)$, it suffices to take an integer majorant of <a id="eq:recurrence"></a>$$\label{eq:recurrence}
 \max\left\{
 \begin{aligned}
 &M_{<d}(t),\quad M_{<d}(b),\quad A(d,1/n,n),\\
 &\frac2b M_{<d}(1)N(d-1,1/n)
 \end{aligned}\right\}.$$ Every inductive term involves a strictly smaller ambient dimension. All thresholds and the complement index depend only on $d,t$. Neither the field, any original coefficient denominator, the number of extracted divisors, nor the auxiliary small perturbations enters this maximum. Thus it defines a finite bound depending only on $d,t$ and closes the induction.

For the original possibly nonnormal component $S$, the injection $H^0(S,\mathcal O_S)\hookrightarrow H^0(S^\nu,\mathcal O_{S^\nu})$ now gives the claimed Stein-degree bound. This proves <a href="#thm:main" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:main">1</a>.$\square$
