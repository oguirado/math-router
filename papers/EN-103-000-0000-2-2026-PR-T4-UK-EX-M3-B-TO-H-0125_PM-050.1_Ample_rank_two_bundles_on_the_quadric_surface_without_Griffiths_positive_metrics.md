## 1. Abstract and result contract

> We give counterexamples to Griffiths' conjecture in rank two on the quadric surface $`\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1`$. We construct an explicit bundle <i>G</i> whose coordinatewise power pullbacks, tensored with $`\mathcal O(1,1)`$, are ample for every positive power, but admit no smooth strictly Griffiths-positive Hermitian metric for all sufficiently large powers.

- **C01** (theorem, [source](#stmt-thm-main)): There is an algebraic vector bundle $G$ of rank two on $S$ such that, for every positive integer $m$, the bundle $$E_m=f_m^*G\otimes P$$ is ample. There is an integer $m_0$ such that $E_m$ admits no smooth Hermitian metric with strictly Griffiths-positive Chern curvature for every $m\ge m_0$.
  - Explanation: Constructs an ample family on the quadric surface whose sufficiently large powers exclude smooth strictly Griffiths-positive Hermitian metrics.
- **C02** (conditional_result, [source](#stmt-lem-matrix-data)): The matrices $A,B$ belong to $\mathop{\mathrm{SL}}_2(\mathbb C)$ and satisfy $$\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}A=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}B=1,\qquad \mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(A^{-1}B)=-\sqrt2,\qquad \mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(AB)=1+\sqrt2.$$ The orders of $A$, $B$, and $A^{-1}B$ in $\mathop{\mathrm{SL}}_2(\mathbb C)$ are $6$, $6$, and $8$, respectively. The induced projective orders of $A$ and $B$ are both $3$. The matrix $AB$ has two positive real reciprocal eigenvalues of distinct moduli, and $A,B$ have no common eigenline.
  - Explanation: Finite relative lift orders ensure graph compatibility, while the expanding product and the absence of a common eigenline provide the dynamical obstruction.
- **C03** (conditional_result, [source](#stmt-lem-graph-divisor)): The divisor $D$ has bidegree $(3,3)$ and exactly six singular points, all ordinary transverse intersections of two components. There are no triple intersections.
  - Explanation: The graph union has only transverse double intersections, allowing descent through nodal local rings.
- **C04** (construction, [source](#stmt-prop-construction)): These three isomorphisms glue to an algebraic isomorphism $$\label{eq:alpha} \alpha:L_1^{-1}\|_D\xrightarrow{\sim}L_2^{-1}\|_D, \qquad \alpha\|_{\Gamma_M}=M^{\otimes b}.$$ Let $\beta=(\alpha^\vee)^{-1}:L_1\|_D\to L_2\|_D$ and define $$\label{eq:equalizer} G=\bigl\{(u_1,u_2)\in L_1\oplus L_2: u_2\|_D=\beta(u_1\|_D)\bigr\}.$$ Then $G$ is an algebraic vector bundle of rank two. Both projections $\rho_i:G\to L_i$ are surjective, with exact sequences $$\label{eq:quotient-sequences} 0\longrightarrow L_i(-D)\longrightarrow G \xrightarrow{\rho_j}L_j\longrightarrow0, \qquad \{i,j\}=\{1,2\}.$$ For $F=G^\vee$, the dual inclusions $\iota_i=\rho_i^\vee:L_i^{-1}\hookrightarrow F$ span each fiber over $S\setminus D$ and satisfy $$\label{eq:dual-gluing} \iota_2\circ\alpha=\iota_1\qquad\text{on }D.$$
  - Explanation: Compatible tensor-power identifications define a locally free equalizer with two line-bundle quotients and prescribed dual gluing.
- **C05** (bound, [source](#stmt-prop-ampleness)): For every positive integer $m$, the bundle $E_m=f_m^*G\otimes P$ is ample. More precisely, $\mathcal O_{\mathbb P(E_m)}(n)$ is very ample for every even integer $n$ with $n-1\geq3m$.
  - Explanation: Alternating the quotient filtrations offsets the negative divisor twist and yields the stated very-ampleness range.
- **C06** (conditional_result, [source](#stmt-prop-limit)): Let $G$ be a holomorphic vector bundle of positive rank on $S=\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1$, and put $F=G^\vee$. Suppose that $f_m^*G\otimes P$ admits a smooth strictly Griffiths-positive Hermitian metric for infinitely many positive integers $m$. Then there is a function $$q:F\longrightarrow [0,\infty)$$ which is plurisubharmonic on the total space, is not identically zero, and has the following property: for every $x\in S$ there is a finite positive semidefinite Hermitian form $H_x$ on $F_x$ such that $$q(x,v)=H_x(v,v)\qquad(v\in F_x).$$ In particular, this fiberwise assertion holds at every base point.
  - Explanation: An infinite sequence of positive metrics yields a nonzero total-space plurisubharmonic limit that is Hermitian quadratic on every fiber.
- **C07** (conditional_result, [source](#stmt-rmk-canonical-limit)): The function in Proposition <a href="#prop:limit" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:limit">6</a> represents a distributional limit; no convergence of restrictions to a divisor is asserted. The graph identities will instead be evaluated directly in this single everywhere-defined function $q$.
  - Explanation: Graph identities are evaluated on the everywhere-defined representative rather than obtained by restricting distributional convergence.
- **C08** (conditional_result, [source](#stmt-lem-ruling-reduction)): Let $G$ be the bundle of Proposition <a href="#prop:construction" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:construction">4</a>, and put $F=G^\vee$. Suppose that $q:F\to[0,\infty)$ is finite, plurisubharmonic, not identically zero, and a Hermitian quadratic form on every fiber. Then there is a finite, nonnegative, nonzero plurisubharmonic function $a_\bullet$ on $\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(-b)$, a Hermitian positive semidefinite quadratic on each fiber, such that $$a_\bullet(Mx,M^{\otimes b}v)=a_\bullet(x,v) \qquad(M=A,B)$$ for every $x\in\mathbb P^1$ and $v\in\mathcal O(-b)_x$.
  - Explanation: The maximum principle in the complementary ruling reduces a nonzero quadratic function to an invariant squared seminorm.
- **C09** (conditional_result, [source](#stmt-lem-seminorm-curvature)): Let $L$ be a holomorphic line bundle on a connected Riemann surface $C$. Suppose that $a_\bullet:L\to[0,\infty)$ is finite, plurisubharmonic, and not identically zero, and that its restriction to every fiber is a Hermitian positive semidefinite quadratic form. In a nonvanishing holomorphic frame $e$ on $U\subset C$, write $$a_\bullet(z,w e(z))=u_e(z)\|w\|^2.$$ Then $\log u_e$, with the value $-\infty$ allowed, is subharmonic and locally integrable. The local measures $i\partial\bar\partial\log u_e$ define a positive Radon measure $\mu$ on $C$.* *If a holomorphic bundle automorphism $\widetilde T:L\to L$ covers an automorphism $T:C\to C$ and satisfies $a_\bullet\circ\widetilde T=a_\bullet$, then $T^*\mu=\mu$. If $C=\mathbb P^1$ and $L=\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(-b)$ with $b\geq1$, then $$\label{eq:seminorm-curvature-mass} \mu(\mathbb P^1)=2\pi b.$$
  - Explanation: Logarithmic weights remain locally integrable at zeros and define an invariant positive measure with the prescribed mass.
- **C10** (conditional_result, [source](#stmt-lem-no-invariant-measure)): The projective transformations induced by the matrices $A$ and $B$ in <a href="#eq:matrices" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:matrices">[eq:matrices]</a> preserve no nonzero finite positive Borel measure on $\mathbb P^1$.
  - Explanation: Expansion forces invariant mass onto fixed points, where the finite-order actions would require an excluded common eigenline.
- **C11** (conditional_result, [source](#stmt-prop-obstruction)): For the bundle $G$ of Proposition <a href="#prop:construction" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:construction">4</a>, every finite nonnegative plurisubharmonic function on the total space of $F=G^\vee$ whose restriction to each fiber is a Hermitian quadratic form is identically zero.
  - Explanation: The invariant-measure contradiction forces every finite nonnegative fiberwise Hermitian plurisubharmonic function on the dual bundle to vanish.

## 2. Definitions, validity and bounds

- **Ample vector bundle** ([source](#sec:introduction)): A holomorphic vector bundle $E$ on a projective variety is ample when the tautological quotient line bundle $\mathcal O_{\mathbb P(E)}(1)$ is ample; here $\mathbb P(E)$ parametrizes one-dimensional quotients (Hartshorne 1966, Proposition 3.2) [[Hartshorne1966](#ref-Hartshorne1966)].
- **Strict Griffiths positivity** ([source](#sec:introduction)): A smooth Hermitian metric is strictly Griffiths-positive if its Chern curvature is positive in every pair of nonzero tangent and fiber directions.
- **Chosen matrices** ([source](#eq:matrices)): Put $$\label{eq:matrices} a=e^{i\pi/3},\qquad s=\frac12-\frac{i(1+2\sqrt2)}{2\sqrt3},\qquad A=\begin{pmatrix}a&0\\0&a^{-1}\end{pmatrix},\qquad B=\begin{pmatrix}s&1\\ \sqrt2/3&1-s\end{pmatrix}.$$
- **Equalizer bundle** ([source](#stmt-prop-construction)): Let $\beta=(\alpha^\vee)^{-1}:L_1\|_D\to L_2\|_D$ and define $$\label{eq:equalizer} G=\bigl\{(u_1,u_2)\in L_1\oplus L_2: u_2\|_D=\beta(u_1\|_D)\bigr\}.$$
- **Bundle family** ([source](#sec:construction)): The bundles in the theorem are $$\label{eq:family} E_m=f_m^*G\otimes P.$$
- **Generated symmetric power** ([source](#sec:ampleness)): Choose $n$ even with $n-1\geq3m$, and set $$\label{eq:generated-symmetric-power} W=\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}^n V\otimes P^{n-1}.$$
- **Trace quadratic** ([source](#sec:limit)): If $t$ is a nonvanishing holomorphic frame of $P^{-1}$ on an open set $U\subset S$, define on $F\|_U$ $$\label{eq:limit-trace} q_{m,t}(x,v) =\frac{1}{m^2}\sum_{y\in f_m^{-1}(x)} \bigl\\|v\otimes(f_m^*t)^{1/m}(y)\bigr\\|_{m,y}^2,$$ where the sum counts multiplicities.

## 4. Engineering Triptych

### Panel 1 - epistemic contract

- **I-ampleness-range**: The stated even-exponent bound makes every filtration bidegree nonnegative.
- **I-metric-scope**: The metric exclusion concerns smooth strictly Griffiths-positive Hermitian metrics for sufficiently large powers.

### Panel 2 - negative knowledge

- **A-cutoff**: Compute an effective metric exclusion threshold from the ampleness bound. -> The ampleness certificate does not determine the metric threshold.
- **A-divisor**: Pass restrictions of the approximating metrics to the graph divisor. -> Distributional convergence does not assert convergence of divisor restrictions.

### Panel 3 - reference implementation and oracle

Applicability: `constructive`. The filtration gives finitely many explicit line-bundle bidegrees for each admissible even exponent. The code checks this arithmetic component; compactness does not give an effective metric cutoff.

```python
# INPUTS: JSON list of integer pairs [m,n].
# PRECONDITIONS: m positive; n even, 2 <= n <= 128; invalid pairs yield an error record.
# OUTPUTS: Ordered filtration bidegrees and whether n-1 >= 3m.
# TIME: O(sum n) exact integer operations, with bit cost depending on m.
# SPACE: O(sum n) integer pairs, with bit size depending on m.
# ARITHMETIC: Exact arbitrary-precision fmpz integers; no floating point.
# FAILURE: Invalid pairs return invalid_input; malformed JSON fails.
import json
import sys
from flint import fmpz

def certificate(pair):
    if not isinstance(pair,list) or len(pair)!=2 or any(type(x) is not int for x in pair):
        return {"error":"invalid_input"}
    mm, nn = pair
    if mm < 1 or nn < 2 or nn > 128 or nn % 2:
        return {"error":"invalid_input"}
    m, n = fmpz(mm), fmpz(nn)
    rows = []
    for j in range(nn//2):
        base = 18*m*j+n-1
        tail = 24*m*(n-2*j)
        rows.extend([[int(base),int(base+tail)],
                     [int(base+tail-3*m),int(base-3*m)]])
    rows.append([int(9*m*n+n-1),int(9*m*n+n-1)])
    return {"admissible":bool(n-1 >= 3*m),"degrees":rows}

print(json.dumps([certificate(pair) for pair in json.load(sys.stdin)],sort_keys=True))
```

## 6. Applicability limits and search

- **L01** (nonconstructive): The metric-exclusion cutoff is ineffective; no effective value of $m_0$ is supplied. Blocked use: ['Computing a numerical metric-exclusion cutoff from the compactness argument.']
- **L02** (counterexample_scope): The counterexample concerns the specified rank-two bundle family on the quadric surface, with metric exclusion only for sufficiently large powers. Blocked use: ['Extending the counterexample to arbitrary bundles or varieties.', 'Claiming metric exclusion for every positive power.']
- **L03** (hypothesis): The explicit very-ampleness certificate requires positive integral $m$, even integral $n$, and $n-1\geq3m$. Blocked use: ['Applying the certificate outside its parity and inequality hypotheses.', 'Treating failure of the sufficient inequality as failure of ampleness.']
- **L04** (approximation): Distributional convergence does not assert convergence after restriction to the graph divisor. Blocked use: ['Passing a distributional limit through restriction to the divisor.']
- **L05** (edge_case): The intrinsic quadratic limit must be defined at every base point; raw pointwise limits need not be plurisubharmonic. Blocked use: ['Using a raw pointwise coefficient limit without recovering its plurisubharmonic representative.']
- **L06** (edge_case): Rank loss is allowed in the limiting quadratic form, but total collapse and loss of fiberwise quadratic structure are excluded. Blocked use: ['Assuming the limiting form is positive definite.', 'Replacing the nonzero quadratic limit by an arbitrary homogeneous function.']
- **L07** (hypothesis): The metric obstruction excludes smooth strictly Griffiths-positive Hermitian metrics on the specified bundles. Blocked use: ['Claiming the same exclusion for semipositive metrics from this theorem.', 'Equating positivity after determinant twists or symmetric powers with positivity on the original bundle.']
- **L08** (hypothesis): The invariant-measure obstruction requires a nonzero finite positive Borel measure jointly preserved by the projective actions. Blocked use: ['Extending the obstruction to infinite or signed measures without a separate proof.', 'Replacing joint invariance by invariance under the product alone.']
- **L09** (edge_case): Zeros of logarithmic weights and atoms of the curvature measure remain part of the distributional mass calculation. Blocked use: ['Excising zeros before calculating curvature mass.', 'Assuming the curvature measure is diffuse or represented by a smooth density.']
- **L10** (hypothesis): Projectivization parametrizes quotient lines; the tautological twists in the very-ampleness argument use that convention. Blocked use: ['Changing to the line-subspace convention without adjusting the tautological bundle.']
- **L11** (hypothesis): Hermitian quadratic dependence is essential to the obstruction; homogeneous Finsler squared norms need not have that dependence. Blocked use: ['Applying the quadratic-limit obstruction to arbitrary Finsler metrics.']
- **L12** (formal_scope): The exact finite arithmetic checks certify numerical hypotheses and matrix identities, while the analytic limit and geometric obstruction require their source proofs. Blocked use: ['Treating finite numerical checks as a proof of the entire counterexample.']

>
> ## Scope
>
> Griffiths' positivity conjecture predicts that every ample holomorphic vector bundle on a smooth complex projective variety admits a smooth Hermitian metric with strictly Griffiths-positive curvature. The formalized counterexample starts with a rank-two algebraic bundle $G$ on $\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1$. Its coordinatewise power pullbacks, tensored with $\mathcal O(1,1)$, are ample for every positive power but admit no such metric for all sufficiently large powers.
>
> The separate very-ampleness result for all admissible even exponents is not included.

abbrev ProjectiveLine := OnePoint ℂ
abbrev QuadricSurface := ProjectiveLine × ProjectiveLine

def reciprocalP1 (x : ProjectiveLine) : ProjectiveLine :=
  x.elim (0 : ℂ) (fun z ↦ if z = 0 then ∞ else (z⁻¹ : ℂ))

def affineP1 (i : Fin 2) (z : ℂ) : ProjectiveLine :=
  if i = 0 then reciprocalP1 z else (z : ProjectiveLine)

def affineQuadric (i : Fin 2 × Fin 2) (z : ℂ × ℂ) : QuadricSurface :=
  (affineP1 i.1 z.1, affineP1 i.2 z.2)

def coordP1 (i : Fin 2) (x : ProjectiveLine) : ℂ :=
  (if i=0 then reciprocalP1 x else x).elim 0 id
def coordQuadric (i : Fin 2 × Fin 2) (x : QuadricSurface) : ℂ × ℂ :=
  (coordP1 i.1 x.1,coordP1 i.2 x.2)

def powerP1 (m : ℕ) (x : ProjectiveLine) : ProjectiveLine :=
  x.elim ∞ (fun z ↦ ((z^m : ℂ) : ProjectiveLine))
def powerQuadric (m : ℕ) (x : QuadricSurface) : QuadricSurface :=
  (powerP1 m x.1,powerP1 m x.2)

abbrev FiberCoordinates := Fin 2 → ℂ
abbrev TwoMatrix := Matrix (Fin 2) (Fin 2) ℂ
abbrev BasePolynomial := MvPolynomial (Fin 2) ℂ
def baseEval (p : BasePolynomial) (z : BaseCoordinates) : ℂ :=
  MvPolynomial.eval ![z.1,z.2] p

def AlgebraicallyRegularOn (U : Set BaseCoordinates) (f : BaseCoordinates → ℂ) : Prop :=
  ∀ z ∈ U, ∃ p q : BasePolynomial, baseEval q z ≠ 0 ∧
    ∀ w ∈ U, baseEval q w ≠ 0 → f w=baseEval p w/baseEval q w

  {x | x ∈ range (affineQuadric i) ∧ baseEval p (coordQuadric i x) ≠ 0}

  chart : ℕ → Fin 2 × Fin 2
  equation : ℕ → BasePolynomial
  finiteCover : ∀ i, numberOfCharts ≤ i → equation i=0

  transition_self : ∀ i x, x ∈ principalChart (chart i) (equation i) →

  transition_mul : ∀ i j k x,

def coordinateDomain (V : AlgebraicRankTwoBundle) (i : ℕ) : Set BaseCoordinates :=

def dualTransition (V : AlgebraicRankTwoBundle) (i j : ℕ) (x : QuadricSurface) : TwoMatrix :=

def hyperplaneTransition (i j : Fin 2) (x : ProjectiveLine) : ℂ :=

def polarizationTransition (i j : Fin 2 × Fin 2) (x : QuadricSurface) : ℂ :=
  hyperplaneTransition i.1 j.1 x.1 * hyperplaneTransition i.2 j.2 x.2

def IsPowerPullbackTwist (G : AlgebraicRankTwoBundle) (m : ℕ)
    (E : AlgebraicRankTwoBundle) : Prop :=
  E.numberOfCharts=G.numberOfCharts ∧ E.chart=G.chart ∧
  (∀ i, E.equation i = (MvPolynomial.bind₁ fun j ↦ MvPolynomial.X j ^ m) (G.equation i)) ∧
  ∀ i j x, x ∈ E.domain i → x ∈ E.domain j →
    E.transition i j x = polarizationTransition (G.chart i) (G.chart j) x •
      G.transition i j (powerQuadric m x)

structure SymmetricSection (V : AlgebraicRankTwoBundle) (n : ℕ) where
  coefficient : ℕ → Fin (n+1) → BaseCoordinates → ℂ
  regular : ∀ i k, AlgebraicallyRegularOn (V.coordinateDomain i) (coefficient i k)
  compatible : ∀ i j x, x ∈ V.domain i → x ∈ V.domain j → ∀ v : FiberCoordinates,
    (∑ k : Fin (n+1), coefficient i k (coordQuadric (V.chart i) x) *
      ((V.dualTransition i j x).mulVec v 0)^k.val *
      ((V.dualTransition i j x).mulVec v 1)^(n-k.val)) =
    ∑ k : Fin (n+1), coefficient j k (coordQuadric (V.chart j) x) * v 0^k.val * v 1^(n-k.val)

def SymmetricSection.value {V : AlgebraicRankTwoBundle} {n : ℕ}
    (s : SymmetricSection V n) (i : ℕ) (x : QuadricSurface) (v : FiberCoordinates) : ℂ :=
  ∑ k : Fin (n+1), s.coefficient i k (coordQuadric (V.chart i) x) * v 0^k.val * v 1^(n-k.val)

def ProjectiveAlgebraicCone {N : ℕ} (C : Set (Fin N → ℂ)) : Prop :=
  ∃ equations : Set (MvPolynomial (Fin N) ℂ),
    (∀ p ∈ equations, ∃ d : ℕ, p.IsHomogeneous d) ∧
    C = {y | ∀ p ∈ equations, MvPolynomial.eval y p=0}

    (v : FiberCoordinates) : Fin N → ℂ := fun j ↦ (s j).value i x v

def GivesClosedProjectiveEmbedding {V : AlgebraicRankTwoBundle} {n N : ℕ}
    (s : Fin N → SymmetricSection V n) : Prop :=
  (∀ i x, x ∈ V.domain i → ∀ v : FiberCoordinates, v ≠ 0 → sectionVector s i x v ≠ 0) ∧
  (∀ i j x y, x ∈ V.domain i → y ∈ V.domain j → ∀ v w : FiberCoordinates,
    v ≠ 0 → w ≠ 0 → (∃ c : ℂ, c ≠ 0 ∧ sectionVector s i x v = c • sectionVector s j y w) →
    x=y ∧ ∃ c : ℂ, c ≠ 0 ∧ v=c • ((V.dualTransition i j x).mulVec w)) ∧

    ∃ v : FiberCoordinates, v ≠ 0 ∧ ∃ c : ℂ, c ≠ 0 ∧ a=c • sectionVector s i x v}) ∧
  ∀ i x, x ∈ V.domain i → ∀ v : FiberCoordinates, v ≠ 0 →
    ∃ j : Fin N, ∃ b : Fin 2, v b ≠ 0 ∧ sectionVector s i x v j ≠ 0 ∧
    ∃ p q : Fin 3 → MvPolynomial (Fin N) ℂ,
      (∀ k, MvPolynomial.eval (fun l ↦ sectionVector s i x v l / sectionVector s i x v j) (q k) ≠ 0) ∧
      ∀ i' x', x' ∈ V.domain i' → ∀ v' : FiberCoordinates, v' ≠ 0 →
        sectionVector s i' x' v' j ≠ 0 →
        (∀ k, MvPolynomial.eval (fun l ↦ sectionVector s i' x' v' l / sectionVector s i' x' v' j) (q k) ≠ 0) →
        x' ∈ V.domain i ∧ ((V.dualTransition i i' x').mulVec v') b ≠ 0 ∧
        (∀ k : Fin 3,
          (![ (coordQuadric (V.chart i) x').1, (coordQuadric (V.chart i) x').2,
             ((V.dualTransition i i' x').mulVec v') (1-b) /
               ((V.dualTransition i i' x').mulVec v') b ] : Fin 3 → ℂ) k =
          MvPolynomial.eval (fun l ↦ sectionVector s i' x' v' l / sectionVector s i' x' v' j) (p k) /
          MvPolynomial.eval (fun l ↦ sectionVector s i' x' v' l / sectionVector s i' x' v' j) (q k))

def IsAmple (V : AlgebraicRankTwoBundle) : Prop :=
  ∃ n : ℕ, 0 < n ∧ ∃ N : ℕ, ∃ s : Fin N → SymmetricSection V n,
    GivesClosedProjectiveEmbedding s

def holomorphicMetricDerivative (H : BaseCoordinates → TwoMatrix)
    (z u : BaseCoordinates) : TwoMatrix :=
  (1/2 : ℂ) • (fderiv ℝ H z u - Complex.I • fderiv ℝ H z (Complex.I • u))

def metricMixedHessian (H : BaseCoordinates → TwoMatrix)
    (z u : BaseCoordinates) : TwoMatrix :=
  (1/4 : ℂ) • ((fderiv ℝ (fun y ↦ fderiv ℝ H y) z u) u +
    (fderiv ℝ (fun y ↦ fderiv ℝ H y) z (Complex.I • u)) (Complex.I • u))

structure SmoothHermitianMetric (V : AlgebraicRankTwoBundle) where
  dualMatrix : ℕ → BaseCoordinates → TwoMatrix
  smooth : ∀ i, ContDiffOn ℝ ((⊤ : ℕ∞) : WithTop ℕ∞) (dualMatrix i) (V.coordinateDomain i)
  positive : ∀ i z, z ∈ V.coordinateDomain i → (dualMatrix i z).PosDef
  compatible : ∀ i j x, x ∈ V.domain i → x ∈ V.domain j →
    dualMatrix j (coordQuadric (V.chart j) x) =
      (V.dualTransition i j x).conjTranspose * dualMatrix i (coordQuadric (V.chart i) x) *
        V.dualTransition i j x

def SmoothHermitianMetric.StrictlyGriffithsPositive {V : AlgebraicRankTwoBundle}
    (h : SmoothHermitianMetric V) : Prop :=
  ∀ i z, z ∈ V.coordinateDomain i → ∀ u : BaseCoordinates, u ≠ 0 →
    (metricMixedHessian (h.dualMatrix i) z u -
      (holomorphicMetricDerivative (h.dualMatrix i) z u).conjTranspose *
        (h.dualMatrix i z)⁻¹ * holomorphicMetricDerivative (h.dualMatrix i) z u).PosDef

def SourceMainTheorem : Prop :=
  ∃ G : AlgebraicRankTwoBundle, ∃ E : ℕ → AlgebraicRankTwoBundle,
    (∀ m, 0 < m → IsPowerPullbackTwist G m (E m)) ∧
    (∀ m, 0 < m → IsAmple (E m)) ∧
    ∃ m₀ : ℕ, 0 < m₀ ∧ ∀ m, m₀ ≤ m →
      ¬ ∃ h : SmoothHermitianMetric (E m), h.StrictlyGriffithsPositive

## 7. Complete mathematical paper


We give counterexamples to Griffiths' conjecture in rank two on the quadric surface $`\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1`$. We construct an explicit bundle <i>G</i> whose coordinatewise power pullbacks, tensored with $`\mathcal O(1,1)`$, are ample for every positive power, but admit no smooth strictly Griffiths-positive Hermitian metric for all sufficiently large powers.

<a id="sec:introduction"></a>
### Introduction

A holomorphic vector bundle $E$ on a projective variety is ample when the tautological quotient line bundle $\mathcal O_{\mathbb P(E)}(1)$ is ample; here $\mathbb P(E)$ parametrizes one-dimensional quotients (Hartshorne 1966, Proposition 3.2) <a id="pm-cite-1"></a>[[Hartshorne1966](#ref-Hartshorne1966)]. A smooth Hermitian metric is strictly Griffiths-positive if its Chern curvature is positive in every pair of nonzero tangent and fiber directions. Such a metric implies ampleness. Griffiths’ conjecture asks for the converse on smooth complex projective varieties (Griffiths 1969, Problem (0.9)) <a id="pm-cite-2"></a>[[Griffiths1969](#ref-Griffiths1969)].

Set $$S=\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1,\qquad P=\mathcal O_S(1,1),$$ where $p_1,p_2:S\to\mathbb P^1$ are the projections, and $\mathcal O_S(r,s)=p_1^*\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(r)\otimes p_2^*\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(s)$. Let $f_m:S\to S$ raise the homogeneous coordinates in each factor to their $m$th powers. Thus $\deg f_m=m^2$ and $f_m^*P\simeq P^m$.

<a id="stmt-thm-main"></a>
<div id="thm:main" class="theorem">

**Theorem 1**. *There is an algebraic vector bundle $G$ of rank two on $S$ such that, for every positive integer $m$, the bundle $$E_m=f_m^*G\otimes P$$ is ample. There is an integer $m_0$ such that $E_m$ admits no smooth Hermitian metric with strictly Griffiths-positive Chern curvature for every $m\ge m_0$.*

</div>

The bundle $G$ is given explicitly in Section <a href="#sec:construction" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:construction">2</a>. The ampleness assertion has a quantitative certificate: Proposition <a href="#prop:ampleness" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:ampleness">5</a> proves that $\mathcal O_{\mathbb P(E_m)}(n)$ is very ample for every even $n$ with $n-1\geq3m$. The threshold $m_0$ is obtained by compactness and is ineffective. Thus Griffiths’ conjecture fails in rank two on the quadric surface.

<a id="pm-h-3"></a>
#### Historical context and related results

Hartshorne developed the algebraic theory of ample vector bundles through symmetric powers, global generation, and the tautological line bundle (Hartshorne 1966) <a id="pm-cite-3"></a>[[Hartshorne1966](#ref-Hartshorne1966)]. Griffiths’ original question used the term “cohomologically positive”; his definition of “ample” in that article included a first-jet generation condition (Griffiths 1969, Definitions (0.2)–(0.3), Problem (0.9)) <a id="pm-cite-4"></a>[[Griffiths1969](#ref-Griffiths1969)]. The conjecture above is the modern formulation of that question. Its significance is the proposed passage from algebraic positivity to a Hermitian curvature condition on the bundle itself, which can then enter differential-geometric vanishing arguments.

For line bundles the algebraic and curvature notions agree (Umemura 1973, Proposition 1.15) <a id="pm-cite-5"></a>[[Umemura1973](#ref-Umemura1973)]. On complex curves, Hartshorne characterized ampleness by positivity of the degrees of all nonzero quotient bundles (Hartshorne 1971, Theorem 2.4) <a id="pm-cite-6"></a>[[Hartshorne1971](#ref-Hartshorne1971)]. Umemura proved the metric equivalence first for elliptic curves and then for compact Riemann surfaces (Umemura 1972, 1973) <a id="pm-cite-7"></a>[[Umemura1972](#ref-Umemura1972), [Umemura1973](#ref-Umemura1973)]; see in particular (Umemura 1973, Theorem 2.6) <a id="pm-cite-8"></a>[[Umemura1973](#ref-Umemura1973)] and the later work of Campana–Flenner (Campana and Flenner 1990) <a id="pm-cite-9"></a>[[CampanaFlenner1990](#ref-CampanaFlenner1990)]. These results make a surface the first possible base dimension for the phenomenon in Theorem <a href="#thm:main" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:main">1</a>.

Several positive results recover strong curvature after modifying the bundle. Nakano positivity is a stronger curvature condition than Griffiths positivity. Berndtsson proved Nakano positivity of $E\otimes\det E$ for ample $E$ through direct-image curvature (Berndtsson 2009, Theorem 1.3) <a id="pm-cite-10"></a>[[Berndtsson2009](#ref-Berndtsson2009)]. Liu–Sun–Yang established Nakano and dual-Nakano positivity for determinant-twisted symmetric powers and for sufficiently large symmetric powers of ample bundles (Liu, Sun, and Yang 2013, Theorems 1.1–1.2 and Corollary 1.3) <a id="pm-cite-11"></a>[[LiuSunYang2013](#ref-LiuSunYang2013)]. The determinant twists and symmetric powers are part of these conclusions; they do not supply a Griffiths-positive metric on $E$ itself.

Analytic approaches to that remaining question include Demailly’s Hermitian–Yang–Mills system and subsequent curvature functionals (Demailly 2021, 2022) <a id="pm-cite-12"></a>[[Demailly2021](#ref-Demailly2021), [Demailly2022](#ref-Demailly2022)]. Pingali identified a limitation of the initial system and solved a related system for direct sums of ample line bundles on Riemann surfaces (Pingali 2021, 2023) <a id="pm-cite-13"></a>[[Pingali2021](#ref-Pingali2021), [Pingali2023](#ref-Pingali2023)]. Murakami subsequently obtained an analytic proof of the full curve case through the Demailly system (Murakami 2026, Theorem 1.2) <a id="pm-cite-14"></a>[[Murakami2026](#ref-Murakami2026)]. These developments concern smooth Hermitian metrics and clarify the analytic difficulty of recovering them from ampleness.

The requirement that the metric be Hermitian is essential to the problem. Kobayashi characterized ampleness of $E$ by negatively curved strongly pseudoconvex complex Finsler structures on $E^*$ (Kobayashi 1975, Theorems 4.1 and 5.1) <a id="pm-cite-15"></a>[[Kobayashi1975](#ref-Kobayashi1975)]. His term “convex” means positivity of the complex fiber Hessian of the squared metric (Kobayashi 1975, sec. 2) <a id="pm-cite-16"></a>[[Kobayashi1975](#ref-Kobayashi1975)]. Their squared norms are homogeneous of degree two but need not be Hermitian quadratics in the fiber variable. More recently, Lempert showed that an equivariant fiberwise rule applied to a prescribed positive tautological metric can fail to produce even a semipositive Hermitian metric (Lempert 2026, Theorem 2.1) <a id="pm-cite-17"></a>[[Lempert2026Construction](#ref-Lempert2026Construction)]. His examples are direct sums of a positive line bundle and also carry positive Hermitian metrics. That result identifies an obstruction to the specified construction rule; the obstruction below concerns every smooth strictly positive metric on the indicated bundles.

In a preprint posted on September 22, 2026, Du–Xie state the existence of a rank-two ample bundle on the abelian surface $C\times C$, where $C$ is the elliptic curve $y^2=x^3-x$, without a smooth Griffiths-semipositive metric (Du and Xie 2026b, Theorem 1.1) <a id="pm-cite-18"></a>[[DuXie2026Counterexample](#ref-DuXie2026Counterexample)]. Their accompanying preprint gives a first-jet characterization of Griffiths positivity in terms of an adjoint-Levi obstruction (Du and Xie 2026a, Theorem 1.1) <a id="pm-cite-19"></a>[[DuXie2026FirstJet](#ref-DuXie2026FirstJet)]. The construction here is on the rational quadric and supplies a specified power-map family, an all-$m$ ampleness certificate, and the general limiting statement of Proposition <a href="#prop:limit" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:limit">6</a>. Its metric conclusion is the absence of smooth *strictly* Griffiths-positive metrics for all sufficiently large $m$.

A subsequent preprint of Liu–Wan, posted on September 29, 2026, states the existence of ample rank-two bundles on every Hirzebruch surface, including $\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1$, that are slope-stable with respect to suitable ample polarizations and admit no smooth strongly pseudoconvex Finsler metric of semipositive Kobayashi curvature (Liu and Wan 2026, Theorem 1.1) <a id="pm-cite-20"></a>[[LiuWan2026](#ref-LiuWan2026)]. That curvature condition is imposed on the original bundle; Kobayashi’s ampleness characterization above uses negative curvature on its dual. The result concerns a different construction and a stronger metric exclusion than the strict Hermitian conclusion proved here.

<a id="pm-h-4"></a>
#### Proof strategy

<a id="pm-h-5"></a>
###### Compatible graph identifications.

Choose two projective transformations $A,B$ of $\mathbb P^1$ with order three and no common fixed point, such that $AB$ is conjugate to $z\mapsto\lambda z$ with $|\lambda|>1$. Their chosen linear lifts, and the relative lift $A^{-1}B$, have finite order. The two properties serve different purposes: finite relative orders allow line maps to agree at graph intersections, whereas the expanding product will prevent a common invariant measure. For $M\in\{I,A,B\}$ put $\Gamma_M=\{(x,Mx):x\in\mathbb P^1\}$ and $D=\Gamma_I\cup\Gamma_A\cup\Gamma_B$. For a suitable common multiple $b$ of the finite lift orders, set $L_1=\mathcal O_S(b,0)$ and $L_2=\mathcal O_S(0,b)$. The lifted maps identify $L_1^{-1}|_D$ with $L_2^{-1}|_D$; duality gives a corresponding identification of $L_1|_D$ and $L_2|_D$. Define $G$ by the pairs of local sections of $L_1\oplus L_2$ that agree under this identification on $D$. This is an elementary transformation in the sense of (Maruyama 1986, Introduction, (0.1)) <a id="pm-cite-21"></a>[[Maruyama1986](#ref-Maruyama1986)]. Section <a href="#sec:construction" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:construction">2</a> checks compatibility at every node, local freeness, and the two line-bundle quotients of $G$. Their dual line subbundles in $F=G^\vee$ span away from $D$ and coincide over $D$ according to the prescribed lifted transformations.

<a id="pm-h-6"></a>
###### Ampleness from the two quotients.

The two quotient sequences give two filtrations of symmetric powers. The product of the two kernel line bundles is positive in both rulings. Alternating the sequences groups the negative twists into powers of this product; each remaining quotient loses at most one additional copy of the graph divisor. After pullback by $f_m$, twisting $\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}^n(f_m^*G)$ by $P^{n-1}$ offsets that loss for sufficiently large even $n$. The line factors are then globally generated and have vanishing first cohomology. Section <a href="#sec:ampleness" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:ampleness">3</a> lifts their sections through the filtration. A relative Veronese map, the base embedding supplied by one further factor $P$, and a Segre embedding then give the stated very-ampleness certificate. This follows the symmetric-power framework of (Hartshorne 1966) <a id="pm-cite-22"></a>[[Hartshorne1966](#ref-Hartshorne1966)], while using the two quotients separately also where their kernels coincide.

<a id="pm-h-7"></a>
###### A nonzero quadratic limit.

Suppose that infinitely many $E_m$ have positive metrics. Their dual squared norms are plurisubharmonic. Average these norms over $f_m$, using a local $m$th root of a pulled-back *twisting frame* to absorb the factor $P$. Each average remains a Hermitian quadratic; the twisting transition factor tends to one as $m\to\infty$. After a scalar normalization, the coefficient matrices are locally uniformly bounded. Proposition <a href="#prop:limit" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:limit">6</a> produces a finite, nonnegative, nonzero plurisubharmonic function $q$ on $F$, quadratic on every fiber. Convolution of negative singular Hermitian metrics is developed in (Berndtsson and Păun 2008; Raufi 2015; Păun and Takayama 2018) <a id="pm-cite-23"></a>[[BerndtssonPaun2008](#ref-BerndtssonPaun2008), [Raufi2015](#ref-Raufi2015), [PaunTakayama2018](#ref-PaunTakayama2018)]. Here noncollapse and the assertion on *every* fiber require separate arguments: the graph relations lie over a divisor and cannot be read from an almost-everywhere representative.

<a id="pm-h-8"></a>
###### Incompatible invariances.

Restricting $q$ to either line subbundle makes its squared seminorm independent of the other $\mathbb P^1$ coordinate, by the maximum principle. The graph relations give a single nonzero squared seminorm on $\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(-b)$, invariant under the lifts of $A$ and $B$. Total-space plurisubharmonicity and quadratic fiber dependence imply subharmonicity of the logarithmic weights. Applying $i\partial\bar\partial$ to them gives a positive measure whose nonzero total mass is fixed by the degree $-b$, even when the seminorm has zeros. Invariance under $AB$ forces this measure to be supported on its two fixed points. Both $A$ and $B$ preserve this nonempty support. Since a permutation of at most two points whose order divides three is trivial, each would fix every support point, giving a common fixed point. Section <a href="#sec:obstruction" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:obstruction">5</a> proves this contradiction, and Section <a href="#sec:conclusion" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:conclusion">6</a> assembles the theorem.

<a id="pm-h-9"></a>
#### Conventions and notation

For a vector bundle $V$, $\mathbb P(V)$ parametrizes one-dimensional quotients, and $\mathcal O_{\mathbb P(V)}(1)$ is the tautological quotient line. The projective line $\mathbb P^1$ used to describe the graph transformations is also identified with the lines in $\mathbb C^2$; its tautological subbundle is $\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(-1)$.

We use the curvature convention of (Demailly 2021) <a id="pm-cite-24"></a>[[Demailly2021](#ref-Demailly2021)]. At a point, in local coordinates and a frame orthonormal at that point, write $$\Theta_{E,h}
 =i\sum_{j,k,\alpha,\beta}
 c_{jk\alpha\beta}\,dz_j\wedge d\bar z_k
 \otimes e_\alpha^*\otimes e_\beta.$$ Strict Griffiths positivity means $$\sum_{j,k,\alpha,\beta}
 c_{jk\alpha\beta}\xi_j\bar\xi_k v_\alpha\bar v_\beta>0
 \quad\text{for all }\xi\ne0,\ v\ne0.$$ The dual has the opposite Griffiths sign. In particular its squared norm is plurisubharmonic on the total bundle; this standard criterion is recalled where it is used. Throughout, “psh” means plurisubharmonic, and $i\partial\bar\partial u=i u_{z\bar z}\,dz\wedge d\bar z$ on a complex curve. A Hermitian quadratic form is allowed to be positive semidefinite when this is stated explicitly.

<a id="sec:construction"></a>
### A bundle defined by three graphs

We construct $G$ together with two line-bundle quotients. Their dual inclusions will span away from three graphs and agree on those graphs through specified linear maps. The quotient sequences will prove ampleness; the graph identities will constrain a quadratic limit on $G^\vee$.

We choose matrices $A,B$ with two different properties. The relative lifts $A$, $B$, and $A^{-1}B$ must have finite order, so that their tensor powers agree at graph intersections. The projective actions of $A$ and $B$ will have order three and no common fixed point, while $AB$ will have eigenvalues of distinct moduli. Together these properties will exclude an invariant measure in Section <a href="#sec:obstruction" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:obstruction">5</a>. As in the introduction, $S=\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1$, and we view $\mathbb P^1$ as the lines in $\mathbb C^2$, with tautological bundle $\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(-1)$.

<a id="eq:matrices"></a>
Put $$\label{eq:matrices}
 a=e^{i\pi/3},\qquad
 s=\frac12-\frac{i(1+2\sqrt2)}{2\sqrt3},\qquad
 A=\begin{pmatrix}a&0\\0&a^{-1}\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 B=\begin{pmatrix}s&1\\ \sqrt2/3&1-s\end{pmatrix}.$$ We use the same letters for their induced automorphisms of $\mathbb P^1$.

<a id="stmt-lem-matrix-data"></a>
<div id="lem:matrix-data" class="lemma">

**Lemma 2**. *The matrices $A,B$ belong to $\mathop{\mathrm{SL}}_2(\mathbb C)$ and satisfy $$\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}A=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}B=1,\qquad
 \mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(A^{-1}B)=-\sqrt2,\qquad
 \mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(AB)=1+\sqrt2.$$ The orders of $A$, $B$, and $A^{-1}B$ in $\mathop{\mathrm{SL}}_2(\mathbb C)$ are $6$, $6$, and $8$, respectively. The induced projective orders of $A$ and $B$ are both $3$. The matrix $AB$ has two positive real reciprocal eigenvalues of distinct moduli, and $A,B$ have no common eigenline.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-matrix-data"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-matrix-data</summary>

*Proof.* The value of $s$ gives $$s(1-s)=1+\frac{\sqrt2}{3},\qquad
 a^{-1}s+a(1-s)=-\sqrt2.$$ These identities prove the determinant and trace assertions except for the trace of $AB$. The characteristic polynomial of each of $A,B$ is $t^2-t+1$, with roots $e^{\pm i\pi/3}$, while that of $A^{-1}B$ is $t^2+\sqrt2t+1$, with roots $e^{\pm3i\pi/4}$. The roots in each pair are distinct, proving the claimed orders; in particular $A^3=B^3=-\mathop{\mathrm{Id}}$.

Cayley–Hamilton gives $A+A^{-1}=\mathop{\mathrm{Id}}$. Multiplication by $B$ and taking traces therefore yield $$\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(AB)=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}B-\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(A^{-1}B)=1+\sqrt2>2.$$ Since $\det(AB)=1$, its eigenvalues are positive real reciprocals, one greater than one. Finally, the eigenlines of $A$ are the two coordinate lines, and neither is preserved by $B$, whose two off-diagonal entries are nonzero. ◻

</details>

For $M\in\{I,A,B\}$ let $$\Gamma_M=\{(x,Mx):x\in\mathbb P^1\},\qquad
 D=\Gamma_I\cup\Gamma_A\cup\Gamma_B,$$ where $D$ has the reduced structure.

<a id="stmt-lem-graph-divisor"></a>
<div id="lem:graph-divisor" class="lemma">

**Lemma 3**. *The divisor $D$ has bidegree $(3,3)$ and exactly six singular points, all ordinary transverse intersections of two components. There are no triple intersections.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-graph-divisor"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-graph-divisor</summary>

*Proof.* Each graph is a smooth divisor of bidegree $(1,1)$. An intersection of $\Gamma_M$ and $\Gamma_N$ corresponds to a fixed point of $M^{-1}N$, hence to an eigenline of that matrix. The relative matrices are $A,B,A^{-1}B$ or their inverses. Each has two distinct eigenvalues, and the multiplier at either projective fixed point is the ratio of those eigenvalues in one order or the other. This ratio is not one. The fixed points are therefore simple, so each pair of graphs meets transversely at two points. A point on all three graphs would give a common eigenline of $A$ and $B$, contrary to Lemma <a href="#lem:matrix-data" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:matrix-data">2</a>. Thus the six pairwise intersections are distinct. Their local equations are products of two regular parameters, and the asserted description follows. ◻

</details>

Set <a id="eq:construction-lines"></a>$$\label{eq:construction-lines}
 b=24,\qquad L_1=p_1^*\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(b),\qquad
 L_2=p_2^*\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(b).$$ The exponent $24$ is a common multiple of the orders $6,6,8$ of the specified relative linear lifts $A,B,A^{-1}B$. It makes the induced tensor-power maps on the two branches of each graph intersection agree on their common fiber, as used in the descent argument below.

Along $\Gamma_M$, the map $M$ on tautological lines induces an isomorphism from $L_1^{-1}$ to $L_2^{-1}$ by taking its $b$-th tensor power.

<a id="stmt-prop-construction"></a>
<div id="prop:construction" class="proposition">

<a id="eq:alpha"></a>
<a id="eq:equalizer"></a>
<a id="eq:quotient-sequences"></a>
<a id="eq:dual-gluing"></a>
**Proposition 4**. *These three isomorphisms glue to an algebraic isomorphism $$\label{eq:alpha}
 \alpha:L_1^{-1}|_D\xrightarrow{\sim}L_2^{-1}|_D,
 \qquad \alpha|_{\Gamma_M}=M^{\otimes b}.$$ Let $\beta=(\alpha^\vee)^{-1}:L_1|_D\to L_2|_D$ and define $$\label{eq:equalizer}
 G=\bigl\{(u_1,u_2)\in L_1\oplus L_2:
          u_2|_D=\beta(u_1|_D)\bigr\}.$$ Then $G$ is an algebraic vector bundle of rank two. Both projections $\rho_i:G\to L_i$ are surjective, with exact sequences $$\label{eq:quotient-sequences}
 0\longrightarrow L_i(-D)\longrightarrow G
   \xrightarrow{\rho_j}L_j\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad \{i,j\}=\{1,2\}.$$ For $F=G^\vee$, the dual inclusions $\iota_i=\rho_i^\vee:L_i^{-1}\hookrightarrow F$ span each fiber over $S\setminus D$ and satisfy $$\label{eq:dual-gluing}
 \iota_2\circ\alpha=\iota_1\qquad\text{on }D.$$*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-prop-construction"><summary>Proof of stmt-prop-construction</summary>

*Proof.* The bundle will be an elementary transformation of $L_1\oplus L_2$ along the effective Cartier divisor $D$, as in (Maruyama 1986, Introduction, (0.1)) <a id="pm-cite-25"></a>[[Maruyama1986](#ref-Maruyama1986)]. We verify the gluing and the local basis directly, since the resulting quotient maps are needed in both subsequent arguments.

At a point of $\Gamma_M\cap\Gamma_N$, let $v$ span the first-coordinate line. For some eigenvalue $\lambda$ of $M^{-1}N$ we have $Nv=\lambda Mv$. By Lemma <a href="#lem:matrix-data" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:matrix-data">2</a>, $\lambda^{24}=1$. Consequently $$(Nv)^{\otimes b}=(Mv)^{\otimes b}.$$ Thus the two maps agree as maps of the actual line fibers. This also covers the reversed pair, for which the eigenvalue is $\lambda^{-1}$.

We check descent at a node. If $R$ is the local ring of $S$ there and $(x),(y)$ are the branch ideals, transversality gives $(x)\cap(y)=(xy)$ and the exact sequence $$0\longrightarrow R/(xy)\longrightarrow R/(x)\oplus R/(y)
   \longrightarrow R/(x,y)\longrightarrow0,$$ whose last map is the difference of the residue values. Tensoring this sequence by the locally free rank-one sheaf $\operatorname{Hom}(L_1^{-1},L_2^{-1})|_D$ shows that the equal fiber values are precisely the compatibility needed to descend the two branch sections. Their common value is nonzero, so the descended scalar is a unit in the nodal local ring. The component maps therefore glue to the isomorphism <a href="#eq:alpha" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:alpha">[eq:alpha]</a>.

<a id="eq:local-basis"></a>
Near a point of $D$, choose frames of $L_1,L_2$ and a local equation $d$ of $D$. The scalar defining $\beta$ modulo $d$ lifts, after shrinking, to a regular unit $h$. In these frames the module <a href="#eq:equalizer" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:equalizer">[eq:equalizer]</a> has basis $$\label{eq:local-basis}
 e=(1,h),\qquad f=(0,d).$$ Indeed, every element has a unique expression $u_1e+kf$, since $u_2-hu_1$ is divisible by the non-zero-divisor $d$. Away from $D$ the sheaf is simply $L_1\oplus L_2$, proving local freeness everywhere.

The first projection maps $e$ to $1$, and the second maps $e$ to the unit $h$, so both are surjective. Setting the $j$-th coordinate equal to zero in <a href="#eq:equalizer" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:equalizer">[eq:equalizer]</a> identifies its kernel with $L_i(-D)$, proving <a href="#eq:quotient-sequences" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:quotient-sequences">[eq:quotient-sequences]</a>. Their duals are line subbundles of $F$, and away from $D$ they span. On $D$, if $v\in L_1^{-1}$ and $(u_1,u_2)\in G$, then $$\bigl(\iota_2(\alpha v)\bigr)(u_1,u_2)
 = (\alpha v)(\beta u_1)=v(u_1)
 =\bigl(\iota_1(v)\bigr)(u_1,u_2),$$ because $\alpha^\vee\beta=\mathop{\mathrm{Id}}$. This is <a href="#eq:dual-gluing" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:dual-gluing">[eq:dual-gluing]</a>. ◻

</details>

For a positive integer $m$, define <a id="eq:power-maps"></a>$$\label{eq:power-maps}
 f_m\bigl([z_0:z_1],[w_0:w_1]\bigr)
   =\bigl([z_0^m:z_1^m],[w_0^m:w_1^m]\bigr).$$ This is finite of degree $m^2$, and $f_m^*\mathcal O_S(r,t)\simeq\mathcal O_S(mr,mt)$. In particular, $f_m^*P\simeq P^{\otimes m}$. The bundles in the theorem are <a id="eq:family"></a>$$\label{eq:family}
 E_m=f_m^*G\otimes P.$$

<a id="sec:ampleness"></a>
### Ampleness of every member of the family

For each $m$, we construct enough symmetric-power sections to embed $\mathbb P(E_m)$. The two quotient sequences are used alternately: their kernels individually have one negative degree, but their tensor product is positive in both rulings. The resulting filtration gives an explicit very-ampleness range within Hartshorne’s symmetric-power framework (Hartshorne 1966, Corollary 2.5 and Proposition 3.2) <a id="pm-cite-26"></a>[[Hartshorne1966](#ref-Hartshorne1966)].

<a id="stmt-prop-ampleness"></a>
<div id="prop:ampleness" class="proposition">

**Proposition 5**. *For every positive integer $m$, the bundle $E_m=f_m^*G\otimes P$ is ample. More precisely, $\mathcal O_{\mathbb P(E_m)}(n)$ is very ample for every even integer $n$ with $n-1\geq3m$.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-prop-ampleness"><summary>Proof of stmt-prop-ampleness</summary>

<a id="eq:pullback-quotients"></a>
<a id="eq:kernel-product"></a>
*Proof.* Fix $m$ and write $$V=f_m^*G,\qquad \widetilde L_i=f_m^*L_i,\qquad
 \widetilde D=f_m^*D,\qquad
 K_i=\widetilde L_i(-\widetilde D),\qquad K=K_1\otimes K_2.$$ Pullback of <a href="#eq:quotient-sequences" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:quotient-sequences">[eq:quotient-sequences]</a> gives $$\label{eq:pullback-quotients}
 0\longrightarrow K_i\longrightarrow V\longrightarrow
 \widetilde L_j\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad\{i,j\}=\{1,2\}.$$ These sequences remain exact because their original quotients are locally free. Here $$K_1=\mathcal O_S(21m,-3m),\qquad K_2=\mathcal O_S(-3m,21m),$$ and hence $$\label{eq:kernel-product}
 K=\mathcal O_S(18m,18m),\qquad
 \mathcal O_S(\widetilde D)=\mathcal O_S(3m,3m).$$ We write powers of line bundles for tensor powers throughout this proof.

**Alternating the quotient sequences.** For an extension $0\to K'\to V'\to L'\to0$ with $K',L'$ line bundles, multiplication gives $$0\longrightarrow K'\otimes\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}^{r-1}V'
   \longrightarrow\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}^r V'\longrightarrow(L')^r
   \longrightarrow0\qquad(r\geq1).$$ Indeed, a local splitting gives a kernel generator $k'$ and a lift $e'$ of a quotient generator. The monomials containing $k'$ form the kernel of the symmetric quotient, and the remaining monomial $(e')^r$ maps to a quotient generator. This is the usual symmetric-power filtration of an extension; compare the proof of (Hartshorne 1966, Corollary 3.4) <a id="pm-cite-27"></a>[[Hartshorne1966](#ref-Hartshorne1966)].

<a id="eq:graded-lines"></a>
Apply this construction first to $V\to\widetilde L_2$ and then to $V\to\widetilde L_1$. For $r\geq2$ it gives <a id="eq:symmetric-first"></a><a id="eq:symmetric-second"></a>$$\begin{aligned}
 0&\longrightarrow K_1\otimes\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}^{r-1}V
   \longrightarrow\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}^r V\longrightarrow\widetilde L_2^{\,r}
   \longrightarrow0,\label{eq:symmetric-first}\\
 0&\longrightarrow K\otimes\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}^{r-2}V
   \longrightarrow K_1\otimes\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}^{r-1}V
   \longrightarrow K_1\otimes\widetilde L_1^{\,r-1}
   \longrightarrow0.\label{eq:symmetric-second}
\end{aligned}$$ Thus each pair of steps leaves the residual bundle $K\otimes\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}^{r-2}V$, with one line-bundle quotient at each step. For even $n$, iteration gives a filtration of $\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}^nV$ with line factors $$\label{eq:graded-lines}
 \begin{gathered}
 K^j\otimes\widetilde L_2^{\,n-2j},\qquad
 K^j\otimes\widetilde L_1^{\,n-2j}(-\widetilde D),
 \qquad 0\leq j<n/2,\\
 K^{n/2}.
 \end{gathered}$$ Indeed, after $j$ pairs the residual bundle is $K^j\otimes\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}^{n-2j}V$, whose second quotient is $K^jK_1\widetilde L_1^{\,n-2j-1}
 =K^j\widetilde L_1^{\,n-2j}(-\widetilde D)$. Only the second quotient contains an additional twist by $-\widetilde D$ beyond the factors absorbed into $K^j$.

Each step uses just one of the locally split sequences <a href="#eq:pullback-quotients" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:pullback-quotients">[eq:pullback-quotients]</a>, so it remains valid when the two kernel lines have the same fiber image. Concretely, in the pulled-back basis $e,f$ of <a href="#eq:local-basis" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:local-basis">[eq:local-basis]</a>, the kernels are generated by $k=de-hf$ and $f$, with $h$ a unit. For $n=2$ the filtration is $$\mathcal O(kf)\subset kV\subset\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}^2V.$$ Since $e,k$ are also a basis of $V$, the module $kV$ has basis $ke,k^2$ and is a direct summand of $\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}^2V$, with complement generated by $e^2$. Moreover, $$kf=\frac{d}{h}ke-\frac1h k^2.$$ The coefficient $-1/h$ is a unit, so $kf$ extends to a basis of $kV$. Both inclusions therefore have locally free quotients. This verification uses no smoothness of $d=0$ and applies at nodes and to nonreduced pullbacks of the divisor.

**Global generation after twisting.** Choose $n$ even with $n-1\geq3m$, and set <a id="eq:generated-symmetric-power"></a>$$\label{eq:generated-symmetric-power}
 W=\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}^n V\otimes P^{n-1}.$$ By <a href="#eq:kernel-product" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:kernel-product">[eq:kernel-product]</a>, the bidegrees of its filtration factors, in the order of <a href="#eq:graded-lines" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:graded-lines">[eq:graded-lines]</a>, are $$\begin{aligned}
 &(18mj+n-1,\;18mj+24m(n-2j)+n-1),\\
 &(18mj+24m(n-2j)-3m+n-1,\;18mj-3m+n-1),\\
 &(9mn+n-1,\;9mn+n-1).
\end{aligned}$$ Every entry is nonnegative: the only possibly negative contribution is $-3m$, and it is offset by $n-1$.

For $c,t\geq0$, products of homogeneous monomials generate $\mathcal O_S(c,t)$. Moreover $H^1(\mathbb P^1,\mathcal O(c))=H^1(\mathbb P^1,\mathcal O(t))=0$, as computed on the two standard affine charts, so the Künneth formula gives $H^1(S,\mathcal O_S(c,t))=0$ (The Stacks Project Authors 2026, Tags 01XD and 0BED) <a id="pm-cite-28"></a>[[StacksProject](#ref-StacksProject)]. If $0\to U\to Z\to Q\to0$ is an extension of globally generated bundles with $H^1(S,U)=H^1(S,Q)=0$, the cohomology sequence gives $H^1(S,Z)=0$ and lifts every global section of $Q$ to $Z$. The lifted generators of $Q$, together with generators of $U$, span each fiber of $Z$. Starting with the innermost line factor and applying this observation successively proves that $W$ is globally generated.

**The projective embedding and its line bundle.** We record the tautological twists in the quotient-projectivization and Segre constructions (The Stacks Project Authors 2026, Tags 01OA, 01N1, and 01WD) <a id="pm-cite-29"></a>[[StacksProject](#ref-StacksProject)]. Let $\pi:\mathbb P(V)\to S$ and $H=H^0(S,W)$. Evaluation $H\otimes\mathcal O_S\to W$ induces a closed immersion $$\mathbb P(W)\hookrightarrow\mathbb P(H)\times S$$ whose projective hyperplane line pulls back to $\mathcal O_{\mathbb P(W)}(1)$. The relative Veronese closed immersion $\mathbb P(V)\to\mathbb P(\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}^n V)$ sends a quotient line $\ell$ to $\ell^{\otimes n}$. Under the natural identification $\mathbb P(\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}^n V)\simeq\mathbb P(W)$, its tautological pullback is $$\mathcal O_{\mathbb P(V)}(n)\otimes\pi^*P^{n-1}.$$ Consequently their composition embeds $\mathbb P(V)$ into $\mathbb P(H)\times S$ with this hyperplane pullback from the first factor. Embedding $S$ by $P=\mathcal O_S(1,1)$ and then applying the Segre embedding gives a closed immersion into projective space whose hyperplane line pulls back to $$\bigl(\mathcal O_{\mathbb P(V)}(n)\otimes\pi^*P^{n-1}\bigr)
       \otimes\pi^*P
 =\bigl(\mathcal O_{\mathbb P(V)}(1)\otimes\pi^*P\bigr)^n.$$ This line is therefore very ample. Finally, $\mathbb P(E_m)=\mathbb P(V\otimes P)\simeq\mathbb P(V)$ identifies $\mathcal O_{\mathbb P(E_m)}(1)$ with $\mathcal O_{\mathbb P(V)}(1)\otimes\pi^*P$, proving the proposition. ◻

</details>

<a id="sec:limit"></a>
### A necessary quadratic limit

The power maps allow the fixed twist by $P$ to disappear in a limit. We prove that the quadratic structure survives at every base point, even where a raw pointwise limit need not be plurisubharmonic. The argument has four steps: trace the dual norms, normalize and extract their coefficient limit, prove that the limit is nonzero, and recover its intrinsic plurisubharmonic representative. We establish this statement for an arbitrary vector bundle on $S$.

<a id="stmt-prop-limit"></a>
<div id="prop:limit" class="proposition">

**Proposition 6**. *Let $G$ be a holomorphic vector bundle of positive rank on $S=\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1$, and put $F=G^\vee$. Suppose that $f_m^*G\otimes P$ admits a smooth strictly Griffiths-positive Hermitian metric for infinitely many positive integers $m$. Then there is a function $$q:F\longrightarrow [0,\infty)$$ which is plurisubharmonic on the total space, is not identically zero, and has the following property: for every $x\in S$ there is a finite positive semidefinite Hermitian form $H_x$ on $F_x$ such that $$q(x,v)=H_x(v,v)\qquad(v\in F_x).$$ In particular, this fiberwise assertion holds at every base point.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-prop-limit"><summary>Proof of stmt-prop-limit</summary>

*Proof.* Put $r=\operatorname{rank}G$. Choose an unbounded sequence of the indicated integers and let $\|\cdot\|_m$ denote the smooth dual metric on $$(f_m^*G\otimes P)^\vee=f_m^*F\otimes P^{-1}.$$ Its squared norm is plurisubharmonic on the total space. We recall the sign in this standard implication. For a smooth Hermitian metric with matrix $h$, choose a holomorphic frame normal at the point in question, so that $h=\mathop{\mathrm{Id}}$ and its first derivatives vanish there. Along a holomorphic disk with coordinate $\zeta$, write the Hermitian curvature as $iC\,d\zeta\wedge d\bar\zeta$. In the convention used here, $$C=-\frac{\partial^2h}{\partial\zeta\,\partial\bar\zeta}$$ at the normal point. Thus, for a holomorphic section $s$ along the disk, <a id="eq:limit-dual-hessian"></a>$$\label{eq:limit-dual-hessian}
\frac{\partial^2}{\partial\zeta\,\partial\bar\zeta}\|s\|^2
=\|D'_{\partial/\partial\zeta}s\|^2-\langle Cs,s\rangle.$$ Duality reverses Griffiths positivity, so the second term on the right is nonnegative for the dual metric. Applying this calculation to the section of the pulled-back bundle defined by any disk in its total space proves the assertion. For this classical norm criterion and duality, see (Umemura 1973, Remark 1.3 and Lemma 1.7) <a id="pm-cite-30"></a>[[Umemura1973](#ref-Umemura1973)] and (Raufi 2015, sec. 2) <a id="pm-cite-31"></a>[[Raufi2015](#ref-Raufi2015)]; the same implication is recorded in (Forstnerič and Kusakabe 2025, Proposition 2.6) <a id="pm-cite-32"></a>[[ForstnericKusakabe](#ref-ForstnericKusakabe)].

<a id="eq:limit-trace"></a>
**Trace quadratics and normalization.** Fix, for each $m$, the holomorphic isomorphism $f_m^*P^{-1}\simeq P^{-m}$. If $t$ is a nonvanishing holomorphic frame of $P^{-1}$ on an open set $U\subset S$, define on $F|_U$ $$\label{eq:limit-trace}
q_{m,t}(x,v)
=\frac{1}{m^2}\sum_{y\in f_m^{-1}(x)}
\bigl\|v\otimes(f_m^*t)^{1/m}(y)\bigr\|_{m,y}^2,$$ where the sum counts multiplicities. The root in this expression is local on the source. Indeed, in a local frame $s$ of $P^{-1}$ the section $f_m^*t$ has the form $\eta(y)s^m$, with $\eta$ holomorphic and nowhere zero. A local holomorphic logarithm of $\eta$ gives the root $\exp((\log\eta)/m)s$. Two choices differ by an $m$-th root of unity, which does not change the squared norm. The expression <a href="#eq:limit-trace" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:limit-trace">[eq:limit-trace]</a> is consequently well defined and is a positive definite Hermitian quadratic in $v$.

We verify its regularity through the branch locus. Off that locus, every local inverse branch of $f_m$ gives a holomorphic map $$(x,v)\longmapsto
\bigl(y(x),v\otimes(f_m^*t)^{1/m}(y(x))\bigr)$$ into $f_m^*F\otimes P^{-1}$. Hence each summand, and their sum, is plurisubharmonic. The unordered multiset of roots of $y^m=x$ varies continuously with $x$, counting coalescing roots with their multiplicities. Applied in the two factors, and using local source frames for the norm in <a href="#eq:limit-trace" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:limit-trace">[eq:limit-trace]</a>, this proves continuity of the sum across the branch locus, including a crossing of its two components.

The continuous trace is plurisubharmonic across the branch locus as well. In a local total-space chart, the inverse image of the branch locus under the bundle projection $F|_U\to U$ is an analytic set, contained in $\{z_1z_2=0\}$ after choosing affine coordinates on the two base factors. It is therefore pluripolar. The trace is locally bounded above there by continuity, so the removable-singularities theorem for plurisubharmonic functions (Demailly 2012, chap. I, Theorem 5.24) <a id="pm-cite-33"></a>[[DemaillyCADG](#ref-DemaillyCADG)] gives a unique plurisubharmonic extension. Its upper-limit values agree with the continuous trace. This proves joint plurisubharmonicity of $q_{m,t}$ on all of $F|_U$.

<a id="eq:limit-frame-law"></a>
<a id="eq:limit-normalization"></a>
<a id="eq:limit-local-bound"></a>
If $t'=gt$ is another nonvanishing frame, then $$\label{eq:limit-frame-law}
q_{m,t'}(x,v)=|g(x)|^{2/m}q_{m,t}(x,v).$$ Choose smooth background Hermitian norms $|\cdot|_0$ on $F$ and $P^{-1}$. By <a href="#eq:limit-frame-law" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:limit-frame-law">[eq:limit-frame-law]</a>, the continuous quadratic $$N_m(x,v)=q_{m,t}(x,v)|t(x)|_0^{-2/m}$$ is globally defined. We use $N_m$ to normalize the traces globally; the local functions $q_{m,t}$ retain the plurisubharmonicity used below. The background unit sphere bundle is compact, and $N_m$ is positive there. Multiplying the dual metric’s squared norm by a positive constant, we may therefore arrange $$\label{eq:limit-normalization}
\max_{x\in S,\ |v|_0=1}N_m(x,v)=1.$$ This rescaling does not change the curvature. Homogeneity gives $$\label{eq:limit-local-bound}
0\le q_{m,t}(x,v)\le |t(x)|_0^{2/m}|v|_0^2.$$

**A measurable coefficient limit.** In a holomorphic trivialization of $F|_U$, write $$q_{m,t}(x,v)=v^*H_m(x)v.$$ On each relatively compact subset of $U$, the positive matrices $H_m$ are uniformly bounded, by <a href="#eq:limit-local-bound" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:limit-local-bound">[eq:limit-local-bound]</a>. Weak compactness in $L^2$ and a diagonal subsequence on a countable collection of charts and compact exhaustions give distributional limits $H$ for all the coefficient matrices. These limits belong to $L^\infty_{\mathrm{loc}}$, are Hermitian, and are positive semidefinite almost everywhere. For example, positivity and the same local upper bound pass to $c^*Hc$ against every nonnegative test function for each $c\in(\mathbb Q+i\mathbb Q)^r$; density then gives the matrix inequalities on a single set of full measure.

The coefficient limits have the untwisted transition law of a form on $F$. More explicitly, let $v=T(x)w$ be a holomorphic change of bundle coordinates and let $t'=gt$. The original matrices satisfy $$H'_m=|g|^{2/m}T^*H_mT.$$ On a compact subset $K$ of the overlap, the error obtained by deleting the scalar factor has, against a smooth compactly supported matrix test function $\Phi$, the bound $$\left|\left\langle
\bigl(|g|^{2/m}-1\bigr)T^*H_mT,\Phi
\right\rangle\right|
\le C_K\|\Phi\|_{L^1}
\sup_K\bigl||g|^{2/m}-1\bigr|\longrightarrow0.$$ Here the local matrix bound and the boundedness of $T$ are absorbed into $C_K$. Multiplication by the fixed smooth matrices $T,T^*$ preserves distributional convergence, as does a change of base coordinates. Consequently $H'=T^*HT$ almost everywhere.

<a id="eq:limit-noncollapse"></a>
**Nonvanishing of the measurable limit.** The limiting measurable form is not zero. Choose maximizing unit vectors $(x_m,v_m)$ in <a href="#eq:limit-normalization" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:limit-normalization">[eq:limit-normalization]</a>. After a further subsequence they converge in the compact unit sphere bundle to $(x_*,v_*)$. This further subsequence retains all the distributional limits already obtained. In one fixed coordinate and frame chart around $x_*$, let $c_m\to c$ be their fiber coordinates. Then $c\ne0$, and a local operator bound $\|H_m\|\le C$ gives $$\bigl|c^*H_m(x_m)c-c_m^*H_m(x_m)c_m\bigr|
\le C|c-c_m|(|c|+|c_m|)\longrightarrow0.$$ On the other hand, $$c_m^*H_m(x_m)c_m=|t(x_m)|_0^{2/m}\longrightarrow1,$$ since the norm of the nonvanishing frame is bounded above and away from zero on a fixed compact neighborhood. Thus the nonnegative plurisubharmonic function $u_m(x)=c^*H_m(x)c$ satisfies $u_m(x_m)\ge\tfrac12$ for large $m$. Choose $\varrho>0$ so that $B(x_*,4\varrho)$ lies in the coordinate chart, and choose a fixed smooth nonnegative function $\chi$ supported in $B(x_*,3\varrho)$ and equal to one on $B(x_*,2\varrho)$. For all sufficiently large $m$, the ball $B(x_m,\varrho)$ lies in that latter ball. The submean inequality yields $$\label{eq:limit-noncollapse}
\int\chi(x)c^*H_m(x)c\,dx
\ge\int_{B(x_m,\varrho)}u_m(x)\,dx
\ge\frac12\operatorname{Vol}(B(0,\varrho)).$$ The left side is a pairing against the fixed smooth matrix test function $\chi cc^*$, so the inequality passes to $H$. It proves that $H$ is nonzero as a measurable form.

**A quadratic psh representative on every fiber.** The measurable form is defined only up to a set of measure zero in the base. Even a pointwise limit of the traces may fail to be plurisubharmonic: in one base variable, tracing $(1+|y|^2)|v|^2$ through $x=y^m$ gives $$(1+|x|^{2/m})|v|^2.$$ Its pointwise limit has coefficient $1$ at $x=0$ and $2$ elsewhere, whereas its plurisubharmonic representative has coefficient $2$ everywhere. We now recover the intrinsic plurisubharmonic representative of our measurable form and show that it remains a Hermitian quadratic on every fiber.

<a id="eq:limit-convolution-bound"></a>
Convolution regularization of negative singular Hermitian metrics appears in (Berndtsson and Păun 2008, sec. 3, Proposition 3.1) <a id="pm-cite-34"></a>[[BerndtssonPaun2008](#ref-BerndtssonPaun2008)] and (Raufi 2015, Proposition 1.3(i)) <a id="pm-cite-35"></a>[[Raufi2015](#ref-Raufi2015)]; the use of polarization to recover an everywhere-defined representative is also discussed in (Păun and Takayama 2018, Remark 2.2.3) <a id="pm-cite-36"></a>[[PaunTakayama2018](#ref-PaunTakayama2018)]. We supply the argument for the present possibly rank-deficient limit, including the compatibility of the representatives on overlaps. Work in a coordinate and bundle chart $U$, and let $\rho$ be a nonnegative smooth radial function on $\mathbb C^2$, supported in the unit ball and of integral one. Put $$\rho_\delta(y)=\delta^{-4}\rho(y/\delta),\qquad
H_\delta=H*\rho_\delta,\qquad
q_\delta(x,v)=v^*H_\delta(x)v,$$ on the interior domain where this convolution is defined. Convolving $H_m$ in the same way gives $$q_{m,\delta}(x,v)
=\int q_{m,t}(x-y,v)\rho_\delta(y)\,dy.$$ It is plurisubharmonic jointly in $(x,v)$, since each base translation $(x,v)\mapsto(x-y,v)$ is holomorphic and the average has nonnegative weights. For fixed $\delta$, distributional convergence of $H_m$ implies convergence of the convolved coefficients and all their derivatives at every interior point: these are pairings against translates of derivatives of the fixed smooth kernel. The Levi matrices of $q_{m,\delta}$ therefore converge pointwise to that of $q_\delta$. Hence $q_\delta$ is smooth and plurisubharmonic, and on each smaller chart $$\label{eq:limit-convolution-bound}
0\le q_\delta(x,v)\le C|v|^2$$ for all sufficiently small $\delta$.

<a id="eq:limit-radial-means"></a>
These quadratics decrease as $\delta$ decreases. To check this for any fixed nonnegative radial kernel, write $\rho(y)=\phi(|y|)$ and let $M_u(x,s)$ denote the spherical mean of a subharmonic function $u$ on $\mathbb R^4$. Polar coordinates give $$\label{eq:limit-radial-means}
(u*\rho_\delta)(x)
=|\mathbb S^3|\int_0^1
M_u(x,\delta s)\phi(s)s^3\,ds.$$ The spherical mean is nondecreasing in its radius. The integration weight in <a href="#eq:limit-radial-means" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:limit-radial-means">[eq:limit-radial-means]</a> is nonnegative and does not depend on $\delta$, so the convolution is nondecreasing in $\delta$. Apply this to $u(x)=q_{m,t}(x,v)$ for each constant $v$, then pass to the limit in $m$ at two fixed radii. It follows that $$H_{\delta_1}(x)\le H_{\delta_2}(x)
\qquad(0<\delta_1<\delta_2)$$ in the order of Hermitian forms.

Thus $q_\delta$ decreases, as $\delta\downarrow0$, to a finite nonnegative plurisubharmonic function $q_U$ on the total-space chart. The decreasing-limit assertion follows also by passing to the submean inequalities; see (Demailly 2012, chap. I, Theorem 5.4) <a id="pm-cite-37"></a>[[DemaillyCADG](#ref-DemaillyCADG)]. Finiteness is supplied by <a href="#eq:limit-convolution-bound" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:limit-convolution-bound">[eq:limit-convolution-bound]</a>. At every fixed base point $x$, this limit is a Hermitian quadratic. Indeed, all entries of $H_\delta(x)$ are determined by the finite set of values at $e_j$, $e_j+e_k$, and $e_j+i e_k$, through $$\begin{aligned}
2\operatorname{Re}(H_\delta)_{jk}
&=q_\delta(x,e_j+e_k)-q_\delta(x,e_j)-q_\delta(x,e_k),\\
2\operatorname{Im}(H_\delta)_{jk}
&=q_\delta(x,e_j)+q_\delta(x,e_k)-q_\delta(x,e_j+i e_k).
\end{aligned}$$ Each value on the right has a finite limit. Hence the entries converge to one Hermitian matrix $\widehat H_U(x)$, and passage to the finite quadratic polynomial gives $$q_U(x,v)=v^*\widehat H_U(x)v\qquad\text{for every }v\in\mathbb C^r.$$ This matrix is positive semidefinite at every $x$, by nonnegativity. At almost every $x$, approximate-identity convergence also gives $\widehat H_U(x)=H(x)$.

Finally these local representatives agree on overlaps. After the holomorphic change of bundle coordinates, their measurable matrices agree outside a base-null set, by the transition law already proved. The quadratic functions therefore agree for every fiber vector outside that set, hence almost everywhere in the total-space chart by Fubini’s theorem. Both functions are locally bounded and plurisubharmonic. Such a function is determined everywhere by its almost-everywhere values: the submean inequality and upper semicontinuity give, in local Euclidean coordinates, $$u(p)=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}
\frac{1}{\operatorname{Vol}(B(p,\varepsilon))}
\int_{B(p,\varepsilon)}u.$$ Thus the local $q_U$ glue to a single function $q$ on $F$. Its coefficients represent the original measurable form almost everywhere, so <a href="#eq:limit-noncollapse" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:limit-noncollapse">[eq:limit-noncollapse]</a> implies $q\not\equiv0$. All the required pointwise and plurisubharmonic properties now hold. ◻

</details>

<a id="stmt-rmk-canonical-limit"></a>
<div id="rmk:canonical-limit" class="remark">

*Remark 7*. The function in Proposition <a href="#prop:limit" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:limit">6</a> represents a distributional limit; no convergence of restrictions to a divisor is asserted. The graph identities will instead be evaluated directly in this single everywhere-defined function $q$.

</div>

<a id="sec:obstruction"></a>
### The invariant measure obstruction

We now exclude the quadratic function produced by Proposition <a href="#prop:limit" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:limit">6</a> for the graph-defined bundle $G$. The two rulings and the three graph identities force a nonzero squared seminorm on $\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(-b)$ that is invariant under the lifts of $A$ and $B$. We then turn that seminorm into a positive measure of mass $2\pi b$ and show that the two transformations cannot preserve such a measure.

<a id="pm-h-14"></a>
#### From the two rulings to one invariant seminorm

<a id="stmt-lem-ruling-reduction"></a>
<div id="lem:ruling-reduction" class="lemma">

**Lemma 8**. *Let $G$ be the bundle of Proposition <a href="#prop:construction" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:construction">4</a>, and put $F=G^\vee$. Suppose that $q:F\to[0,\infty)$ is finite, plurisubharmonic, not identically zero, and a Hermitian quadratic form on every fiber. Then there is a finite, nonnegative, nonzero plurisubharmonic function $a_\bullet$ on $\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(-b)$, a Hermitian positive semidefinite quadratic on each fiber, such that $$a_\bullet(Mx,M^{\otimes b}v)=a_\bullet(x,v)
 \qquad(M=A,B)$$ for every $x\in\mathbb P^1$ and $v\in\mathcal O(-b)_x$.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-ruling-reduction"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-ruling-reduction</summary>

*Proof.* Write $q(x,v)=H_x(v,v)$; nonnegativity says that every $H_x$ is positive semidefinite. Pull $q$ back by the line inclusions $$\iota_i:L_i^{-1}=p_i^*\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(-b)\longrightarrow F,
 \qquad i=1,2.$$ For fixed $x_i\in\mathbb P^1$ and $v\in\mathcal O(-b)_{x_i}$, the function obtained by holding $v$ fixed while varying the other base coordinate is finite and subharmonic on the complementary $\mathbb P^1$. It is constant by the maximum principle. Consequently $q\circ\iota_i$ descends to a finite nonnegative squared seminorm $a_i$ on $\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(-b)$ over the $i$-th factor. Fixing any point in the other factor realizes $a_i$ as a holomorphic pullback of $q$, so $a_i$ is plurisubharmonic on the total line bundle.

<a id="eq:seminorm-graph-identities"></a>
On $\Gamma_M$, the identity $\iota_2\alpha=\iota_1$ from Proposition <a href="#prop:construction" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:construction">4</a> and the definition of $\alpha$ give $$\label{eq:seminorm-graph-identities}
 a_1(x,v)=a_2(Mx,M^{\otimes b}v),
 \qquad M\in\{\mathop{\mathrm{Id}},A,B\}.$$ The case $M=\mathop{\mathrm{Id}}$ identifies $a_1$ and $a_2$ as a single function $a_\bullet$ on $\mathcal O(-b)$. This function is not identically zero. Indeed, if it were zero, then for every $x\notin D$, the form $H_x$ would vanish on each of two spanning lines. The inequality $$|H_x(v,w)|^2\leq H_x(v,v)H_x(w,w)$$ would then force $H_x=0$. Hence $q$ would vanish on the full-measure complement of the inverse image of $D$ in $F$. Nonnegativity and the submean inequality on small total-space coordinate balls would give $q=0$ everywhere, a contradiction.

The cases $M=A,B$ of <a href="#eq:seminorm-graph-identities" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:seminorm-graph-identities">[eq:seminorm-graph-identities]</a> give the asserted invariances under the holomorphic lifts $A^{\otimes b}$ and $B^{\otimes b}$. ◻

</details>

<a id="pm-h-15"></a>
#### Logarithmic weights and their measure

The seminorm in Lemma <a href="#lem:ruling-reduction" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:ruling-reduction">8</a> may vanish in some fibers. The next lemma shows that its logarithmic weights still define a positive measure with the mass dictated by the negative line bundle. This is the rank-one squared-norm/logarithmic-norm principle from singular Hermitian metric theory (Berndtsson and Păun 2008, sec. 3) <a id="pm-cite-38"></a>[[BerndtssonPaun2008](#ref-BerndtssonPaun2008)] and (Raufi 2015, Definition 1.2 and Section 2) <a id="pm-cite-39"></a>[[Raufi2015](#ref-Raufi2015)]. We prove local integrability at zeros before taking distributional curvature, so all possible atoms remain in the mass calculation.

<a id="stmt-lem-seminorm-curvature"></a>
<div id="lem:seminorm-curvature" class="lemma">

**Lemma 9** (Logarithmic curvature of a squared seminorm). *Let $L$ be a holomorphic line bundle on a connected Riemann surface $C$. Suppose that $a_\bullet:L\to[0,\infty)$ is finite, plurisubharmonic, and not identically zero, and that its restriction to every fiber is a Hermitian positive semidefinite quadratic form. In a nonvanishing holomorphic frame $e$ on $U\subset C$, write $$a_\bullet(z,w e(z))=u_e(z)|w|^2.$$ Then $\log u_e$, with the value $-\infty$ allowed, is subharmonic and locally integrable. The local measures $i\partial\bar\partial\log u_e$ define a positive Radon measure $\mu$ on $C$.*

<a id="eq:seminorm-curvature-mass"></a>
*If a holomorphic bundle automorphism $\widetilde T:L\to L$ covers an automorphism $T:C\to C$ and satisfies $a_\bullet\circ\widetilde T=a_\bullet$, then $T^*\mu=\mu$. If $C=\mathbb P^1$ and $L=\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(-b)$ with $b\geq1$, then $$\label{eq:seminorm-curvature-mass}
 \mu(\mathbb P^1)=2\pi b.$$*

</div>

Where $u_e$ is smooth and strictly positive, $i\partial\bar\partial\log u_e$ is the negative of the Chern curvature of the corresponding metric in our convention. This accounts for the positive mass when $L=\mathcal O(-b)$.

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-seminorm-curvature"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-seminorm-curvature</summary>

*Proof.* Fix a coordinate disk $U$ and a frame $e$, and abbreviate $u_e$ to $u$. The function $u$ is finite, nonnegative, subharmonic, and locally bounded, by restriction to the holomorphic section $e$. Choose a nonnegative smooth radial function $\rho$ supported in the unit disk, with integral one, and put $$\rho_\delta(z)=\delta^{-2}\rho(z/\delta),
 \qquad u_\delta=u*\rho_\delta+\delta.$$ These functions are defined on the interior of $U$ for sufficiently small $\delta>0$. Averaging the holomorphic base translations $(z,w)\mapsto(z-\zeta,w)$ shows that $(u*\rho_\delta)(z)|w|^2$ is plurisubharmonic. Adding $\delta|w|^2$ preserves this property, so $u_\delta>0$ and $u_\delta(z)|w|^2$ is smooth and plurisubharmonic. Its Levi matrix at $w=1$ is $$\begin{pmatrix}
  (u_\delta)_{z\bar z} & (u_\delta)_z\\
  (u_\delta)_{\bar z} & u_\delta
 \end{pmatrix}.$$ Its nonnegative determinant gives $$(\log u_\delta)_{z\bar z}
 =\frac{u_\delta(u_\delta)_{z\bar z}
             -|(u_\delta)_z|^2}{u_\delta^2}\geq0.$$ Thus $f_\delta:=\log u_\delta$ is subharmonic.

<a id="eq:log-negative-part"></a>
The radial convolutions are nonnegative weighted averages of circular means of $u$. These means increase with their radii, and converge to $u$ as the radii tend to zero. Consequently $$u_\delta\downarrow u,
 \qquad f_\delta\downarrow\log u
 \quad\text{as }\delta\downarrow0.$$ We establish local integrability before taking distributional derivatives of the limit. Suppose that $u(z_0)>0$, and choose $R>0$ with $\overline{B(z_0,R)}\subset U$. For all sufficiently small $\delta$, local boundedness supplies a constant $C_0\geq0$ such that $f_\delta\leq C_0$ on this disk. Moreover $f_\delta(z_0)\geq\log u(z_0)$. Integrating the circular submean inequality over the radius gives $$\int_{B(z_0,R)} f_\delta\,dA
 \geq \pi R^2\log u(z_0).$$ Writing $f^-:=\max\{-f,0\}$, we obtain the explicit bound $$\label{eq:log-negative-part}
 \int_{B(z_0,R)} f_\delta^-\,dA
 \leq \pi R^2\bigl(C_0-\log u(z_0)\bigr).$$ The negative parts increase as $\delta$ decreases, whereas the positive parts are bounded by $C_0$. Monotone convergence therefore proves $\log u\in L^1(B(z_0,R))$. It also gives $f_\delta\to\log u$ in $L^1$ on this disk.

<a id="eq:log-frame-change"></a>
This conclusion propagates throughout $C$. Indeed, let $\Omega$ consist of the points near which a local logarithmic weight is integrable. A frame change $e'=g e$ gives $$\label{eq:log-frame-change}
 \log u_{e'}=\log u_e+\log|g|^2,$$ so $\Omega$ is independent of the chosen frame. It is open and nonempty: nontriviality of $a_\bullet$ supplies a point with $u>0$, to which <a href="#eq:log-negative-part" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:log-negative-part">[eq:log-negative-part]</a> applies. If $x$ is in the boundary of $\Omega$, choose a coordinate disk containing $\overline{B(x,4r)}$. There is a small open disk contained in $\Omega\cap B(x,r)$. On that disk integrability of $\log u$ implies $u>0$ almost everywhere, so it contains a point $z_0$ with $u(z_0)>0$. The disk $B(z_0,2r)$ has closure in the original chart and contains a neighborhood of $x$. Applying <a href="#eq:log-negative-part" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:log-negative-part">[eq:log-negative-part]</a> on this larger disk proves $x\in\Omega$. Thus $\Omega$ is also closed, and connectedness gives $\Omega=C$.

On every relatively compact disk, $C_0-f_\delta$ increases to the integrable function $C_0-\log u$. Hence the local convergence is in $L^1$, and the decreasing limit $\log u$ is subharmonic. In particular, $i\partial\bar\partial\log u$ is a positive distribution and thus a positive Radon measure. Since $g$ in <a href="#eq:log-frame-change" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:log-frame-change">[eq:log-frame-change]</a> is holomorphic and nowhere zero, $\log|g|^2$ is harmonic. The local measures therefore glue to $\mu$.

For the invariance assertion, choose frames $e$ on $U$ and $e'$ on $V$ with $T(U)\subset V$, and write $$\widetilde T(e(z))=c(z)e'(Tz),$$ where $c$ is holomorphic and nowhere zero. The isometry condition gives $$u_e(z)=|c(z)|^2u_{e'}(Tz),
 \qquad
 \log u_e=\log|c|^2+(\log u_{e'})\circ T.$$ Taking distributional $i\partial\bar\partial$, which commutes with pullback by the holomorphic diffeomorphism $T$, gives $\mu=T^*\mu$. Here pullback is that of a top-degree current: for a test function $\varphi$, <a id="eq:measure-pullback"></a>$$\label{eq:measure-pullback}
 \int_C\varphi\,d(T^*\mu)
   =\int_C(\varphi\circ T^{-1})\,d\mu.$$ Equivalently, $(T^*\mu)(E)=\mu(T(E))$ for Borel sets $E$. This is ordinary measure invariance under the bijection $T$; in particular, $T^*\delta_p=\delta_{T^{-1}p}$ with unchanged atom mass.

Finally suppose $C=\mathbb P^1$ and $L=\mathcal O(-b)$. Let $h_0$ be the smooth Hermitian metric on $\mathcal O(-b)$ induced by the standard norm on $\mathbb C^2$. In the tautological affine frame $e(z)=(1,z)^{\otimes b}$ its weight is $$u_0(z)=h_0(e(z),e(z))=(1+|z|^2)^b.$$ The local functions $\log u_e-\log h_0(e,e)$ define a single globally integrable function $\Phi$ on $\mathbb P^1$, by the common frame law. If $\mu_0=i\partial\bar\partial\log h_0(e,e)$, then $$\mu-\mu_0=i\partial\bar\partial\Phi,
 \qquad
 \int_{\mathbb P^1}(\mu-\mu_0)
   =\langle i\partial\bar\partial\Phi,1\rangle=0.$$ The measure $\mu$ is finite because it is locally finite on the compact sphere. With the convention $i\partial\bar\partial f=i f_{z\bar z}\,dz\wedge d\bar z$, the smooth reference measure has mass $$\int_{\mathbb P^1}\mu_0
 =\int_{\mathbb C}\frac{b\,i\,dz\wedge d\bar z}{(1+|z|^2)^2}
 =4\pi b\int_0^\infty\frac{r\,dr}{(1+r^2)^2}
 =2\pi b.$$ This proves <a href="#eq:seminorm-curvature-mass" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:seminorm-curvature-mass">[eq:seminorm-curvature-mass]</a>. Zeros of $u_e$ and any atoms of $\mu$ are included in the distributional calculation; no points have been excised. ◻

</details>

<a id="pm-h-16"></a>
#### The incompatible invariances

We now use the special dynamics of the two matrices. Concentration of invariant measures on proper projective subsets is a classical mechanism; see (Furstenberg 1976, Lemma 2 and the proof of Lemma 3) <a id="pm-cite-40"></a>[[Furstenberg1976](#ref-Furstenberg1976)]. The following elementary argument gives precisely the rank-two statement needed here.

<a id="stmt-lem-no-invariant-measure"></a>
<div id="lem:no-invariant-measure" class="lemma">

**Lemma 10**. *The projective transformations induced by the matrices $A$ and $B$ in <a href="#eq:matrices" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:matrices">[eq:matrices]</a> preserve no nonzero finite positive Borel measure on $\mathbb P^1$.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-no-invariant-measure"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-no-invariant-measure</summary>

*Proof.* Suppose that $\mu$ is such a measure. It is invariant under $T=AB$. The two eigenvalues of $AB$ have distinct moduli, so after a projective change of coordinate $T$ acts as $z\mapsto\lambda z$ with $R:=|\lambda|>1$. The Borel sets $$C_n=\{z\in\mathbb C^*:R^n\leq|z|<R^{n+1}\},\qquad n\in\mathbb Z,$$ are pairwise disjoint, cover $\mathbb C^*$, and satisfy $T(C_n)=C_{n+1}$. Each has the same measure. Finiteness and positivity imply that this common measure is zero, and countable additivity gives $\mu(\mathbb C^*)=0$. The half-open annuli assign every boundary circle to exactly one set, so this argument applies equally to atomic and diffuse measures and to nonreal $\lambda$.

Thus the nonempty support of $\mu$ consists of one or two of the fixed points of $T$. Invariance under $A$ and $B$ preserves this support as a set: the inverse image of an open neighborhood of the image of a support point has positive measure. Both projective transformations have order three. Their induced permutations on a set of at most two points are therefore trivial. Each point of the support would be fixed by both $A$ and $B$, and hence would be a common eigenline of the two matrices. Lemma <a href="#lem:matrix-data" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:matrix-data">2</a> excludes such a line. ◻

</details>

The preceding results rule out exactly the object supplied by Proposition <a href="#prop:limit" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:limit">6</a>.

<a id="stmt-prop-obstruction"></a>
<div id="prop:obstruction" class="proposition">

**Proposition 11**. *For the bundle $G$ of Proposition <a href="#prop:construction" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:construction">4</a>, every finite nonnegative plurisubharmonic function on the total space of $F=G^\vee$ whose restriction to each fiber is a Hermitian quadratic form is identically zero.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-prop-obstruction"><summary>Proof of stmt-prop-obstruction</summary>

*Proof.* If such a function were nonzero, Lemma <a href="#lem:ruling-reduction" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:ruling-reduction">8</a> would give a nonzero squared seminorm on $\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(-b)$ invariant under the lifts of $A$ and $B$. Lemma <a href="#lem:seminorm-curvature" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:seminorm-curvature">9</a> would then produce a finite positive measure on $\mathbb P^1$ of mass $2\pi b>0$, invariant under both projective transformations. This contradicts Lemma <a href="#lem:no-invariant-measure" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:no-invariant-measure">10</a>. ◻

</details>

<a id="sec:conclusion"></a>
### Proof of the main theorem

<details id="proof-stmt-prop-obstruction-2"><summary>Proof of stmt-prop-obstruction</summary>

*Proof of Theorem <a href="#thm:main" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:main">1</a>.* Take the algebraic rank-two bundle $G$ of Proposition <a href="#prop:construction" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:construction">4</a>. By Proposition <a href="#prop:ampleness" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:ampleness">5</a>, every $E_m=f_m^*G\otimes P$ is ample in the quotient convention.

If the set of positive integers $m$ for which $E_m$ has a smooth strictly Griffiths-positive metric were infinite, it would contain a sequence tending to infinity. Proposition <a href="#prop:limit" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:limit">6</a> would then give a finite, nonnegative, nonzero psh function on the total space of $G^\vee$ that is a Hermitian positive semidefinite quadratic form on every fiber. Proposition <a href="#prop:obstruction" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:obstruction">11</a> excludes precisely such a function. Consequently that set of integers is finite. Choose $m_0$ larger than all its elements, taking $m_0=1$ if the set is empty. Every $m\ge m_0$ gives the required counterexample. ◻

</details>

The limit in the proof is permitted to lose rank, but not to vanish identically or to lose its quadratic structure at any base point. These are exactly the properties needed to transfer the graph relations to a nonzero invariant measure. The argument requires no uniform estimates for the input metrics or their curvature, and it does not determine an effective value of $m_0$.
