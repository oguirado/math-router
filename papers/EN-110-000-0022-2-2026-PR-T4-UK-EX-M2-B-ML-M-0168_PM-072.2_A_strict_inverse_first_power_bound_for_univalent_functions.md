## 1. Abstract and result contract

> We prove a uniform upper bound for inverse-first-power integral means of normalized univalent disk maps with exponent strictly below 1/4. Consequently, the bounded universal integral-means spectrum satisfies $`B_b(-1)\lt 1/4`$, disproving Kraetzer's conjectured spectrum at <i>p</i> = −1.

- **C1** (theorem, [source](#stmt-thm-main)): There exist constants $0<\varepsilon<1/4$ and $C<\infty$ such that $$\label{eq:uniform} M_{-1}[f'](r)\le C(1-r)^{-1/4+\varepsilon} \qquad\text{for every }f\in\mathcal S\text{ and every }1/2\le r<1.$$ Consequently $B_b(-1)<1/4$, and Kraetzer’s conjectured identity <a href="#eq:kraetzer" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:kraetzer">[eq:kraetzer]</a> is false.
  - Explanation: Inverse-first-power integral means admit a uniform exponent strictly below the conjectured threshold.
- **C2** (theorem, [source](#stmt-lem-classical)): For every $f\in\mathcal S$ and $\|z\|=r<1$, $$\label{eq:koebe} \frac{1-r}{(1+r)^3}\le \|f'(z)\|\le \frac{1+r}{(1-r)^3},\qquad B(0,1/4)\subset f(\mathbb D).$$ If $F$ is univalent on the Euclidean disk $B(z_0,s)$, then $$\label{eq:local-quarter} B\bigl(F(z_0),s\|F'(z_0)\|/4\bigr) \subset F(B(z_0,s)).$$
  - Explanation: Classical distortion and image containment control normalized univalent maps and their local rescalings.
- **C3** (theorem, [source](#stmt-lem-compact)): The locally uniform topology makes $\mathcal K$ compact metrizable. Evaluation of $F,Q_F$ and all their derivatives is jointly continuous in $F,z$, and uniformly bounded on each compact source set. Moreover, uniformly in $F\in\mathcal K$, $$\label{eq:strip} \|Q_F(x+iy)\|\le C/y\quad(\|x\|\le2,\ 0<y\le1), \qquad \|F(z)\|\ge c>0\quad(\|m(z)\|\ge1/2).$$
  - Explanation: The normalized half-plane family is compact and admits uniform local evaluation bounds.
- **C4** (theorem, [source](#stmt-prop-disk)): There is a universal constant $C_0$ such that, for $f\in\mathcal S$, $$M_{-1}[f'](r)\le C_0\\|L^n\\| \quad\text{if}\quad 1/2\le r<1,\quad v=2^{-n}\le1-r<2v.$$ Consequently $B_b(-1)\le\beta$.
  - Explanation: Dyadic operator growth controls inverse-first-power circular means uniformly over disk maps.
- **C5** (conditional_result, [source](#stmt-prop-law)): Assume $\beta=\log_2\rho\ge1/4$, where $\rho$ is defined in <a href="#eq:rho" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:rho">[eq:rho]</a>. There is a Borel probability $P$ on $\mathcal K$ such that, writing $\mathbb E$ for expectation under $P$, $$\label{eq:law} \mathbb E\bigl[\|Q_F(x+iy)\|\,\Phi(T_{x+iy}F)\bigr] =y^{-\beta}\mathbb E\Phi(F)$$ for every $x+iy\in\mathbb H$ and every nonnegative Borel test $\Phi:\mathcal K\to[0,\infty]$.
  - Explanation: Under the contrary growth hypothesis a probability law has exact weighted affine scaling.
- **C6** (theorem, [source](#stmt-lem-moments)): The law satisfies $$\label{eq:moments} \mathbb Ea_F(i)=-\beta,\qquad \mathbb E\|q_F(i)\|^2=\beta(\beta+1).$$
  - Explanation: Differentiating the weighted affine law fixes logarithmic-derivative moments.
- **C7** (theorem, [source](#stmt-prop-compact-levels)): For $P$-almost every $F$, and then for area-almost every $\xi\in\mathbb C$, every closed positive superlevel $\{V_\xi^F\ge\delta\}$, $\delta>0$, is compact in $\mathbb H$.
  - Explanation: Positive potential superlevels are compact for almost every map and then almost every target.
- **C8** (theorem, [source](#stmt-lem-area-tail)): Writing $S_0=\{z\in\mathbb H:\|m(z)\|\ge1/2\}$, almost every $F$ satisfies $$\label{eq:area-tail} I(F):=\int_{S_0}y^\beta\|F(z)\|^{-3}\,dA(z)<\infty.$$
  - Explanation: The weighted reciprocal-image area tail is finite almost surely.
- **C9** (conditional_result, [source](#stmt-lem-infinity)): If $I(F)<\infty$, then $$\label{eq:infinity} \frac{y^k}{\|F(x+iy)\|}\longrightarrow0 \qquad\text{as } \|x\|+y\longrightarrow\infty.$$ For every compact target set $K$, its preimage restricted to $y\ge\epsilon>0$ is compact, its whole preimage has bounded height, and $$\label{eq:horizontal} \sup_{s>0}\int_\mathbb R\mathbf 1_K(F(x+is))\,dx<\infty.$$
  - Explanation: Finite weighted area tail prevents source escape and bounds horizontal preimage length.
- **C10** (theorem, [source](#stmt-lem-good)): Goodness is Borel in $(F,\xi)$. For $P$-almost every $F$, area-almost every $\xi$ is good.
  - Explanation: Goodness is measurable and excludes singular targets and tied critical values almost everywhere.
- **C11** (conditional_result, [source](#stmt-lem-count)): For a good pair and $h>0$, $$\label{eq:count} \#\{z:\ z\text{ is a finite maximum},\ V_\xi^F(z)\ge h\} \ \ge\ \#\{z:\ z\text{ is a saddle},\ V_\xi^F(z)\ge h\}.$$ Both counts are finite.
  - Explanation: On good fibers every positive superlevel contains at least as many finite maxima as saddles.
- **C12** (conditional_result, [source](#stmt-prop-pair)): On the Borel domain $$\mathcal D=\{(F,z):J_F(z)>0,\ (F,G_F(z))\in\mathcal G\}$$ there is a Borel assignment $R_F(z)$ to a finite maximum over the same target, injective on each target fiber, such that $$\label{eq:strict-value} V_{G_F(z)}^F(R_F(z))>V_{G_F(z)}^F(z).$$ It is equivariant: $$R_F(t\cdot w)=t\cdot R_{T_tF}(w)$$ on the corresponding domains.
  - Explanation: Measurable rank pairing sends saddles injectively to higher finite maxima and commutes with rebasing.
- **C13** (theorem, [source](#stmt-lem-base-good)): The positive-Jacobian mass at the base point is supported on good fibers: $$\label{eq:base-good} \mathbb E\bigl[J_{F,+}(i) \mathbf 1_{\{(F,G_F(i))\text{ not good}\}}\bigr]=0.$$
  - Explanation: Positive Jacobian weight transfers target-generic goodness to the normalized base point.
- **C14** (conditional_result, [source](#stmt-prop-strict-transport)): Assume $\beta\ge1/4$, let $P$ be the law of Proposition <a href="#prop:law" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:law">5</a>, and set $k=(\beta+2)/3$. Then $\mathbb EJ_F(i)\le0$. Equality is possible only if $J_F(i)=0$ almost surely.
  - Explanation: Strict transport makes the expected signed Jacobian nonpositive and excludes nonzero Jacobian in equality.
- **C15** (conditional_result, [source](#stmt-lem-no-zero-j)): For $k=3/4$, no $F\in\mathcal K$ has $J_F\equiv0$.
  - Explanation: A harmonic lower barrier excludes an identically vanishing Jacobian at the equality exponent.

## 2. Definitions, validity and bounds

- **Normalized disk classes** ([source](#sec:introduction)): Let $\mathbb D=\{z\in\mathbb C:\|z\|<1\}$, and let $\mathcal S$ consist of all injective holomorphic maps $f:\mathbb D\to\mathbb C$ with $f(0)=0$ and $f'(0)=1$.
- **Bounded-image subclass** ([source](#sec:introduction)): Write $\mathcal S_b$ for the subclass of maps with bounded image.
- **Integral means and spectrum** ([source](#sec:introduction)): For $p\in\mathbb R$ and $0\le r<1$, define $$\begin{aligned} M_p[f'](r)&=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\|f'(re^{i\theta})\|^p\,d\theta,\\ \beta_f(p)&=\limsup_{r\uparrow1} \frac{\log M_p[f'](r)}{\log(1/(1-r))}, &B_b(p)&=\sup_{f\in\mathcal S_b}\beta_f(p). \end{aligned}$$
- **Affine rebasings** ([source](#pm-h-8)): For $F\in\mathcal K$, define $$(T_zF)(w)=\frac{F(z\cdot w)-F(z)}{yF'(z)}.$$
- **Positive sampling operator** ([source](#pm-h-8)): On $C(\mathcal K)$, with its supremum norm, put $$(A_z\Phi)(F)=\|Q_F(z)\|\,\Phi(T_zF),\qquad L=\tfrac12(A_{-1/2+i/2}+A_{1/2+i/2}).$$
- **Operator growth exponent** ([source](#eq:rho)): Submultiplicativity defines $$\label{eq:rho} \rho=\lim_{n\to\infty}\\|L^n\\|^{1/n}, \qquad \beta=\log_2\rho,\qquad 0\le\beta\le1.$$
- **Potential** ([source](#sec:escape)): For $F\in\mathcal K$ and $\xi\in\mathbb C$, put $$V_\xi^F(x+iy)=\frac{y^k}{\|F(x+iy)-\xi\|},$$ with value $+\infty$ at the possible pole.
- **Good pairs** ([source](#pm-h-13)): Let $\mathcal G\subset\mathcal K\times\mathbb C$ be the set of pairs $(F,\xi)$ for which all positive closed superlevels of $V_\xi^F$ are compact, $\xi$ is a regular value of $G_F$, and distinct preimages under $G_F$ have distinct $V_\xi^F$-values. We call these pairs *good*.

## 4. Engineering Triptych

### Panel 1 - epistemic contract

- **uniform_inverse_mean**: The same constants bound the integral mean of the derivative over all normalized univalent disk maps in the stated radius regime.
- **normalization_invariance**: Multiplication by a nonzero constant preserves the limiting integral-means exponent and hence the bounded-class spectrum.
- **strict_fiber_pairing**: Every saddle in the good-fiber domain is assigned a strictly higher finite maximum over the same target; partners are distinct within each target fiber.
- **weighted_base_goodness**: Exceptional targets contribute no positive-Jacobian mass at the normalized base point.

### Panel 2 - negative knowledge

- **explicit_tolerance**: Use the uniform estimate as an explicit numerical error tolerance. -> The positive gap and multiplicative constant are not evaluated.
- **positive_exponent**: Apply the strict spectrum bound to $p=1$. -> The theorem addresses the inverse first power and leaves the positive exponent unsettled.
- **spectrum_class**: Substitute the unrestricted disk spectrum into the proposed bounded-class spectrum at positive exponents. -> The unrestricted spectrum includes the Koebe contribution, changing the comparison.
- **fixed_target_genericity**: Treat area-generic goodness as almost-sure goodness at the normalized base target. -> The normalized target is fixed, so an area-null exceptional set can contain it.
- **common_strict_gain**: Assign a common positive gain to every saddle-to-maximum pair. -> Strict increases may become arbitrarily small across pairs.

### Panel 3 - reference implementation and oracle

Applicability: `not_applicable`. The proof gives existence of a positive gap but does not evaluate it numerically. Its operator acts on a compact family of holomorphic maps, and the measurable rank pairing requires good target fibers rather than a supplied finite representation with a certified list of critical points. The paper supplies no finite input procedure that computes the uniform constants or certifies these analytic hypotheses.

> ## Scope
>
> Brennan's conjecture asserts that $|\phi'|^s$ is area-integrable for $4/3<s<4$ when $\phi$ conformally maps a simply connected plane domain with nontrivial spherical boundary onto the disk. The formalization establishes this interval, along with area integrability of $|f'|^t$ for univalent disk maps when $-2<t<2/3$.
>
> It also gives the uniform inverse-square radial-mean bound with exponent $-1-\varepsilon$ for every $\varepsilon>0$ and the spectrum value $B_{\mathcal S}(-2)=1$. The Koebe map and its inverse give divergence at all four boundary exponents.
>
> Kraetzer's proposed integral-means spectrum predicts $B_b(-1)=1/4$ for bounded univalent maps. The formalized result proves $B_b(-1)<1/4$, contradicting that prediction.
>
> The underlying estimate gives constants $0<\varepsilon<1/4$ and $C<\infty$ such that the normalized circular mean of $|f'|^{-1}$ is at most $C(1-r)^{-1/4+\varepsilon}$ for every normalized univalent disk map and $1/2\le r<1$. The bound requires neither bounded image nor boundary regularity, and no numerical value of $\varepsilon$ is specified.

def NormalizedUnivalent (f : ℂ → ℂ) : Prop :=
  DifferentiableOn ℂ f disk ∧ Set.InjOn f disk ∧ f 0 = 0 ∧ deriv f 0 = 1

def BoundedNormalizedUnivalent (f : ℂ → ℂ) : Prop :=
  NormalizedUnivalent f ∧ ∃ R : ℝ, ∀ z ∈ disk, ‖f z‖ ≤ R

noncomputable def integralMean (p : ℝ) (f : ℂ → ℂ) (r : ℝ) : ℝ :=
  (2 * Real.pi)⁻¹ * ∫ θ in -Real.pi..Real.pi,

  Filter.limsup (fun r : ℝ =>

    (𝓝[<] (1 : ℝ))

<a id="sec:introduction"></a>
### Introduction

For a univalent function, the derivative can become large or small near the boundary even when the image domain is bounded. Integral means measure the rate of this concentration. Their universal spectrum records the largest possible growth exponent over a class of conformal maps; it brings questions about derivatives, coefficients, and the geometry of image boundaries into a common framework.

Let $\mathbb D=\{z\in\mathbb C:|z|<1\}$, and let $\mathcal S$ consist of all injective holomorphic maps $f:\mathbb D\to\mathbb C$ with $f(0)=0$ and $f'(0)=1$. Write $\mathcal S_b$ for the subclass of maps with bounded image. The bound on the image may depend on $f$, and no boundary regularity is imposed. For $p\in\mathbb R$ and $0\le r<1$, define $$\begin{aligned}
 M_p[f'](r)&=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|f'(re^{i\theta})|^p\,d\theta,\\
 \beta_f(p)&=\limsup_{r\uparrow1}
 \frac{\log M_p[f'](r)}{\log(1/(1-r))},
 &B_b(p)&=\sup_{f\in\mathcal S_b}\beta_f(p).
\end{aligned}$$ The derivative of a univalent map never vanishes, so all real powers are defined. The supremum in $B_b$ is taken after each map’s limiting exponent. Multiplying a map by a nonzero constant leaves that exponent unchanged, so normalization of the derivative does not change the bounded-class spectrum.

<a id="pm-h-3"></a>
#### The conjecture and the uniform bound

<a id="eq:kraetzer"></a>
Kraetzer’s experiments with conformal maps onto bounded domains led to the prediction $$\label{eq:kraetzer}
 B_b(p)=
 \begin{cases}
 p^2/4,&|p|\le2,\\
 |p|-1,&|p|\ge2.
 \end{cases}$$ The quadratic part is stated in his 1996 paper and explicitly restated in his thesis (Kraetzer 1996, 2000) <a id="pm-cite-1"></a>[[Kraetzer](#ref-Kraetzer), [KraetzerThesis](#ref-KraetzerThesis)]; the full quadratic–linear formulation also appears in Beliaev’s discussion of numerical spectra (Beliaev 2008) <a id="pm-cite-2"></a>[[Beliaev](#ref-Beliaev)]. We prove a strict upper bound at the negative interior exponent $p=-1$.

<a id="stmt-thm-main"></a>
<div id="thm:main" class="theorem">

<a id="eq:uniform"></a>
**Theorem 1** (Uniform inverse-first-power bound). *There exist constants $0<\varepsilon<1/4$ and $C<\infty$ such that $$\label{eq:uniform}
 M_{-1}[f'](r)\le C(1-r)^{-1/4+\varepsilon}
 \qquad\text{for every }f\in\mathcal S\text{ and every }1/2\le r<1.$$ Consequently $B_b(-1)<1/4$, and Kraetzer’s conjectured identity <a href="#eq:kraetzer" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:kraetzer">[eq:kraetzer]</a> is false.*

</div>

Both constants are independent of the map, including its image size. The proof gives existence of a positive gap but does not evaluate it numerically. The conclusion concerns the inverse first power; it does not settle the proposed value at $p=1$.

<a id="pm-h-4"></a>
#### Context and the role of strictness

The distinction between bounded and unrestricted disk maps matters. Carleson and Jones connected coefficient problems with conformal dimension (Carleson and Jones 1992) <a id="pm-cite-3"></a>[[CJ](#ref-CJ)]. Makarov’s comparison theorem identifies the unrestricted spectrum with the maximum of the bounded spectrum and the Koebe contribution $3p-1$ (Makarov 1999, Theorem 5.4) <a id="pm-cite-4"></a>[[Makarov](#ref-Makarov)]. In particular, the bounded spectrum in <a href="#eq:kraetzer" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:kraetzer">[eq:kraetzer]</a> should not be replaced by the unrestricted disk spectrum at positive exponents. Hedenmalm and Shimorin developed a weighted Bergman-space approach to bounds for these spectra, keeping this distinction explicit (Hedenmalm and Shimorin 2005) <a id="pm-cite-5"></a>[[HedenmalmShimorin](#ref-HedenmalmShimorin)]. Their upper bound at $p=-1$ for the unrestricted normalized disk class is $0.403$ (Hedenmalm and Shimorin 2005, Table 1) <a id="pm-cite-6"></a>[[HedenmalmShimorin](#ref-HedenmalmShimorin)]. Sola subsequently obtained the bound $0.388$ by related Bergman-space methods (Sola 2006) <a id="pm-cite-7"></a>[[Sola](#ref-Sola)]; see also the author’s account in (Sola 2007, 6) <a id="pm-cite-8"></a>[[SolaThesis](#ref-SolaThesis)]. These estimates remain above the conjectured value $1/4$; Theorem <a href="#thm:main" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:main">1</a> establishes a uniform exponent strictly below it.

Random conformal snowflakes provide another approach. Beliaev and Smirnov constructed random conformal maps whose averaged spectrum approaches the universal spectrum at any prescribed exponent, and related lower bounds to positive integral operators (Beliaev and Smirnov 2010, secs. 3–4) <a id="pm-cite-9"></a>[[BeliaevSmirnov](#ref-BeliaevSmirnov)]. Beliaev further investigated these operators numerically, distinguishing the numerical estimates from rigorous error control (Beliaev 2008) <a id="pm-cite-10"></a>[[Beliaev](#ref-Beliaev)]. The common feature with the present argument is the use of a law on conformal maps and a positive operator. Here the operator samples inverse derivatives through affine changes of base point, and its growth controls an upper bound uniformly over the entire class $\mathcal S$.

The affine-law and saddle-to-maximum construction is also used for inverse-square means in the companion article *Brennan’s conjecture and sharp inverse-square integral means* (OpenAI 2026) <a id="pm-cite-11"></a>[[BrennanMethod](#ref-BrennanMethod)]. The two arguments share the compact normalization of half-plane maps, dyadic sampling, and a comparison of critical points over a common target. We prove every inverse-first-power estimate and every transport assertion needed here; the companion’s inverse-square endpoint theorem is not an input.

The obstacle specific to the present theorem is strictness at the candidate exponent $1/4$. A non-strict comparison of saddle and maximum weights only rules out larger exponents. We retain the exact operator growth parameter when constructing the probability law. For almost every target, distinct critical points have distinct values, so the rank pairing sends each saddle to a strictly higher maximum. This strict increase forces a vanishing Jacobian in the remaining equality case. A harmonic lower barrier then excludes that case. The strict increase can be arbitrarily small from one pair to another; no common pointwise gain is required.

<a id="pm-h-5"></a>
#### Proof outline

Section <a href="#sec:operator" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:operator">2</a> transfers disk maps to the compact family of univalent maps on the upper half-plane $\mathbb H=\{x+iy:y>0\}$, normalized by $F(i)=0$, $F'(i)=1$. A positive operator $L$ samples $1/|F'|$ at two affine subscales. The exponential growth rate of $\|L^n\|$, denoted by $2^\beta$, bounds all the disk means in Theorem <a href="#thm:main" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:main">1</a>. If $\beta\ge1/4$, Section <a href="#sec:law" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:law">3</a> constructs a probability law whose inverse-derivative weighted rebasings scale exactly as $y^{-\beta}$. Differentiating this identity fixes two moments of the logarithmic derivative.

For a target $\xi\in\mathbb C$, the potential $y^{(\beta+2)/3}/|F(x+iy)-\xi|$ turns those moment identities into a geometric comparison. Section <a href="#sec:escape" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:escape">4</a> proves compactness of its positive superlevels for almost every map and target. Section <a href="#sec:pairing" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:pairing">5</a> identifies its critical points through a smooth map $G_F$, proves the target-generic absence of degeneracies and ties, and constructs the measurable rank pairing. The nullity of singular values is the planar instance of Sard’s theorem (Sard 1942) <a id="pm-cite-12"></a>[[Sard](#ref-Sard)]; injectivity supplies the separate exclusion of tied critical values. The planar index count follows the tangent-turning argument of Hopf (Hopf 1935) <a id="pm-cite-13"></a>[[Hopf](#ref-Hopf)]. The moment identities make the expected normalized signed Jacobian of $G_F$ positive when $\beta>1/4$, and zero when $\beta=1/4$.

Section <a href="#sec:transport" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:transport">6</a> changes variables through $G_F$ and compares the paired weights on expanding source rectangles. It also transfers exceptional-target nullity to the fixed normalized point, where area-genericity alone would be insufficient. The comparison forces the expected signed Jacobian to be nonpositive; strictness of the pairing forces the Jacobian itself to vanish almost surely in the remaining equality case. Section <a href="#sec:equality" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:equality">7</a> rules out the vanishing-Jacobian equality case and converts $\beta<1/4$ into the uniform estimate.

### A compact model and its growth operator

This section replaces the boundary-growth problem by growth of a single positive operator on a compact family of normalized maps.

<a id="pm-h-7"></a>
#### Univalence estimates

We use the classical quarter and derivative-distortion theorems in exactly the following form (Duren 1983, vol. 259, chap. 2) <a id="pm-cite-14"></a>[[Duren](#ref-Duren)].

<a id="stmt-lem-classical"></a>
<div id="lem:classical" class="lemma">

<a id="eq:koebe"></a>
<a id="eq:local-quarter"></a>
**Lemma 2** (Classical univalence estimates). *For every $f\in\mathcal S$ and $|z|=r<1$, $$\label{eq:koebe}
 \frac{1-r}{(1+r)^3}\le |f'(z)|\le
 \frac{1+r}{(1-r)^3},\qquad
 B(0,1/4)\subset f(\mathbb D).$$ If $F$ is univalent on the Euclidean disk $B(z_0,s)$, then $$\label{eq:local-quarter}
 B\bigl(F(z_0),s|F'(z_0)|/4\bigr)
 \subset F(B(z_0,s)).$$*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-classical"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-classical</summary>

*Proof.* The first assertions are the quoted classical estimates for normalized univalent disk maps. Apply the quarter theorem to $(F(z_0+s\zeta)-F(z_0))/(sF'(z_0))$ to obtain the last assertion. ◻

</details>

Let $\mathbb H=\{x+iy:y>0\}$, and define $$\mathcal K=\{F:\mathbb H\to\mathbb C\text{ univalent}:F(i)=0,\ F'(i)=1\}.$$ For $F\in\mathcal K$, set $Q_F=1/F'$. With $m(z)=(z-i)/(z+i)$, the maps of $\mathcal K$ are exactly $F=2i f\circ m$, $f\in\mathcal S$.

<a id="stmt-lem-compact"></a>
<div id="lem:compact" class="lemma">

<a id="eq:strip"></a>
**Lemma 3** (Compact normalized half-plane family). *The locally uniform topology makes $\mathcal K$ compact metrizable. Evaluation of $F,Q_F$ and all their derivatives is jointly continuous in $F,z$, and uniformly bounded on each compact source set. Moreover, uniformly in $F\in\mathcal K$, $$\label{eq:strip}
 |Q_F(x+iy)|\le C/y\quad(|x|\le2,\ 0<y\le1),
 \qquad |F(z)|\ge c>0\quad(|m(z)|\ge1/2).$$*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-compact"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-compact</summary>

*Proof.* Integrating the derivative bound in the disk gives local uniform boundedness. Montel’s theorem gives convergent subsequences. Limits retain the normalization by convergence of derivatives and remain injective by Hurwitz’s theorem: for a fixed source point, subtract its value and apply Hurwitz on the punctured domain. The nonconstant normalization excludes the identically zero alternative. A countable compact exhaustion metrizes this topology. Interior holomorphic convergence, together with nonvanishing derivatives, gives the asserted continuity and bounds.

The identities $$1-|m(z)|^2=\frac{4y}{|z+i|^2},
 \qquad |Q_F(z)|=\frac{|z+i|^2}{4|f'(m(z))|}$$ and <a href="#eq:koebe" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:koebe">[eq:koebe]</a> imply the strip estimate. The image under $f$ of the disk of radius $1/2$ contains $B(0,1/8)$ by <a href="#eq:local-quarter" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:local-quarter">[eq:local-quarter]</a>. Injectivity gives the second assertion, with $c=1/4$ for $F=2i f\circ m$. ◻

</details>

<a id="pm-h-8"></a>
#### Affine rebasings and dyadic samples

<a id="eq:action"></a>
<a id="eq:dyadic"></a>
<a id="eq:rho"></a>
Identify a point $z=x+iy\in\mathbb H$ with the affine map $$z\cdot w=x+yw,\qquad z^\#=(-x+i)/y.$$ This is a group with identity $i$ and inverse $z^\#$. For $F\in\mathcal K$, define $$(T_zF)(w)=\frac{F(z\cdot w)-F(z)}{yF'(z)}.$$ Then $T_zF\in\mathcal K$, and direct substitution gives $$\label{eq:action}
 T_w(T_zF)=T_{z\cdot w}F,\qquad
 Q_{T_zF}(w)=\frac{Q_F(z\cdot w)}{Q_F(z)}.$$ On $C(\mathcal K)$, with its supremum norm, put $$(A_z\Phi)(F)=|Q_F(z)|\,\Phi(T_zF),\qquad
 L=\tfrac12(A_{-1/2+i/2}+A_{1/2+i/2}).$$ These are bounded positive operators. For each continuous $\Phi$, $A_z\Phi$ is jointly continuous in $F,z$; rebasings send compact source sets to compact sets locally in the parameter $z$. Equation <a href="#eq:action" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:action">[eq:action]</a> gives $A_zA_w=A_{z\cdot w}$. In particular, if $v=2^{-n}$ and $u_j=-1+(2j+1)v$, then $$\label{eq:dyadic}
 L^n=v\sum_{j=0}^{2^n-1}A_{u_j+iv},\qquad
 \|L^n\|=\max_{F\in\mathcal K}(L^n1)(F).$$ The abscissae are the sums $\sum_{\ell=1}^n\pm2^{-\ell}$; positivity proves the norm identity. Testing the affine map $F(z)=z-i$, and using <a href="#eq:strip" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:strip">[eq:strip]</a>, we obtain $1\le\|L^n\|\le C2^n$. The same operator will control every map. Submultiplicativity defines $$\label{eq:rho}
 \rho=\lim_{n\to\infty}\|L^n\|^{1/n},
 \qquad \beta=\log_2\rho,\qquad 0\le\beta\le1.$$ Indeed, writing $n=qN+r$, $0\le r<N$, bounds the upper root limit by $\|L^N\|^{1/N}$; take the infimum over $N$.

<a id="stmt-prop-disk"></a>
<div id="prop:disk" class="proposition">

**Proposition 4** (From dyadic samples to disk means). *There is a universal constant $C_0$ such that, for $f\in\mathcal S$, $$M_{-1}[f'](r)\le C_0\|L^n\|
 \quad\text{if}\quad 1/2\le r<1,\quad
 v=2^{-n}\le1-r<2v.$$ Consequently $B_b(-1)\le\beta$.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-prop-disk"><summary>Proof of stmt-prop-disk</summary>

*Proof.* For $3\pi/4\le\theta\le5\pi/4$, write $$m^{-1}(re^{i\theta})=x+iy,\qquad
 x=\frac{-2r\sin\theta}{d},\quad
 y=\frac{1-r^2}{d},\quad
 d=1-2r\cos\theta+r^2.$$ Here $|x|\le1$, $1/2\le y/v\le4$, and $$\frac{dx}{d\theta}
 =\frac{4r^2-2r(1+r^2)\cos\theta}{d^2}\ge\frac1{16}.$$ If $|x-u_j|\le v$, the point $(x-u_j)/v+i y/v$ lies in a fixed compact rectangle in $\mathbb H$. Apply Lemma <a href="#lem:compact" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:compact">3</a> to $Q_{T_{u_j+iv}F}$ and <a href="#eq:action" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:action">[eq:action]</a> to get $$|Q_F(x+iy)|\le C|Q_F(u_j+iv)|.$$ For $F=2i f\circ m$, $|f'(re^{i\theta})|^{-1}=4|Q_F(x+iy)|/|x+i(y+1)|^2$. The denominator is bounded below. Integrating over the cells gives a bound $Cv\sum_j|Q_F(u_j+iv)|\le C\|L^n\|$ for this arc. Four arcs obtained by applying the same argument to $e^{-ia}f(e^{ia}\zeta)$ cover the circle. All constants are independent of $f$. Finally $n/\log(1/(1-r))\to1/\log2$; use <a href="#eq:rho" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:rho">[eq:rho]</a>, take each map’s limit superior, and then take the supremum. ◻

</details>

<a id="sec:law"></a>
### A weighted affine law

<a id="eq:contrary"></a>
We next turn excessive operator growth into an exact scaling identity for a probability law. For the remainder of the argument suppose, toward a contradiction, that $$\label{eq:contrary}
 \beta\ge1/4.$$ All laws below are constructed at this fixed exponent.

<a id="stmt-prop-law"></a>
<div id="prop:law" class="proposition">

<a id="eq:law"></a>
**Proposition 5** (Exact-parameter affine law). *Assume $\beta=\log_2\rho\ge1/4$, where $\rho$ is defined in <a href="#eq:rho" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:rho">[eq:rho]</a>. There is a Borel probability $P$ on $\mathcal K$ such that, writing $\mathbb E$ for expectation under $P$, $$\label{eq:law}
 \mathbb E\bigl[|Q_F(x+iy)|\,\Phi(T_{x+iy}F)\bigr]
 =y^{-\beta}\mathbb E\Phi(F)$$ for every $x+iy\in\mathbb H$ and every nonnegative Borel test $\Phi:\mathcal K\to[0,\infty]$.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-prop-law"><summary>Proof of stmt-prop-law</summary>

*Proof.* We use the representation of positive functionals on continuous functions of a compact metric space by Borel measures (Folland 1999, Theorem 7.2) <a id="pm-cite-15"></a>[[Folland](#ref-Folland)]. Compactness of probabilities follows by taking a diagonal limit on a countable dense family of continuous tests and applying this representation theorem.

*The operator eigenmeasure.* First construct a probability $\mu$ with $\mu L=\rho\mu$, where measures act on the left as integration functionals. For $\tau>\rho$, the series $$R_\tau=\sum_{n\ge0}\tau^{-n}L^n,\qquad
 g_\tau=R_\tau1,\qquad M_\tau=\max g_\tau$$ converges in operator norm. Since $$Lg_\tau=\tau(g_\tau-1)
 \le\tau(1-1/M_\tau)g_\tau,\qquad 1\le g_\tau\le M_\tau,$$ positivity gives $\|L^n\|\le M_\tau[\tau(1-1/M_\tau)]^n$. Thus $\rho\le\tau(1-1/M_\tau)$, so $M_\tau\ge\tau/(\tau-\rho)\to\infty$. At a maximizer $F_\tau$ of $g_\tau$, the functional $\mu_\tau=\delta_{F_\tau}R_\tau/M_\tau$ is a probability and $$\mu_\tau L=\tau\mu_\tau-\frac{\tau}{M_\tau}\delta_{F_\tau}.$$ A weak limit as $\tau\downarrow\rho$ gives the required $\mu$.

*Horizontal and scale averages.* The composition law gives $A_{u+iv}A_{2+i}=A_{u+2v+iv}$: right translation by $2$ shifts the grid in <a href="#eq:dyadic" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:dyadic">[eq:dyadic]</a> by one cell of width $2v$. Using $\mu=\rho^{-n}\mu L^n$, all but the two endpoint terms cancel: $$\mu A_{2+i}-\mu
 =\rho^{-n}v\bigl(\mu A_{1+v+iv}-\mu A_{-1+v+iv}\bigr).$$ The norm of this difference is at most $2C\rho^{-n}$ by <a href="#eq:strip" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:strip">[eq:strip]</a>, and tends to zero because $\beta>0$. Therefore $\mu A_{2+i}=\mu$. It follows that $t\mapsto\mu A_{t+ih}$ has period two for every fixed $h>0$.

Define $\bar\mu=\tfrac12\int_0^2\mu A_{x+i}\,dx$ on continuous tests. Periodicity gives $\bar\mu A_{s+i}=\bar\mu$ for all $s\in\mathbb R$. Moreover, $$\rho\bar\mu
 =\tfrac12\int_0^2\mu L A_{x+i}\,dx
 =\tfrac12\int_{-1/2}^{3/2}\mu A_{t+i/2}\,dt
 =\bar\mu A_{i/2}.$$ The middle equality uses $t=-1/2+x/2$ and $t=1/2+x/2$ for the two summands of $L$; the last uses period two. Consequently $h^\beta\bar\mu A_{ih}$ is multiplicatively two-periodic in $h>0$. Each of these functionals is horizontally invariant on the right, because $A_{ih}A_{s+i}=A_{hs+i}A_{ih}$. The functional $$\nu=\int_1^2 h^\beta\bar\mu A_{ih}\,\frac{dh}{h}$$ has finite, strictly positive mass by compact-family continuity. Periodicity and the substitution $u=hy$ give $\nu A_{iy}=y^{-\beta}\nu$. Horizontal invariance persists, so normalization of $\nu$ gives <a href="#eq:law" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:law">[eq:law]</a> for continuous tests. For each fixed $x+iy$, the two sides define finite Borel measures (one is a weighted pushforward). Equality on continuous tests makes them equal as measures, proving the assertion for all nonnegative Borel tests. ◻

</details>

The law now supplies the differential moments needed for the signed Jacobian comparison. For $z=x+iy$, define <a id="eq:qk"></a>$$\label{eq:qk}
 q_F(z)=iy\,\frac{Q_F'(z)}{Q_F(z)}
       =-iy\,\frac{F''(z)}{F'(z)}=a_F(z)+ib_F(z),
 \qquad k=\frac{\beta+2}{3}.$$ Thus $3(k-1)=\beta-1$ and $3/4\le k\le1$. Equation <a href="#eq:action" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:action">[eq:action]</a> implies $q_{T_zF}(w)=q_F(z\cdot w)$.

<a id="stmt-lem-moments"></a>
<div id="lem:moments" class="lemma">

<a id="eq:moments"></a>
**Lemma 6** (Logarithmic-derivative moments). *The law satisfies $$\label{eq:moments}
 \mathbb Ea_F(i)=-\beta,\qquad
 \mathbb E|q_F(i)|^2=\beta(\beta+1).$$*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-moments"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-moments</summary>

*Proof.* The constant test in <a href="#eq:law" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:law">[eq:law]</a> gives $\mathbb E|Q_F(x+iy)|=y^{-\beta}$. Since $\log|Q_F|$ is harmonic, $$\partial_y|Q_F|=\frac{|Q_F|a_F}{y},\qquad
 \Delta|Q_F|=|Q_F|\left|\frac{Q_F'}{Q_F}\right|^2.$$ Differentiate at $i$, where $Q_F(i)=1$. Lemma <a href="#lem:compact" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:compact">3</a> and local nonvanishing give the uniform smoothness bounds needed to differentiate under expectation. ◻

</details>

<a id="sec:escape"></a>
### Compact superlevels for almost every target

The affine law must next control escape in the source half-plane. Our objective is compactness of positive superlevels, which will make the subsequent critical-point counts finite above each positive value. For $F\in\mathcal K$ and $\xi\in\mathbb C$, put $$V_\xi^F(x+iy)=\frac{y^k}{|F(x+iy)-\xi|},$$ with value $+\infty$ at the possible pole. The choice $3k=\beta+2$ lets a submean estimate for $|1/F|^3$ convert the weighted area tail below into decay of $V_0^F$ at source infinity. We write $dA$ for planar Lebesgue area.

<a id="stmt-prop-compact-levels"></a>
<div id="prop:compact-levels" class="proposition">

**Proposition 7** (Compact positive superlevels). *For $P$-almost every $F$, and then for area-almost every $\xi\in\mathbb C$, every closed positive superlevel $\{V_\xi^F\ge\delta\}$, $\delta>0$, is compact in $\mathbb H$.*

</div>

We prove this by controlling infinity and then the real boundary. The order of these two controls is useful: the first supplies a horizontal preimage-length bound for the second. That bound is uniform in the height, with the map and compact target set fixed.

<a id="stmt-lem-area-tail"></a>
<div id="lem:area-tail" class="lemma">

<a id="eq:area-tail"></a>
**Lemma 8** (The weighted area tail). *Writing $S_0=\{z\in\mathbb H:|m(z)|\ge1/2\}$, almost every $F$ satisfies $$\label{eq:area-tail}
 I(F):=\int_{S_0}y^\beta|F(z)|^{-3}\,dA(z)<\infty.$$*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-area-tail"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-area-tail</summary>

*Proof.* For fixed $z\ne i$, apply <a href="#eq:law" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:law">[eq:law]</a> to $\Phi(F)=|F(z^\#)|^{-3}$. The identity $(T_zF)(z^\#)=-F(z)Q_F(z)/y$ gives $$\mathbb E\bigl[|F'(z)|^2|F(z)|^{-3}\bigr]
 =y^{-\beta-3}\mathbb E|F(z^\#)|^{-3}.$$ The integral of the left side over $S_0$ is uniformly finite: by injectivity and <a href="#eq:strip" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:strip">[eq:strip]</a>, it is bounded by $\int_{|\omega|\ge c}|\omega|^{-3}\,dA(\omega)$. The involution $z\mapsto z^\#$ preserves $|m(z)|$, inverts the height, and has absolute Jacobian $y^{-3}$. Changing variables on the right leaves exactly the weight $y^\beta$. Tonelli proves finite expectation of $I$, hence the assertion. ◻

</details>

<a id="stmt-lem-infinity"></a>
<div id="lem:infinity" class="lemma">

<a id="eq:infinity"></a>
<a id="eq:horizontal"></a>
**Lemma 9** (Escape at infinity and horizontal length). *If $I(F)<\infty$, then $$\label{eq:infinity}
 \frac{y^k}{|F(x+iy)|}\longrightarrow0
 \qquad\text{as } |x|+y\longrightarrow\infty.$$ For every compact target set $K$, its preimage restricted to $y\ge\epsilon>0$ is compact, its whole preimage has bounded height, and $$\label{eq:horizontal}
 \sup_{s>0}\int_\mathbb R\mathbf 1_K(F(x+is))\,dx<\infty.$$*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-infinity"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-infinity</summary>

*Proof.* If $w=x'+iy'\in B(z,y/2)$, then $y/2\le y'\le3y/2$ and $|x|+y\le |x'|+3y'$. Thus these disks escape every bounded $|x'|+y'$ region as their centers do. The complement of $S_0$ is bounded, so the disks are eventually contained in $S_0$ and avoid the zero of $F$. Submean for $|1/F|^3$, together with $3k=\beta+2$, gives $$\left(\frac{y^k}{|F(z)|}\right)^3
 \le C_\beta\int_{B(z,y/2)}
      (\operatorname{Im}w)^\beta|F(w)|^{-3}\,dA(w).$$ The right side tends to zero as a tail of <a href="#eq:area-tail" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:area-tail">[eq:area-tail]</a>. For $|F(z)|$ bounded and $y\ge\epsilon$, this excludes source infinity, proving strip compactness. It also excludes unbounded heights in the whole preimage of $K$.

Choose a nonnegative smooth compactly supported $\psi\ge\mathbf 1_K$ and put $H(y)=\int_\mathbb R\psi(F(x+iy))\,dx$. For fixed $s>0$, the support in $y\ge s/2$ is compact by the strip assertion. Hence differentiation and integration by parts give $$\begin{aligned}
 H(s)&=\int_s^\infty(y-s)H''(y)\,dy\\
 &=\int_{y>s}(y-s)|F'(z)|^2(\Delta\psi)(F(z))\,dA(z).
\end{aligned}$$ Here the integral of $\partial_{xx}(\psi\circ F)$ is zero and $\Delta(\psi\circ F)=|F'|^2(\Delta\psi)\circ F$. If $Y_\psi$ bounds the preimage heights of $\operatorname{supp}\psi$, the absolute value is at most $$Y_\psi\int_\mathbb H|F'(z)|^2|(\Delta\psi)(F(z))|\,dA(z)
 \le Y_\psi\int_\mathbb C|\Delta\psi|\,dA.$$ The last inequality uses injectivity, and the bound is independent of $s$. This proves <a href="#eq:horizontal" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:horizontal">[eq:horizontal]</a>. ◻

</details>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-infinity-2"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-infinity</summary>

*Proof of Proposition <a href="#prop:compact-levels" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:compact-levels">7</a>.* Fix $F$ satisfying Lemma <a href="#lem:area-tail" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:area-tail">8</a>. The preceding lemma controls source infinity. It remains to control approach to the real boundary, uniformly in the horizontal coordinate for almost every target. For omitted targets, meaning $\xi\notin F(\mathbb H)$, restrict to $|\xi|\le R$ and fix $\delta>0$. At scale $h=2^{-\ell}$, $\ell\ge0$, partition the line into cells $[jh,(j+1)h)$. Mark a cell if some point $z=x+iy$ with $x$ in that cell and $h\le y\le2h$ satisfies $|F(z)-\xi|\le y^k/\delta$ for at least one such omitted target. At a witness, <a href="#eq:local-quarter" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:local-quarter">[eq:local-quarter]</a> on $B(z,y)$ gives $y|F'(z)|\le4|F(z)-\xi|$. For every $x'$ in the same cell, the rebased point $(x'-x)/y+i h/y$ belongs to the compact rectangle $[-1,1]+i[1/2,1]$. Lemma <a href="#lem:compact" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:compact">3</a> applied to $T_zF$ therefore gives <a id="eq:cell-cover"></a>$$\label{eq:cell-cover}
 |F(x'+ih)-\xi|\le C_\delta h^k.$$ This bound holds for every witness and its target.

Each marked cell’s entire segment at height $h$ is mapped into $\{|\omega|\le R+C_\delta\}$. Equation <a href="#eq:horizontal" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:horizontal">[eq:horizontal]</a> thus bounds the number of marked cells by $C/h$. Disks of radius $C_\delta h^k$ centered at their midpoint images cover all targets witnessed at this scale, and have summed area $O(h^{2k-1})$. The sum over dyadic scales is finite because $k>1/2$. The limsup of these measurable covering unions has area zero. Taking integer $R>0$ and $\delta=1/n$, $n\ge1$, shows that for almost every omitted target, $$\sup_x V_\xi^F(x+iy)\longrightarrow0\qquad(y\downarrow0).$$ Indeed a failure at arbitrarily small heights gives witnesses in infinitely many dyadic bands for one such threshold.

If $\xi\in F(\mathbb H)$, a small closed disk about $\xi$ contained in $F(\mathbb H)$ has compact inverse image under the homeomorphism onto the image. Below its minimum preimage height, $|F(z)-\xi|$ is uniformly positive, giving the same limit. At heights bounded below, <a href="#eq:infinity" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:infinity">[eq:infinity]</a> gives $|F(z)|\to\infty$ and $V_\xi^F(z)\to0$ at source infinity. Finally $\{V_\xi^F\ge\delta\}$ is relatively closed, since it is $\{|F(z)-\xi|\le y^k/\delta\}$, also at a pole. The two escape exclusions prove its compactness. ◻

</details>

<a id="sec:pairing"></a>
### Critical points and strict pairing

For $P$-almost every map and area-almost every target, our next goal is to pair each saddle of the potential with a higher non-pole local maximum. Compact superlevels and nondegeneracy will supply finite critical-point counts above positive levels.

<a id="pm-h-12"></a>
#### The critical map

<a id="eq:Jacobian"></a>
Define the smooth real map and its normalized signed Jacobian by <a id="eq:G"></a>$$\label{eq:G}
 G_F(z)=F(z)-\frac{iyF'(z)}k,\qquad
 J_F(z)=\frac{\det DG_F(z)}{|F'(z)|^2}.$$ Its real derivative columns are $(F'/k)(k+q_F)$ and $(iF'/k)(k-1+q_F)$. Consequently $$\label{eq:Jacobian}
 k^2J_F=k(k-1)+(2k-1)a_F+|q_F|^2,\qquad
 k^2\mathbb EJ_F(i)=\frac{(4\beta-1)(\beta+2)}9.$$ The second identity uses <a href="#eq:moments" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:moments">[eq:moments]</a> and $k=(\beta+2)/3$.

<a id="eq:critical-value"></a>
Off the possible pole, write $\ell=\log V_\xi^F$ and $r_0=F'/(F-\xi)$. Then $$\Delta\ell=-k/y^2,\qquad
 (\ell_x,\ell_y)=(-\operatorname{Re}r_0,\ k/y+\operatorname{Im}r_0).$$ Critical points are therefore exactly the preimages $G_F(z)=\xi$. A pole is not such a preimage. At a critical point, $$\label{eq:critical-value}
 V_\xi^F(z)=k y^{k-1}|Q_F(z)|,\qquad
 r_0'=\frac{k(k+q_F)}{y^2}.$$ Since $3(k-1)=\beta-1$, cubing the critical value produces $k^3y^{\beta-1}|Q_F(z)|^3$. This is the weight, up to the constant $k^3$, that the target change of variables in Section <a href="#sec:transport" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:transport">6</a> will assign to each critical point. Direct differentiation yields <a id="eq:hessian"></a>$$\label{eq:hessian}
 \operatorname{Hess}\ell
 =\frac{k}{y^2}
 \begin{pmatrix}-(k+a_F)&b_F\\b_F&k-1+a_F\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 \det\operatorname{Hess}\ell=-\frac{k^4}{y^4}J_F.$$ Its trace is negative. Thus $J_F>0$ gives a nondegenerate saddle, and $J_F<0$ a nondegenerate local maximum. We call the latter a *finite maximum*, excluding a pole.

<a id="pm-h-13"></a>
#### Good targets

Let $\mathcal G\subset\mathcal K\times\mathbb C$ be the set of pairs $(F,\xi)$ for which all positive closed superlevels of $V_\xi^F$ are compact, $\xi$ is a regular value of $G_F$, and distinct preimages under $G_F$ have distinct $V_\xi^F$-values. We call these pairs *good*.

<a id="stmt-lem-good"></a>
<div id="lem:good" class="lemma">

**Lemma 10** (Generic targets and Borel goodness). *Goodness is Borel in $(F,\xi)$. For $P$-almost every $F$, area-almost every $\xi$ is good.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-good"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-good</summary>

*Proof.* The superlevel assertion is Proposition <a href="#prop:compact-levels" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:compact-levels">7</a>. For any fixed $F$, singular values of $G_F$ have area zero. This is the plane case of Sard’s theorem (Sard 1942, Theorem 4.1) <a id="pm-cite-16"></a>[[Sard](#ref-Sard)]. Here is the elementary estimate needed for this smooth plane map. On a compact source box, cover singular points by grid squares of side $h$. Linearize at one singular point of each square. Bounded second derivatives put its image in an $O(h^2)$-neighborhood of a segment of length $O(h)$. Its area is $O(h^3)$. There are $O(h^{-2})$ squares, giving total area tending to zero. Exhaust the source by compact boxes.

Cover the nonsingular locus by countably many inverse-function charts. On an overlap where two branches $z_1(\xi),z_2(\xi)$ are distinct, the target differential of their critical log values is $$d_\xi\bigl[\log V_\xi^F(z_j(\xi))\bigr](v)
 =\operatorname{Re}\frac{v}{F(z_j(\xi))-\xi}.$$ The source derivative vanishes by criticality. The two linear functionals differ by injectivity of $F$. Hence the difference of log values has nonzero differential, and its zero set is locally a smooth curve. Countably many chart pairs give an area-null tie set.

For measurability, use $C_m=\{|x|\le m,\ 1/m\le y\le m\}$. For every integer $n\ge1$, require the open set $\{V_\xi^F>1/n\}=\{|F(z)-\xi|<ny^k\}$ to lie in some $C_m$. Failure of a given containment is detected at rational-coordinate points outside the closed box. This is a Borel test. It is equivalent to compactness of all closed positive superlevels: for a closed level choose a strictly smaller reciprocal-integer threshold and use relative closedness.

Failures of regularity or absence of ties are witnessed by one or two points in a common $C_m$. For a tied pair impose separation at least $1/n$, and test the values by the continuous expression in <a href="#eq:critical-value" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:critical-value">[eq:critical-value]</a>. Equality conditions and this separation condition have closed parameter projections because the source boxes are compact. Countably many $m,n$ prove the other Borel tests. ◻

</details>

<a id="eq:G-action"></a>
<a id="eq:V-action"></a>
If $t\in\mathbb H$, $Y=\operatorname{Im}t$, and $\eta=(\xi-F(t))/(YF'(t))$, direct calculation gives $$\begin{aligned}
 G_{T_tF}(w)&=\frac{G_F(t\cdot w)-F(t)}{YF'(t)},&
 J_{T_tF}(w)&=J_F(t\cdot w),\label{eq:G-action}\\
 V_\eta^{T_tF}(w)&=Y^{1-k}|F'(t)|\,V_\xi^F(t\cdot w).
 &&\label{eq:V-action}
\end{aligned}$$ Thus goodness corresponds in both directions under rebasing.

<a id="pm-h-14"></a>
#### The superlevel count

<a id="stmt-lem-count"></a>
<div id="lem:count" class="lemma">

<a id="eq:count"></a>
**Lemma 11** (The superlevel index count). *For a good pair and $h>0$, $$\label{eq:count}
 \#\{z:\ z\text{ is a finite maximum},\ V_\xi^F(z)\ge h\}
 \ \ge\
 \#\{z:\ z\text{ is a saddle},\ V_\xi^F(z)\ge h\}.$$ Both counts are finite.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-count"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-count</summary>

*Proof.* The fiber $G_F^{-1}(\xi)$ is closed and discrete, and the closed positive superlevel is compact. This proves finiteness. There can be no accumulation at a pole either, because a pole is not on the fiber.

<a id="eq:index-count"></a>
First count in $U=\{V_\xi^F>\lambda\}$ for a positive regular level $\lambda$. We will show that each component is bounded by one simple closed curve and has total gradient index one, giving $$\label{eq:index-count}
 \#\text{components}
 =\#\text{finite maxima}-\#\text{saddles}+\#\text{poles}
 \quad\text{in }U.$$ The boundary of $U$ is the compact smooth one-manifold $\{V_\xi^F=\lambda\}$, away from any pole. It is a finite disjoint union of smooth simple closed curves. For detail, the components are open in this compact manifold, so there are finitely many. Each component is one orbit of the smooth unit tangent field obtained by rotating $\nabla\ell/|\nabla\ell|$. The orbits are complete and open. A nonperiodic single orbit would be a bijective local homeomorphism from $\mathbb R$ to the compact component, which is impossible. Its least period gives a simple closed curve.

The bounded Jordan interior of each curve has closure in $\mathbb H$: the region below the curve’s minimum height lies in the unbounded complement. Everywhere inside, $V_\xi^F>\lambda$. Otherwise $\ell$ would have an interior minimum, contrary to $\Delta\ell=-k/y^2<0$. If a pole is present, remove a sufficiently small neighborhood where $\ell$ is large before taking this minimum. Thus curves cannot be nested, and their interiors are exactly the components of $U$. Indeed each component meets the level boundary, has its superlevel side locally inside one of the curves, and cannot leave that interior without crossing the level.

On each positively oriented curve, $\nabla\ell$ points inward and has winding one. We recall the tangent-turning argument of Hopf (Hopf 1935, sec. 2) <a id="pm-cite-17"></a>[[Hopf](#ref-Hopf)]. Parametrize a simple regular curve by $\gamma:[0,1]\to\mathbb C$, with matching endpoint derivatives, starting at a leftmost point. The positive tangent initially points down. The unit secant direction from $\gamma(s)$ to $\gamma(t)$, $0\le s\le t\le1$, extends continuously to the diagonal by the tangent, and to $(0,1)$ by the negative initial tangent. Lift its angle continuously on this triangle. On $(0,t)$ it lies in the closed right semicircle and changes angle by $\pi$. On $(s,1)$ it is the negative of the direction at $(0,s)$, giving another $\pi$. The diagonal therefore records tangent winding one, unchanged for the inward normal.

Remove disjoint small disks about the finitely many critical points and possible pole in each component. Stokes applied to the closed angle form $$\frac{X_1\,dX_2-X_2\,dX_1}{|X|^2},\qquad X=\nabla\ell,$$ shows that the sum of interior indices is one. At a nondegenerate critical point the index is the sign of the Hessian determinant, hence $+1$ for a maximum and $-1$ for a saddle. At the pole $z_0$, $\ell=-\log|z-z_0|+\text{a smooth function}$, giving index $+1$. Summing over components proves <a href="#eq:index-count" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:index-count">[eq:index-count]</a>. There is at most one pole; if present it belongs to a component. Thus the finite-maximum count is at least the saddle count. This also covers the empty superlevel.

Finally choose a regular $\lambda<h$ with no critical value in $[\lambda,h)$. Such a choice is possible by finiteness above $h/2$. The counts in $U$ are then the counts in <a href="#eq:count" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:count">[eq:count]</a>. ◻

</details>

<a id="pm-h-15"></a>
#### Measurable rank pairing

The count gives an intrinsic assignment: match the two decreasing lists by rank. Measurability requires a separate argument, because we will integrate the assignment over maps and source points. General countable-section uniformization is supplied by the Lusin–Novikov theory; see (Moschovakis 2009, 155:4F.6 and 4F.17) <a id="pm-cite-18"></a>[[Moschovakis](#ref-Moschovakis)]. In the present setting, local inverse branches give an elementary enumeration, while intrinsic ranks ensure equivariance.

<a id="stmt-prop-pair"></a>
<div id="prop:pair" class="proposition">

**Proposition 12** (Strict measurable rank pairing). *On the Borel domain $$\mathcal D=\{(F,z):J_F(z)>0,\ (F,G_F(z))\in\mathcal G\}$$ there is a Borel assignment $R_F(z)$ to a finite maximum over the same target, injective on each target fiber, such that <a id="eq:strict-value"></a>$$\label{eq:strict-value}
 V_{G_F(z)}^F(R_F(z))>V_{G_F(z)}^F(z).$$ It is equivariant: $$R_F(t\cdot w)=t\cdot R_{T_tF}(w)$$ on the corresponding domains.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-prop-pair"><summary>Proof of stmt-prop-pair</summary>

*Proof.* On each good fiber order saddles by decreasing critical value, and separately order finite maxima the same way. Each point has finitely many predecessors. A nonempty list has a first point: choose any one point and inspect the finite nonempty set at or above its value. Repeating gives integer ranks for all points. Lemma <a href="#lem:count" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:count">11</a> supplies at least as many maxima as saddles at or above each saddle’s value. Pair the saddle of rank $j$ with the maximum of rank $j$. No ties are allowed, so the paired value is strictly larger, as illustrated in Figure <a href="#fig:ranks" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="fig:ranks">1</a>. Different ranks give different partners. Equation <a href="#eq:V-action" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:V-action">[eq:V-action]</a> preserves ranks, proving equivariance.

We prove the measurability assertion directly using the nonsingularity of each root. The nonsingular solution graph $G_F(z)=\xi,\ J_F(z)\ne0$ has countably many continuous local root branches $b_j(F,\xi)$, defined on open parameter sets. To obtain a branch near $(F_0,\xi_0,z_0)$, set $M=DG_{F_0}(z_0)$. On a sufficiently small closed source ball, $$w\longmapsto w-M^{-1}(G_F(w)-\xi)$$ has Lipschitz constant at most $1/2$ for all nearby parameters. Shrinking the parameter neighborhood makes the displacement of the center less than half the radius. The map is a contraction of the ball into itself, with a unique interior nonsingular root. Compactness and uniqueness imply continuous dependence on the parameters. These branches form relatively open graph patches; second countability of $\mathcal K\times\mathbb C\times\mathbb H$ gives a countable cover.

At each parameter activate branch $j$ only when it is defined and its value differs from every earlier defined branch value. This Borel test enumerates each root once. The signs of $J$, critical values <a href="#eq:critical-value" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:critical-value">[eq:critical-value]</a>, and comparisons of such values are Borel. On good fibers, counting active predecessors of the same sign gives Borel integer ranks. Selecting the unique active negative-sign branch of the same rank as a given active positive-sign branch is therefore Borel. Substitute $\xi=G_F(z)$ to obtain the claimed assignment. ◻

</details>

<a id="sec:transport"></a>
### Strict weighted transport

The pairing will imply $\mathbb EJ_F(i)\le0$, with equality only if $J_F(i)=0$ almost surely. Comparing this conclusion with Equation <a href="#eq:Jacobian" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:Jacobian">[eq:Jacobian]</a> will reduce the contradiction to a rigidity argument.

<a id="eq:area-formula"></a>
<a id="eq:target-weight"></a>
Put $J_{F,\pm}=\max(\pm J_F,0)$ and $\omega_F(z)=y^{\beta-1}|Q_F(z)|$. For any fixed $F$ and nonnegative Borel $h$, local change of variables gives $$\label{eq:area-formula}
 \int_\mathbb Hh(z)\omega_F(z)J_{F,\pm}(z)\,dA(z)
 =
 \int_\mathbb C\!
 \sum_{\substack{G_F(z)=\xi\\ \pm J_F(z)>0}}
 h(z)y^{\beta-1}|Q_F(z)|^3\,dA(\xi).$$ Indeed cover the indicated sign locus by countably many local diffeomorphism charts, disjointify into Borel pieces, and use absolute Jacobian $|J_F||F'|^2$. No global properness of $G_F$ is required. At a preimage, <a href="#eq:critical-value" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:critical-value">[eq:critical-value]</a> and $3(k-1)=\beta-1$ give $$\label{eq:target-weight}
 y^{\beta-1}|Q_F(z)|^3=(V_\xi^F(z)/k)^3.$$

<a id="stmt-lem-base-good"></a>
<div id="lem:base-good" class="lemma">

<a id="eq:base-good"></a>
**Lemma 13** (Goodness at the normalized point). *The positive-Jacobian mass at the base point is supported on good fibers: $$\label{eq:base-good}
 \mathbb E\bigl[J_{F,+}(i)
       \mathbf 1_{\{(F,G_F(i))\text{ not good}\}}\bigr]=0.$$*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-base-good"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-base-good</summary>

*Proof.* For almost every $F$, bad targets have area zero by Lemma <a href="#lem:good" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:good">10</a>. Formula <a href="#eq:area-formula" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:area-formula">[eq:area-formula]</a> therefore gives $$\int_{\{|x|\le1,\ 1\le y\le2\}}
 \omega_F(z)J_{F,+}(z)
 \mathbf 1_{\{(F,G_F(z))\text{ not good}\}}\,dA(z)=0.$$ The product of $J_{F,+}$ and the indicator equals its base-point version for $T_zF$, by <a href="#eq:G-action" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:G-action">[eq:G-action]</a>–<a href="#eq:V-action" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:V-action">[eq:V-action]</a>. Take expectations and apply <a href="#eq:law" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:law">[eq:law]</a> and Tonelli. The spatial density is $dx\,dy/y$; its integral on this box is $2\log2>0$. This proves <a href="#eq:base-good" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:base-good">[eq:base-good]</a>. ◻

</details>

The weighted assertion is essential: $G_F(i)=-i/k$ for every $F$, so area-genericity alone says nothing about this fixed target. We need only the weighted conclusion, not almost-sure goodness at the base point without the Jacobian factor.

<a id="stmt-prop-strict-transport"></a>
<div id="prop:strict-transport" class="proposition">

**Proposition 14** (Strict weighted transport). *Assume $\beta\ge1/4$, let $P$ be the law of Proposition <a href="#prop:law" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:law">5</a>, and set $k=(\beta+2)/3$. Then $\mathbb EJ_F(i)\le0$. Equality is possible only if $J_F(i)=0$ almost surely.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-prop-strict-transport"><summary>Proof of stmt-prop-strict-transport</summary>

*Proof.* For $N\ge1$, restrict the pairing domain $\mathcal D$ to $\mathcal D_N$ by requiring, for $z'=R_F(z)=x'+iy'$, $$1/N\le y'/y\le N,\qquad |x'-x|\le Ny.$$ These Borel sets increase to $\mathcal D$, and their indicators at $(F,z)$ equal their indicators at $(T_zF,i)$. Define the equivariant Borel ratio $$c_F(z)=
 \left(\frac{V_{G_F(z)}^F(R_F(z))}{V_{G_F(z)}^F(z)}\right)^3>1
 \quad\text{on }\mathcal D,$$ and set it to zero elsewhere. For $S>0$, $Y>1$, use the boxes $$B=\{|x|\le S,\ 1\le y\le Y\},\qquad
 B'=\{|x|\le S+NY,\ 1/N\le y\le NY\}.$$ Every included saddle in $B$ has its partner in $B'$. On the target side of <a href="#eq:area-formula" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:area-formula">[eq:area-formula]</a>, multiplication by $c_F$ makes its weight exactly the partner’s weight by <a href="#eq:target-weight" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:target-weight">[eq:target-weight]</a>. Fiberwise injectivity of the pairing therefore proves, for every $F$, $$\int_B\omega_FJ_{F,+}c_F\mathbf 1_{\mathcal D_N}(F,z)\,dA(z)
 \le\int_{B'}\omega_FJ_{F,-}\,dA(z).$$ Take expectations and apply <a href="#eq:law" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:law">[eq:law]</a> to the equivariant nonnegative tests. The resulting inequality is $$\begin{aligned}
 2S\log Y\,
 \mathbb E[J_{F,+}(i)c_F(i)\mathbf 1_{\mathcal D_N}(F,i)]
 \le
 2(S+NY)(\log Y+2\log N)\,\mathbb EJ_{F,-}(i).
\end{aligned}$$ Here $J_F(i)$ is bounded by compact-family continuity, so the right side is finite. At fixed $N$, first let $S\to\infty$, then $Y\to\infty$. Finally let $N\to\infty$ by monotone convergence. This gives $$A:=\mathbb E[J_{F,+}(i)c_F(i)\mathbf 1_{\mathcal D}(F,i)]
 \le d:=\mathbb EJ_{F,-}(i).$$ Lemma <a href="#lem:base-good" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:base-good">13</a> gives $b:=\mathbb EJ_{F,+}(i)=\mathbb E[J_{F,+}(i)\mathbf 1_{\mathcal D}(F,i)]$. Thus $b\le A\le d$, proving $\mathbb EJ_F(i)\le0$. If equality holds, then $b=d$ and $$0\le\mathbb E[J_{F,+}(i)(c_F(i)-1)\mathbf 1_{\mathcal D}(F,i)]
 =A-b\le0.$$ The integrand is strictly positive wherever $J_{F,+}(i)>0$ on $\mathcal D$. Equation <a href="#eq:base-good" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:base-good">[eq:base-good]</a> removes positive mass outside this domain, so $b=0$, and hence $d=0$. No uniform positive gap in $c_F-1$ is needed. ◻

</details>

<a id="sec:equality"></a>
### The harmonic equality barrier and the uniform gap

The moment identity and strict transport can coexist at $\beta\ge1/4$ only in the equality case $\beta=1/4$. We first rule out its deterministic consequence, an identically vanishing Jacobian, and then transfer that obstruction back to the law.

<a id="stmt-lem-no-zero-j"></a>
<div id="lem:no-zero-J" class="lemma">

**Lemma 15** (The harmonic equality barrier). *For $k=3/4$, no $F\in\mathcal K$ has $J_F\equiv0$.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-no-zero-j"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-no-zero-j</summary>

*Proof.* By <a href="#eq:Jacobian" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:Jacobian">[eq:Jacobian]</a>, such a map would satisfy $$|q_F(z)+1/4|^2=1/4.$$ Therefore $|q_F(z)|\ge1/4$ and $|F''(z)/F'(z)|\ge1/(4y)$. The pre-Schwarzian has no zeros, so $u=\log|F''/F'|$ is harmonic and $u\ge-\log4-\log y$. For $0<\epsilon<1/2$, the harmonic function $$b_\epsilon(x,y)=-\log4+
 \log(1/\epsilon)\cos(\pi x/2)
 \frac{\sinh(\pi(1-y)/2)}{\sinh(\pi/2)}$$ lies below $u$ on the boundary of $[-1,1]\times[\epsilon,1]$. On its sides and top the added term is zero; on its bottom the cosine-sinh factor is between zero and one. The minimum principle gives $u\ge b_\epsilon$ inside. At $i/2$, the coefficient of $\log(1/\epsilon)$ is fixed and positive, contradicting finiteness as $\epsilon\downarrow0$. ◻

</details>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-no-zero-j-2"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-no-zero-j</summary>

*Proof of Theorem <a href="#thm:main" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:main">1</a>.* Under <a href="#eq:contrary" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:contrary">[eq:contrary]</a>, Equation <a href="#eq:Jacobian" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:Jacobian">[eq:Jacobian]</a> and Proposition <a href="#prop:strict-transport" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:strict-transport">14</a> force $\beta=1/4$, $k=3/4$, and $J_F(i)=0$ almost surely. Apply <a href="#eq:law" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:law">[eq:law]</a> to the test $|J_F(i)|$. By <a href="#eq:G-action" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:G-action">[eq:G-action]</a> and the strictly positive weight $|Q_F(z)|$, $J_F(z)=0$ almost surely at every fixed $z$. A countable dense set and continuity give $J_F\equiv0$ for almost every $F$, contradicting Lemma <a href="#lem:no-zero-J" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:no-zero-J">15</a>. Thus the single exponent in <a href="#eq:rho" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:rho">[eq:rho]</a> satisfies $\beta<1/4$.

Choose $b$ with $\beta<b<1/4$. The root limit in <a href="#eq:rho" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:rho">[eq:rho]</a> supplies a constant $C_b$ such that $\|L^n\|\le C_b2^{bn}$ for all $n$. Proposition <a href="#prop:disk" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:disk">4</a>, with $2^{-n}\le1-r<2^{1-n}$, then gives $M_{-1}[f'](r)\le C(1-r)^{-b}$, uniformly in $f\in\mathcal S$. Taking $\varepsilon=1/4-b$ proves <a href="#eq:uniform" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:uniform">[eq:uniform]</a> and the stated disproof. ◻

</details>
