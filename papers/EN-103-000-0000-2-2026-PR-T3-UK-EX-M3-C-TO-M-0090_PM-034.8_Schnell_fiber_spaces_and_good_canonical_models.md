## 1. Abstract and result contract

> We prove the Campana–Peternell inequality $`\kappa(X)\geq\kappa(D)`$ for a smooth connected projective complex variety <i>X</i> and an effective Cartier divisor <i>D</i> whenever $`m_0K_X-D`$ is pseudo-effective for some positive integer <i>m</i><sub>0</sub>. For an algebraic fiber space $`f\colon X\to Y`$ between smooth connected projective complex varieties, the hypothesis that $`m_0K_X-f^*H`$ is pseudo-effective with <i>H</i> ample Cartier gives $`\kappa(X)=\kappa(F)+\dim Y`$ for a very general smooth fiber <i>F</i>, as well as nonzero sections of $`mK_X-f^*H`$ for all sufficiently large divisible <i>m</i>.

- **C1** (theorem, [source](#stmt-thm-schnell)): Let $f\colon X\to Y$ be a surjective morphism with connected fibers between smooth connected projective complex varieties. Let $F$ be its smooth geometric generic fiber and assume $\kappa(F)=0$. Suppose that there are an ample Cartier divisor $H$ on $Y$ and a positive integer $m_0$ such that $m_0K_X-f^*H$ is pseudo-effective. Then $$\kappa(X)=\dim Y.$$
  - Explanation: Under the ample-pullback numerical comparison and zero Kodaira dimension of the geometric generic fiber, the total-space Kodaira dimension equals the base dimension.
- **C2** (theorem, [source](#stmt-cor-campana-peternell)): Let $X$ be a smooth connected projective complex variety, let $D$ be an effective Cartier divisor on $X$, and let $m_0$ be a positive integer. If $m_0K_X-D$ is pseudo-effective, then $$\kappa(X)\geq\kappa(D).$$
  - Explanation: An effective Cartier divisor numerically dominated by a positive canonical multiple cannot have Iitaka dimension exceeding the total-space Kodaira dimension.
- **C3** (theorem, [source](#stmt-cor-schnell-general)): Let $f\colon X\to Y$ be a surjective morphism with connected fibers between smooth connected projective complex varieties, and let $F$ be a very general smooth fiber. Suppose that $H$ is an ample Cartier divisor on $Y$ and $m_0$ is a positive integer such that $m_0K_X-f^*H$ is pseudo-effective. Then $$\kappa(X)=\kappa(F)+\dim Y.$$ Moreover, there are positive integers $r$ and $\ell_0$ such that $$H^0\bigl(X,\mathcal O_X(\ell rK_X-f^*H)\bigr)\ne0 \qquad\text{for every integer }\ell\geq\ell_0.$$
  - Explanation: The numerical comparison gives the fiber-space equality and eventual nonvanishing after subtracting the original ample pullback; the divisible degree and threshold depend on the instance.
- **C4** (conditional_result, [source](#stmt-thm-good-model-case)): Let $f\colon X\to Y$ be a surjective morphism with connected fibers between smooth connected projective complex varieties. Let $F$ be its smooth geometric generic fiber and suppose $\kappa(F)=0$. Assume that $X$ has a projective good klt minimal model. If $H$ is an ample Cartier divisor on $Y$ and $m_0$ is a positive integer such that $$\label{eq:geometric-numerical-hypothesis} m_0K_X-f^*H\in\overline{\operatorname{Eff}}(X),$$ then $\kappa(X)=\dim Y$.
  - Explanation: The fiber-space equality follows under the additional existence of a projective good klt minimal model of the total space, with the original ample numerical comparison still required.
- **C5** (bound, [source](#stmt-lem-easy-addition)): Let $f\colon X\to Y$ be a surjective morphism with connected fibers between smooth connected projective complex varieties. If its smooth geometric generic fiber $F$ satisfies $\kappa(F)=0$, then $\kappa(X)\leq\dim Y$.
  - Explanation: The zero-Kodaira geometric generic fiber bounds every pluricanonical image by the base dimension, including the case with no nonempty pluricanonical systems.
- **C6** (construction, [source](#stmt-lem-mixed-positivity)): Let $q\colon W\to V$ be a birational morphism from a smooth projective complex $d$-fold to a normal projective variety. Let $D$ be a globally generated Cartier divisor on $V$, and let $g\colon V\to Z\subseteq\mathbb P^N$ be the morphism of $\|D\|$ onto its image. Write $k=\dim Z$, and let $A$ be a very ample Cartier divisor on $V$. Suppose $h\colon W\to Y$ is a surjective morphism to a smooth projective variety with $y=\dim Y>k$. For every ample Cartier divisor $H$ on $Y$, the numerical one-cycle class $$C=(q^*D)^k(q^*A)^{d-k-1}$$ satisfies the following properties:* 1. *$B\cdot C\geq0$ for every pseudo-effective real divisor class $B$ on $W$;* 2. *$E\cdot C=0$ for every $q$-exceptional rational divisor $E$;* 3. *$q^*D\cdot C=0$ and $h^*H\cdot C>0$.
  - Explanation: A mixed curve class from divisors on the birational target is nonnegative on pseudo-effective classes, kills exceptional divisors and the semiample pullback, and detects the independent ample base pullback.
- **C7** (theorem, [source](#stmt-thm-canonical-good-models)): Let $T$ be a smooth connected projective complex variety with pseudo-effective $K_T$. Then there exist a normal projective $\mathbb Q$-factorial klt variety $V$ and a $K_T$-negative birational contraction $T\dashrightarrow V$ such that $K_V$ is $\mathbb Q$-Cartier and semiample. On a smooth projective common resolution $T\xleftarrow{p}W\xrightarrow{q}V$, compatible canonical divisors satisfy $$p^*K_T=q^*K_V+E,\qquad E\geq0,\qquad q_*E=0.$$
  - Explanation: The cited companion theorem supplies a semiample canonical model together with an effective target-exceptional comparison. Its proof is external to this paper.

## 2. Definitions, validity and bounds

- **Algebraic fiber space** ([source](#sec:introduction)): Here an *algebraic fiber space* means a surjective morphism with connected fibers between projective varieties.
- **Good klt minimal model** ([source](#eq:intro-comparison)): A good klt minimal model of $X$ consists of a normal projective $\mathbb Q$-factorial klt variety $V$ with semiample canonical divisor and a $K_X$-negative birational contraction $X\dashrightarrow V$.
- **Iitaka dimension** ([source](#pm-h-5)): For a rational Cartier divisor $L$, the Iitaka dimension $\kappa(L)$ is the maximum dimension of the rational images of its nonempty complete systems $\|mL\|$, with $m>0$ clearing the Cartier index. It is $-\infty$ if all these systems are empty.
- **Kodaira dimension of a variety** ([source](#pm-h-5)): We write $\kappa(X)=\kappa(K_X)$.
- **Closed effective cone** ([source](#pm-h-5)): We write $\overline{\operatorname{Eff}}(X)$ for the closure of the cone of effective real divisor classes in $N^1(X)_\mathbb R$.
- **Pseudo-effective divisor** ([source](#pm-h-5)): A divisor is pseudo-effective when its numerical class belongs to this cone.
- **Semiample rational Cartier divisor** ([source](#pm-h-5)): A rational Cartier divisor is semiample when a positive Cartier multiple is globally generated.
- **Mixed numerical curve class** ([source](#stmt-lem-mixed-positivity)): For every ample Cartier divisor $H$ on $Y$, the numerical one-cycle class $$C=(q^*D)^k(q^*A)^{d-k-1}$$ satisfies the following properties:* 1. *$B\cdot C\geq0$ for every pseudo-effective real divisor class $B$ on $W$;* 2. *$E\cdot C=0$ for every $q$-exceptional rational divisor $E$;* 3. *$q^*D\cdot C=0$ and $h^*H\cdot C>0$.

## 4. Engineering Triptych

### Panel 1 - epistemic contract

- **P3-I1**: The total-space Kodaira dimension equals the base dimension under the full zero-Kodaira fiber-space comparison hypothesis.
- **P3-I2**: The Kodaira dimension of X is at least the Iitaka dimension of the effective Cartier divisor D under the stated numerical comparison.
- **P3-I3**: The general fiber-space equality and eventual divisible-degree nonvanishing hold under the original ample-pullback comparison.
- **P3-I4**: The mixed curve class is nonnegative on pseudo-effective classes, annihilates exceptional divisors and the pulled-back generated divisor, and detects the ample base class.
- **P3-I5**: The cited good-model input supplies a semiample canonical divisor and an effective target-exceptional comparison on a common resolution.

### Panel 2 - negative knowledge

- **P3-A1**: Infer the total-space equality from zero-Kodaira fibers alone. -> The source product example has zero-Kodaira fibers and total-space Kodaira dimension minus infinity.
- **P3-A2**: Treat pseudo-effectivity as an already available nonzero section. -> The proof needs the actual globally generated multiple on a semiample model and the section transfer.
- **P3-A3**: Implement a uniform bound for the first section degree. -> The asserted degrees and threshold are existential and are not given uniformly.
- **P3-A4**: Replace the effective divisor in the Campana–Peternell comparison with an arbitrary pseudo-effective divisor. -> The rational-divisor extension still requires effectivity.
- **P3-A5**: Use scalar intersection identities or dimension enumeration to certify the geometric theorem. -> Such checks cannot establish pseudo-effectivity, existence of a good model, or its section transfer for arbitrary input varieties.

### Panel 3 - reference implementation and oracle

Applicability: `not_applicable`. No finite constructive computational result is supplied. Theorem 7 (stmt-thm-canonical-good-models) invokes existence of a good model from a companion article; the mixed-intersection proof assumes geometric data and chooses globally generated multiples and hyperplanes without an effective input algorithm. Corollary 3 (stmt-cor-schnell-general) expressly states that no uniform choice of r or ell_0 is asserted. The finite increasing-dimension reduction in sec:general-consequences gives termination of a geometric argument, but supplies no executable procedure for producing its models, divisors, or sections. Finite input encoding, effective thresholds, runtime/space bounds, and independent geometric certificates: ABSENT.

## 6. Applicability limits and search

- **L-smooth-projective-complex** (hypothesis): The fiber-space conclusions require smooth connected projective complex source and target, surjectivity, and connected fibers. Smooth source and target do not assert that every special fiber is smooth. Blocked use: ['Applying the theorem to arbitrary smooth manifolds, nonprojective varieties, positive characteristic, or disconnected fibers.']
- **L-ample-comparison** (hypothesis): The ample Cartier comparison and positive integer multiplier are essential numerical hypotheses; fiber Kodaira dimension alone does not imply the conclusion. Blocked use: ['Replacing the ample Cartier divisor by an arbitrary divisor.', 'Dropping pseudo-effectivity of the comparison.']
- **L-zero-fiber** (hypothesis): The zero-fiber theorem uses the smooth geometric generic fiber; the general conclusion uses a very general smooth fiber, not every special fiber. Blocked use: ['Applying the zero-fiber theorem with positive fiber Kodaira dimension.', 'Replacing very general fibers by arbitrary singular special fibers.']
- **L-product-counterexample** (counterexample_scope): The paper gives a product with torsion-canonical fiber over the projective line whose total Kodaira dimension is negative infinity; the compared divisor fails pseudo-effectivity. This refutes removing the numerical hypothesis, rather than the theorem. Blocked use: ['Inferring the total-space equality solely from zero fiber Kodaira dimension.']
- **L-effective-divisor** (hypothesis): The Campana–Peternell comparison requires an effective Cartier divisor. The rational extension requires clearing denominators and retains effectivity. Blocked use: ['Replacing the effective divisor by an arbitrary pseudo-effective divisor.']
- **L-model-dependency** (formal_scope): Existence of the canonical good model is imported from the companion article; this paper supplies the geometric argument using that input rather than an independent proof of the model-existence theorem. Blocked use: ['Treating this paper as a self-contained proof of canonical good-model existence.', 'Claiming independent formal verification of the companion result from this corpus slice.']
- **L-two-model-properties** (hypothesis): The canonical comparison has two separate requirements: effectivity transfers sections and target-exceptionality kills the mixed pairing. Neither can substitute for the other. Blocked use: ['Using an arbitrary birational comparison without effective target-exceptional error.']
- **L-actual-semiampleness** (hypothesis): The proof needs actual globally generated Cartier multiples, not numerical positivity alone. Blocked use: ['Replacing semiampleness by nefness or numerical pseudo-effectivity when constructing sections.']
- **L-mixed-test-regime** (hypothesis): The strict positive mixed test requires base dimension larger than the globally generated divisor image dimension; its factors come from the birational target. Blocked use: ['Applying strict positivity without the dimension inequality.', 'Replacing target pullbacks by arbitrary divisor factors.']
- **L-degenerate-cases** (edge_case): Zero canonical image dimension, zero power of the very ample factor, and relative dimension zero are explicitly included, subject to the other hypotheses. Blocked use: ['Excluding these cases because an intersection product has no factors.']
- **L-no-uniform-threshold** (nonconstructive): Eventual section existence concerns sufficiently large divisible pluricanonical degrees; no uniform multiplier or threshold is asserted. Blocked use: ['Claiming sections at every positive degree.', 'Deriving a universal numerical threshold or runtime bound from this statement.']
- **L-no-finite-algorithm** (nonconstructive): The paper proves geometric existence and section transfer, but supplies no finite input representation or effective procedure for finding the semiample multiple, model, or section threshold. A finite arithmetic toy check would not certify these geometric conclusions. Blocked use: ['Presenting an integer simulation as a certified implementation of the theorem.', 'Treating the existential model as an executable finite algorithm.']

## 7. Complete mathematical paper


We prove the Campana–Peternell inequality $`\kappa(X)\geq\kappa(D)`$ for a smooth connected projective complex variety <i>X</i> and an effective Cartier divisor <i>D</i> whenever $`m_0K_X-D`$ is pseudo-effective for some positive integer <i>m</i><sub>0</sub>. For an algebraic fiber space $`f\colon X\to Y`$ between smooth connected projective complex varieties, the hypothesis that $`m_0K_X-f^*H`$ is pseudo-effective with <i>H</i> ample Cartier gives $`\kappa(X)=\kappa(F)+\dim Y`$ for a very general smooth fiber <i>F</i>, as well as nonzero sections of $`mK_X-f^*H`$ for all sufficiently large divisible <i>m</i>.

<a id="sec:introduction"></a>
### Introduction

The pluricanonical systems of an algebraic fiber space reflect both the geometry of its fibers and the positivity available from its base. When the geometric generic fiber has Kodaira dimension zero, the easy-addition bound says that the total space has Kodaira dimension at most the dimension of the base. The zero-Kodaira case of Schnell’s question asks whether a numerical comparison with an ample divisor on the base forces equality. Here an *algebraic fiber space* means a surjective morphism with connected fibers between projective varieties.

We approach this question through a good canonical model of the total space. The geometric argument below shows what such a model supplies; the smooth canonical good-model theorem of (OpenAI 2026, Corollary 11.2) <a id="pm-cite-1"></a>[[LogAbundance2026](#ref-LogAbundance2026)] then provides the model. Its nonvanishing consequence also permits Schnell’s reduction to pass from zero-Kodaira fibers to the Campana–Peternell inequality and the general fiber-space conclusion stated below.

The formulation in (Kim 2025, Conjecture 1.2) <a id="pm-cite-2"></a>[[Kim2025](#ref-Kim2025)] compares a positive multiple of $K_X$ with the pullback of an ample Cartier divisor on the base. The difficulty is that pseudo-effectivity concerns a numerical divisor class, whereas Kodaira dimension measures actual sections. The following theorem gives the positive conclusion in this formulation; its assertion has also been proved by Zou, as discussed below.

<a id="stmt-thm-schnell"></a>
<div id="thm:schnell" class="theorem">

**Theorem 1** (Schnell’s zero-Kodaira fiber-space conclusion). *Let $f\colon X\to Y$ be a surjective morphism with connected fibers between smooth connected projective complex varieties. Let $F$ be its smooth geometric generic fiber and assume $\kappa(F)=0$. Suppose that there are an ample Cartier divisor $H$ on $Y$ and a positive integer $m_0$ such that $m_0K_X-f^*H$ is pseudo-effective. Then $$\kappa(X)=\dim Y.$$*

</div>

The ample comparison is essential. For example, let $F$ be smooth, connected and projective with torsion canonical bundle. The projection $F\times\mathbb P^1\to\mathbb P^1$ has fibers of Kodaira dimension zero, but its total space has Kodaira dimension $-\infty$. The class $m_0K_{F\times\mathbb P^1}-f^*H$ has negative degree on the moving curves $\{x\}\times\mathbb P^1$ for every ample $H$; it therefore cannot be pseudo-effective: for any fixed effective divisor, a general member of this family is not contained in its support and has nonnegative intersection with it. In contrast, for a smooth connected projective $Y$ with $K_Y$ ample, the projection $F\times Y\to Y$ satisfies the numerical hypothesis with $H=K_Y$ for every $m_0\geq1$, since the compared class is numerically $(m_0-1)f^*K_Y$. Its Kodaira dimension is $\dim Y$.

The canonical good-model theorem used below also supplies canonical nonvanishing in every dimension. With this input, Schnell’s reduction (Schnell 2022, secs. 4–10) <a id="pm-cite-3"></a>[[Schnell2022](#ref-Schnell2022)] promotes Theorem <a href="#thm:schnell" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:schnell">1</a> to the Kodaira-dimension form of the Campana–Peternell conjecture.

<a id="stmt-cor-campana-peternell"></a>
<div id="cor:campana-peternell" class="corollary">

**Corollary 2** (Campana–Peternell). *Let $X$ be a smooth connected projective complex variety, let $D$ be an effective Cartier divisor on $X$, and let $m_0$ be a positive integer. If $m_0K_X-D$ is pseudo-effective, then $$\kappa(X)\geq\kappa(D).$$*

</div>

The same reduction gives the general fiber-space conclusion, including the existence of sections after subtracting the original ample pullback.

<a id="stmt-cor-schnell-general"></a>
<div id="cor:schnell-general" class="corollary">

**Corollary 3** (Schnell’s general fiber-space conclusion). *Let $f\colon X\to Y$ be a surjective morphism with connected fibers between smooth connected projective complex varieties, and let $F$ be a very general smooth fiber. Suppose that $H$ is an ample Cartier divisor on $Y$ and $m_0$ is a positive integer such that $m_0K_X-f^*H$ is pseudo-effective. Then $$\kappa(X)=\kappa(F)+\dim Y.$$ Moreover, there are positive integers $r$ and $\ell_0$ such that $$H^0\bigl(X,\mathcal O_X(\ell rK_X-f^*H)\bigr)\ne0
 \qquad\text{for every integer }\ell\geq\ell_0.$$*

</div>

The last assertion is effectivity for all sufficiently large and divisible pluricanonical degrees, with no uniform choice of $r$ or $\ell_0$ asserted. The numerical hypothesis itself implies $\kappa(F)\geq0$ by restriction and canonical nonvanishing; it is not replaced by that weaker fiber condition. Corollary <a href="#cor:campana-peternell" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="cor:campana-peternell">2</a> also applies to an effective rational divisor after clearing denominators, as explained in Section <a href="#sec:general-consequences" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:general-consequences">4</a>.

<a id="pm-h-3"></a>
#### The problem and previous approaches

Campana and Peternell formulated the comparison $NK_X=A+B$, with $N$ a positive integer, $A$ effective and $B$ pseudo-effective, in their study of positivity of the cotangent bundle (Campana and Peternell 2011, Conjecture 2.4) <a id="pm-cite-4"></a>[[CP2011](#ref-CP2011)]. The proposed inequality $\kappa(X)\geq\kappa(A)$ includes canonical nonvanishing when $A=0$: pseudo-effective $K_X$ should have a nonzero pluricanonical section. For general $A$, the conclusion also requires the canonical systems to have at least the image dimension supplied by $A$. When $\kappa(X)\geq0$, Campana and Peternell reduce this comparison to the general fibers of the Iitaka fibration of $X$, which have Kodaira dimension zero (Campana and Peternell 2011, Proposition 2.6) <a id="pm-cite-5"></a>[[CP2011](#ref-CP2011)]. In that zero-Kodaira setting, a good minimal model has torsion canonical class; their pushforward argument then forces $\kappa(A)=0$ (Campana and Peternell 2011, Proposition 2.7) <a id="pm-cite-6"></a>[[CP2011](#ref-CP2011)]. This already exhibits how a good model can convert numerical information into a statement about sections.

Schnell developed this circle of questions in his study of singular metrics, nonvanishing and the Campana–Peternell conjecture (Schnell 2022, secs. 4–10) <a id="pm-cite-7"></a>[[Schnell2022](#ref-Schnell2022)]. His Conjecture 10.1 allows fibers of nonnegative Kodaira dimension and asks for nonvanishing after subtracting an ample pullback. Under canonical nonvanishing, his Section 9 reduces the conjecture to fibers of Kodaira dimension zero; Lemma 7.1 supplies an effective pluricanonical divisor after a positive base twist. If the fiber has positive Kodaira dimension, the Iitaka fibration of that divisor has a strictly larger base, while retaining the numerical comparison. Iteration reaches the zero-Kodaira case. Section 8 connects the resulting equality to effectivity after subtracting the original ample pullback. This uses the Iitaka-addition criterion of Fujita and Mori, in the form recorded in (Popa and Schnell 2014, Lemma 4.6) <a id="pm-cite-8"></a>[[PopaSchnell2014](#ref-PopaSchnell2014)]. He proves a special case when the canonical divisor of the base is pseudo-effective (Schnell 2022, Theorem 12.1) <a id="pm-cite-9"></a>[[Schnell2022](#ref-Schnell2022)].

Kim applies the canonical bundle formula to the same fiber-space problem. His Theorem 1.3 proves the conclusion when $K_Y+(1-\varepsilon)B_Y$ is pseudo-effective for some $\varepsilon>0$, where $B_Y$ is the discriminant on the birational setup of that theorem, or when the canonical class of the general fiber is rigid (Kim 2025) <a id="pm-cite-10"></a>[[Kim2025](#ref-Kim2025)]. Here rigidity means uniqueness of the closed positive $(1,1)$-current representing that class. Kim also recalls a known algebraic proof under the existence of a good minimal model of the general fiber (Kim 2025, sec. 1.3) <a id="pm-cite-11"></a>[[Kim2025](#ref-Kim2025)]. Zou obtains the same conclusion as Theorem <a href="#thm:schnell" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:schnell">1</a>, without these additional hypotheses, by a canonical-bundle-formula argument (Zou 2025, Theorems 1.3 and 5.1) <a id="pm-cite-12"></a>[[Zou2025](#ref-Zou2025)].

We give a direct mixed-intersection proof of Theorem <a href="#thm:schnell" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:schnell">1</a> from a good minimal model of the *total space*. We separate that geometric argument from the model-existence theorem that it uses. The latter is the smooth canonical good-model theorem of (OpenAI 2026, Corollary 11.2) <a id="pm-cite-13"></a>[[LogAbundance2026](#ref-LogAbundance2026)], quoted in Theorem <a href="#thm:canonical-good-models" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:canonical-good-models">7</a>. Its nonvanishing consequence and Schnell’s published reduction then yield Corollaries <a href="#cor:campana-peternell" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="cor:campana-peternell">2</a> and <a href="#cor:schnell-general" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="cor:schnell-general">3</a>.

<a id="pm-h-4"></a>
#### How the numerical condition produces the lower bound

<a id="eq:intro-comparison"></a>
A good klt minimal model of $X$ consists of a normal projective $\mathbb Q$-factorial klt variety $V$ with semiample canonical divisor and a $K_X$-negative birational contraction $X\dashrightarrow V$. For compatible canonical divisors, the comparison on a smooth common resolution $X\xleftarrow{p}W\xrightarrow{q}V$ has the form $$\label{eq:intro-comparison}
 p^*K_X=q^*K_V+E,\qquad E\geq0,\qquad q_*E=0.$$ The last condition means that every component of $E$ is exceptional over $V$. The model therefore provides sections and also specifies the error in transferring them to $X$.

Choose a globally generated Cartier multiple $rK_V$. The comparison makes $rE$ an effective Cartier divisor. Pulling back sections of $rK_V$ and multiplying by the canonical section of $rE$ gives a pluricanonical subsystem on $X$ with the same image dimension. It remains to force that dimension to be at least $\dim Y$.

If the image were smaller, Lemma <a href="#lem:mixed-positivity" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:mixed-positivity">6</a> would provide a mixed complete-intersection curve class $C$ on $W$ with three properties: it pairs nonnegatively with every pseudo-effective divisor, it annihilates both $q^*K_V$ and $E$, and it pairs positively with $(fp)^*H$. Its divisor factors are pulled back from $V$, so the exceptional vanishing follows from the Cartier projection formula. The strict positivity uses a cotangent direction from the map to $Y$ beyond those supplied by the canonical morphism. Thus $$(m_0p^*K_X-(fp)^*H)\cdot C=-(fp)^*H\cdot C<0,$$ contrary to the pulled-back numerical hypothesis. Section <a href="#sec:geometry" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:geometry">2</a> constructs the class and proves each of these properties.

This argument uses the classical intersection theory of Cartier divisors and proper pushforward (Fulton 1998, vol. 2, chap. 2) <a id="pm-cite-14"></a>[[Fulton1998](#ref-Fulton1998)]. In particular, the maps to $Y$ and to the canonical image need no factorization relation. Section <a href="#sec:geometry" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:geometry">2</a> gives the complete geometric proof, including the upper bound and the transfer of sections. Section <a href="#sec:good-model-interface" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:good-model-interface">3</a> states the exact good-model input, verifies its hypotheses and completes Theorem <a href="#thm:schnell" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:schnell">1</a>. Section <a href="#sec:general-consequences" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:general-consequences">4</a> explains the nonvanishing and fiber interfaces, then proves the Campana–Peternell and general Schnell conclusions.

<a id="pm-h-5"></a>
#### Conventions

All varieties are integral and projective over $\mathbb C$ unless specified otherwise. Smoothness of an algebraic fiber space refers here to its source and target; special fibers of the morphism may be singular. Canonical divisors on birational models are chosen compatibly. For a rational Cartier divisor $L$, the Iitaka dimension $\kappa(L)$ is the maximum dimension of the rational images of its nonempty complete systems $|mL|$, with $m>0$ clearing the Cartier index. It is $-\infty$ if all these systems are empty. We write $\kappa(X)=\kappa(K_X)$.

We write $\overline{\operatorname{Eff}}(X)$ for the closure of the cone of effective real divisor classes in $N^1(X)_\mathbb R$. A divisor is pseudo-effective when its numerical class belongs to this cone. A rational Cartier divisor is semiample when a positive Cartier multiple is globally generated. The section constructions below use this actual line-bundle property.

<a id="sec:geometry"></a>
### The numerical argument on a good minimal model

We prove the fiber-space conclusion assuming that the total space has a good klt minimal model. The upper bound comes from the geometric generic fiber. For the lower bound, we construct a mixed intersection that detects the ample class from the base and kills both terms in the canonical comparison.

<a id="stmt-thm-good-model-case"></a>
<div id="thm:good-model-case" class="theorem">

<a id="eq:geometric-numerical-hypothesis"></a>
**Theorem 4** (The good-model case). *Let $f\colon X\to Y$ be a surjective morphism with connected fibers between smooth connected projective complex varieties. Let $F$ be its smooth geometric generic fiber and suppose $\kappa(F)=0$. Assume that $X$ has a projective good klt minimal model. If $H$ is an ample Cartier divisor on $Y$ and $m_0$ is a positive integer such that $$\label{eq:geometric-numerical-hypothesis}
 m_0K_X-f^*H\in\overline{\operatorname{Eff}}(X),$$ then $\kappa(X)=\dim Y$.*

</div>

We use the good-model convention and effective comparison <a href="#eq:intro-comparison" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:intro-comparison">[eq:intro-comparison]</a> stated in the introduction. This is the $K_X$-negative convention of (Birkar et al. 2010, Definitions 3.6.1 and 3.6.7) <a id="pm-cite-15"></a>[[BCHM2010](#ref-BCHM2010)].

<a id="pm-h-7"></a>
#### The upper bound from the generic fiber

We first account for the use of $\kappa(F)=0$. It bounds every pluricanonical image of $X$, before any model is chosen.

<a id="stmt-lem-easy-addition"></a>
<div id="lem:easy-addition" class="lemma">

**Lemma 5** (The geometric generic fiber bound). *Let $f\colon X\to Y$ be a surjective morphism with connected fibers between smooth connected projective complex varieties. If its smooth geometric generic fiber $F$ satisfies $\kappa(F)=0$, then $\kappa(X)\leq\dim Y$.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-easy-addition"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-easy-addition</summary>

<a id="eq:canonical-generic-restriction"></a>
*Proof.* Put $K=\mathbb C(Y)$ and $\eta=\operatorname{Spec}K$. Generic smoothness and Stein factorization give a smooth geometrically integral generic fiber $X_\eta$ (Hartshorne 1977, III, Sections 10–11) <a id="pm-cite-16"></a>[[Hartshorne1977](#ref-Hartshorne1977)]. The cotangent sequence along this fiber gives $$\label{eq:canonical-generic-restriction}
 \omega_X|_{X_\eta}
 \simeq\omega_{X_\eta/K}\otimes_K\ell,
 \qquad \ell=\omega_Y|_\eta.$$ Here $\ell$ is a one-dimensional $K$-vector space. The factor from the base therefore cancels in ratios of pluricanonical sections.

Fix $m>0$ such that $H^0(X,mK_X)\ne0$, and choose a basis $s_0,\ldots,s_N$ with $s_0\ne0$. A nonzero section is nonzero at the generic point of $X$, so it stays nonzero on $X_\eta$ and after extension to the geometric generic fiber. By <a href="#eq:canonical-generic-restriction" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:canonical-generic-restriction">[eq:canonical-generic-restriction]</a>, the restricted system on $F$ is a nonzero subsystem of $|mK_F|$. Its image has dimension zero, since $\kappa(F)=0$.

Let $\phi_m$ be the rational map of the complete system $|mK_X|$, and let $T$ be the closure of the image of $(f,\phi_m)\colon X\dashrightarrow Y\times\mathbb P^N$. Its function field is $$L=K(s_1/s_0,\ldots,s_N/s_0)\subseteq\mathbb C(X).$$ The dimension of its generic image over $Y$ is $\operatorname{trdeg}_K L$. This dimension is unchanged by algebraic extension of $K$. Geometric integrality of $X_\eta$ identifies that extended image with the image of the same section ratios on $F$. Thus $\operatorname{trdeg}_K L=0$, and $\dim T=\dim Y$. Projection to $\mathbb P^N$ gives $\dim\phi_m(X)\leq\dim Y$. Taking the maximum over all nonempty pluricanonical systems proves the lemma. If every system is empty, the inequality is immediate. ◻

</details>

<a id="pm-h-8"></a>
#### A curve class that detects the base

The lower bound requires a different ingredient. The following lemma constructs a numerical test from a globally generated divisor on $V$. All its factors come from $V$, which is why exceptional errors vanish. Its strict positivity uses the independent morphism to $Y$.

<a id="stmt-lem-mixed-positivity"></a>
<div id="lem:mixed-positivity" class="lemma">

**Lemma 6** (A mixed intersection test). *Let $q\colon W\to V$ be a birational morphism from a smooth projective complex $d$-fold to a normal projective variety. Let $D$ be a globally generated Cartier divisor on $V$, and let $g\colon V\to Z\subseteq\mathbb P^N$ be the morphism of $|D|$ onto its image. Write $k=\dim Z$, and let $A$ be a very ample Cartier divisor on $V$. Suppose $h\colon W\to Y$ is a surjective morphism to a smooth projective variety with $y=\dim Y>k$. For every ample Cartier divisor $H$ on $Y$, the numerical one-cycle class $$C=(q^*D)^k(q^*A)^{d-k-1}$$ satisfies the following properties:*

1.  *$B\cdot C\geq0$ for every pseudo-effective real divisor class $B$ on $W$;*

2.  *$E\cdot C=0$ for every $q$-exceptional rational divisor $E$;*

3.  *$q^*D\cdot C=0$ and $h^*H\cdot C>0$.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-mixed-positivity"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-mixed-positivity</summary>

*Proof.* The inequality $k<y\leq d$ makes all exponents nonnegative; a product with no factors has its usual meaning. We prove first that the test is nonnegative, then that it has the two required vanishing properties, and finally that it detects the ample divisor from $Y$.

*Nonnegativity.* The divisors $q^*D$ and $q^*A$ are globally generated. If $B$ is effective, choose their members successively so that no member contains an irreducible component of the preceding intersection on $B$. Global generation permits all the finitely many required avoidances. The resulting Cartier intersections form an effective zero-cycle, possibly empty. Hence $B\cdot C\geq0$ (Fulton 1998, vol. 2, chap. 2) <a id="pm-cite-17"></a>[[Fulton1998](#ref-Fulton1998)]. Extend by linearity to effective real divisors and by continuity on $N^1(W)_\mathbb R$ to its closed effective cone. This proves (1).

*Vanishing on exceptional and semiample classes.* For a $q$-exceptional divisor the Cartier projection formula gives <a id="eq:exceptional-annihilation"></a>$$\label{eq:exceptional-annihilation}
 E\cdot(q^*D)^k(q^*A)^{d-k-1}
   =q_*E\cdot D^kA^{d-k-1}=0;$$ see (Fulton 1998, Proposition 2.3(c)) <a id="pm-cite-18"></a>[[Fulton1998](#ref-Fulton1998)]. Indeed, every component of $E$ maps to codimension at least two, so $q_*E=0$ as a divisor cycle. This reasoning is valid on the possibly singular variety $V$. Also $D=g^*\mathcal O_Z(1)$, and $k+1$ general hyperplanes miss the $k$-dimensional image $Z$. Consequently $(q^*D)^{k+1}=0$. For $k=0$, the morphism $g$ is constant and $\mathcal O_V(D)$ is trivial, which gives the same conclusion. These observations prove (2) and the first assertion of (3).

*Strict positivity.* Choose $b>0$ such that $bH$ is very ample. Use this divisor to embed $Y$, and use $A$ to embed $V$. There is a point $w\in W$ at which $q$ is an isomorphism onto a smooth open subset of $V$ and $$\operatorname{rank}d(gq)_w=k,
 \qquad\operatorname{rank}dh_w=y,
 \qquad\operatorname{rank}dq_w=d.$$ Each condition holds on a dense open subset. For the rank statements, the generic differential rank in characteristic zero equals the dimension of the image, by separability of the function-field extension. We choose $w$ in the intersection of these open subsets.

The differentials of pulled-back hyperplanes through $gq(w)$ span a $k$-dimensional subspace $U\subseteq T_w^*W$. Choose $k$ of them whose differentials form a basis of $U$. The hyperplanes through $h(w)$ supply a $y$-dimensional subspace. Since $y>k$, one such hyperplane pulls back to a divisor with differential outside $U$. Finally, hyperplanes from the embedding by $A$ supply all cotangent directions at $w$, because $q$ is a local isomorphism there. Choose $d-k-1$ of them completing the chosen differentials to a basis of $T_w^*W$. These $d$ divisors meet transversely at $w$.

To use this local intersection in the global intersection number, perturb the hyperplanes slightly in their complex parameter spaces. The transverse point persists for every sufficiently small perturbation, by the implicit function theorem. Tuples whose pullbacks meet properly at every successive step form a nonempty Zariski open subset of the product of the hyperplane parameter spaces. Nonemptiness follows by successively avoiding the finitely many components already present, using global generation; openness follows from upper semicontinuity of fiber dimension for the projective universal intersections. This parameter space is irreducible, so the open subset is dense also in the complex analytic topology. It therefore meets the perturbation neighborhood.

The resulting proper global intersection is an effective zero-cycle that contains a transverse point of multiplicity one. It follows that $$h^*(bH)\cdot(q^*D)^k(q^*A)^{d-k-1}>0.$$ Division by $b$ proves (3). Only one cotangent direction from $h$ outside $U$ was needed; the two morphisms $gq$ and $h$ need not factor through each other. ◻

</details>

<a id="pm-h-9"></a>
#### Sections and the numerical contradiction

We now combine the two lemmas. The good model supplies actual sections on $X$. The mixed test forces their image to have at least the dimension of $Y$.

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-mixed-positivity-2"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-mixed-positivity</summary>

*Proof of Theorem <a href="#thm:good-model-case" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:good-model-case">4</a>.* If $Y$ is a point, then $F=X$ and $\kappa(F)=0$ is the required conclusion. Hence assume $d=\dim X\geq y=\dim Y>0$.

<a id="eq:canonical-comparison"></a>
Let $V$ be a good klt minimal model of $X$. Resolve the closure of the graph of $X\dashrightarrow V$ projectively (Hironaka 1964) <a id="pm-cite-19"></a>[[Hironaka1964](#ref-Hironaka1964)], obtaining a smooth projective common resolution $X\xleftarrow{p}W\xrightarrow{q}V$. By the good-model comparison, compatible canonical divisors satisfy $$\label{eq:canonical-comparison}
 p^*K_X=q^*K_V+E,\qquad E\geq0,\qquad q_*E=0.$$ Pulling back to a further common resolution preserves the effectivity and target-exceptionality of this error.

Choose $r>0$ such that $D=rK_V$ is Cartier and globally generated, and let $g\colon V\to Z\subseteq\mathbb P^N$ be its morphism onto its image. Put $k=\dim Z$. Since $K_X$ is Cartier, <a href="#eq:canonical-comparison" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:canonical-comparison">[eq:canonical-comparison]</a> shows that $rE=p^*(rK_X)-q^*D$ is an integral Cartier divisor on $W$. The two maps whose dimensions we will compare appear in Figure <a href="#fig:common-resolution" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="fig:common-resolution">1</a>.

<figure id="fig:common-resolution">

<figcaption>The common resolution carries the two morphisms $h = fp$ to $Y$ and $gq$ to $Z$. The divisor factors defining the mixed class are pulled back from $V$, so the class annihilates the $q$-exceptional error. Its pairing with $h^{*}H$ detects the ample class from the separate base $Y$.</figcaption>
</figure>

<a id="eq:section-transfer"></a>
<a id="eq:canonical-image-lower-bound"></a>
Pull back sections of $D$ by $q$ and multiply by the canonical section of the effective Cartier divisor $rE$. We obtain an injection $$\label{eq:section-transfer}
 H^0(V,D)\hookrightarrow H^0(W,rp^*K_X)=H^0(X,rK_X).$$ The equality follows from $p_*\mathcal O_W=\mathcal O_X$ and the sheaf projection formula for the proper birational map to the normal variety $X$ (Hartshorne 1977, III, Corollary 11.4 and its proof; II, Exercise 5.1(d)) <a id="pm-cite-20"></a>[[Hartshorne1977](#ref-Hartshorne1977)]. Multiplication by the section of $rE$ leaves section ratios unchanged on the complement of its support. Thus the subsystem in <a href="#eq:section-transfer" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:section-transfer">[eq:section-transfer]</a> has image dimension $k$, and $$\label{eq:canonical-image-lower-bound}
 \kappa(X)\geq k.$$

Suppose, for a contradiction, that $k<y$. Choose a very ample Cartier divisor $A$ on $V$ and apply Lemma <a href="#lem:mixed-positivity" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:mixed-positivity">6</a> with $$h=fp,\qquad C=(q^*D)^k(q^*A)^{d-k-1}.$$ The canonical comparison and the vanishing assertions of the lemma give <a id="eq:canonical-test-zero"></a>$$\label{eq:canonical-test-zero}
 p^*K_X\cdot C
 =\frac1r q^*D\cdot C+E\cdot C=0.$$ Pullback by $p$ preserves pseudo-effectivity: on smooth varieties it takes effective real divisors to effective real divisors and induces a continuous linear map on numerical divisor spaces. Therefore the pullback of <a href="#eq:geometric-numerical-hypothesis" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:geometric-numerical-hypothesis">[eq:geometric-numerical-hypothesis]</a>, paired with $C$, yields $$0\leq(m_0p^*K_X-h^*H)\cdot C=-h^*H\cdot C<0,$$ a contradiction. Hence $k\geq y$. Equation <a href="#eq:canonical-image-lower-bound" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:canonical-image-lower-bound">[eq:canonical-image-lower-bound]</a> and Lemma <a href="#lem:easy-addition" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:easy-addition">5</a> now give $y\leq k\leq\kappa(X)\leq y$, proving the theorem. ◻

</details>

The proof includes $k=0$, $d-k-1=0$ and relative dimension zero. Effectivity of $E$ supplies the section injection, while target-exceptionality makes its mixed pairing vanish. These are separate uses of the canonical comparison, and both are needed.

<a id="sec:good-model-interface"></a>
### The canonical good model and the main theorem

The geometric argument has used the existence of a good model of $X$ through its sections and its effective exceptional comparison. We now supply exactly that model. The following theorem is (OpenAI 2026, Corollary 11.2) <a id="pm-cite-21"></a>[[LogAbundance2026](#ref-LogAbundance2026)]; its proof belongs to that companion article.

<a id="stmt-thm-canonical-good-models"></a>
<div id="thm:canonical-good-models" class="theorem">

**Theorem 7** (Smooth canonical good models). *Let $T$ be a smooth connected projective complex variety with pseudo-effective $K_T$. Then there exist a normal projective $\mathbb Q$-factorial klt variety $V$ and a $K_T$-negative birational contraction $T\dashrightarrow V$ such that $K_V$ is $\mathbb Q$-Cartier and semiample. On a smooth projective common resolution $T\xleftarrow{p}W\xrightarrow{q}V$, compatible canonical divisors satisfy $$p^*K_T=q^*K_V+E,\qquad E\geq0,\qquad q_*E=0.$$*

</div>

Thus the input provides both the actual globally generated multiple used for sections and the exceptional equality used for intersections.

<details id="proof-stmt-thm-canonical-good-models"><summary>Proof of stmt-thm-canonical-good-models</summary>

*Proof of Theorem <a href="#thm:schnell" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:schnell">1</a>.* If $Y$ is a point, then its geometric generic fiber is $X$, and the conclusion is the hypothesis $\kappa(F)=0$. For a positive-dimensional base, a positive multiple of the ample divisor $H$ has an effective representative. Its pullback shows that $f^*H$ is pseudo-effective. Adding it to the original numerical hypothesis gives $$K_X=\frac1{m_0}\bigl((m_0K_X-f^*H)+f^*H\bigr)\in\overline{\operatorname{Eff}}(X).$$ The variety $X$ is smooth, connected, projective and complex, so Theorem <a href="#thm:canonical-good-models" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:canonical-good-models">7</a> applies. Its output is the projective good klt minimal model required by Theorem <a href="#thm:good-model-case" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:good-model-case">4</a>. Applying that theorem to the original $f$, $H$ and $m_0$ gives $\kappa(X)=\dim Y$. ◻

</details>

Throughout the proof the assumed comparison is numerical. The pluricanonical sections are obtained from the semiample model through <a href="#eq:section-transfer" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:section-transfer">[eq:section-transfer]</a>; they are not assumed as a reformulation of pseudo-effectivity.

<a id="sec:general-consequences"></a>
### Campana–Peternell and general Schnell fiber spaces

The good-model input supplies canonical nonvanishing as well as the model used in the mixed-intersection argument. These two outputs fit the reduction of (Schnell 2022, secs. 4–10) <a id="pm-cite-22"></a>[[Schnell2022](#ref-Schnell2022)].

<a id="pm-h-12"></a>
#### Nonvanishing and very general fibers

<a id="eq:canonical-nonvanishing"></a>
Let $T$ be a smooth connected projective complex variety with pseudo-effective $K_T$, and take the model $V$ and comparison divisor $E$ supplied by Theorem <a href="#thm:canonical-good-models" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:canonical-good-models">7</a>. Choose a positive multiple $rK_V$ that is Cartier and globally generated. The comparison then makes $rE$ an effective integral Cartier divisor. The section transfer in <a href="#eq:section-transfer" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:section-transfer">[eq:section-transfer]</a> gives $$0\ne H^0(V,\mathcal O_V(rK_V))
 \lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow H^0(T,\mathcal O_T(rK_T)).$$ Consequently, $$\label{eq:canonical-nonvanishing}
 K_T\in\overline{\operatorname{Eff}}(T)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \kappa(T)\geq0.$$ This is the canonical nonvanishing input used by Schnell.

We will use smooth closed complex fibers when applying <a href="#eq:canonical-nonvanishing" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:canonical-nonvanishing">[eq:canonical-nonvanishing]</a>. They have the same Kodaira dimension as the geometric generic fiber when chosen very generally. Indeed, let $f\colon X\to Y$ be a surjective morphism with connected fibers between smooth connected projective complex varieties, and write $X_{\bar\eta}$ for its geometric generic fiber. Over a nonempty open subset, $f$ is smooth; there $\omega_{X/Y}=\omega_X\otimes f^*\omega_Y^{-1}$ restricts to the canonical bundle of each fiber. For each positive integer $m$, after shrinking this open subset, formation of $f_*(\omega_{X/Y}^{\otimes m})$ commutes with base change. Outside the union of the resulting countably many proper closed subsets, a smooth fiber $F$ therefore satisfies $$h^0(F,\omega_F^{\otimes m})
 =
 h^0(X_{\bar\eta},\omega_{X_{\bar\eta}}^{\otimes m})
 \qquad\text{for every }m>0.$$ Here the dimension on the right is over the algebraic closure of $\mathbb C(Y)$. Thus the plurigenus sequences, and hence the Kodaira dimensions, agree. Such complex fibers exist because $\mathbb C$ is uncountable. In particular, the zero-Kodaira fiber condition in the reduction below is precisely the geometric generic fiber condition of Theorem <a href="#thm:schnell" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:schnell">1</a>.

We also use the restriction observation in (Schnell 2022, sec. 7) <a id="pm-cite-23"></a>[[Schnell2022](#ref-Schnell2022)]. A pseudo-effective real Cartier divisor $L$ on $X$ restricts to a pseudo-effective divisor on a very general fiber. To see this, choose effective real divisors whose numerical classes converge to $[L]$. After excluding a countable union of proper closed subsets of $Y$, a fiber is contained in none of their supports. Their restrictions are effective, and their numerical classes converge to the class of $L|_F$. Applying this to $L=m_0K_X-f^*H$ gives $$K_F\in\overline{\operatorname{Eff}}(F),\qquad \kappa(F)\geq0,$$ where the second assertion follows from <a href="#eq:canonical-nonvanishing" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:canonical-nonvanishing">[eq:canonical-nonvanishing]</a>. The very general fiber can be chosen to satisfy this restriction property and the plurigenus comparison simultaneously.

<a id="pm-h-13"></a>
#### The Campana–Peternell inequality

<details id="proof-stmt-thm-canonical-good-models-2"><summary>Proof of stmt-thm-canonical-good-models</summary>

*Proof of Corollary <a href="#cor:campana-peternell" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="cor:campana-peternell">2</a>.* Since $D$ is effective, $\kappa(D)\geq0$. Also $$K_X=\frac1{m_0}\bigl((m_0K_X-D)+D\bigr)\in\overline{\operatorname{Eff}}(X).$$ If $\kappa(D)=0$, canonical nonvanishing <a href="#eq:canonical-nonvanishing" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:canonical-nonvanishing">[eq:canonical-nonvanishing]</a> proves the assertion. We may therefore assume $\kappa(D)>0$.

<a id="eq:initial-cp-reduction"></a>
Schnell’s reduction in (Schnell 2022, secs. 4–6) <a id="pm-cite-24"></a>[[Schnell2022](#ref-Schnell2022)] first resolves a system $|nD|$ whose image has dimension $\kappa(D)$, then takes its Stein factorization and resolves the base. It produces an algebraic fiber space $f_0\colon X_0\to Y_0$ between smooth connected projective complex varieties, with $X_0$ birational to $X$, an ample Cartier divisor $H_0$ on $Y_0$, and a positive integer $a_0$ such that $$\label{eq:initial-cp-reduction}
 \dim Y_0=\kappa(D),\qquad
 a_0K_{X_0}-f_0^*H_0\in\overline{\operatorname{Eff}}(X_0).$$ The effective exceptional terms from resolving $X$ preserve pseudo-effectivity; after resolving the base, Schnell replaces the big and nef pullback of the original ample divisor by a suitable ample divisor. In particular, the output has the precise ample Cartier hypothesis of Theorem <a href="#thm:schnell" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:schnell">1</a>.

We recall the finite iteration in (Schnell 2022, sec. 7 and 9) <a id="pm-cite-25"></a>[[Schnell2022](#ref-Schnell2022)]. Suppose that $f_i\colon X_i\to Y_i$ is such a fiber space, with ample Cartier $H_i$ and $a_iK_{X_i}-f_i^*H_i\in\overline{\operatorname{Eff}}(X_i)$ for a positive integer $a_i$. For a very general smooth fiber $F_i$, the preceding restriction and nonvanishing argument gives $\kappa(F_i)\geq0$. If $\kappa(F_i)>0$, the construction in (Schnell 2022, Lemma 7.1) <a id="pm-cite-26"></a>[[Schnell2022](#ref-Schnell2022)] chooses positive integers $r_i,b_i$ and an effective Cartier divisor $L_i$ such that $$L_i\sim r_iK_{X_i}+f_i^*(b_iH_i),\qquad
 \kappa(L_i)=\kappa(F_i)+\dim Y_i.$$ The resulting comparison is $$(a_ib_i+r_i)K_{X_i}-L_i
 \sim b_i(a_iK_{X_i}-f_i^*H_i).$$ Its right side is pseudo-effective, so the same is true of its left side, since linear equivalence preserves numerical classes. Applying the divisor-to-fiber-space reduction of Sections 4–6 of Schnell’s paper to $L_i$ gives another algebraic fiber space $f_{i+1}\colon X_{i+1}\to Y_{i+1}$ of the same smooth projective complex type, with $X_{i+1}$ birational to $X_i$, an ample Cartier divisor $H_{i+1}$, and a positive integer $a_{i+1}$ such that $$\dim Y_{i+1}=\kappa(F_i)+\dim Y_i,\qquad
 a_{i+1}K_{X_{i+1}}-f_{i+1}^*H_{i+1}\in\overline{\operatorname{Eff}}(X_{i+1}).$$

As long as $\kappa(F_i)>0$, the integer $\dim Y_i$ strictly increases. It is bounded by $\dim X$, so this process reaches an index $j$ with $\kappa(F_j)=0$. The geometric generic fiber has Kodaira dimension zero by the comparison above. Theorem <a href="#thm:schnell" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:schnell">1</a> applies at this last stage. Birational invariance of Kodaira dimension and <a href="#eq:initial-cp-reduction" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:initial-cp-reduction">[eq:initial-cp-reduction]</a> give $$\kappa(X)=\kappa(X_j)=\dim Y_j
 \geq\dim Y_0=\kappa(D).$$ ◻

</details>

If $D$ is an effective rational divisor instead, choose a positive integer $q$ such that $qD$ is an integral Cartier divisor. Multiplying the numerical hypothesis by $q$ gives $qm_0K_X-qD\in\overline{\operatorname{Eff}}(X)$. Corollary <a href="#cor:campana-peternell" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="cor:campana-peternell">2</a> applied to $qD$ gives the same conclusion because $\kappa(qD)=\kappa(D)$. This extension still requires an effective divisor; it makes no assertion for an arbitrary pseudo-effective divisor in its place.

<a id="pm-h-14"></a>
#### Equality and eventual effectivity for general fibers

<details id="proof-stmt-thm-canonical-good-models-3"><summary>Proof of stmt-thm-canonical-good-models</summary>

*Proof of Corollary <a href="#cor:schnell-general" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="cor:schnell-general">3</a>.* If $Y$ is a point, then $F=X$ and $H$ is linearly equivalent to zero. The equality is tautological, and the numerical hypothesis gives pseudo-effective $K_X$. A nonzero section supplied by <a href="#eq:canonical-nonvanishing" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:canonical-nonvanishing">[eq:canonical-nonvanishing]</a>, together with its powers, gives the asserted sections.

Assume $\dim Y>0$. The restriction argument above gives $\kappa(F)\geq0$. As in (Schnell 2022, Lemma 7.1) <a id="pm-cite-27"></a>[[Schnell2022](#ref-Schnell2022)], choose positive integers $a,b$ and an effective Cartier divisor $L$ with $$L\sim aK_X+f^*(bH),\qquad \kappa(L)=\kappa(F)+\dim Y.$$ The linear equivalence $$(m_0b+a)K_X-L\sim b(m_0K_X-f^*H)$$ shows that its left side is pseudo-effective. Corollary <a href="#cor:campana-peternell" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="cor:campana-peternell">2</a>, applied to the effective Cartier divisor $L$, gives $$\kappa(X)\geq\kappa(L)=\kappa(F)+\dim Y.$$ The reverse inequality is the easy-addition inequality used in (Schnell 2022, Lemma 7.1) <a id="pm-cite-28"></a>[[Schnell2022](#ref-Schnell2022)]. This proves the equality.

To obtain sections after subtracting the original $f^*H$, we use the Fujita–Mori criterion recalled in (Popa and Schnell 2014, Lemma 4.6) <a id="pm-cite-29"></a>[[PopaSchnell2014](#ref-PopaSchnell2014)]; this is the criterion behind (Schnell 2022, sec. 8) <a id="pm-cite-30"></a>[[Schnell2022](#ref-Schnell2022)]. Since $\kappa(F)\geq0$, the equality just proved implies that there are a big Cartier divisor $B$ on $Y$ and a positive integer $u$ with a nonzero section $$\sigma\in H^0\bigl(X,\mathcal O_X(uK_X-f^*B)\bigr).$$ Bigness of $B$ gives a positive integer $v$ and a nonzero section $\tau\in H^0(Y,\mathcal O_Y(vB-H))$. Thus, with $r=uv$, $$s=\sigma^v f^*\tau
 \in H^0\bigl(X,\mathcal O_X(rK_X-f^*H)\bigr)$$ is nonzero. This step replaces the big divisor supplied by the criterion with the ample divisor in the original hypothesis.

Finally, ampleness of $H$ supplies a nonzero section $\tau_\ell\in H^0(Y,\mathcal O_Y((\ell-1)H))$ for every sufficiently large integer $\ell$. The products $$s^\ell f^*\tau_\ell
 \in H^0\bigl(X,\mathcal O_X(\ell rK_X-f^*H)\bigr)$$ are nonzero. Choosing $\ell_0$ beyond this ampleness threshold proves the asserted conclusion for every integer $\ell\geq\ell_0$. ◻

</details>
