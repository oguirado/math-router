## 1. Abstract and result contract

> We prove the Friedgut–Kalai sharp-threshold conjecture. For every integer <i>n</i> ≥ 2, every nontrivial increasing family of graphs on <i>n</i> vertices invariant under all vertex permutations, and every $`0\lt \varepsilon\lt 1/2`$, the edge probabilities at which its probability equals <i>ε</i> and $`1-\varepsilon`$ differ by at most $`C\log(1/(2\varepsilon))/(\log n)^2`$, for a universal constant <i>C</i>.

- **claim-1** (theorem, [source](#stmt-thm-threshold)): For every integer $n\ge2$, every nontrivial increasing family $\mathcal P$ of graphs on $n$ vertices invariant under all vertex permutations, and every $0<\varepsilon<1/2$, $$\label{eq:threshold} p_{1-\varepsilon}(\mathcal P)-p_\varepsilon(\mathcal P) \le \frac{2^{19}}{(\log n)^2}\log\frac1{2\varepsilon}.$$
  - Explanation: Bounds the quantile transition width of every nontrivial increasing vertex-symmetric graph property. It does not locate the transition.
- **claim-2** (theorem, [source](#stmt-thm-variance)): For every integer $n\ge2$, every Boolean function $f$ on $E_n$ invariant under vertex permutations, and every $0<p<1$, $$\label{eq:variance} \mathop{\mathrm{Var}}_p(f)\le\frac{2^{17}}{(\log n)^2}I_p(f).$$ No monotonicity assumption is needed.
  - Explanation: Controls the variance of a vertex-symmetric Boolean graph function uniformly over the interior Bernoulli biases; monotonicity is unnecessary.
- **claim-3** (bound, [source](#stmt-lem-low-degree)): Let $m\ge2$ be real and let $h:\{0,1\}^J\to\{0,1\}$ satisfy $I_i(h)\le I(h)/m$ for every $i\in J$. If $$k=\frac{\sigma\log m}{64},$$ then $$\label{eq:low-degree} \sum_{1\le \|S\|\le k}\widehat h(S)^2\le m^{-1/4}I(h).$$ Every degree cutoff is interpreted literally for integer degrees $\|S\|$; in particular, the sum is empty when $k<1$.
  - Explanation: Bounds low-degree Fourier mass when each individual pivotal influence is small relative to the total. Degrees are integers even though the cutoff is real.
- **claim-4** (bound, [source](#stmt-lem-restriction)): Let $F\subseteq E_n$, put $H=E_n\setminus F$, and, for $y\in\{0,1\}^{H}$, let $f_y(x)=f(x,y)$ for $x\in\{0,1\}^{F}$. If $y$ has the Bernoulli-$p$ product law, then, for every real $k>0$, $$\begin{aligned} \mathbb E_y\sum_{\substack{A\subseteq F\\1\le \|A\|\le k}} \widehat{f_y}(A)^2 &=\sum_{\substack{S\subseteq E_n\\1\le\|S\cap F\|\le k}} \widehat f(S)^2, \label{eq:restriction-mass}\\ \mathbb E_y I(f_y)&=\sum_{e\in F}I_e(f). \label{eq:restriction-influence} \end{aligned}$$
  - Explanation: Exactly averages conditional Fourier mass and pivotal influence over independently sampled exterior coordinates, without imposing an exterior-degree cutoff.
- **claim-5** (bound, [source](#stmt-prop-block)): Let $3\le m\le n$ be an integer, let $B$ be uniform among the $m$-element subsets of $[n]$, and set $$F_B=\{e\in E_n:e\cap B\ne\varnothing\}, \qquad k=\frac{\sigma\log m}{64}.$$ Then $$\sum_{S\subseteq E_n} \Pr_B\{1\le \|S\cap F_B\|\le k\}\widehat f(S)^2 \le \frac{2m}{n}\,m^{-1/4}I(f). \label{eq:block-average}$$
  - Explanation: Uses the surviving block permutations to control the average restricted low-degree mass; the free coordinates include every edge touching the block.
- **claim-6** (bound, [source](#stmt-lem-block-capture)): Suppose $t=\log n\ge16$, and put $$m=\lfloor\sqrt n\rfloor, \qquad k=\frac{\sigma\log m}{64}.$$ Then $$\sum_{\substack{S\subseteq E_n\\1\le \|S\|\le k^2/2}} \widehat f(S)^2 \le 4m^{-1/4}I(f). \label{eq:captured-mass}$$
  - Explanation: Turns conditional degree control into original Fourier control through a random block that meets a support at one nonisolated vertex.

## 2. Definitions, validity and bounds

- **Vertex set and complete simple edge coordinates** ([source](#sec:introduction)): For an integer $n\ge2$, write $[n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}$ and let $E_n=\binom{[n]}2$ be the edge set of the complete simple graph on $n$ vertices.
- **Graph encoding** ([source](#sec:introduction)): We identify a graph with an element of $\{0,1\}^{E_n}$.
- **Graph property** ([source](#sec:introduction)): A *graph property* is a family $\mathcal P\subseteq\{0,1\}^{E_n}$ invariant under every permutation of $[n]$.
- **Increasing and nontrivial families** ([source](#sec:introduction)): It is *increasing*, or *monotone*, if adding edges preserves membership, and *nontrivial* if it is neither empty nor the whole space.
- **Product graph measure** ([source](#sec:introduction)): Write $\mu_p(\mathcal P)$ for the probability that a graph whose edges are present independently with probability $p$ belongs to $\mathcal P$.
- **Quantile location** ([source](#sec:introduction)): For $0<a<1$, define $$p_a(\mathcal P)=\inf\{p\in[0,1]:\mu_p(\mathcal P)\ge a\}.$$
- **Pivotal and total influence** ([source](#sec:introduction)): For a Boolean function $f:\{0,1\}^{E_n}\to\{0,1\}$, define its *pivotal influence* at edge $e$ and its *total influence* as follows. Here $x_{e\leftarrow b}$ denotes $x$ with coordinate $e$ set to $b\in\{0,1\}$, and the probability is over all coordinates other than $e$. $$I_{e,p}(f)=\Pr_p\bigl(f(x_{e\leftarrow1})\ne f(x_{e\leftarrow0})\bigr), \qquad I_p(f)=\sum_{e\in E_n}I_{e,p}(f).$$
- **Product Fourier characters** ([source](#eq:parseval)): Define $$\chi_i(x)=\frac{x_i-p}{\sigma},\qquad \chi_S(x)=\prod_{i\in S}\chi_i(x),\qquad \chi_\varnothing=1.$$
- **Finite product and bias scale** ([source](#eq:parseval)): Let $J$ be a finite coordinate set, let $0<p<1$, and put $\sigma=\sqrt{p(1-p)}$.

- **Low-degree orthogonal projection** ([source](#eq:projection-norm)): For any real $d\ge0$, let $P_{\le d}$ be the orthogonal projection onto the span of the characters of degree at most $d$.
- **Restricted Boolean function** ([source](#stmt-lem-restriction)): Let $F\subseteq E_n$, put $H=E_n\setminus F$, and, for $y\in\{0,1\}^{H}$, let $f_y(x)=f(x,y)$ for $x\in\{0,1\}^{F}$.
- **Median threshold** ([source](#sec:threshold)): The same log-odds estimate describes the transition around its median $p_*=p_{1/2}$.
- **Block-incident free edge set and conditional cutoff** ([source](#stmt-prop-block)): Let $3\le m\le n$ be an integer, let $B$ be uniform among the $m$-element subsets of $[n]$, and set $$F_B=\{e\in E_n:e\cap B\ne\varnothing\}, \qquad k=\frac{\sigma\log m}{64}.$$
- **Block size and original-degree cutoff parameter** ([source](#stmt-lem-block-capture)): Suppose $t=\log n\ge16$, and put $$m=\lfloor\sqrt n\rfloor, \qquad k=\frac{\sigma\log m}{64}.$$

## 4. Engineering Triptych

### Panel 1 - epistemic contract

- **I-threshold**: The stated quantile-width bound applies to nontrivial increasing vertex-permutation-invariant graph families.
- **I-restriction-influence**: The expected conditional total pivotal influence equals the sum of the original pivotal influences over the free set.
- **I-variance**: The variance-influence bound also covers nonmonotone vertex-symmetric Boolean functions.

### Panel 2 - negative knowledge

- **A1**: Infer threshold location from a width estimate. -> A narrow transition does not identify its median or any quantile numerically.
- **A2**: Use threshold width for labeled or nonmonotone dependencies. -> The required graph-family invariance or monotonicity may fail.
- **A3**: Replace the exterior product law with correlated or unequal-bias states. -> Exact conditional weights and influence averaging in the supplied implementation no longer match the source model.
- **A4**: Treat this finite self-test as proof of the universal squared-logarithm bound. -> Exhaustive admitted small instances cannot certify arbitrary vertex counts or real biases.

## 6. Applicability limits and search

- **L-simple-graph** (hypothesis): The edge coordinates encode simple undirected graphs on a fixed vertex set. Transfers to directed, weighted, typed or multigraph models require a separately justified reduction. Blocked use: ['Direct application to directed dependencies or heterogeneous weighted links.']
- **L-product-measure** (hypothesis): The probability model has independent edge states with one common edge-presence probability. Correlated or unequal-bias edges are outside the stated model. Blocked use: ['Applying the bound to correlated failures or edge-specific probabilities.']
- **L-symmetry** (hypothesis): Membership must be preserved by every permutation of the vertex set. Coordinate transitivity alone does not supply the strengthened bound stated here. Blocked use: ['Direct application to label-dependent roles or fixed physical layouts.']
- **L-monotone-nontrivial** (hypothesis): The quantile-width theorem requires both monotonicity and nontriviality. These conditions are not required together by the variance theorem. Blocked use: ['Quantile-width inference for nonmonotone or constant predicates.']
- **L-size-log** (hypothesis): The scale parameter counts vertices and the logarithms are natural. Edge-coordinate counts cannot be substituted for the vertex count without changing the formula. Blocked use: ['Substituting edges, transactions or features for the vertex count.']
- **L-quantile-regime** (edge_case): The threshold theorem is restricted to the vertex-count and interior quantile regime in its statement. Endpoint quantiles and a singleton vertex set are not supplied by that statement. Blocked use: ['Using the displayed logarithmic bound outside its quantified regime.']
- **L-variance-distinction** (hypothesis): The variance theorem allows nonmonotone Boolean functions, but still requires vertex symmetry and an interior common bias. This does not make the monotone threshold theorem valid for nonmonotone predicates. Blocked use: ['Rejecting every nonmonotone variance application; transferring a variance bound into monotone quantile conclusions without justification.']
- **L-low-degree-precondition** (hypothesis): The low-degree lemma requires each individual influence to satisfy its stated relative bound; the cutoff is not an arbitrary tuning parameter. Blocked use: ['Applying the low-degree estimate without the individual-influence condition.']
- **L-literal-cutoff** (edge_case): Fourier degrees are integers while the cutoff is real. An empty low-degree sum yields no positive-degree capture information. Blocked use: ['Rounding the cutoff upward or claiming nonempty captured levels when the sum is empty.']
- **L-block-orbits** (hypothesis): The block proposition has a minimum block-size hypothesis ensuring every free-edge orbit has sufficient size. Blocked use: ['Applying its orbit-based individual-influence argument to smaller blocks.']
- **L-all-incident** (hypothesis): The free set includes both internal and boundary edges of the chosen block. Keeping only boundary edges changes the restriction being proved. Blocked use: ['Replacing the free set with only cross-block edges.']
- **L-capture-regime** (edge_case): The capture lemma has a large-logarithm regime. The paper handles the remaining vertex counts separately in the variance proof. Blocked use: ['Using the capture-lemma probability estimate below its stated regime.']
- **L-supportwise** (counterexample_scope): The detection bound is applied separately to each Fourier support; it does not assert one block captures every support at once. A captured support can retain more than one edge. Blocked use: ['Interpreting the argument as simultaneous capture or exactly one surviving support edge.']
- **L-restriction-exterior** (hypothesis): The restriction averaging retains contributions from exterior Fourier coordinates. Exterior Fourier degree is unrestricted. Blocked use: ['Dropping coefficients merely because their exterior degree is positive.']
- **L-location** (counterexample_scope): The result bounds transition width rather than supplying a property-specific threshold location, recognition algorithm or efficient quantile evaluator. Those algorithms are ABSENT from the provided source. Blocked use: ['Treating a universal width bound as a numerical threshold locator or a complexity guarantee.']
- **L-finite-verification** (formal_scope): A finite restriction-identity checker can certify its declared finite inputs only. A universal formal proof artifact for the main theorem is ABSENT from the provided source; finite examples cannot supply it. Blocked use: ['Treating finite exact tests, sampling or solver witnesses as a universal theorem certificate.']

For every integer <i>n</i> ≥ 2, every nontrivial increasing family of graphs on <i>n</i> vertices invariant under all vertex permutations, and every $`0\lt \varepsilon\lt 1/2`$, the edge probabilities at which its probability equals <i>ε</i> and $`1-\varepsilon`$ differ by at most $`C\log(1/(2\varepsilon))/(\log n)^2`$, for a universal constant <i>C</i>.

<a id="sec:introduction"></a>
### Introduction

For an integer $n\ge2$, write $[n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}$ and let $E_n=\binom{[n]}2$ be the edge set of the complete simple graph on $n$ vertices. We identify a graph with an element of $\{0,1\}^{E_n}$. A *graph property* is a family $\mathcal P\subseteq\{0,1\}^{E_n}$ invariant under every permutation of $[n]$. It is *increasing*, or *monotone*, if adding edges preserves membership, and *nontrivial* if it is neither empty nor the whole space.

Write $\mu_p(\mathcal P)$ for the probability that a graph whose edges are present independently with probability $p$ belongs to $\mathcal P$. For $0<a<1$, define $$p_a(\mathcal P)=\inf\{p\in[0,1]:\mu_p(\mathcal P)\ge a\}.$$ All logarithms in this paper are natural. In particular, $n$ always counts vertices, not edge coordinates.

<a id="stmt-thm-threshold"></a>
<div id="thm:threshold" class="theorem">

<a id="eq:threshold"></a>
**Theorem 1** (Friedgut–Kalai sharp-threshold conjecture). *For every integer $n\ge2$, every nontrivial increasing family $\mathcal P$ of graphs on $n$ vertices invariant under all vertex permutations, and every $0<\varepsilon<1/2$, $$\label{eq:threshold}
 p_{1-\varepsilon}(\mathcal P)-p_\varepsilon(\mathcal P)
 \le \frac{2^{19}}{(\log n)^2}\log\frac1{2\varepsilon}.$$*

</div>

The influence theorem of Kahn, Kalai, and Linial <a id="pm-cite-1"></a>[[KKL88](#ref-KKL88)] connected Fourier analysis with the sensitivity of Boolean functions to individual coordinates. Bourgain, Kahn, Kalai, Katznelson, and Linial extended the influence theorem to product spaces <a id="pm-cite-2"></a>[[BKKKL92](#ref-BKKKL92)]. Friedgut and Kalai combined that extension with the Margulis–Russo probability-derivative identity to show that every increasing family invariant under a transitive coordinate group has a narrow transition <a id="pm-cite-3"></a>[[FK96](#ref-FK96), Section 2]. For graph properties, vertex permutations act transitively on the edge coordinates, and their theorem gives the universal width bound $C\log(1/(2\varepsilon))/\log n$ <a id="pm-cite-4"></a>[[FK96](#ref-FK96), Theorem 1.1]. They conjectured the denominator $(\log n)^2$ with the same dependence on $\varepsilon$ <a id="pm-cite-5"></a>[[FK96](#ref-FK96), Conjecture 1.2]. Theorem <a href="#thm:threshold" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:threshold">1</a> resolves that conjecture positively; its numerical constant is only a convenient explicit choice.

The squared logarithm is the optimal order in $n$ when $\varepsilon$ is fixed. Friedgut and Kalai already pointed to the property of containing a clique with order proportional to $\log n$: its transition width can be of order $(\log n)^{-2}$ <a id="pm-cite-6"></a>[[FK96](#ref-FK96), p. 2994]. Bourgain and Kalai went beyond coordinate transitivity by using the action of the symmetry group on sets of coordinates <a id="pm-cite-7"></a>[[BK97](#ref-BK97)]. For every fixed $\eta>0$ and $0<\varepsilon<1/2$, their graph-property bound is $$p_{1-\varepsilon}(\mathcal P)-p_\varepsilon(\mathcal P)
 \le C_{\eta,\varepsilon}(\log n)^{-2+\eta}.$$ Thus their result approached the conjectured exponent arbitrarily closely. Further progress through Fourier estimates is described below; Friedgut gives a broader account of the influence method in <a id="pm-cite-8"></a>[[Friedgut22](#ref-Friedgut22)].

Two companion papers address complementary threshold-location questions: comparison of integral and fractional covers of increasing families <a id="pm-cite-9"></a>[[ExpectationEquivalence2026](#ref-ExpectationEquivalence2026), Theorem 1.1], and comparison of graph-containment thresholds with expected subgraph counts <a id="pm-cite-10"></a>[[SecondKahnKalai2026](#ref-SecondKahnKalai2026), Theorem 1.1]. The present theorem controls the width of a transition for arbitrary increasing graph properties. Its proof does not use either comparison.

Here is the stronger estimate that drives the proof. For a Boolean function $f:\{0,1\}^{E_n}\to\{0,1\}$, define its *pivotal influence* at edge $e$ and its *total influence* as follows. Here $x_{e\leftarrow b}$ denotes $x$ with coordinate $e$ set to $b\in\{0,1\}$, and the probability is over all coordinates other than $e$. $$I_{e,p}(f)=\Pr_p\bigl(f(x_{e\leftarrow1})\ne f(x_{e\leftarrow0})\bigr),
 \qquad I_p(f)=\sum_{e\in E_n}I_{e,p}(f).$$

<a id="stmt-thm-variance"></a>
<div id="thm:variance" class="theorem">

<a id="eq:variance"></a>
**Theorem 2** (Variance and influence). *For every integer $n\ge2$, every Boolean function $f$ on $E_n$ invariant under vertex permutations, and every $0<p<1$, $$\label{eq:variance}
 \mathop{\mathrm{Var}}_p(f)\le\frac{2^{17}}{(\log n)^2}I_p(f).$$ No monotonicity assumption is needed.*

</div>

At the unbiased measure, Kelman, Kindler, Lifshitz, Minzer, and Safra proved the graph-symmetric estimate $$I_{1/2}(f)\ge
 c\,\frac{(\log n)^2}{(\log\log n)^2}\mathop{\mathrm{Var}}_{1/2}(f)$$ for all sufficiently large $n$ and an absolute $c>0$ <a id="pm-cite-11"></a>[[KKLMS20](#ref-KKLMS20), arXiv version 2, Section 7.4]. This is an influence estimate at $p=1/2$; a threshold-width bound requires control throughout the transition interval. Theorem <a href="#thm:variance" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:variance">2</a> removes the iterated-logarithm loss and holds uniformly for every $0<p<1$, including for nonmonotone properties.

Random restrictions and symmetry of Fourier coefficients are central to the arguments of Bourgain–Kalai and Kelman–Kindler–Lifshitz–Minzer–Safra <a id="pm-cite-12"></a>[[BK97](#ref-BK97), [KKLMS20](#ref-KKLMS20)]. Here the restriction is chosen through the vertices: leave free all edges touching a random block $B$ of about $\sqrt n$ vertices, while conditioning on every other edge. Permutations inside $B$ survive every such conditioning. Their edge orbits bound each conditional influence, to which an elementary Fourier estimate applies. The resulting estimate is linear in total influence, so it can be averaged over the conditioned coordinates. Section <a href="#sec:fourier" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:fourier">2</a> proves this estimate directly, including its dependence on the bias.

The second ingredient regards each edge set indexing a Fourier coefficient as a graph. Any nonempty such graph with at most $k^2/2$ edges has a nonisolated vertex of degree at most $k$. With sufficient probability, $B$ meets its nonisolated vertices only at that vertex. The restriction then leaves between one and $k$ edges of this graph free. This turns control of conditional Fourier degrees up to $k$ into control of original degrees up to $k^2/2$. Taking $k$ proportional to $\sqrt{p(1-p)}\log n$ yields <a href="#eq:variance" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:variance">[eq:variance]</a>; the bias factor cancels in the estimate for the remaining Fourier degrees. Section <a href="#sec:threshold" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:threshold">4</a> then integrates the probability derivative to obtain the full quantile bound.

<a id="sec:fourier"></a>
### A low-degree estimate on biased products

We bound the mass of low-degree Fourier coefficients for Boolean functions whose individual influences are small compared with their total influence. The bound is linear in total influence, so it can be averaged over the conditioned coordinates in Section <a href="#sec:graph" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:graph">3</a>.

<a id="eq:parseval"></a>
Let $J$ be a finite coordinate set, let $0<p<1$, and put $\sigma=\sqrt{p(1-p)}$. Expectations and norms in this section are with respect to the product Bernoulli-$p$ measure on the indicated coordinates; in particular, $\|g\|_q=(\mathbb E|g|^q)^{1/q}$. Define $$\chi_i(x)=\frac{x_i-p}{\sigma},\qquad
  \chi_S(x)=\prod_{i\in S}\chi_i(x),\qquad \chi_\varnothing=1.$$ Each $\chi_i$ has mean zero and second moment one. Independence therefore makes the $2^{|J|}$ functions $\chi_S$, $S\subseteq J$, orthonormal. They form a basis of the $2^{|J|}$-dimensional space of real functions on $\{0,1\}^J$. Thus, with $\widehat g(S)=\mathbb E[g\chi_S]$, $$\label{eq:parseval}
  g=\sum_{S\subseteq J}\widehat g(S)\chi_S,\qquad
  \mathbb Eg^2=\sum_{S\subseteq J}\widehat g(S)^2,\qquad
  \mathop{\mathrm{Var}}(g)=\sum_{\varnothing\ne S\subseteq J}\widehat g(S)^2.$$

<a id="eq:dirichlet"></a>
For $i\in J$, write $\Delta_i g(x)=g(x_{J\setminus\{i\}},1)-g(x_{J\setminus\{i\}},0)$, a function on the remaining coordinates. Integrating just coordinate $i$ gives $$\mathbb E_i[g\chi_i]
  =\frac{p(1-p)}{\sigma}
    \bigl(g(x_{J\setminus\{i\}},1)-g(x_{J\setminus\{i\}},0)\bigr)
  =\sigma\Delta_i g.$$ Consequently, for $A\subseteq J\setminus\{i\}$, $\widehat g(A\cup\{i\})=\sigma\widehat{\Delta_i g}(A)$, where the latter Fourier coefficient is taken on $J\setminus\{i\}$. For Boolean $h:\{0,1\}^J\to\{0,1\}$, set $$I_i(h)=\Pr(\Delta_i h\ne0),\qquad I(h)=\sum_{i\in J}I_i(h).$$ Since $\Delta_i h\in\{-1,0,1\}$, Parseval on the remaining coordinates and then summation over $i$ give $$\label{eq:dirichlet}
  \sum_{S\ni i}\widehat h(S)^2=\sigma^2 I_i(h),\qquad
  \sum_{S\subseteq J}|S|\widehat h(S)^2=\sigma^2 I(h).$$ These identities do not require monotonicity.

The next estimate follows the hypercontractive influence method of Kahn, Kalai, and Linial <a id="pm-cite-13"></a>[[KKL88](#ref-KKL88)]. We prove the biased two-point inequality directly and use the standard product argument of Bonami <a id="pm-cite-14"></a>[[Bonami1970](#ref-Bonami1970), Chapter III, Lemma 1], keeping the dependence on the edge probability explicit.

<div class="samepage">

<a id="stmt-lem-low-degree"></a>
<div id="lem:low-degree" class="lemma">

<a id="eq:low-degree"></a>
**Lemma 3**. *Let $m\ge2$ be real and let $h:\{0,1\}^J\to\{0,1\}$ satisfy $I_i(h)\le I(h)/m$ for every $i\in J$. If $$k=\frac{\sigma\log m}{64},$$ then $$\label{eq:low-degree}
  \sum_{1\le |S|\le k}\widehat h(S)^2\le m^{-1/4}I(h).$$ Every degree cutoff is interpreted literally for integer degrees $|S|$; in particular, the sum is empty when $k<1$.*

</div>

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-low-degree"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-low-degree</summary>

*Proof.* We first prove a product norm bound. Put $\rho=\sigma/4$, so $0<\rho\le1/8$, and define the linear operator $T_\rho$ by $T_\rho\chi_S=\rho^{|S|}\chi_S$. For one coordinate, the bound $|\chi_i|\le\sigma^{-1}$ and the second moment identity imply $$|\mathbb E\chi_i^3|\le\sigma^{-1},\qquad
  \mathbb E\chi_i^4\le\sigma^{-2}.$$ For real $a,b$, expansion and $2|ab^3|\le a^2b^2+b^4$ yield $$\begin{aligned}
  \mathbb E(a+\rho b\chi_i)^4
  &\le a^4+6\rho^2a^2b^2
       +\frac{4\rho^3}{\sigma}|ab^3|
       +\frac{\rho^4}{\sigma^2}b^4\\
  &\le a^4+\frac{13\sigma^2}{32}a^2b^2
       +\frac{9\sigma^2}{256}b^4
   \le(a^2+b^2)^2,
\end{aligned}$$ where the last inequality uses $\sigma^2\le1/4$.

<a id="eq:noise-norm"></a>
To tensorize this inequality, induct on $|J|$, the empty product being immediate. Write $g=a+b\chi_i$ with $a,b$ functions of the other coordinates, and set $A=T_\rho a$, $B=T_\rho b$, using the operator on those coordinates. Apply the scalar inequality pointwise and then the triangle inequality in $L^2$ to obtain $$\|T_\rho g\|_4^2
  \le \|A^2+B^2\|_2
  \le \|A\|_4^2+\|B\|_4^2
  \le \|a\|_2^2+\|b\|_2^2
  =\|g\|_2^2.$$ The penultimate inequality is the induction hypothesis. We have proved $$\label{eq:noise-norm}
  \|T_\rho g\|_4\le\|g\|_2$$ on every finite product.

<a id="eq:projection-norm"></a>
For any real $d\ge0$, let $P_{\le d}$ be the orthogonal projection onto the span of the characters of degree at most $d$. If $u=P_{\le d}u$, the operator $T_\rho$ is invertible and Parseval gives $$\|T_\rho^{-1}u\|_2^2
  =\sum_{|S|\le d}\rho^{-2|S|}\widehat u(S)^2
  \le\rho^{-2d}\|u\|_2^2.$$ Applying <a href="#eq:noise-norm" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:noise-norm">[eq:noise-norm]</a> to $T_\rho^{-1}u$ proves $\|u\|_4\le\rho^{-d}\|u\|_2$. Duality in this finite-dimensional subspace, followed by Hölder’s inequality, now gives for every real function $v$ $$\label{eq:projection-norm}
  \|P_{\le d}v\|_2
  =\sup_{\substack{u=P_{\le d}u\\\|u\|_2=1}}|\mathbb E[vu]|
  \le\rho^{-d}\|v\|_{4/3}.$$

If $k<1$, <a href="#eq:low-degree" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:low-degree">[eq:low-degree]</a> is immediate, so assume $k\ge1$. Since $|\Delta_i h|$ is the indicator of a pivotal event, $\|\Delta_i h\|_{4/3}=I_i(h)^{3/4}$. Every nonempty Fourier set is counted at least once when summing over its coordinates. The derivative coefficient identity and <a href="#eq:projection-norm" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:projection-norm">[eq:projection-norm]</a>, applied on $J\setminus\{i\}$ with $d=k$, therefore show $$\begin{aligned}
  \sum_{1\le |S|\le k}\widehat h(S)^2
  &\le\sum_{i\in J}\sum_{\substack{S\ni i\\|S|\le k}}
       \widehat h(S)^2\\
  &=\sigma^2\sum_{i\in J}
       \sum_{\substack{A\subseteq J\setminus\{i\}\\|A|\le k-1}}
       \widehat{\Delta_i h}(A)^2\\
  &\le\sigma^2\rho^{-2k}\sum_{i\in J}I_i(h)^{3/2}.
\end{aligned}$$ The function $s\mapsto s\log(4/s)$ is increasing on $(0,1/2]$, since its derivative is $\log(4/s)-1>0$. Hence $\sigma\log(4/\sigma)\le\tfrac12\log8<2$, and $$\rho^{-2k}
  =m^{\sigma\log(4/\sigma)/32}\le m^{1/16}.$$ If $I(h)\le m^{1/4}$, the hypothesis implies $$\sum_i I_i(h)^{3/2}
  \le\sqrt{\frac{I(h)}m}\,I(h)
  \le m^{-3/8}I(h).$$ The preceding estimates bound the desired sum by $\sigma^2m^{-5/16}I(h)\le m^{-1/4}I(h)$, including when $I(h)=0$. If $I(h)>m^{1/4}$, Parseval and Booleanity instead give $$\sum_{1\le |S|\le k}\widehat h(S)^2
  \le\mathbb Eh^2\le1<m^{-1/4}I(h).$$ This proves the estimate for every $0<p<1$, without a lower bound on $\sigma$. ◻

</details>

<a id="sec:graph"></a>
### Restricting to the edges incident with a vertex block

Let $E_n=\binom{[n]}2$ be the edge set of the complete graph on $[n]$, and let $f\colon\{0,1\}^{E_n}\to\{0,1\}$ be invariant under vertex permutations. Fix $p\in(0,1)$ and put $\sigma=\sqrt{p(1-p)}$. All Fourier coefficients, variances, and influences in this section use this bias. Monotonicity is not needed.

The purpose of a vertex block is to connect two different notions of degree. A restriction to the edges incident with the block retains enough symmetry to apply Lemma <a href="#lem:low-degree" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:low-degree">3</a>. If the block meets the nonisolated vertices of a Fourier support at just one vertex $u$, then the surviving Fourier degree is the graph degree of $u$. An $s$-edge graph with $s\ge1$ has a nonisolated vertex of degree at most $\sqrt{2s}$. Thus a bound for conditional Fourier degrees up to $k$ will control original degrees up to $k^2/2$.

The restriction identity is the biased-product form of the standard Fourier restriction identity; compare <a id="pm-cite-15"></a>[[KKLMS20](#ref-KKLMS20), arXiv version 2, Lemma 2.7]. We include its short proof. The next identities hold for every Boolean $f$, without a symmetry assumption.

<a id="stmt-lem-restriction"></a>
<div id="lem:restriction" class="lemma">

<a id="eq:restriction-mass"></a>
<a id="eq:restriction-influence"></a>
**Lemma 4** (Exact averaging over a restriction). *Let $F\subseteq E_n$, put $H=E_n\setminus F$, and, for $y\in\{0,1\}^{H}$, let $f_y(x)=f(x,y)$ for $x\in\{0,1\}^{F}$. If $y$ has the Bernoulli-$p$ product law, then, for every real $k>0$, $$\begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_y\sum_{\substack{A\subseteq F\\1\le |A|\le k}}
        \widehat{f_y}(A)^2
 &=\sum_{\substack{S\subseteq E_n\\1\le|S\cap F|\le k}}
        \widehat f(S)^2,
 \label{eq:restriction-mass}\\
 \mathbb E_y I(f_y)&=\sum_{e\in F}I_e(f).
 \label{eq:restriction-influence}
\end{aligned}$$*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-restriction"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-restriction</summary>

*Proof.* Expand $f$ in the product Fourier basis and integrate over $x$. For each $A\subseteq F$ this gives the exact finite identity $$\widehat{f_y}(A)
 =\sum_{D\subseteq H}\widehat f(A\cup D)\chi_D(y).$$ Orthonormality on the exterior coordinates therefore gives $\mathbb E_y\widehat{f_y}(A)^2
=\sum_{D\subseteq H}\widehat f(A\cup D)^2$. Summing over $A$ proves <a href="#eq:restriction-mass" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:restriction-mass">[eq:restriction-mass]</a>. In particular, there is no constraint on the exterior Fourier degree $|D|$.

For $e\in F$, the pivotal event for $f_y$ at $e$ is the pivotal event for $f$ at $e$ with the exterior coordinates fixed to $y$. Averaging over $y$ and the remaining free coordinates gives $\mathbb E_y I_e(f_y)=I_e(f)$; summation proves <a href="#eq:restriction-influence" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:restriction-influence">[eq:restriction-influence]</a>. ◻

</details>

<a id="stmt-prop-block"></a>
<div id="prop:block" class="proposition">

<a id="eq:block-average"></a>
**Proposition 5** (The block estimate). *Let $3\le m\le n$ be an integer, let $B$ be uniform among the $m$-element subsets of $[n]$, and set $$F_B=\{e\in E_n:e\cap B\ne\varnothing\},
 \qquad k=\frac{\sigma\log m}{64}.$$ Then $$\sum_{S\subseteq E_n}
 \Pr_B\{1\le |S\cap F_B|\le k\}\widehat f(S)^2
 \le \frac{2m}{n}\,m^{-1/4}I(f).
 \label{eq:block-average}$$*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-prop-block"><summary>Proof of stmt-prop-block</summary>

*Proof.* Fix $B$, and fix an arbitrary assignment $y$ to $H_B=E_n\setminus F_B$. The free set $F_B$ contains *all* edges with at least one endpoint in $B$: both edges within $B$ and edges from $B$ to its complement. Every permutation supported on $B$ fixes each edge of $H_B$ individually, and hence fixes $y$. The restricted function $f_y$ is consequently invariant under this permutation group even if the graph encoded by $y$ has no symmetry.

The free-edge orbits are precisely the $\binom m2$ edges within $B$ and, for each *fixed* $w\in[n]\setminus B$, the $m$ edges $\{\{u,w\}:u\in B\}$. Every orbit has size at least $m$, since $m\ge3$. The product law and the pivotal events are preserved by these permutations, so influences agree within each orbit. It follows that $$I_e(f_y)\le\frac{I(f_y)}m\qquad(e\in F_B).$$ Lemma <a href="#lem:low-degree" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:low-degree">3</a> applies separately to every $y$. Its right-hand side is linear in $I(f_y)$, so averaging and using Lemma <a href="#lem:restriction" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:restriction">4</a> gives $$\sum_{\substack{S\subseteq E_n\\1\le |S\cap F_B|\le k}}
       \widehat f(S)^2
 \le m^{-1/4}\sum_{e\in F_B}I_e(f).$$ Finally average over $B$. Each endpoint of a fixed edge belongs to $B$ with probability $m/n$, whence $\Pr_B(e\in F_B)\le2m/n$. This proves <a href="#eq:block-average" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:block-average">[eq:block-average]</a>. ◻

</details>

<a id="stmt-lem-block-capture"></a>
<div id="lem:block-capture" class="lemma">

<a id="eq:captured-mass"></a>
**Lemma 6** (Capturing low Fourier levels). *Suppose $t=\log n\ge16$, and put $$m=\lfloor\sqrt n\rfloor,
 \qquad k=\frac{\sigma\log m}{64}.$$ Then $$\sum_{\substack{S\subseteq E_n\\1\le |S|\le k^2/2}}
       \widehat f(S)^2
 \le 4m^{-1/4}I(f).
 \label{eq:captured-mass}$$*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-block-capture"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-block-capture</summary>

*Proof.* Fix a nonempty $S\subseteq E_n$ with $s=|S|\le k^2/2$, viewed as the edge set of a simple graph. Let $V(S)$ be its set of nonisolated vertices and write $v=|V(S)|$. Since $s\le\binom v2<v^2/2$, its average degree over these vertices satisfies $$\frac{2s}{v}<\sqrt{2s}.$$ Choose a vertex $u\in V(S)$ whose degree $d_S(u)$ is at most this average. Then <a id="eq:support-degree"></a>$$1\le d_S(u)\le\sqrt{2s}\le k,
 \qquad v\le2s\le k^2.
 \label{eq:support-degree}$$

Choose $B$ uniformly among the $m$-element vertex sets. Conditioned on $u\in B$, each other specified vertex belongs to $B$ with probability $(m-1)/(n-1)$. A union bound gives <a id="eq:one-vertex-event"></a>$$\Pr_B\{B\cap V(S)=\{u\}\}
 \ge \frac mn
 \left(1-(v-1)\frac{m-1}{n-1}\right).
 \label{eq:one-vertex-event}$$ On this event, $S\cap F_B$ consists of *every* support edge incident with $u$, and contains no other support edge. Its size is therefore $d_S(u)$, between $1$ and $k$; it need not be $1$. Figure <a href="#fig:block" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="fig:block">1</a> illustrates this distinction.

<a id="eq:graph-tail"></a>
<a id="eq:graph-variance-preconstant"></a>
*Proof of Theorem <a href="#thm:variance" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:variance">2</a>.* First suppose $t=\log n\ge16$, and choose $m$ and $k$ as in Lemma <a href="#lem:block-capture" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:block-capture">6</a>; in particular $m\ge3$ and $k>0$. For the remaining Fourier degrees, the Dirichlet identity <a href="#eq:dirichlet" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:dirichlet">[eq:dirichlet]</a> gives $$\sum_{|S|>k^2/2}\widehat f(S)^2
 \le\frac2{k^2}\sum_S|S|\widehat f(S)^2
 =\frac{2\sigma^2}{k^2}I(f).
 \label{eq:graph-tail}$$ This remains valid for $k^2/2<1$, when the left side includes all nonconstant coefficients. Combining <a href="#eq:parseval" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:parseval">[eq:parseval]</a>, <a href="#eq:captured-mass" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:captured-mass">[eq:captured-mass]</a>, and <a href="#eq:graph-tail" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:graph-tail">[eq:graph-tail]</a> yields $$\mathop{\mathrm{Var}}(f)\le
 \left(4m^{-1/4}+\frac{2\sigma^2}{k^2}\right)I(f).
 \label{eq:graph-variance-preconstant}$$

For completeness, all constants can be bounded independently of $p$ and $n$. Since $m\ge\sqrt n/2$ and $t\ge16$, $\log m\ge t/2-\log2\ge t/3$. Thus $$\frac{2\sigma^2}{k^2}
 =\frac{8192}{(\log m)^2}\le\frac{73728}{t^2}.$$ The cancellation of $\sigma^2$ is exact, even when $p$ is arbitrarily close to $0$ or $1$. Also, $t^2e^{-t/8}$ is nonincreasing for $t\ge16$, so $$4m^{-1/4}t^2
 \le4\,2^{1/4}t^2e^{-t/8}
 \le4\,2^{1/4}\,256e^{-2}<256.$$ Thus the right-hand side of <a href="#eq:graph-variance-preconstant" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:graph-variance-preconstant">[eq:graph-variance-preconstant]</a> is at most $(73728+256)I(f)/t^2$, which is at most $2^{17}I(f)/t^2$.

If instead $2\le n<e^{16}$, the same Fourier identities immediately give $$\mathop{\mathrm{Var}}(f)\le\sigma^2I(f)\le\frac14 I(f)
 \le\frac{64}{(\log n)^2}I(f).$$ Hence the single choice $C_0=2^{17}$ works for every integer $n\ge2$ and every $p\in(0,1)$, as asserted. ◻

<a id="sec:threshold"></a>
### From influence to threshold width

We pass from influence to transition width using the Russo–Margulis probability-derivative formula; see Russo <a id="pm-cite-16"></a>[[Russo81](#ref-Russo81), Lemma 3] and Friedgut–Kalai <a id="pm-cite-17"></a>[[FK96](#ref-FK96), Lemma 2.2]. We include the finite calculation with our pivotal-influence convention and then integrate the resulting differential inequality.

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-block-capture-3"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-block-capture</summary>

<a id="eq:russo"></a>
*Proof of Theorem <a href="#thm:threshold" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:threshold">1</a>.* Put $f=\mathbf 1_{\mathcal P}$, $\mu(p)=\mathbb E_p f$, and $C_0=2^{17}$. We first verify the probability-derivative identity with the influence convention used here. If the edge probabilities are allowed to vary separately, $\mathbb Ef$ is a multilinear polynomial in those probabilities. Its partial derivative in the probability at $e$ is the expectation of $$\Delta_e f=f(x_{e\leftarrow1})-f(x_{e\leftarrow0}).$$ For increasing $f$, this difference takes values in $\{0,1\}$, so its expectation is $I_{e,p}(f)$ when all other biases are $p$. Differentiating along the diagonal therefore gives $$\label{eq:russo}
 \mu'(p)=I_p(f).$$ This is the usual probability-derivative formula underlying the sharp-threshold method; the calculation above proves it directly in the present finite setting.

<a id="eq:differential"></a>
Since $f$ is Boolean, $\mathop{\mathrm{Var}}_p(f)=\mu(p)(1-\mu(p))$. Theorem <a href="#thm:variance" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:variance">2</a> and <a href="#eq:russo" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:russo">[eq:russo]</a> imply $$\label{eq:differential}
 \mu'(p)\ge \frac{(\log n)^2}{C_0}\mu(p)(1-\mu(p))
 \qquad(0<p<1).$$ Nontriviality and monotonicity give $f(\varnothing)=0$ and $f(E_n)=1$. Both configurations have positive probability for every interior bias, hence $0<\mu(p)<1$ there, while $\mu(0)=0$ and $\mu(1)=1$. Equation <a href="#eq:differential" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:differential">[eq:differential]</a> now gives $\mu'(p)>0$ throughout $(0,1)$. Thus, by continuity, every quantile $p_a$ is the unique interior point satisfying $\mu(p_a)=a$.

On the interval $[p_\varepsilon,p_{1-\varepsilon}]$, division in <a href="#eq:differential" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:differential">[eq:differential]</a> is legitimate and gives $$\frac{d}{dp}\log\frac{\mu(p)}{1-\mu(p)}
 =\frac{\mu'(p)}{\mu(p)(1-\mu(p))}
 \ge\frac{(\log n)^2}{C_0}.$$ Integration yields $$p_{1-\varepsilon}-p_\varepsilon
 \le\frac{2C_0}{(\log n)^2}
          \log\frac{1-\varepsilon}{\varepsilon}.$$ Finally, $4\varepsilon(1-\varepsilon)\le1$ implies $$\log\frac{1-\varepsilon}{\varepsilon}
 \le 2\log\frac1{2\varepsilon}.$$ This proves <a href="#eq:threshold" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:threshold">[eq:threshold]</a> with $4C_0=2^{19}$ for the entire range $0<\varepsilon<1/2$. ◻

</details>

To recover the additive formulation of <a id="pm-cite-18"></a>[[FK96](#ref-FK96), Conjecture 1.2], set $$\delta=\frac{2^{19}}{(\log n)^2}\log\frac1{2\varepsilon},$$ and suppose $\mu(p)>\varepsilon$. Then $p>p_\varepsilon$, so Theorem <a href="#thm:threshold" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:threshold">1</a> gives $p+\delta>p_{1-\varepsilon}$. Hence $\mu(p+\delta)>1-\varepsilon$ whenever $p+\delta\le1$; for larger increments, the endpoint $1$ already has probability one. Conversely, when $p_\varepsilon+\delta<1$, apply this strict formulation at $p>p_\varepsilon$ and let $p\downarrow p_\varepsilon$. Continuity gives $\mu(p_\varepsilon+\delta)\ge1-\varepsilon$, which is the quantile bound. When $p_\varepsilon+\delta\ge1$, that bound is automatic.

The same log-odds estimate describes the transition around its median $p_*=p_{1/2}$. The log odds vanish at $p_*$, so integration from the median gives, with $C_0=2^{17}$, $$\begin{aligned}
 \mu(p_*-s)
 &\le \frac{1}{1+\exp\!\bigl(s(\log n)^2/C_0\bigr)}
 &&(0\le s\le p_*),\\
 1-\mu(p_*+s)
 &\le \frac{1}{1+\exp\!\bigl(s(\log n)^2/C_0\bigr)}
 &&(0\le s\le1-p_*).
\end{aligned}$$ The endpoint cases follow by continuity.
