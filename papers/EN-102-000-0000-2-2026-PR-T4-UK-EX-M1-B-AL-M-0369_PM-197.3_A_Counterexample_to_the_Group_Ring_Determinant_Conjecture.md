## 1. Abstract and result contract

> We disprove the unrestricted group-ring Determinant Conjecture. We construct a finitely generated group <i>G</i> and a square matrix over $`\mathbb Z[G]`$ that is invertible over $`\mathbb Q[G]`$ and has Fuglede–Kadison determinant strictly between zero and one. The logarithmic integral defining the determinant is finite.

- **C1** (counterexample, [source](#stmt-thm-main)): There are a finitely generated discrete group $G_D$, an integer $n\ge1$, and $A\in\operatorname{M}_n(\mathbb Z[G_D])$ invertible over $\mathbb Q[G_D]$ such that $$0<\det_{\mathcal N(G_D)}(T_A)<1.$$ The integral in Equation <a href="#eq:conjecture" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:conjecture">[eq:conjecture]</a> is finite and strictly negative. Thus the unrestricted group-ring Determinant Conjecture has a negative resolution.
  - Explanation: An integral group-ring matrix can have a rational group-ring inverse and a finite negative logarithmic determinant.
- **C2** (theorem, [source](#stmt-thm-input)): There exist a finite field $K_2$ of characteristic two, a finitely generated group $H_2$, and finite sums $a_2,b_2\in K_2[H_2]$ with $a_2b_2=1$ and $b_2a_2\ne1$.
  - Explanation: A scalar failure of direct finiteness in characteristic two supplies the existence input.
- **C3** (construction, [source](#stmt-lem-binary)): Let $K_2$ be a finite field of characteristic two and $H_2$ a group. For $d=[K_2:\mathbb F_2]$, there is an injective unital ring homomorphism $$\rho:K_2[H_2]\longrightarrow\operatorname{M}_d(\mathbb F_2[H_2]).$$ For $a_2,b_2\in K_2[H_2]$ with $a_2b_2=1\ne b_2a_2$, their images $X,Y$ satisfy $XY=I_d$ and $C:=I_d-YX\ne0$, with $$\label{eq:defect} C^2=C,\qquad XC=0,\qquad CY=0.$$
  - Explanation: The finite coefficient field embeds into binary matrices, producing a nonzero idempotent defect.
- **C4** (construction, [source](#stmt-prop-separation)): Let $X,Y\in\operatorname{M}_d(\mathbb F_2[H_2])$, $d\ge1$, satisfy $XY=I_d\ne YX$. In the group $G_D=(\bigoplus_{h\in H_2}C_3)\rtimes H_2$, where $H_2$ acts by left translation on coordinate labels, there is an orthogonal projection $P\in\operatorname{M}_{d+1}(\mathbb Z[1/3][G_D])$ and a finite set $S\subseteq H_2\setminus\{1\}$ such that $$\tau_{d+1}(P)=d+\theta,\qquad \theta=\frac13\left(\frac23\right)^{\|S\|}>0, \qquad \bar P=uv,$$ where $u$ has size $(d+1)\times d$ and $v$ has size $d\times(d+1)$ over $\mathbb F_2[G_D]$. The integer $D=3^{1+\|S\|}$ clears all coefficients of $P$.
  - Explanation: Commuting cyclic-coordinate projections isolate a defect coefficient and create a positive rational trace gap with a narrower binary factorization.
- **C5** (construction, [source](#stmt-lem-lift)): Let $G$ be a group, $m,r\ge1$, $D$ an odd positive integer, and $P\in\operatorname{M}_m(\mathbb Q[G])$ satisfy $P^2=P$ and $DP\in\operatorname{M}_m(\mathbb Z[G])$. Suppose $\bar P=uv$ over $\mathbb F_2[G]$, where $u$ and $v$ have respective sizes $m\times r$ and $r\times m$. For $q=2^k$, $k\ge1$, the reduction $e=P_q$ has a factorization $e=u_qv_q$ of the same sizes over $(\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z)[G]$.
  - Explanation: A finite geometric series in the projection corner lifts the binary factorization to a power of two without increasing its column count.
- **C6** (construction, [source](#stmt-lem-integral)): Suppose $P=P^*=P^2\in\operatorname{M}_m(\mathbb Q[G])$, an odd $D>0$ clears its denominators, and $\bar P=uv$ factors through $r\ge1$ columns. Let $q=2^k>1$ satisfy $q\equiv1\pmod D$. Choose arbitrary integral lifts $U,V$ of the factors supplied by Lemma <a href="#lem:lift" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:lift">5</a>. Then $$\label{eq:integral-matrix} A=\begin{pmatrix} qI_r&V\\ U&q^{-1}\bigl(UV-P-q(I_m-P)\bigr) \end{pmatrix} \in\operatorname{M}_{r+m}(\mathbb Z[G])$$ has an inverse in $\operatorname{M}_{r+m}(\mathbb Q[G])$.
  - Explanation: Modular factorization and the denominator congruence make the prescribed block matrix integral; block shears give its rational inverse.
- **C7** (conditional_result, [source](#stmt-lem-logderivative)): For a continuously differentiable path $M:[0,1]\to\mathcal A_n$ of invertible elements, $$\frac{d}{dx}\tau_n\bigl(\log(M(x)^*M(x))\bigr) =2\operatorname{Re}\tau_n\bigl(M'(x)M(x)^{-1}\bigr).$$
  - Explanation: Cyclicity of the continuous trace turns the logarithmic derivative into the real part of the ordered inverse product.
- **C8** (conditional_result, [source](#stmt-lem-shears)): Let $r,m\ge1$ and let $B\in\mathcal A_{r+m}$ be invertible. Let $S,T$ have respective sizes $m\times r$ and $r\times m$ over the operator-norm closure of $\mathbb C[G]$. For $x\in[0,1]$, let $L_x,R_x$ be lower and upper block shears with diagonal blocks $I_r,I_m$ and off-diagonal blocks $xS,xT$, respectively. Then $\tau_{r+m}(\log((L_xBR_x)^*(L_xBR_x)))$ is constant.
  - Explanation: Upper and lower block shears preserve the trace of the logarithm of the positive square.
- **C9** (conditional_result, [source](#stmt-prop-criterion)): Let $G$ be a discrete group, $m,r\ge1$, and let $P=P^*=P^2\in\operatorname{M}_m(\mathbb Q[G])$. Suppose an odd $D>0$ clears its denominators and $\bar P=uv$ with $u\in\operatorname{M}_{m,r}(\mathbb F_2[G])$ and $v\in\operatorname{M}_{r,m}(\mathbb F_2[G])$. For every $q=2^k>1$ congruent to one modulo $D$, the matrix $A$ of Lemma <a href="#lem:integral" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:integral">6</a> satisfies $$\begin{aligned} \tau_{r+m}(\log(T_A^*T_A)) &=2(r-\tau_m(P))\log q,\label{eq:criterion-trace}\\ \log\det_{\mathcal N(G)}(T_A)&=(r-\tau_m(P))\log q. \label{eq:criterion-det} \end{aligned}$$ It is integral and rationally invertible, and its positive-square spectral measure has support in a compact subinterval of $(0,\infty)$.
  - Explanation: The exact determinant follows from the expansion of the coordinate summands and contraction of the projection range; bounded invertibility keeps the spectrum away from zero.

## 2. Definitions, validity and bounds

- **Ordinary group ring** ([source](#eq:conjecture)): Let $G$ be a discrete group and let $\mathbb Z[G]$ denote its ordinary group ring of finite sums.
- **Left regular representation** ([source](#eq:conjecture)): Use the left regular representation $$(L_g\xi)(h)=\xi(g^{-1}h),\qquad \xi\in\ell^2(G).$$
- **Group-ring matrix operator** ([source](#eq:conjecture)): For $A\in\operatorname{M}_n(\mathbb Z[G])$, write $T_A$ for its operator on $\ell^2(G)^n$ and $e_i$ for the vector with $\delta_1$ in component $i$.
- **Positive-square probability spectral measure** ([source](#eq:conjecture)): The probability spectral measure of the positive square is $$\mu_A(E)=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n \langle \mathbf1_E(T_A^*T_A)e_i,e_i\rangle \quad(E\subseteq[0,\infty)\text{ Borel}).$$
- **Unnormalized matrix trace** ([source](#sec:introduction)): Write $\mathcal N(G)$ for the group von Neumann algebra and use the *unnormalized* matrix trace $\tau_n(T)=\sum_i\langle Te_i,e_i\rangle$, so $\tau_n(I_n)=n$.
- **Direct finiteness** ([source](#pm-h-3)): A unital ring is directly finite if $ab=1$ implies $ba=1$.
- **Target group** ([source](#pm-h-5)): We enlarge the group to $$G_D=\left(\bigoplus_{h\in H_2}C_3\right)\rtimes H_2,$$ where $H_2$ acts by left translation on the coordinate labels.
- **Orthogonal projection** ([source](#sec:projection)): Thus an orthogonal projection satisfies $P=P^*=P^2$ in the regular representation.
- **Coordinate averaging projection** ([source](#sec:projection)): Let $z_h$ generate the cyclic coordinate labelled by $h$ and put $$p_h=\frac{1+z_h+z_h^2}{3},\qquad p=p_1\prod_{s\in S}(1-p_s).$$
- **Operator algebra closure** ([source](#sec:analysis)): Let $\mathcal A_n$ be the operator-norm closure of $\operatorname{M}_n(\mathbb C[G])$ in its left regular representation.
- **Identity-coefficient trace** ([source](#sec:analysis)): For finite group-ring matrices define $$\tau_n(C)=\sum_{i=1}^n[1]C_{ii}.$$
- **Diagonal expansion and contraction** ([source](#sec:lifting)): The diagonal operator with the desired expansion and contraction is $$J=-q^{-1}P-(I_m-P),\qquad B=\operatorname{diag}(qI_r,J).$$

## 3. Proof roadmap

- **R1** [claim](#stmt-thm-input) / [proof](#pm-h-3): Import the characteristic-two scalar existence theorem. -> Scalar one-sided inverse with a nonzero reverse-product defect.
- **R2** [claim](#stmt-lem-binary) / [proof](#proof-stmt-lem-binary): Represent field multiplication on a binary basis. -> Binary matrix one-sided inverse and nonzero idempotent defect.

- **R4** [claim](#stmt-lem-lift) / [proof](#proof-stmt-lem-lift): Invert the corner element by a finite geometric series in its even defect. -> Factorization modulo a power of two with the same column sizes.
- **R5** [claim](#stmt-lem-integral) / [proof](#proof-stmt-lem-integral): Choose the denominator congruence and assemble the ordered block shears. -> Integral block matrix and explicit rational group-ring inverse.
- **R6** [claim](#stmt-lem-logderivative) / [proof](#proof-stmt-lem-logderivative): Differentiate the uniformly convergent logarithmic series and use trace cyclicity. -> Tracial logarithmic derivative identity.
- **R7** [claim](#stmt-lem-shears) / [proof](#proof-stmt-lem-shears): Apply the logarithmic derivative to traceless block shears. -> Constant logarithmic positive-square trace along the shear path.
- **R8** [claim](#stmt-prop-criterion) / [proof](#proof-stmt-prop-criterion): Remove the shears and evaluate the logarithm of the orthogonal diagonal blocks. -> Exact determinant and compact positive spectral support.
- **R9** [claim](#stmt-thm-main) / [proof](#sec:assembly): Substitute the projection trace gap and prove finite generation of the enlarged group. -> Strictly positive determinant below the conjectured lower bound and finite negative logarithmic integral.

## 4. Engineering Triptych

### Panel 1 - epistemic contract

- **INV-LIFT**: The modular lift preserves the binary factorization column count and returns u_q v_q=e.
- **INV-CORNER**: The geometric sum is a two-sided inverse of Z in the e-corner, whose identity is e.
- **INV-FINITE**: The lifted factors keep finite group-ring supports.
- **INV-INTEGRAL**: The block endpoint is integral and has a rational group-ring inverse.

### Panel 2 - negative knowledge

- **AU-CORNER**: Use Q as a full matrix-ring inverse of Z. -> A proper corner has identity e rather than I_m.
- **AU-ORDER**: Reorder factors in the modular lifting routine. -> Noncommuting factors can change the product.
- **AU-CONGRUENCE**: Feed an arbitrary power of two into the integral endpoint. -> The denominator cancellation is not guaranteed.
- **AU-EXISTENCE**: Treat small finite-ring tests as an executed determinant counterexample. -> The tests do not supply the companion group or its scalar one-sided inverses.
- **AU-ANALYSIS**: Apply the determinant trace calculation to a non-self-adjoint idempotent. -> Idempotence alone does not establish the positive-square formula used by Proposition 9.

### Panel 3 - reference implementation and oracle

Applicability: `constructive`. Lemma 5 gives a finite geometric-series construction once a finite exact ring instance is supplied. This panel executes its modular-matrix specialization only; the paper explicitly does not execute its companion existence input.

```python
# INPUTS: JSON cases of k, e, u0, v0 with integer residue matrices.
# PRECONDITIONS: 1<=k<=4, 1<=m,r<=2; e^2=e mod 2^k; u0*v0=e mod 2.
# OUTPUTS: Canonical residue matrices Q, uq, vq witnessing a corner inverse and factorization.
# TIME: O(N*k*(m^3+m^2*r)) exact arithmetic operations for N<=1024 cases.
# SPACE: O(N*(m^2+m*r)) residues including the returned batch.
# ARITHMETIC: python-flint fmpz_mat integer products followed by exact modular reduction.
# FAILURE: Invalid shape, residue, bound or algebraic precondition raises ValueError.
import json
import sys
from flint import fmpz_mat


def lift(case):
    k = case['k']
    if type(k) is not int or not 1 <= k <= 4:
        raise ValueError('bounded power required')
    q = 2**k
    m = len(case['e'])
    r = len(case['v0'])
    def matrix(rows, height, width):
        if len(rows) != height or any(len(row) != width for row in rows):
            raise ValueError('shape')
        if any(type(x) is not int or not 0 <= x < q for row in rows for x in row):
            raise ValueError('canonical residues required')
        return fmpz_mat(rows)
    if not 1 <= m <= 2 or not 1 <= r <= 2:
        raise ValueError('bounded dimensions required')
    e = matrix(case['e'], m, m)
    u0 = matrix(case['u0'], m, r)
    v0 = matrix(case['v0'], r, m)
    def reduce(a, modulus=q):
        return fmpz_mat([[int(a[i,j]) % modulus for j in range(a.ncols())] for i in range(a.nrows())])
    if reduce(e*e) != e or reduce(u0*v0, 2) != reduce(e, 2):
        raise ValueError('idempotence or binary factorization')
    Z = reduce(e*u0*v0*e)
    W = reduce(e-Z)
    Q = e
    term = e
    for exponent in range(1, k):
        term = reduce(term*W)
        Q = reduce(Q+term)
    uq = reduce(Q*e*u0)
    vq = reduce(v0*e)
    def encode(a):
        return [[int(a[i,j]) for j in range(a.ncols())] for i in range(a.nrows())]
    return {'Q': encode(Q), 'uq': encode(uq), 'vq': encode(vq)}


batch = json.load(sys.stdin)['cases']
if not 1 <= len(batch) <= 1024:
    raise ValueError('bounded batch required')
print(json.dumps({'results': [lift(case) for case in batch]}, sort_keys=True, separators=(',', ':')))
```

- **L-external_input** (nonconstructive): The counterexample uses the companion existence theorem; this paper does not execute a numerical instance or supply a group-word decision procedure. - **L-binary_field** (hypothesis): The premise requires a finite field of characteristic two; the field is not asserted to be the binary prime field. Blocked use: ['Replacing the input with an odd-characteristic counterexample', 'Assuming the scalar coefficients already lie in the binary prime field']
- **L-characteristic** (hypothesis): An odd-characteristic direct-finiteness failure alone does not provide the binary premise. Blocked use: ['Reusing an odd-characteristic existence theorem as the required input']
- **L-torsion** (counterexample_scope): The construction makes no torsion-free claim; the companion uses torsion and the target group contains cyclic coordinates. Blocked use: ['Inferring a torsion-free counterexample', 'Identifying the target group with the companion group']
- **L-ordinary_ring** (hypothesis): The algebraic construction concerns ordinary group rings of finite sums, rather than unrestricted formal infinite sums. Blocked use: ['Extending finite algebraic lifting to arbitrary infinite-support series']
- **L-projection** (hypothesis): The integral determinant criterion assumes a rational orthogonal projection, odd denominator clearing, and a binary factorization; a general idempotent is only sufficient for the modular lifting step. Blocked use: ['Applying the spectral determinant formula to a non-self-adjoint idempotent', 'Claiming a determinant formula without the factorization premise']
- **L-modulus** (hypothesis): The integral endpoint construction requires a nontrivial power of two congruent to one modulo the odd denominator-clearing integer. Blocked use: ['Choosing an arbitrary modulus for the integral endpoint', 'Omitting denominator clearing']
- **L-corner_inverse** (edge_case): The geometric inverse is a corner-ring inverse and does not assert invertibility in the full matrix ring. Blocked use: ['Treating the corner inverse as an ambient matrix inverse']
- **L-empty_support** (edge_case): The coefficient-separation support may be empty, with the single-coordinate projection and its stated trace; implementations must admit this boundary. Blocked use: ['Requiring a nonempty separation set']
- **L-overlap** (edge_case): Coefficient separation does not require disjoint coordinate-label collections and tolerates inverse pairs or finite-order elements. Blocked use: ['Adding a disjointness assumption', 'Rejecting supports containing inverse pairs or finite-order elements']
- **L-path_integrality** (edge_case): The simplifying path is not required to stay integral; integrality is asserted at the constructed endpoint. Blocked use: ['Requiring every shear-path matrix to be integral']

- **L-invertible_path** (hypothesis): The logarithmic derivative is asserted for a continuously differentiable path of invertible elements, not a path crossing singular operators. Blocked use: ['Applying the derivative formula through singularities']

Blocked use: ['Using nonsoficity as a constructive premise', 'Claiming the construction disproves the determinant bound for sofic groups']

> ## Scope
>
> Kaplansky's direct-finiteness conjecture asserts that $ab=1$ implies $ba=1$ for $a,b\in K[G]$, for every field $K$ and group $G$. The formalized results construct a finite field of characteristic two and a group algebra violating this implication. One statement records the counterexample for a finitely generated group; the more detailed construction gives a finitely presented group with an element of odd prime order.
>
> The detailed construction also gives a precise recipe for choosing the data and proves that the required search terminates. The further conclusion that the group is nonsofic is outside these statements.
>
> A nonsingular integer matrix has absolute determinant at least $1$. The group-ring Determinant Conjecture extends this bound to matrices over $\mathbb Z[G]$ for every discrete group $G$. If $T_A$ is the operator induced by such a matrix $A$ on finite direct sums of $\ell^2(G)$, and $\mu_A$ is the spectral measure of $T_A^*T_A$ with respect to the group trace, the conjecture asserts
>
> $\displaystyle \int_{(0,\infty)}\log t\,d\mu_A(t)\ge0,$
>
> with the zero spectral atom omitted. For an invertible square matrix, this is equivalent to its Fuglede–Kadison determinant being at least $1$.
>
> The formalized result contradicts that bound. It gives a finitely generated group $G$ and an $n\times n$ matrix over $\mathbb Z[G]$, with $n\ge1$, that is invertible over $\mathbb Q[G]$. Its bounded left-regular operator is invertible and has determinant strictly between $0$ and $1$.
>
> The formalization uses the trace of $\log(T^*T)$ to define the determinant for the invertible operator $T$. The paper's additional spectral-measure integral conclusion is not included.
>
> Kaplansky's direct-finiteness conjecture asserts that $ab=1$ implies $ba=1$ in every group algebra over a field. Let $p$ be the smallest prime divisor of $\bigl(\binom{1200}{600}!\bigr)^2+1$; this prime is odd. The formalized result constructs a field $K$ of order $p^4$, a finitely generated group $G$ with torsion, and elements $a,b$ violating this implication. The associated cellular automaton on all configurations $K^G$ is injective but not surjective.
>
> The formalization also contains fixed-field transfer results, separate from the statement linked below.

```lean
import Mathlib

namespace OAI

noncomputable section
open scoped BigOperators

namespace GroupRingDeterminant

/-- The Hilbert space l²(G)^n, with the product coordinates flattened. -/
abbrev Hilbert (G : Type) (n : ℕ) := lp (fun _ : Fin n × G => ℂ) 2

/-- The identity vector in component i. -/
def identityVector {G : Type} [Group G] {n : ℕ} (i : Fin n) : Hilbert G n :=
  by
    classical
    exact lp.single 2 (i, 1) 1

/-- The unnormalized matrix trace, linear in its operator argument. -/
def groupTrace {G : Type} [Group G] {n : ℕ}
    (T : Hilbert G n →L[ℂ] Hilbert G n) : ℂ :=
  ∑ i : Fin n, inner ℂ (identityVector i) (T (identityVector i))

/-- The invertible-case Fuglede--Kadison determinant.
It is used below only for invertible regular operators. -/
def fkDet {G : Type} [Group G] {n : ℕ}
    (T : Hilbert G n →L[ℂ] Hilbert G n) : ℝ :=
  Real.exp ((groupTrace (CFC.log (star T * T))).re / 2)

/-- Exact coordinate specification of the bounded left-regular operator of A. -/
def IsRegularOperator {G : Type} [Group G] {n : ℕ}
    (A : Matrix (Fin n) (Fin n) (MonoidAlgebra ℤ G))
    (T : Hilbert G n →L[ℂ] Hilbert G n) : Prop :=
  ∀ (ξ : Hilbert G n) (i : Fin n) (h : G),
    (T ξ) (i, h) = ∑ j : Fin n,
      (A i j).coeff.sum fun g z => (z : ℂ) * ξ (j, g⁻¹ * h)

/-- Coefficientwise extension from the integral to the rational group ring. -/
def rationalMatrix {G : Type} [Group G] {n : ℕ}
    (A : Matrix (Fin n) (Fin n) (MonoidAlgebra ℤ G)) :
    Matrix (Fin n) (Fin n) (MonoidAlgebra ℚ G) :=
  A.map (MonoidAlgebra.mapRingHom G (Int.castRingHom ℚ))

/-- An integral group-ring matrix with rational and bounded inverses and determinant in (0, 1). -/
def MainStatement : Prop :=
  ∃ (G : Type) (_ : Group G) (_ : Group.FG G) (n : ℕ),
    1 ≤ n ∧ ∃ (A : Matrix (Fin n) (Fin n) (MonoidAlgebra ℤ G))
      (T : Hilbert G n →L[ℂ] Hilbert G n),
      IsUnit (rationalMatrix A) ∧ IsRegularOperator A T ∧ IsUnit T ∧
        0 < fkDet T ∧ fkDet T < 1

end GroupRingDeterminant

end
```

<a id="sec:introduction"></a>
### Introduction and proof overview

A nonsingular integer matrix has determinant of absolute value at least one. The group-ring Determinant Conjecture asks for an analogue when the entries are finite integer combinations of elements of a discrete group. The relevant determinant, introduced by Fuglede and Kadison (Fuglede and Kadison 1952) <a id="pm-cite-1"></a>[[FK1952](#ref-FK1952)], uses the trace of a logarithm rather than a finite-dimensional determinant polynomial.

<a id="eq:conjecture"></a>
Here are the conventions that fix this question. Let $G$ be a discrete group and let $\mathbb Z[G]$ denote its ordinary group ring of finite sums. Use the left regular representation $$(L_g\xi)(h)=\xi(g^{-1}h),\qquad \xi\in\ell^2(G).$$ For $A\in\operatorname{M}_n(\mathbb Z[G])$, write $T_A$ for its operator on $\ell^2(G)^n$ and $e_i$ for the vector with $\delta_1$ in component $i$. The probability spectral measure of the positive square is $$\mu_A(E)=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n
 \langle \mathbf1_E(T_A^*T_A)e_i,e_i\rangle
 \quad(E\subseteq[0,\infty)\text{ Borel}).$$ The square-matrix instance of the conjecture is $$\label{eq:conjecture}
 \int_{(0,\infty)}\log t\,d\mu_A(t)\ge0.$$ The zero atom is omitted and the general integral may be $-\infty$. The usual conjecture also permits rectangular matrices (Lück 2002, Conjecture 13.2) <a id="pm-cite-2"></a>[[Luck2002](#ref-Luck2002)]; a square counterexample already contradicts it. Our example will have a bounded inverse, so its spectrum stays away from zero.

Write $\mathcal N(G)$ for the group von Neumann algebra and use the *unnormalized* matrix trace $\tau_n(T)=\sum_i\langle Te_i,e_i\rangle$, so $\tau_n(I_n)=n$. For invertible $T_A$ the determinant convention is <a id="eq:normalization"></a>$$\label{eq:normalization}
 \log\det_{\mathcal N(G)}(T_A)
 =\frac12\tau_n\bigl(\log(T_A^*T_A)\bigr)
 =\frac n2\int_{(0,\infty)}\log t\,d\mu_A(t).$$ This extends the absolute value of the ordinary determinant. The inversion unitary $\delta_g\mapsto\delta_{g^{-1}}$ identifies our integral-matrix convention with right multiplication by $A^*$ on row vectors. It fixes the identity vectors, and therefore preserves the corresponding positive-square spectral measures.

<a id="stmt-thm-main"></a>
<div id="thm:main" class="theorem">

**Theorem 1**. *There are a finitely generated discrete group $G_D$, an integer $n\ge1$, and $A\in\operatorname{M}_n(\mathbb Z[G_D])$ invertible over $\mathbb Q[G_D]$ such that $$0<\det_{\mathcal N(G_D)}(T_A)<1.$$ The integral in Equation <a href="#eq:conjecture" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:conjecture">[eq:conjecture]</a> is finite and strictly negative. Thus the unrestricted group-ring Determinant Conjecture has a negative resolution.*

</div>

<a id="pm-h-3"></a>
###### The exact existence input.

A unital ring is directly finite if $ab=1$ implies $ba=1$. We use the following scalar failure of direct finiteness from the complete characteristic-two companion.

<a id="stmt-thm-input"></a>
<div id="thm:input" class="theorem">

**Theorem 2** ((OpenAI 2026, Theorem 1.1)). *There exist a finite field $K_2$ of characteristic two, a finitely generated group $H_2$, and finite sums $a_2,b_2\in K_2[H_2]$ with $a_2b_2=1$ and $b_2a_2\ne1$.*

</div>

The companion’s Section 6 specifies its field, group presentation and scalar sums by a terminating finite prescription. We use the existence theorem, not an executed numerical instance or a group-word decision procedure. The coefficient field is finite but is not asserted to be $\mathbb F_2$. There is no torsion-free hypothesis; the companion construction uses torsion. All steps after this exact input are proved below. In particular, a result only in odd characteristic would not supply the binary premise used here.

<a id="pm-h-4"></a>
###### Context and mathematical ancestry.

Determinant bounds are part of the theory of $L^2$-invariants (Lück 2002) <a id="pm-cite-3"></a>[[Luck2002](#ref-Luck2002)]. Approximation and closure arguments established such bounds for large classes of groups, including amenable groups (Schick 2001, Theorem 1.21, with its erratum) <a id="pm-cite-4"></a>[[Schick2001](#ref-Schick2001)]; Elek and Szabó proved the determinant bound for sofic groups (Elek and Szabó 2005, Theorem 5) <a id="pm-cite-5"></a>[[ElekSzabo2005](#ref-ElekSzabo2005)]. Their proof uses finite graph approximations and the integrality of products of nonzero eigenvalues of positive integral matrices. Our construction instead starts from failure of direct finiteness in characteristic two. The cited sofic theorem implies, as a separate consequence, that its resulting group is not sofic.

The lower-bound question is distinct from determinant *approximation*. Kammeyer’s counterexample to a finite-quotient limiting formula has determinant $2$ over the discrete Heisenberg group, while its principal congruence quotients of levels $3^i$, $i\ge1$, have determinants at most $5^{1/3}$ (Kammeyer 2026, Theorem 2 and Section 2) <a id="pm-cite-6"></a>[[Kammeyer2026](#ref-Kammeyer2026)]. That discrepancy does not violate the lower bound one.

The finite-coordinate projection construction below is related to the commuting wreath-product projections of Dicks and Schick (Dicks and Schick 2002, sec. 3) <a id="pm-cite-7"></a>[[DicksSchick2002](#ref-DicksSchick2002)] and of Lehner, Neuhauser and Woess (Lehner, Neuhauser, and Woess 2008, sec. 2) <a id="pm-cite-8"></a>[[LehnerNeuhauserWoess2008](#ref-LehnerNeuhauserWoess2008)]. Here the projections separate one coefficient of a binary defect; we do not import a spectral-distribution theorem from those works. The analytic step is the classical tracial logarithmic derivative (Fuglede and Kadison 1952, Lemma 2) <a id="pm-cite-9"></a>[[FK1952](#ref-FK1952)], also present in the path-integral determinant viewpoint of de la Harpe and Skandalis (Harpe and Skandalis 1984, Lemma 1 and Proposition 2(d)) <a id="pm-cite-10"></a>[[deLaHarpeSkandalis1984](#ref-deLaHarpeSkandalis1984)]. We give its proof and its application to block shears locally.

<a id="pm-h-5"></a>
###### How a binary defect produces a real trace gap.

Set $d=[K_2:\mathbb F_2]$. Representing multiplication by field elements on the $d$-dimensional binary vector space $K_2$ gives matrices $X,Y\in\operatorname{M}_d(\mathbb F_2[H_2])$ with $XY=I_d$ and a nonzero idempotent $C=I_d-YX$. One entry of $C$ has a nonzero coefficient. We enlarge the group to $$G_D=\left(\bigoplus_{h\in H_2}C_3\right)\rtimes H_2,$$ where $H_2$ acts by left translation on the coordinate labels. Averaging over the cyclic group at one coordinate and taking complementary averages at finitely many other coordinates isolates that coefficient. The result is a rational orthogonal projection $P$ of size $d+1$ with trace $d+\theta$, for a positive rational number $\theta$. Its reduction modulo two nevertheless factors through $d$ columns. Section <a href="#sec:projection" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:projection">2</a> proves the coefficient separation and this factorization in full.

<a id="pm-h-6"></a>
###### How the gap becomes an integral determinant.

For a general rational projection $P$ of size $m$, suppose an odd integer $D$ clears its denominators and $\bar P=uv$ factors through $r$ columns over $\mathbb F_2[G]$. Choose a power $q$ of two congruent to one modulo $D$. Section <a href="#sec:lifting" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:lifting">3</a> lifts the factorization modulo $q$ by a finite geometric series in a corner ring. Arbitrary integral lifts then give an integral block matrix with an explicit rational inverse. Section <a href="#sec:analysis" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:analysis">4</a> removes its two block shears without changing the trace of the logarithm of its positive square. The remaining diagonal matrix expands the first $r$ coordinate summands by $q$ and contracts the range of $P$ by $q^{-1}$, giving $$\log\det(T_A)=(r-\tau_m(P))\log q.$$ Finally Section <a href="#sec:assembly" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:assembly">5</a> uses $r=d$ and $\tau_{d+1}(P)=d+\theta$ to obtain the exact value $q^{-\theta}$. The projection construction, modular lifting and trace calculation have distinct roles; none replaces the scalar existence input.

<a id="sec:projection"></a>
### From the scalar defect to a projection

We first produce the trace gap. All group-ring sums in this section are finite, and matrix multiplication keeps the indicated order. For a group-ring sum $c$, write $[1]c$ for its identity coefficient. The involution $*$ inverts group elements and conjugates coefficients; on matrices it also transposes the indices. Thus an orthogonal projection satisfies $P=P^*=P^2$ in the regular representation.

<a id="stmt-lem-binary"></a>
<div id="lem:binary" class="lemma">

<a id="eq:defect"></a>
**Lemma 3**. *Let $K_2$ be a finite field of characteristic two and $H_2$ a group. For $d=[K_2:\mathbb F_2]$, there is an injective unital ring homomorphism $$\rho:K_2[H_2]\longrightarrow\operatorname{M}_d(\mathbb F_2[H_2]).$$ For $a_2,b_2\in K_2[H_2]$ with $a_2b_2=1\ne b_2a_2$, their images $X,Y$ satisfy $XY=I_d$ and $C:=I_d-YX\ne0$, with $$\label{eq:defect}
 C^2=C,\qquad XC=0,\qquad CY=0.$$*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-binary"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-binary</summary>

*Proof.* Choose a binary basis of $K_2$. The matrix of multiplication by $\lambda\in K_2$ is a unital ring representation; it is injective because its value on $1\in K_2$ recovers $\lambda$. Apply this map coefficientwise to a group-ring sum. The coefficient matrices commute with the formal group symbols, so products are preserved. Comparing the coefficient matrix at each group element proves injectivity. Thus $XY=I_d$ and $I_d-YX\ne0$. Now $(YX)^2=Y(XY)X=YX$, $X(I_d-YX)=X-(XY)X=0$ and $(I_d-YX)Y=Y-Y(XY)=0$, proving all the identities. ◻

</details>

<a id="stmt-prop-separation"></a>
<div id="prop:separation" class="proposition">

**Proposition 4**. *Let $X,Y\in\operatorname{M}_d(\mathbb F_2[H_2])$, $d\ge1$, satisfy $XY=I_d\ne YX$. In the group $G_D=(\bigoplus_{h\in H_2}C_3)\rtimes H_2$, where $H_2$ acts by left translation on coordinate labels, there is an orthogonal projection $P\in\operatorname{M}_{d+1}(\mathbb Z[1/3][G_D])$ and a finite set $S\subseteq H_2\setminus\{1\}$ such that $$\tau_{d+1}(P)=d+\theta,\qquad
 \theta=\frac13\left(\frac23\right)^{|S|}>0,
 \qquad \bar P=uv,$$ where $u$ has size $(d+1)\times d$ and $v$ has size $d\times(d+1)$ over $\mathbb F_2[G_D]$. The integer $D=3^{1+|S|}$ clears all coefficients of $P$.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-prop-separation"><summary>Proof of stmt-prop-separation</summary>

<a id="eq:support"></a>
*Proof.* Put $C=I_d-YX$. Choose an entry $C_{ij}$ and a group element $h_0$ whose coefficient in that entry is nonzero. The coefficient is $1$ because the field is $\mathbb F_2$. Left multiplication by $h_0^{-1}$ therefore gives $$\label{eq:support}
 h_0^{-1}C_{ij}=1+\sum_{s\in S}s$$ for a finite $S\subseteq H_2\setminus\{1\}$, possibly empty.

To isolate the identity term in Equation <a href="#eq:support" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:support">[eq:support]</a>, we seek a nonzero rational projection $p$ with $psp=0$ for every $s\in S$. The cyclic coordinates provide complementary projections that enforce these vanishing products. Let $z_h$ generate the cyclic coordinate labelled by $h$ and put $$p_h=\frac{1+z_h+z_h^2}{3},\qquad
 p=p_1\prod_{s\in S}(1-p_s).$$ The $p_h$ are self-adjoint idempotents: averaging the nine products in $(1+z_h+z_h^2)^2$ gives three copies of each element of $C_3$. They commute because distinct coordinates commute. Consequently $p=p^*=p^2$, and the displayed product shows that $3^{1+|S|}p$ is integral. This is the elementary commuting-coordinate method in the works cited above; compare (Dicks and Schick 2002, Lemmas 3.1 and 3.4) <a id="pm-cite-11"></a>[[DicksSchick2002](#ref-DicksSchick2002)].

<a id="eq:separation"></a>
Conjugation obeys $s p_hs^{-1}=p_{sh}$. For $s\in S$, the product $sps^{-1}$ contains $p_s$, while $p$ contains $1-p_s$. All these coordinate factors commute, so $$\label{eq:separation}
 psp=p(sps^{-1})s=0.$$ This argument does not need disjointness of the two collections of coordinate labels. Inverse pairs or finite-order elements in $S$ cause no change.

<a id="eq:theta"></a>
The original coordinates $\{1\}\cup S$ are distinct. The coefficient of the identity in their product is therefore the product of the individual identity coefficients. Hence $$\label{eq:theta}
 \tau_1(p)=[1]p=\frac13\prod_{s\in S}\frac23=\theta>0.$$ For empty $S$ this means $p=p_1$ and $\theta=1/3$.

The map $\mathbb Z[1/3]\to\mathbb F_2$ is defined because $3$ is odd. Reduce the projection modulo two and combine Equations <a href="#eq:support" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:support">[eq:support]</a> and <a href="#eq:separation" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:separation">[eq:separation]</a> to obtain $$\bar p h_0^{-1} C_{ij}\bar p=\bar p.$$ Let $f$ be the row of length $d$ with entry $\bar p h_0^{-1}$ at position $i$ and zero elsewhere. Let $w$ be the column with entry $\bar p$ at position $j$ and zero elsewhere. Thus $fCw=\bar p$. Define $$P=\operatorname{diag}(I_d,p),\qquad
 u=\begin{pmatrix}X\\fC\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 v=\begin{pmatrix}Y&Cw\end{pmatrix}.$$ Using Equation <a href="#eq:defect" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:defect">[eq:defect]</a> in this order gives $$uv=\begin{pmatrix}XY&XCw\\fCY&fC^2w\end{pmatrix}
   =\operatorname{diag}(I_d,\bar p)=\bar P.$$ The rational trace is $d+\theta$ by Equation <a href="#eq:theta" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:theta">[eq:theta]</a>. It is the rational orthogonal projection, not its binary reduction, that has this strictly positive extra trace. ◻

</details>

We have obtained a projection of real trace greater than $d$ but a binary factorization through $d$ columns. The next step produces a factorization through the same number of columns modulo a power of two, so that it can be inserted into an integral matrix.

<a id="sec:lifting"></a>
### Lifting the factorization and preserving integrality

We now work with any group $G$, an orthogonal rational projection $P\in\operatorname{M}_m(\mathbb Q[G])$, and a factorization of $\bar P$ through $r$ columns over $\mathbb F_2[G]$. Assume that an odd integer $D$ clears the denominators of $P$. Here orthogonality means $P^2=P=P^*$, where $*$ transposes the matrix and inverts group elements.

For a power $q>1$ of two, the trace gap suggests the absolute value of the operator we want: expand $r$ coordinate summands by $q$ and contract the range of $P$ by $q^{-1}$. Its logarithmic determinant should be $(r-\tau_m(P))\log q$, which is negative when $\tau_m(P)>r$. The difficulty is to realize this determinant by an integral matrix. We will add block shears whose off-diagonal entries come from a factorization of $P$ modulo $q$. The analytic justification that these shears preserve the determinant is given in Section <a href="#sec:analysis" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:analysis">4</a>.

The first lemma lifts the binary factorization to every power $q$ of two; only idempotence is needed for this step.

<a id="stmt-lem-lift"></a>
<div id="lem:lift" class="lemma">

**Lemma 5**. *Let $G$ be a group, $m,r\ge1$, $D$ an odd positive integer, and $P\in\operatorname{M}_m(\mathbb Q[G])$ satisfy $P^2=P$ and $DP\in\operatorname{M}_m(\mathbb Z[G])$. Suppose $\bar P=uv$ over $\mathbb F_2[G]$, where $u$ and $v$ have respective sizes $m\times r$ and $r\times m$. For $q=2^k$, $k\ge1$, the reduction $e=P_q$ has a factorization $e=u_qv_q$ of the same sizes over $(\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z)[G]$.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-lift"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-lift</summary>

*Proof.* Since $D$ is odd, every coefficient of $P$ has a well-defined reduction modulo $q$. Set $\mathcal R=(\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z)[G]$ and choose arbitrary finite lifts $u_0,v_0$ of $u,v$ over $\mathcal R$. In the corner ring $e\operatorname{M}_m(\mathcal R)e$, whose identity is $e$, put $$Z=eu_0v_0e,\qquad W=e-Z.$$ Modulo two, $Z$ is $\bar Puv\bar P=\bar P^3=\bar P$. Every coefficient of $W$ is therefore even. A product of $k$ such coefficients is divisible by $2^k$, giving $W^k=0$. This is nilpotence in the even ideal of the corner ring; the corner ring itself need not be nilpotent.

Because $Z=e-W$, the finite geometric sum $$Q=e+W+\cdots+W^{k-1}$$ satisfies $QZ=ZQ=e-W^k=e$. When $k=1$, this sum is just $e$. Thus $Q$ is a corner inverse of $Z$, not a claimed inverse in the entire matrix ring. Set $$u_q=Qeu_0,\qquad v_q=v_0e.$$ Then $u_qv_q=Qeu_0v_0e=QZ=e$. Only finite sums and products were used, so the resulting supports remain finite. ◻

</details>

For the integral construction, choose $q=2^k>1$ with $q\equiv1\pmod D$. Such a power exists because two is a unit modulo the odd integer $D$; for $D=1$, any $2^k>1$ works. Choose integral lifts $U,V$ of the factors from Lemma <a href="#lem:lift" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:lift">5</a>. The diagonal operator with the desired expansion and contraction is $$J=-q^{-1}P-(I_m-P),\qquad B=\operatorname{diag}(qI_r,J).$$ The minus signs leave its positive square unchanged and will produce the subtraction $UV-P$ needed for integrality. Prescribe the upper-left block $qI_r$ and off-diagonal blocks $V,U$. Eliminating these off-diagonal blocks subtracts $U(qI_r)^{-1}V=UV/q$ from the lower-right block. To leave $J$ as the Schur complement, that block must therefore be $UV/q+J$. Thus the factorization modulo $q$ is exactly what is needed to cancel the denominators in this block.

<a id="stmt-lem-integral"></a>
<div id="lem:integral" class="lemma">

<a id="eq:integral-matrix"></a>
**Lemma 6**. *Suppose $P=P^*=P^2\in\operatorname{M}_m(\mathbb Q[G])$, an odd $D>0$ clears its denominators, and $\bar P=uv$ factors through $r\ge1$ columns. Let $q=2^k>1$ satisfy $q\equiv1\pmod D$. Choose arbitrary integral lifts $U,V$ of the factors supplied by Lemma <a href="#lem:lift" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:lift">5</a>. Then $$\label{eq:integral-matrix}
 A=\begin{pmatrix}
 qI_r&V\\
 U&q^{-1}\bigl(UV-P-q(I_m-P)\bigr)
 \end{pmatrix}
 \in\operatorname{M}_{r+m}(\mathbb Z[G])$$ has an inverse in $\operatorname{M}_{r+m}(\mathbb Q[G])$.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-integral"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-integral</summary>

*Proof.* The matrix $P+q(I_m-P)=qI_m+(1-q)P$ is integral because $D\mid(q-1)$. Its reduction modulo $q$ is $e=P_q$, which is also the reduction of $UV$. Thus every coefficient of $UV-P-q(I_m-P)$ is an integer divisible by $q$; the bottom-right block of Equation <a href="#eq:integral-matrix" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:integral-matrix">[eq:integral-matrix]</a> is integral.

<a id="eq:path"></a>
For $J$ and $B$ defined above, the complementary projection identities give $J^{-1}=-qP-(I_m-P)$. For $x\in[0,1]$ put $$L_x=\begin{pmatrix}I_r&0\\xU/q&I_m\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 R_x=\begin{pmatrix}I_r&xV/q\\0&I_m\end{pmatrix}.$$ Their inverses negate the off-diagonal blocks, and multiplication without interchanging any factors gives $$\label{eq:path}
 M(x):=L_xBR_x=
 \begin{pmatrix}qI_r&xV\\xU&x^2UV/q+J\end{pmatrix}.$$ In particular $A=M(1)$, with the rational group-ring inverse $R_1^{-1}B^{-1}L_1^{-1}$. For general real $x$, both $M(x)$ and its inverse lie in the real group ring and depend continuously differentiably on $x$. No identities $PU=U$, $VP=V$, or $V=U^*$ are required of the chosen integer lifts. ◻

</details>

The construction is integral at its endpoint, though its simplifying path need not be integral. We now show that this path preserves the logarithmic determinant.

<a id="sec:analysis"></a>
### The trace calculation

Let $\mathcal A_n$ be the operator-norm closure of $\operatorname{M}_n(\mathbb C[G])$ in its left regular representation. For finite group-ring matrices define $$\tau_n(C)=\sum_{i=1}^n[1]C_{ii}.$$ The identity-coefficient formula gives $\tau_n(CD)=\tau_n(DC)$ by summing $(C_{ij})_g(D_{ji})_{g^{-1}}$ over $i,j,g$. Moreover $|\tau_n(C)|\le n\|C\|$ and $\tau_n(C^*)=\overline{\tau_n(C)}$. Thus it extends to a continuous trace on the unital $C^*$-algebra $\mathcal A_n$.

<a id="stmt-lem-logderivative"></a>
<div id="lem:logderivative" class="lemma">

**Lemma 7**. *For a continuously differentiable path $M:[0,1]\to\mathcal A_n$ of invertible elements, $$\frac{d}{dx}\tau_n\bigl(\log(M(x)^*M(x))\bigr)
 =2\operatorname{Re}\tau_n\bigl(M'(x)M(x)^{-1}\bigr).$$*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-logderivative"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-logderivative</summary>

*Proof.* Put $N=M^*M$. Continuity of $M$ and its inverse on the compact interval gives constants $0<\delta<c$ with $\delta I\le N(x)\le cI$ for all $x$. Write $W=I-N/c$, so $\|W\|\le\rho=1-\delta/c<1$. Functional calculus gives the uniformly norm-convergent series $$\log N=(\log c)I-\sum_{j\ge1}\frac{W^j}{j}.$$ Its derivative series is also uniformly norm-convergent, since $$\left\|\frac{d}{dx}\frac{W^j}{j}\right\|
 \le\sup_{y\in[0,1]}\|W'(y)\|\rho^{j-1}.$$ In the derivative of $W^j$, cyclicity makes the traces of its $j$ terms equal; commutation of $N$ and $N'$ is not assumed. Using $W'=-N'/c$ therefore gives $$\frac{d}{dx}\tau_n(\log N)
 =\frac1c\sum_{j\ge1}\tau_n(W^{j-1}N')
 =\tau_n(N^{-1}N').$$ Since $N'=M'^*M+M^*M'$, the second summand contributes $\tau_n(M'M^{-1})$. Cyclicity and $MN^{-1}=(M^*)^{-1}$ turn the first into $$\tau_n((M^*)^{-1}M'^*)
 =\overline{\tau_n(M'M^{-1})}.$$ Their sum is the asserted real part. This is the tracial logarithmic derivative of (Fuglede and Kadison 1952, Lemma 2) <a id="pm-cite-12"></a>[[FK1952](#ref-FK1952)], written with the unnormalized trace used here. ◻

</details>

<a id="stmt-lem-shears"></a>
<div id="lem:shears" class="lemma">

**Lemma 8**. *Let $r,m\ge1$ and let $B\in\mathcal A_{r+m}$ be invertible. Let $S,T$ have respective sizes $m\times r$ and $r\times m$ over the operator-norm closure of $\mathbb C[G]$. For $x\in[0,1]$, let $L_x,R_x$ be lower and upper block shears with diagonal blocks $I_r,I_m$ and off-diagonal blocks $xS,xT$, respectively. Then $\tau_{r+m}(\log((L_xBR_x)^*(L_xBR_x)))$ is constant.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-shears"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-shears</summary>

*Proof.* Write $M=L_xBR_x$. Both shears have inverses obtained by negating their off-diagonal blocks. The ordered product rule gives $$M'M^{-1}=L_x'L_x^{-1}
 +(L_xB)(R_x'R_x^{-1})(L_xB)^{-1}.$$ The matrices $L_x'L_x^{-1}$ and $R_x'R_x^{-1}$ have zero diagonal blocks, so each has trace zero. Cyclicity gives trace zero also for the conjugate $(L_xB)(R_x'R_x^{-1})(L_xB)^{-1}$. Lemma <a href="#lem:logderivative" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:logderivative">7</a> now gives zero derivative. ◻

</details>

<a id="stmt-prop-criterion"></a>
<div id="prop:criterion" class="proposition">

<a id="eq:criterion-trace"></a>
<a id="eq:criterion-det"></a>
**Proposition 9**. *Let $G$ be a discrete group, $m,r\ge1$, and let $P=P^*=P^2\in\operatorname{M}_m(\mathbb Q[G])$. Suppose an odd $D>0$ clears its denominators and $\bar P=uv$ with $u\in\operatorname{M}_{m,r}(\mathbb F_2[G])$ and $v\in\operatorname{M}_{r,m}(\mathbb F_2[G])$. For every $q=2^k>1$ congruent to one modulo $D$, the matrix $A$ of Lemma <a href="#lem:integral" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:integral">6</a> satisfies $$\begin{aligned}
 \tau_{r+m}(\log(T_A^*T_A))
   &=2(r-\tau_m(P))\log q,\label{eq:criterion-trace}\\
 \log\det_{\mathcal N(G)}(T_A)&=(r-\tau_m(P))\log q.
 \label{eq:criterion-det}
\end{aligned}$$ It is integral and rationally invertible, and its positive-square spectral measure has support in a compact subinterval of $(0,\infty)$.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-prop-criterion"><summary>Proof of stmt-prop-criterion</summary>

<a id="eq:criterion-integral"></a>
*Proof.* Lemma <a href="#lem:integral" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:integral">6</a> has already established integrality and the finite-support rational inverse. Apply Lemma <a href="#lem:shears" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:shears">8</a> to Equation <a href="#eq:path" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:path">[eq:path]</a>. At $x=0$ the matrix is $B=\operatorname{diag}(qI_r,-q^{-1}P-(I_m-P))$. Orthogonality gives $$B^*B=\operatorname{diag}(q^2I_r,q^{-2}P+I_m-P),\qquad
 \log(B^*B)=2\log q\,\operatorname{diag}(I_r,-P).$$ Taking the trace and using shear invariance proves Equation <a href="#eq:criterion-trace" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:criterion-trace">[eq:criterion-trace]</a>; half this trace is Equation <a href="#eq:criterion-det" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:criterion-det">[eq:criterion-det]</a>. The regular representation of the rational group-ring inverse is bounded, so $$\|T_A^{-1}\|^{-2}I\le T_A^*T_A\le\|T_A\|^2I.$$ Its logarithm is therefore bounded. In the probability measure convention from the introduction this also gives $$\label{eq:criterion-integral}
 \int_{(0,\infty)}\log t\,d\mu_A(t)
 =\frac{2(r-\tau_m(P))\log q}{r+m}.$$ ◻

</details>

<a id="sec:assembly"></a>
### Assembly and exact determinant

<details id="proof-stmt-prop-criterion-2"><summary>Proof of stmt-prop-criterion</summary>

*Proof of Theorem <a href="#thm:main" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:main">1</a>.* Take $K_2,H_2,a_2,b_2$ from Theorem <a href="#thm:input" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:input">2</a>. Apply Lemma <a href="#lem:binary" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:binary">3</a> with $d=[K_2:\mathbb F_2]\ge1$ and then Proposition <a href="#prop:separation" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:separation">4</a>. It gives the group $G_D=(\bigoplus_{H_2}C_3)\rtimes H_2$ and a projection $P$ of size $d+1$ whose binary reduction factors through $d$ columns. Its trace is $d+\theta$, where $$\theta=\frac13\left(\frac23\right)^{|S|}>0,
 \qquad D=3^{1+|S|}.$$ The odd integer $D$ clears the coefficients of $P$. Choose $k\ge1$ with $q=2^k\equiv1\pmod D$. Proposition <a href="#prop:criterion" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:criterion">9</a>, with $r=d$ and $m=d+1$, produces $A\in\operatorname{M}_{2d+1}(\mathbb Z[G_D])$ with a rational group-ring inverse and $$\log\det_{\mathcal N(G_D)}(T_A)=-\theta\log q.$$ Since $q>1$ and $\theta>0$, this proves <a id="eq:final-det"></a>$$\label{eq:final-det}
 0<\det_{\mathcal N(G_D)}(T_A)=q^{-\theta}<1.$$ With $n=2d+1$, Equation <a href="#eq:criterion-integral" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:criterion-integral">[eq:criterion-integral]</a> reads <a id="eq:final-integral"></a>$$\label{eq:final-integral}
 \int_{(0,\infty)}\log t\,d\mu_A(t)
 =-\frac{2\theta\log q}{2d+1}<0.$$ It is finite because of the spectral bounds in Proposition <a href="#prop:criterion" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:criterion">9</a>. Finally, a finite generating set of $H_2$ together with $z_1$ generates $G_D$, since $z_h=hz_1h^{-1}$. Thus $G_D$ is finitely generated. ◻

</details>

The target group differs from the companion’s group and contains the cyclic coordinates of order three. The construction has a finite negative logarithmic determinant and no zero spectral mass; it is a failure of the asserted lower bound, not of determinant class. Applying the sofic determinant bound of (Elek and Szabó 2005, Theorem 5) <a id="pm-cite-13"></a>[[ElekSzabo2005](#ref-ElekSzabo2005)] to the positive integral matrix $A^*A$ proves that $G_D$ is not sofic. This last implication is an external consequence, not an ingredient in constructing $A$.
