## 1. Abstract and result contract

> For a countably branching tree, the closed real <i>L</i><sup>1</sup> spans of products of independent exponential or Gaussian-square multipliers along its paths have positive averaged asymptotic midpoint moduli and admit no equivalent asymptotically uniformly convex norm. The renorming obstruction holds for every positive nonconstant mean-one multiplier with finite second moment.

- **C1** (theorem, [source](#stmt-thm-main)): For every multiplier $W$ as above, the Banach space $X_W$ is infinite dimensional and has no equivalent AUC norm. For the exponential and Gaussian-square laws, the inherited norms satisfy $$\begin{aligned} \widehat\delta_{X_{\mathrm{exp}}}(t) &\ge \frac{7t}{30}c(10/t)>0 &&(t>0),\label{eq:main-exp}\\ \widehat\delta_{X_{\mathrm{sq}}}(t) &\ge\frac{\mathbb P\bigl(\|G\|\ge4\sqrt2(2+\sqrt2)/t\bigr)}{8}>0 &&(t>0),\label{eq:main-sq24}\\ \widehat\delta_{X_{\mathrm{sq}}}(t) &\ge\frac{\mathbb P\bigl(\|G\|\ge8\sqrt6/t\bigr)}{4}>0 &&(t>0).\label{eq:main-sq12} \end{aligned}$$
  - Explanation: The full theorem separates AMUC of the inherited norms for the two specified laws from the obstruction to every equivalent AUC norm for the general multiplier class.
- **C2** (bound, [source](#stmt-prob-first-moment)): For the representation <a href="#prob:first-exit" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="prob:first-exit">[prob:first-exit]</a>, $$\begin{aligned} \lVert y\rVert_1&\le\bigl(\sqrt{m_2-1}+2\bigr)\mathbb E\sigma, \label{prob:first-moment-centered}\\ \lVert y\rVert_1&\le2\sqrt{m_2}\,\mathbb E\sigma. \label{prob:first-moment-signed} \end{aligned}$$ Both inequalities also hold conditionally on $\mathcal G_T$, with $\mathbb E(\|y\|\mid\mathcal G_T)$ and $\mathbb E(\sigma\mid\mathcal G_T)$ in place of the unconditional first moments.
  - Explanation: Conditional symmetrization bounds the tail norm by the expected Euclidean coefficient size; both constants are available.
- **C3** (conditional_result, [source](#stmt-lem-affine)): Fix $t>0$ and nonnegative constants $A,B$. Suppose that, for every finite predecessor-closed $T$, every $u\in E_T$, and every finite-product combination $y\in F_T$, $$M_t(u,y)\ge A\lVert y\rVert_1-B\lVert u\rVert_1.$$ Then this inequality holds for all $y\in F_T$, and $$\widehat\delta_{X_W}(t)\ge\max\{0,A-B\}.$$
  - Explanation: Density and continuity extend finite-tail affine bounds to closed tails; approximating each center yields the averaged modulus bound.
- **C4** (bound, [source](#stmt-prop-exponential)): For every $t,K>0$, the exponential product space satisfies $$\label{eq:exponential-parameter} \widehat\delta_{X_{\mathrm{exp}}}(t) \ge c(K)\left(\frac t3-\frac1K\right)_+.$$ In particular, the exponential estimate in Theorem <a href="#thm:main" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:main">1</a> holds.
  - Explanation: The Gaussian scale-mixture excess supplies a parametric exponential-law bound.
- **C5** (bound, [source](#stmt-rem-finite-head)): The excess estimate also permits a direct passage using a fixed positive approximation tolerance, rather than letting the center error tend to zero as in Lemma <a href="#lem:affine" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:affine">3</a>. Fix $t>0$, a finite predecessor-closed subtree $T$ containing the root, $u\in E_T$, and a finite-product combination $y\in F_T$. If $\lVert u\rVert_1\le2$ and $\lVert y\rVert_1\ge9/10$, then $\mathbb E\sigma\ge3/10$. On $A=\{\|u\|\le10\sigma\}$, at least $1/10$ of the expected coefficient size remains, because $\mathbb E(\sigma\mathbf1_{A^c})\le1/5$. Consequently <a href="#prob:exponential-excess" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="prob:exponential-excess">[prob:exponential-excess]</a>, with $K=10/t$, gives $$M_t(u,y)\ge \frac t{10}c(10/t).$$ Finite-tail approximation extends this last bound to every $y\in F_T$ with $\lVert y\rVert_1\ge1$. Put $L_t=t c(10/t)/10$. Approximating a unit center with error less than $\min\{1,L_t/4\}$ ensures $\lVert u\rVert_1<2$ and loses less than $L_t/2$ by <a href="#prob:continuity" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="prob:continuity">[prob:continuity]</a>. Thus this passage gives the weaker estimate $\widehat\delta_{X_{\mathrm{exp}}}(t)\ge t c(10/t)/20$ for every $t>0$ directly, without using the affine conclusion of Lemma <a href="#lem:affine" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:affine">3</a>.
  - Explanation: A fixed positive center tolerance gives an alternative weaker exponential bound.
- **C6** (bound, [source](#stmt-prob-gaussian-24)): With $C=2+\sqrt2$, for every $t,\lambda>0$, $$\label{eq:gaussian-parameter-24} \widehat\delta_{X_{\mathrm{sq}}}(t) \ge\frac{\mathbb P(\|G\|\ge\sqrt2\lambda)}{24} \left(\frac{t\lambda}C-1\right)_+.$$
  - Explanation: A union bound transfers Gaussian-difference tails to arbitrary shifts, with the centered first-moment constant.
- **C7** (bound, [source](#stmt-prob-gaussian-12)): For every $t,K>0$, the same norm satisfies $$\label{eq:gaussian-parameter-12} \widehat\delta_{X_{\mathrm{sq}}}(t) \ge\frac{\mathbb P(\|G\|\ge\sqrt2K)}{12} \left(\frac{tK}{2\sqrt3}-1\right)_+.$$
  - Explanation: Even convexity transfers a Gaussian-difference tail directly to midpoint gain, with the signed first-moment constant.

## 2. Definitions, validity and bounds

- **Finite-codimensional subspaces** ([source](#pm-h-2)): For a norm $N$ on an infinite-dimensional real Banach space $X$, let $\operatorname{cof}(X)$ denote the closed linear subspaces of finite codimension.
- **Averaged midpoint and one-sided asymptotic moduli** ([source](#pm-h-2)): For $t>0$, define the averaged asymptotic midpoint modulus and the usual one-sided asymptotic modulus (Johnson et al. 2002, sec. 2.1) [[JLPS2002](#ref-JLPS2002)] by $$\begin{aligned} \widehat\delta_N(t) &=\inf_{N(x)=1}\sup_{F\in\operatorname{cof}(X)} \inf_{\substack{y\in F\\N(y)\ge1}} \left(\frac{N(x+ty)+N(x-ty)}2-1\right),\\ \overline\delta_N(t) &=\inf_{N(x)=1}\sup_{F\in\operatorname{cof}(X)} \inf_{\substack{y\in F\\N(y)=1}}\bigl(N(x+ty)-1\bigr). \end{aligned}$$
- **AUC** ([source](#pm-h-2)): The norm is *asymptotically uniformly convex* (AUC) if $\overline\delta_N(t)>0$ for every $t>0$.
- **AMUC** ([source](#pm-h-2)): A norm is *asymptotically midpoint uniformly convex* (AMUC) when this maximum modulus is positive for every $t>0$ (Dilworth et al. 2016, Definition 2.2) [[DKRRZ2016](#ref-DKRRZ2016)].
- **Countably branching tree** ([source](#pm-h-2)): Let $\mathcal T=\mathbb N^{<\omega}$ be the tree of finite sequences of positive integers, with root $\varnothing$.
- **Multiplier hypotheses** ([source](#pm-h-2)): Fix a positive nonconstant real random variable $W$ such that $$\mathbb EW=1,\qquad m_2:=\mathbb EW^2<\infty.$$
- **Path products and closed real product span** ([source](#pm-h-2)): On a countable product probability space take independent copies $W_v$, indexed by the nonroot vertices, and define $$P_{\varnothing}=1,\qquad P_v=P_{v^-}W_v, \qquad X_W= \overline{\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}\{P_v:v\in\mathcal T\}}^{\,L^1}.$$
- **Exponential and Gaussian-square spaces** ([source](#pm-h-2)): Write $X_{\mathrm{exp}}$ for the law with density $e^{-w}$ on $(0,\infty)$, and $X_{\mathrm{sq}}$ for the law of $G^2$, where $G$ is a standard real Gaussian.
- **Gaussian excess constant and positive part** ([source](#pm-h-2)): For $K>0$ set $$c(K)=\frac1{16}\mathbb E(\|G\|-2K)_+,\qquad r_+=\max\{r,0\}.$$
- **Finite conditional head** ([source](#prob:head-projection)): Write $\mathcal G_T$ for the $\sigma$-field generated by the coordinates $W_v$ with $v\in T\setminus\{\varnothing\}$.
- **Conditional projection kernel** ([source](#prob:head-projection)): Consequently $Q_T$ restricts to a contractive projection of $X_W$ onto the finite-dimensional space $$E_T=\operatorname{span}\{P_v:v\in T\}, \qquad F_T=\ker(Q_T\|_{X_W})\in\operatorname{cof}(X_W).$$
- **Child increments** ([source](#sec:obstruction)): For each fixed vertex $v$ and its children $v^\frown n$, put $$D_{v,n}=P_{v^\frown n}-P_v=P_v(W_{v^\frown n}-1).$$
- **First-exit coefficients** ([source](#prob:first-exit)): Factoring out the multiplier at each such exit gives $$d=\sum_{v\in S\cap T}\alpha_vP_v,\qquad B_i=P_{i^-}\sum_{\substack{v\in S\\i\preceq v}} \alpha_v\prod_{i\prec w\preceq v}W_w\quad(i\in I),$$ where an empty product is $1$.
- **Coefficient means** ([source](#prob:first-exit)): Put $m_i=\mathbb E(B_i\mid\mathcal G_T)$.
- **Conditional coefficient field** ([source](#prob:first-exit)): Write $$\mathcal H_T=\mathcal G_T\vee\sigma(B_i:i).$$

## 6. Applicability limits and search

- **L1** (hypothesis): The renorming obstruction requires a positive nonconstant mean-one multiplier with finite second moment, independent vertex copies, and the specified closed real L1 product span. Blocked use: ['Applying the obstruction when positivity, nonconstancy, independence, mean one, finite second moment, or the product-span model is unverified.']
- **L2** (counterexample_scope): Positive averaged midpoint estimates are established only for the exponential and Gaussian-square laws; the general multiplier conclusion is the renorming obstruction. Blocked use: ['Claiming positive averaged midpoint bounds for every admissible multiplier law.']
- **L3** (counterexample_scope): Averaged midpoint growth implies the maximum midpoint bound but does not establish AUC renormability for these spaces. Blocked use: ['Inferring one-sided modulus positivity or an equivalent AUC norm from an averaged midpoint estimate.']
- **L4** (formal_scope): The averaged modulus uses directions with norm at least one, whereas the standard maximum midpoint modulus uses unit directions; the estimates concern the displayed average. Blocked use: ['Replacing the average by a one-sided norm or interchanging the direction constraints without proof.']
- **L5** (unknown): Reflexivity, measure precompactness of the unit ball, and the Daugavet property are not asserted for the constructed closed product span. Blocked use: ['Transferring those properties from background constructions to X_W.']
- **L6** (unknown): Optimality of the displayed constants is ABSENT. Blocked use: ['Presenting the modulus lower bounds as optimal values or exact modulus formulas.']
- **L7** (hypothesis): The conditional projection construction requires a finite subtree containing the root and all predecessors. Blocked use: ['Using the finite-rank projection claim for an arbitrary infinite head or a set missing predecessors.']
- **L8** (approximation): First-exit coefficients are constructed for finite-product combinations; completed-tail bounds are obtained by continuity and density rather than an infinite first-exit representation. Blocked use: ['Assuming an infinite coefficient expansion or extra integrability for an arbitrary completed tail.']
- **L9** (hypothesis): The head subspace is chosen from the center and scale before all tail directions are tested; uniformity over directions must be preserved. Blocked use: ['Choosing a separate finite-codimensional subspace for each direction to claim a modulus lower bound.']
- **L10** (edge_case): Zero coefficient size must be treated without normalization in the exponential scalar estimate. Blocked use: ['Dividing by sigma when all first-exit coefficients vanish.']
- **L11** (formal_scope): Neither Gaussian route assumes symmetry of the shifted original variable. Blocked use: ['Justifying a shifted Gaussian-square tail bound by unsupported symmetry of the original variable.']
- **L12** (hypothesis): The weak-null proof needs a finite L2 bound at each fixed parent; it does not provide a depth-uniform L2 estimate. Blocked use: ['Assuming the child-increment second moments remain uniformly bounded over tree depth.']
- **L13** (nonconstructive): The Banach-space renorming obstruction and positive moduli quantify over an infinite-dimensional completion and all finite-codimensional directions. Finite enumeration alone cannot certify these global conclusions. Blocked use: ['Presenting a finite sample, finite tree truncation, or numerical simulation as a certificate of the full theorem.']

> ## Scope
>
> The paper constructs a Banach space whose midpoint geometry is asymptotically uniformly convex even though no equivalent norm is asymptotically uniformly convex in the usual one-sided sense. The formalization proves that the full dual of the specified segment-norm completion is infinite-dimensional, separable, and reflexive; its averaged midpoint modulus is at least $\sqrt{1+t^2/12}-1$ for every $t>0$, while every equivalent norm fails asymptotic uniform convexity. It also proves that an embedding of a depth-$k$ countably branching diamond has distortion at least $\sqrt{1+k/12}$.
>
> The general-forest and word-forest results use the closed span of the coordinate functionals. Their conclusions retain the finite-ancestor and equivalent-norm hypotheses, including the scale $\alpha/(2\beta)$ when $0<\alpha\le\beta$ and the new norm lies between $\alpha$ and $\beta$ times the original norm; they do not identify that span with the full dual or assert reflexivity for every forest.
>
> The paper controls midpoint lenses in Banach spaces defined by tree-segment norms. The formalization proves the following estimate in both the full dual of the finite-height forest space and the coordinate predual of the infinite-tree space. If $x$ is supported on a finite ancestral set $H$, $R\ge0$, and $\|x+y\|,\|x-y\|\le R$, then the part of $y$ outside $H$ has norm at most $2\sqrt{R^2-\|x\|^2}$.
>
> The formalization also covers selected consequences for averaged midpoint moduli and separated families of vectors, together with reflexivity and renorming obstructions. This scope concerns the segment-space results; the companion results on diamond energies remain separate.
>
> The paper studies embeddings of countably branching diamond graphs into Banach spaces built from tree segments. The formalization proves that every embedding of the depth-$k$ diamond with distortion $D$, at any positive scale, satisfies $1+k/4\le D^2$ in either of two spaces: the full dual of the completed finite-height forest space and the closed coordinate span in the dual of the infinite-tree space. The bound applies to all pairs of vertices and is unconditional.

```lean
import Mathlib

namespace OAI

noncomputable section

open MeasureTheory ProbabilityTheory Set Filter
open scoped ENNReal NNReal Topology

universe uX

namespace IndependentProducts

/-- The tree of finite sequences of positive integers. -/
abbrev Vertex := List ℕ+
/-- Coordinates are attached only to nonroot vertices. -/
abbrev Edge := {v : Vertex // v ≠ []}
abbrev Sample := Edge → ℝ

/-- The product law of independent copies of the multiplier. -/
def productMeasure (μ : Measure ℝ) : Measure Sample :=
  Measure.infinitePi (fun _ : Edge => μ)

/-- The nonroot prefix ending at position `j`. -/
def pathPrefix (v : Vertex) (j : Fin v.length) : Edge :=
  ⟨v.take (j.val + 1), by
    intro h
    have hl := congrArg List.length h
    simp only [List.length_take, List.length_nil] at hl
    have := j.isLt
    omega⟩

/-- Root product is the empty product 1; every other product uses the path prefixes. -/
def pathProduct (v : Vertex) (ω : Sample) : ℝ :=
  ∏ j : Fin v.length, ω (pathPrefix v j)

/-- The closed real L1 span of the path products. -/
def productSpan (μ : Measure ℝ) : Submodule ℝ (Lp ℝ 1 (productMeasure μ)) :=
  (Submodule.span ℝ {f : Lp ℝ 1 (productMeasure μ) | ∃ v : Vertex,
    (f : Sample → ℝ) =ᵐ[productMeasure μ] pathProduct v}).topologicalClosure

/-- Positivity, nonconstancy, mean one, and finite second moment of the multiplier law. -/
structure MultiplierHypotheses (μ : Measure ℝ) : Prop where
  probability : IsProbabilityMeasure μ
  positive : ∀ᵐ w ∂μ, 0 < w
  nonconstant : ∀ c : ℝ, ¬ (∀ᵐ w ∂μ, w = c)
  mean_one : ∫ w, w ∂μ = 1
  second_moment : MemLp (fun w : ℝ => w) 2 μ

variable (X : Type uX) [NormedAddCommGroup X] [NormedSpace ℝ X]

/-- Closed subspaces of finite codimension, with respect to the inherited topology. -/
def CofiniteSubspaces : Set (Submodule ℝ X) :=
  {F | IsClosed (F : Set X) ∧ FiniteDimensional ℝ (X ⧸ F)}

/-- The averaged asymptotic midpoint modulus, with directions of norm at least 1. -/
def averagedMidpointModulus (N : Seminorm ℝ X) (t : ℝ) : ℝ :=
  sInf {r | ∃ x : X, N x = 1 ∧ r =
    sSup {a | ∃ F ∈ CofiniteSubspaces X, a =
      sInf {b | ∃ y : X, y ∈ F ∧ 1 ≤ N y ∧
        b = (N (x + t • y) + N (x - t • y)) / 2 - 1}}}

/-- The usual one-sided modulus, whose directions have norm exactly 1. -/
def oneSidedModulus (N : Seminorm ℝ X) (t : ℝ) : ℝ :=
  sInf {r | ∃ x : X, N x = 1 ∧ r =
    sSup {a | ∃ F ∈ CofiniteSubspaces X, a =
      sInf {b | ∃ y : X, y ∈ F ∧ N y = 1 ∧ b = N (x + t • y) - 1}}}

/-- A seminorm with these bounds is a genuine norm equivalent to the original norm. -/
def IsEquivalentNorm (N : Seminorm ℝ X) : Prop :=
  ∃ a b : ℝ, 0 < a ∧ a ≤ b ∧ ∀ x, a * ‖x‖ ≤ N x ∧ N x ≤ b * ‖x‖

def IsAUC (N : Seminorm ℝ X) : Prop := ∀ t : ℝ, 0 < t → 0 < oneSidedModulus X N t

/-- Gaussian-tail probability, in real rather than extended-nonnegative-real notation. -/
def gaussianTail (r : ℝ) : ℝ :=
  (gaussianReal 0 1 {x : ℝ | r ≤ |x|}).toReal

/-- The Gaussian excess constant at threshold twice the parameter. -/
def excessConstant (K : ℝ) : ℝ :=
  (1 / 16 : ℝ) * ∫ x : ℝ, max (|x| - 2 * K) 0 ∂gaussianReal 0 1

/-- The density exp(-w) on the positive half-line. -/
def exponentialLaw : Measure ℝ := expMeasure 1

/-- The law of the square of a standard real Gaussian. -/
def gaussianSquareLaw : Measure ℝ := (gaussianReal 0 1).map (fun x : ℝ => x ^ 2)

theorem main_general (μ : Measure ℝ) (hμ : MultiplierHypotheses μ) :
    ¬ FiniteDimensional ℝ (productSpan μ) ∧
    ∀ N : Seminorm ℝ (productSpan μ),
      IsEquivalentNorm (productSpan μ) N → ¬ IsAUC (productSpan μ) N := by
  sorry

theorem main_exponential (t : ℝ) (ht : 0 < t) :
    (7 * t / 30) * excessConstant (10 / t) ≤
      averagedMidpointModulus (productSpan exponentialLaw)
        (normSeminorm ℝ (productSpan exponentialLaw)) t ∧
    0 < (7 * t / 30) * excessConstant (10 / t) := by
  sorry

theorem main_gaussian_24 (t : ℝ) (ht : 0 < t) :
    gaussianTail (4 * Real.sqrt 2 * (2 + Real.sqrt 2) / t) / 8 ≤
      averagedMidpointModulus (productSpan gaussianSquareLaw)
        (normSeminorm ℝ (productSpan gaussianSquareLaw)) t ∧
    0 < gaussianTail (4 * Real.sqrt 2 * (2 + Real.sqrt 2) / t) / 8 := by
  sorry

theorem main_gaussian_12 (t : ℝ) (ht : 0 < t) :
    gaussianTail (8 * Real.sqrt 6 / t) / 4 ≤
      averagedMidpointModulus (productSpan gaussianSquareLaw)
        (normSeminorm ℝ (productSpan gaussianSquareLaw)) t ∧
    0 < gaussianTail (8 * Real.sqrt 6 / t) / 4 := by
  sorry

end IndependentProducts

end

end OAI
```

## 7. Complete mathematical paper


For a countably branching tree, the closed real <i>L</i><sup>1</sup> spans of products of independent exponential or Gaussian-square multipliers along its paths have positive averaged asymptotic midpoint moduli and admit no equivalent asymptotically uniformly convex norm. The renorming obstruction holds for every positive nonconstant mean-one multiplier with finite second moment.

### Introduction

A norm may force the average distance to two opposite points to grow in every sufficiently large direction, yet have no equivalent norm with the corresponding one-sided growth. We construct two explicit real $L^1$ spaces that exhibit this distinction. In both spaces, multiplying along a tree has two effects: fresh variables force midpoint growth, while path products of $L^1$ norm one prevent asymptotically uniformly convex renormings.

For a norm $N$ on an infinite-dimensional real Banach space $X$, let $\operatorname{cof}(X)$ denote the closed linear subspaces of finite codimension. For $t>0$, define the averaged asymptotic midpoint modulus and the usual one-sided asymptotic modulus (Johnson et al. 2002, sec. 2.1) <a id="pm-cite-1"></a>[[JLPS2002](#ref-JLPS2002)] by $$\begin{aligned}
 \widehat\delta_N(t)
 &=\inf_{N(x)=1}\sup_{F\in\operatorname{cof}(X)}
   \inf_{\substack{y\in F\\N(y)\ge1}}
   \left(\frac{N(x+ty)+N(x-ty)}2-1\right),\\
 \overline\delta_N(t)
 &=\inf_{N(x)=1}\sup_{F\in\operatorname{cof}(X)}
   \inf_{\substack{y\in F\\N(y)=1}}\bigl(N(x+ty)-1\bigr).
\end{aligned}$$ The norm is *asymptotically uniformly convex* (AUC) if $\overline\delta_N(t)>0$ for every $t>0$. The subspace $F$ may depend on the center $x$ and on $t$; it must work for all the indicated directions. The standard asymptotic midpoint modulus uses unit directions $N(y)=1$ and replaces the displayed average by the maximum of the two norms. A norm is *asymptotically midpoint uniformly convex* (AMUC) when this maximum modulus is positive for every $t>0$ (Dilworth et al. 2016, Definition 2.2) <a id="pm-cite-2"></a>[[DKRRZ2016](#ref-DKRRZ2016)]. All numerical midpoint estimates below concern the displayed average. They imply the same lower bounds for the maximum, and hence AMUC, because the maximum dominates the average.

Let $\mathcal T=\mathbb N^{<\omega}$ be the tree of finite sequences of positive integers, with root $\varnothing$. For a nonroot vertex $v$, write $v^-$ for its parent. Fix a positive nonconstant real random variable $W$ such that $$\mathbb EW=1,\qquad m_2:=\mathbb EW^2<\infty.$$ On a countable product probability space take independent copies $W_v$, indexed by the nonroot vertices, and define $$P_{\varnothing}=1,\qquad P_v=P_{v^-}W_v,
 \qquad X_W=
 \overline{\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}\{P_v:v\in\mathcal T\}}^{\,L^1}.$$ We use the inherited real $L^1$ norm, denoted by $\lVert \cdot\rVert_1$. Write $X_{\mathrm{exp}}$ for the law with density $e^{-w}$ on $(0,\infty)$, and $X_{\mathrm{sq}}$ for the law of $G^2$, where $G$ is a standard real Gaussian. For $K>0$ set $$c(K)=\frac1{16}\mathbb E(|G|-2K)_+,\qquad r_+=\max\{r,0\}.$$

<a id="stmt-thm-main"></a>
<div id="thm:main" class="theorem">

**Theorem 1**. *For every multiplier $W$ as above, the Banach space $X_W$ is infinite dimensional and has no equivalent AUC norm. For the exponential and Gaussian-square laws, the inherited norms satisfy <a id="eq:main-exp"></a><a id="eq:main-sq24"></a><a id="eq:main-sq12"></a>$$\begin{aligned}
 \widehat\delta_{X_{\mathrm{exp}}}(t)
 &\ge \frac{7t}{30}c(10/t)>0 &&(t>0),\label{eq:main-exp}\\
 \widehat\delta_{X_{\mathrm{sq}}}(t)
 &\ge\frac{\mathbb P\bigl(|G|\ge4\sqrt2(2+\sqrt2)/t\bigr)}{8}>0
 &&(t>0),\label{eq:main-sq24}\\
 \widehat\delta_{X_{\mathrm{sq}}}(t)
 &\ge\frac{\mathbb P\bigl(|G|\ge8\sqrt6/t\bigr)}{4}>0
 &&(t>0).\label{eq:main-sq12}
\end{aligned}$$*

</div>

The three estimates come from different scalar arguments, whose parameter-dependent forms are proved below. None of the displayed constants is asserted to be optimal. The general multiplier hypothesis is used for the renorming obstruction; the positive midpoint estimates are proved for the two specified laws.

#### Context and method

Dilworth, Kutzarova, Randrianarivony, Revalski, and Zhivkov introduced AMUC and distinguished it from AUC for a given norm: they constructed an equivalent norm on $\ell_2$ that is AMUC but is not AUC (Dilworth et al. 2016, Theorem 2.4) <a id="pm-cite-3"></a>[[DKRRZ2016](#ref-DKRRZ2016)]. The renorming question is different. They proved that an AMUC space with an unconditional Schauder basis admits an equivalent AUC norm, and asked whether this remains true for every AMUC space (Dilworth et al. 2016, sec. 5 and Proposition 5.1) <a id="pm-cite-4"></a>[[DKRRZ2016](#ref-DKRRZ2016)].

Baudier answered that question negatively. He proved that a closed subspace of $L^1$ whose unit ball is relatively compact in measure is AMUC, and applied this result to the Kadets–Werner space, which admits no equivalent AUC norm (Baudier 2026, Theorem 1 and Corollary 1) <a id="pm-cite-5"></a>[[Baudier2026](#ref-Baudier2026)]. His example also satisfies a linear lower bound for the maximum midpoint modulus (Baudier 2026, final paragraph) <a id="pm-cite-6"></a>[[Baudier2026](#ref-Baudier2026)]. Theorem <a href="#thm:main" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:main">1</a> instead gives explicit spaces obtained from one repeated multiplier law, with direct proofs of quantitative bounds for the averaged modulus and of the renorming obstruction.

The construction has a useful relation to the multiplication step in the Bourgain–Rosenthal construction (Bourgain and Rosenthal 1980) <a id="pm-cite-7"></a>[[BourgainRosenthal1980](#ref-BourgainRosenthal1980)] as developed by Kadets and Werner (Kadets and Werner 2004, Lemmas 2.2 and 2.4) <a id="pm-cite-8"></a>[[KW2004](#ref-KW2004)]. Their enlargement multiplies selected old vectors by fresh nonnegative variables, chosen anew at successive enlargement stages. Here one fixed positive law with finite second moment is repeated at every vertex. Our arguments concern this closed product span directly; they make no assertion of reflexivity, measure precompactness of its unit ball, or the Daugavet property.

The main difficulty in the midpoint estimate is uniformity over all directions in a subspace of finite codimension. Conditioning on the variables in a finite predecessor-closed subtree $T$ produces a finite-rank projection on $X_W$. In a finite linear combination of products in its kernel, the terms beyond $T$ can be grouped by their first vertices outside $T$. Each group factors into the multiplier at its first exit and a coefficient depending only on old coordinates and coordinates strictly below that exit. Fixing these coefficients leaves the exit multipliers independent. An independent-copy and random-sign argument controls the vector’s $L^1$ norm by the expected Euclidean size of its coefficient list. The scalar probability estimates then force a definite midpoint gain for every such list, even in the presence of an arbitrary shift.

The three scalar mechanisms are elementary but distinct. For exponential multipliers, a difference of independent copies is a Gaussian scale mixture, which gives positive expected excess past every fixed threshold. For Gaussian squares, an orthogonal change of variables turns the difference into a conditionally Gaussian variable. One proof transfers its tail probability back to the original shifted variable by a union bound. The other uses even convexity of the midpoint increment directly on the difference.

Section <a href="#sec:conditional" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:conditional">2</a> establishes the finite-rank projections, the two coefficient estimates, and the common passage to completed tails and arbitrary centers. Section <a href="#sec:exponential" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:exponential">3</a> treats the exponential law. Section <a href="#sec:gaussian" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:gaussian">4</a> gives the two Gaussian routes. Section <a href="#sec:obstruction" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:obstruction">5</a> proves the renorming obstruction locally: path products have norm one, but their child increments are weakly null with fixed positive norm. An AUC renorming would force geometric growth along a selected branch, contradicting boundedness.

### Finite conditional kernels and fresh multipliers

We first construct the subspaces of finite codimension needed for the midpoint estimates. The point is that conditioning on finitely many coordinates has finite rank on the actual closed span $X_W$.

<a id="prob:product-moments"></a>
Independence and positivity give $$\label{prob:product-moments}
 \lVert P_v\rVert_1=1,\qquad \mathbb EP_v^2=m_2^{|v|}.$$ As a closed subspace of $L^1$, $X_W$ is a Banach space. The first-generation differences $P_{(n)}-1=W_{(n)}-1$ are nonzero and pairwise orthogonal in $L^2$, so every finite subset is linearly independent, also as elements of $L^1$. Thus $X_W$ is infinite dimensional. The first-generation products themselves are also linearly independent: taking expectations in a relation $\sum_n a_nW_{(n)}=0$ gives $\sum_n a_n=0$, reducing it to the corresponding relation among the orthogonal differences. Rational finite combinations of the countable family of products are dense, so $X_W$ is separable.

<a id="prob:head-projection"></a>
Let $T\subset\mathcal T$ be finite, contain the root, and contain every predecessor of each of its vertices. Write $\mathcal G_T$ for the $\sigma$-field generated by the coordinates $W_v$ with $v\in T\setminus\{\varnothing\}$. For $v\in\mathcal T$, let $v_T$ be its last ancestor in $T$. Conditional expectation satisfies $$\label{prob:head-projection}
 Q_TP_v:=\mathbb E(P_v\mid\mathcal G_T)=P_{v_T}.$$ Consequently $Q_T$ restricts to a contractive projection of $X_W$ onto the finite-dimensional space $$E_T=\operatorname{span}\{P_v:v\in T\},
 \qquad F_T=\ker(Q_T|_{X_W})\in\operatorname{cof}(X_W).$$ This assertion on the completion follows from the continuity of conditional expectation and the closedness of $E_T$. Applying $I-Q_T$ to finite-product approximations shows that finite-product combinations in $F_T$ are dense in $F_T$.

The same space and tails can also be described using increments. Put $u_v=P_v-P_{v^-}$ for $v\ne\varnothing$. Telescoping along paths shows that $\{1,u_v:v\ne\varnothing\}$ and $\{P_v:v\in\mathcal T\}$ have the same algebraic span. Formula <a href="#prob:head-projection" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="prob:head-projection">[prob:head-projection]</a> gives $Q_Tu_v=u_v$ for $v\in T$ and $Q_Tu_v=0$ for $v\notin T$. Applying $I-Q_T$ to the algebraic span and then passing to its closure proves <a id="prob:jump-tail"></a>$$\label{prob:jump-tail}
 F_T=\overline{\operatorname{span}\{u_v:v\notin T\}}^{\,L^1}.$$

For $t>0$ and $x,y\in X_W$, define the midpoint increment $$M_t(x,y)=\frac{\lVert x+ty\rVert_1+\lVert x-ty\rVert_1}2-\lVert x\rVert_1.$$ The real scalar identity $(|a+b|+|a-b|)/2=\max\{|a|,|b|\}$ gives <a id="prob:midpoint-identity"></a>$$\label{prob:midpoint-identity}
 M_t(x,y)=\mathbb E(t|y|-|x|)_+.$$ For $u\in E_T$ and $y\in F_T$, we seek a lower bound on this excess in terms of $\lVert y\rVert_1$ and $\lVert u\rVert_1$, uniform over $T$. Since $u$ is $\mathcal G_T$-measurable, we first separate the fresh multipliers in $y$ from the coordinates determining $u$.

<a id="prob:first-exit"></a>
Now write a finite-product combination in the kernel as $y=\sum_{v\in S}\alpha_vP_v\in F_T$, where $S\subset\mathcal T$ is finite. Write $w\preceq v$ when $w$ is an ancestor of $v$, including $v$ itself, and use $w\prec v$ for strict ancestry. Let $I$ be the finite set of first vertices outside $T$ on paths to vertices of $S\setminus T$. Factoring out the multiplier at each such exit gives $$d=\sum_{v\in S\cap T}\alpha_vP_v,\qquad
 B_i=P_{i^-}\sum_{\substack{v\in S\\i\preceq v}}
       \alpha_v\prod_{i\prec w\preceq v}W_w\quad(i\in I),$$ where an empty product is $1$. Thus $d$ is $\mathcal G_T$-measurable, and $B_i$ depends only on old coordinates and on strict descendants of $i$, not on $W_i$. Put $m_i=\mathbb E(B_i\mid\mathcal G_T)$. The factorization and the condition $Q_Ty=0$ yield $$\label{prob:first-exit}
 y=d+\sum_{i\in I}W_iB_i,\qquad d=-\sum_{i\in I}m_i,
 \qquad \sigma=\Big(\sum_{i\in I}B_i^2\Big)^{1/2}.$$ Distinct first-exit vertices have disjoint descendant subtrees. Hence, conditionally on $\mathcal G_T$, the $B_i$ are independent, and their entire vector is independent of the first-exit variables $W_i$. In particular, conditioning further on all $B_i$ leaves the $W_i$ independent with their original law. Write $$\mathcal H_T=\mathcal G_T\vee\sigma(B_i:i).$$ We will condition on $\mathcal G_T$ when copying entire coefficients, and on $\mathcal H_T$ when resampling only first-exit multipliers. Figure <a href="#fig:exit" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="fig:exit">1</a> illustrates this distinction. All quantities used here are integrable; in fact the finite products belong to $L^2$.

The next two estimates measure the size of a tail vector by the expected Euclidean size of its coefficient list. Their upper bounds on $\lVert y\rVert_1$ give lower bounds on $\mathbb E\sigma$, which the scalar estimates in Sections <a href="#sec:exponential" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:exponential">3</a> and <a href="#sec:gaussian" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:gaussian">4</a> will convert into midpoint gains. They use independent-copy symmetrization followed by averaging over independent signs; this is the standard symmetrization method, here applied conditionally on the old coordinates (Bartlett and Mendelson 2002, proof of Theorem 8, p. 468) <a id="pm-cite-9"></a>[[BartlettMendelson2002](#ref-BartlettMendelson2002)]. Both constants will be useful: they come from different ways of centering the sum.

<div id="prob:first-moment" class="lemma">

<a id="prob:first-moment-centered"></a>
<a id="prob:first-moment-signed"></a>
**Lemma 2** (Two first-moment estimates). *For the representation <a href="#prob:first-exit" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="prob:first-exit">[prob:first-exit]</a>, $$\begin{aligned}
 \lVert y\rVert_1&\le\bigl(\sqrt{m_2-1}+2\bigr)\mathbb E\sigma,
 \label{prob:first-moment-centered}\\
 \lVert y\rVert_1&\le2\sqrt{m_2}\,\mathbb E\sigma.
 \label{prob:first-moment-signed}
\end{aligned}$$ Both inequalities also hold conditionally on $\mathcal G_T$, with $\mathbb E(|y|\mid\mathcal G_T)$ and $\mathbb E(\sigma\mid\mathcal G_T)$ in place of the unconditional first moments.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-prob-first-moment"><summary>Proof of stmt-prob-first-moment</summary>

*Proof.* Work conditionally on $\mathcal G_T$, suppressing this conditioning in the expectation notation throughout the proof. Integrating the resulting inequalities gives the unconditional statements. First write $$y=\sum_iB_i(W_i-1)+\sum_i(B_i-m_i).$$ After fixing all $B_i$, the first sum has mean zero and second moment $(m_2-1)\sigma^2$. Its expected absolute value is thus at most $\sqrt{m_2-1}\,\mathbb E\sigma$ after averaging over the $B_i$. For the second sum, take independent copies $B_i'$ of the independent $B_i$. Jensen’s inequality, independent random signs $\varepsilon_i$, and Cauchy–Schwarz give $$\begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E\Big|\sum_i(B_i-m_i)\Big|
 &\le\mathbb E\Big|\sum_i(B_i-B_i')\Big|\\
 &=\mathbb E\mathbb E_{\varepsilon}\Big|\sum_i\varepsilon_i(B_i-B_i')\Big|\\
 &\le\mathbb E\Big(\sum_i(B_i-B_i')^2\Big)^{1/2}
 \le2\mathbb E\sigma.
\end{aligned}$$ The equality uses independence and symmetry of the differences. Combining the two estimates proves <a href="#prob:first-moment-centered" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="prob:first-moment-centered">[prob:first-moment-centered]</a> conditionally.

For the other estimate, put $U_i=W_iB_i$. These variables are independent, and $y=\sum_i(U_i-\mathbb EU_i)$. Symmetrizing with independent copies $U_i'$ and inserting independent signs as above yields $$\begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E|y|
 &\le\mathbb E\mathbb E_{\varepsilon}\Big|\sum_i\varepsilon_i(U_i-U_i')\Big|\\
 &\le2\mathbb E\mathbb E_{\varepsilon}\Big|\sum_i\varepsilon_iU_i\Big|
 \le2\mathbb E\Big(\sum_iW_i^2B_i^2\Big)^{1/2}.
\end{aligned}$$ With the $B_i$ fixed, concavity of the square root bounds the expectation over the $W_i$ of $\bigl(\sum_iW_i^2B_i^2\bigr)^{1/2}$ by $\sqrt{m_2}\,\sigma$. Averaging over the $B_i$ proves <a href="#prob:first-moment-signed" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="prob:first-moment-signed">[prob:first-moment-signed]</a> conditionally on $\mathcal G_T$. ◻

</details>

<a id="prob:continuity"></a>
The preceding construction applies to finite-product tails. To pass midpoint estimates to their closure, the triangle inequality gives $$\label{prob:continuity}
 |M_t(x,y)-M_t(x',y')|
 \le2\lVert x-x'\rVert_1+t\lVert y-y'\rVert_1.$$ Thus all tail estimates below pass to the completed tail subspace before we impose the condition $\lVert y\rVert_1\ge1$.

<a id="stmt-lem-affine"></a>
<div id="lem:affine" class="lemma">

**Lemma 3** (Passage to completed tails and arbitrary centers). *Fix $t>0$ and nonnegative constants $A,B$. Suppose that, for every finite predecessor-closed $T$, every $u\in E_T$, and every finite-product combination $y\in F_T$, $$M_t(u,y)\ge A\lVert y\rVert_1-B\lVert u\rVert_1.$$ Then this inequality holds for all $y\in F_T$, and $$\widehat\delta_{X_W}(t)\ge\max\{0,A-B\}.$$*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-affine"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-affine</summary>

*Proof.* The first assertion follows from density in $F_T$ and <a href="#prob:continuity" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="prob:continuity">[prob:continuity]</a>. For a unit vector $x$ and any $\varepsilon>0$, choose a finite-product combination $u$ with $\lVert x-u\rVert_1<\varepsilon$, and let $T$ contain the root and all its support paths. Then $u\in E_T$, $\lVert u\rVert_1\le1+\varepsilon$, and every $y\in F_T$ with $\lVert y\rVert_1\ge1$ satisfies $$M_t(x,y)\ge A-B-(B+2)\varepsilon.$$ For this fixed $x$, take the supremum over finite-codimensional subspaces and let $\varepsilon\downarrow0$. The resulting bound $A-B$ is independent of $x$, so it survives the final infimum. The scalar midpoint identity also gives the lower bound zero. ◻

</details>

The lemma is applied to norm inequalities, not to an infinite first-exit expansion. Thus no representation or extra integrability of a general completed tail is needed. It also fixes the order of choices: $t$ and $x$ determine $T$ before the direction $y$ is allowed to vary.

<a id="sec:exponential"></a>
### Exponential products: a uniform excess estimate

<a id="prob:exponential-excess"></a>
The exponential law supplies positive expected excess beyond every multiple of the coefficient size. A Gaussian scale mixture makes that excess uniform over all real coefficient lists and all shifts. Take $W=Z$ to have the exponential distribution with density $e^{-z}$ on $(0,\infty)$, and write $X_{\mathrm{exp}}=X_Z$. Here $m_2=2$, so Lemma <a href="#prob:first-moment" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prob:first-moment">2</a> gives $\lVert y\rVert_1\le3\mathbb E\sigma$. Let $G$ denote a standard real Gaussian and put, for $K>0$, <a id="prob:excess-constant"></a>$$\label{prob:excess-constant}
 c(K)=\frac1{16}\mathbb E(|G|-2K)_+>0.$$ The Laplace distribution as a Gaussian scale mixture belongs to the theory of normal scale mixtures (Andrews and Mallows 1974) <a id="pm-cite-10"></a>[[AndrewsMallows1974](#ref-AndrewsMallows1974)]; the characteristic-function calculation below proves the exact identity needed here. We prove the uniform conditional bound $$\label{prob:exponential-excess}
 \mathbb E\bigl((|y|-K\sigma)_+\mid\mathcal G_T,(B_i)_i\bigr)
 \ge c(K)\sigma.$$ After conditioning as indicated, all coefficients, $d$, and $\sigma$ are fixed. Resample only the exponential multipliers to obtain $y'$ with the same conditional law. The triangle inequality and the positive-part inequality give $$2\mathbb E(|y|-K\sigma)_+
 \ge\mathbb E(|y-y'|-2K\sigma)_+.$$ The difference of two independent standard exponentials has characteristic function $(1+q^2)^{-1}$ at $q\in\mathbb R$. This also equals $\mathbb E\exp(-q^2U)$ for a standard exponential $U$, so that difference has the law of $\sqrt{2U}\,G$, with $U$ and $G$ independent. Using independent such representations for the different multipliers, if $\sigma>0$ the law of $(y-y')/\sigma$ is a centered Gaussian mixture with variance $$V=\frac{2\sum_iB_i^2U_i}{\sigma^2},\qquad
 \mathbb EV=2,\qquad
 \mathbb EV^2=4\left(1+\frac{\sum_iB_i^4}{\sigma^4}\right)\le8.$$ Since $\mathbb E[V\mathbf1_{\{V\ge1\}}]\ge1$, Cauchy–Schwarz gives $\mathbb P(V\ge1)\ge1/8$. On that event the Gaussian excess above $2K$ is at least $\mathbb E(|G|-2K)_+$. Hence $$\mathbb E(|y-y'|-2K\sigma)_+
 \ge\frac{\sigma}{8}\mathbb E(|G|-2K)_+,$$ which proves <a href="#prob:exponential-excess" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="prob:exponential-excess">[prob:exponential-excess]</a>. For $\sigma=0$ the asserted lower bound is zero, so the conclusion holds without normalization.

<a id="stmt-prop-exponential"></a>
<div id="prop:exponential" class="proposition">

**Proposition 4**. *For every $t,K>0$, the exponential product space satisfies <a id="eq:exponential-parameter"></a>$$\label{eq:exponential-parameter}
 \widehat\delta_{X_{\mathrm{exp}}}(t)
 \ge c(K)\left(\frac t3-\frac1K\right)_+.$$ In particular, the exponential estimate in Theorem <a href="#thm:main" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:main">1</a> holds.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-prop-exponential"><summary>Proof of stmt-prop-exponential</summary>

*Proof.* Fix $u\in E_T$ and a finite-product combination $y\in F_T$. The event $A=\{|u|\le tK\sigma\}$ is fixed under conditioning on $\mathcal H_T$. Since $\sigma\mathbf1_{A^c}\le |u|/(tK)$, the conditional excess estimate gives $$\begin{aligned}
 M_t(u,y)
 &\ge t\mathbb E\bigl[\mathbf1_A(|y|-K\sigma)_+\bigr]
 \ge t c(K)\mathbb E(\sigma\mathbf1_A)\\
 &\ge t c(K)\mathbb E\sigma-\frac{c(K)}K\lVert u\rVert_1
 \ge c(K)\left(\frac t3\lVert y\rVert_1-\frac1K\lVert u\rVert_1\right).
\end{aligned}$$ Apply Lemma <a href="#lem:affine" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:affine">3</a>. At $K=10/t$ this gives $7t c(10/t)/30$, as asserted in Theorem <a href="#thm:main" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:main">1</a>. ◻

</details>

<a id="stmt-rem-finite-head"></a>
<div id="rem:finite-head" class="remark">

*Remark 5* (A fixed-error passage from finite tails). The excess estimate also permits a direct passage using a fixed positive approximation tolerance, rather than letting the center error tend to zero as in Lemma <a href="#lem:affine" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:affine">3</a>. Fix $t>0$, a finite predecessor-closed subtree $T$ containing the root, $u\in E_T$, and a finite-product combination $y\in F_T$. If $\lVert u\rVert_1\le2$ and $\lVert y\rVert_1\ge9/10$, then $\mathbb E\sigma\ge3/10$. On $A=\{|u|\le10\sigma\}$, at least $1/10$ of the expected coefficient size remains, because $\mathbb E(\sigma\mathbf1_{A^c})\le1/5$. Consequently <a href="#prob:exponential-excess" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="prob:exponential-excess">[prob:exponential-excess]</a>, with $K=10/t$, gives $$M_t(u,y)\ge \frac t{10}c(10/t).$$ Finite-tail approximation extends this last bound to every $y\in F_T$ with $\lVert y\rVert_1\ge1$. Put $L_t=t c(10/t)/10$. Approximating a unit center with error less than $\min\{1,L_t/4\}$ ensures $\lVert u\rVert_1<2$ and loses less than $L_t/2$ by <a href="#prob:continuity" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="prob:continuity">[prob:continuity]</a>. Thus this passage gives the weaker estimate $\widehat\delta_{X_{\mathrm{exp}}}(t)\ge t c(10/t)/20$ for every $t>0$ directly, without using the affine conclusion of Lemma <a href="#lem:affine" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:affine">3</a>.

</div>

<a id="sec:gaussian"></a>
### Gaussian squares: two scalar mechanisms

Now take $W=G^2$, where $G$ is standard Gaussian, and put $X_{\mathrm{sq}}=X_{G^2}$. Here $m_2=3$. In particular, the product and jump descriptions above give the same closed subspace with the same norm. We first establish the Gaussian difference calculation that both estimates will use, while keeping their subsequent probability and convexity steps separate.

<a id="prob:gaussian-difference"></a>
For fixed real coefficients $a_1,\ldots,a_k$ and a fixed $b\in\mathbb R$, put $$H=b+\sum_i a_i(G_i^2-1),\qquad
 s=\Big(\sum_i a_i^2\Big)^{1/2},$$ where the $G_i$ are independent standard Gaussians. Let $H'$ be an independent copy with the same coefficients and shift. The Gaussian polarization identity used in decoupling (Levina and Vershynin 2012, proof of Lemma 3.2) <a id="pm-cite-11"></a>[[LevinaVershynin2012](#ref-LevinaVershynin2012)] is especially simple here. The orthogonal change of variables $A_i=(G_i+G_i')/\sqrt2$, $C_i=(G_i-G_i')/\sqrt2$ shows that $$H-H'\ \text{has law}\ 2\sum_i a_iA_iC_i,$$ where all $A_i,C_i$ are independent standard Gaussians. Conditionally on the $A_i$, this is Gaussian with variance $4V$, where $$V=\sum_i a_i^2A_i^2,\qquad
 \mathbb EV=s^2,\qquad
 \mathbb EV^2=s^4+2\sum_i a_i^4\le3s^4.$$ This is the elementary second-moment step usually called the Paley–Zygmund inequality (Paley and Zygmund 1932b, 1932a) <a id="pm-cite-12"></a>[[PaleyZygmund1932Series](#ref-PaleyZygmund1932Series), [PaleyZygmund1932Note](#ref-PaleyZygmund1932Note)]. We include the argument: for $s>0$, the inequality $\mathbb E[V\mathbf1_{\{V\ge s^2/2\}}]\ge s^2/2$ and Cauchy–Schwarz yield $\mathbb P(V\ge s^2/2)\ge1/12$. On this event the conditional standard deviation of $H-H'$ is at least $\sqrt2s$. Consequently, for every $K>0$, $$\label{prob:gaussian-difference}
 \mathbb P(|H-H'|\ge2Ks)
 \ge q_K,\qquad q_K=\frac{\mathbb P(|G|\ge\sqrt2K)}{12}>0.$$ The event in <a href="#prob:gaussian-difference" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="prob:gaussian-difference">[prob:gaussian-difference]</a> has probability one when $s=0$, so the same statement remains valid in that case.

<a id="stmt-prob-gaussian-24"></a>
<div id="prob:gaussian-24" class="proposition">

<a id="eq:gaussian-parameter-24"></a>
**Proposition 6** (Shifted Gaussian tails). *With $C=2+\sqrt2$, for every $t,\lambda>0$, $$\label{eq:gaussian-parameter-24}
 \widehat\delta_{X_{\mathrm{sq}}}(t)
 \ge\frac{\mathbb P(|G|\ge\sqrt2\lambda)}{24}
       \left(\frac{t\lambda}C-1\right)_+.$$*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-prob-gaussian-24"><summary>Proof of stmt-prob-gaussian-24</summary>

*Proof.* For the preceding fixed-coefficient variables, $|H-H'|\ge2\lambda s$ requires $|H|\ge\lambda s$ or $|H'|\ge\lambda s$. Their equal laws and <a href="#prob:gaussian-difference" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="prob:gaussian-difference">[prob:gaussian-difference]</a> therefore imply the shift-uniform estimate <a id="prob:gaussian-shifted"></a>$$\label{prob:gaussian-shifted}
 \mathbb P(|H|\ge\lambda s)\ge p_\lambda,
 \qquad p_\lambda=\frac{\mathbb P(|G|\ge\sqrt2\lambda)}{24}.$$ Again the zero-coefficient case is immediate.

For a finite-product $y\in F_T$, condition on the old coordinates and all $B_i$ in <a href="#prob:first-exit" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="prob:first-exit">[prob:first-exit]</a>. It has the required form with $a_i=B_i$ and shift $b=d+\sum_iB_i$. Thus, for $x_0\in E_T$, <a id="prob:gaussian-24-gain"></a>$$\begin{aligned}
 M_t(x_0,y)
 &\ge p_\lambda\mathbb E(t\lambda\sigma-|x_0|)_+\notag\\
 &\ge p_\lambda\bigl(t\lambda\mathbb E\sigma-\lVert x_0\rVert_1\bigr)
 \ge p_\lambda\left(\frac{t\lambda}{C}\lVert y\rVert_1-\lVert x_0\rVert_1\right).
 \label{prob:gaussian-24-gain}
\end{aligned}$$ The last step uses <a href="#prob:first-moment-centered" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="prob:first-moment-centered">[prob:first-moment-centered]</a> with $m_2=3$. Lemma <a href="#lem:affine" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:affine">3</a> now proves <a href="#eq:gaussian-parameter-24" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:gaussian-parameter-24">[eq:gaussian-parameter-24]</a>. Taking $\lambda=4C/t$ gives the first Gaussian bound in Theorem <a href="#thm:main" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:main">1</a> for every $t>0$. ◻

</details>

<a id="stmt-prob-gaussian-12"></a>
<div id="prob:gaussian-12" class="proposition">

<a id="eq:gaussian-parameter-12"></a>
**Proposition 7** (Convex comparison with a Gaussian difference). *For every $t,K>0$, the same norm satisfies $$\label{eq:gaussian-parameter-12}
 \widehat\delta_{X_{\mathrm{sq}}}(t)
 \ge\frac{\mathbb P(|G|\ge\sqrt2K)}{12}
       \left(\frac{tK}{2\sqrt3}-1\right)_+.$$*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-prob-gaussian-12"><summary>Proof of stmt-prob-gaussian-12</summary>

<a id="prob:conditional-gaussian-difference"></a>
*Proof.* For a finite-product $y\in F_T$, fix the old coordinates and the $B_i$, and resample only the first-exit Gaussian squares to obtain $\widetilde y$. These variables have the same conditional law. Applying <a href="#prob:gaussian-difference" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="prob:gaussian-difference">[prob:gaussian-difference]</a> to their difference gives $$\label{prob:conditional-gaussian-difference}
 \mathbb P\bigl(|y-\widetilde y|\ge2K\sigma
       \mid\mathcal G_T,(B_i)_i\bigr)\ge q_K.$$ For fixed old coordinates and $x_0\in E_T$, the function $\phi(v)=(t|v|-|x_0|)_+$ is even and convex. Hence $$\phi\bigl((y-\widetilde y)/2\bigr)
 \le\tfrac12\bigl(\phi(y)+\phi(-\widetilde y)\bigr).$$ Taking conditional expectations given $\mathcal H_T$ and then integrating gives $\mathbb E\phi(y)\ge\mathbb E\phi((y-\widetilde y)/2)$. Combining this inequality with <a href="#prob:conditional-gaussian-difference" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="prob:conditional-gaussian-difference">[prob:conditional-gaussian-difference]</a> and <a href="#prob:first-moment-signed" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="prob:first-moment-signed">[prob:first-moment-signed]</a>, now with $m_2=3$, yields <a id="prob:gaussian-12-gain"></a>$$\begin{aligned}
 M_t(x_0,y)
 &\ge\mathbb E(t|y-\widetilde y|/2-|x_0|)_+\notag\\
 &\ge q_K\mathbb E(tK\sigma-|x_0|)_+\notag\\
 &\ge q_K\left(\frac{tK}{2\sqrt3}\lVert y\rVert_1-\lVert x_0\rVert_1\right).
 \label{prob:gaussian-12-gain}
\end{aligned}$$ This argument uses the probability of a difference directly; the convexity step is what preserves the denominator $12$ in $q_K$. Apply Lemma <a href="#lem:affine" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:affine">3</a> to this affine bound. This proves <a href="#eq:gaussian-parameter-12" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:gaussian-parameter-12">[eq:gaussian-parameter-12]</a>. Taking $K=8\sqrt3/t$ yields the second Gaussian bound in Theorem <a href="#thm:main" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:main">1</a> for every $t>0$. ◻

</details>

The two proofs control different scalar quantities. Proposition <a href="#prob:gaussian-24" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prob:gaussian-24">6</a> first estimates a shifted original variable and pays a factor of two in the union bound. Proposition <a href="#prob:gaussian-12" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prob:gaussian-12">7</a> estimates a difference and uses even convexity directly in the midpoint increment, preserving the denominator $12$. Their first-moment constants are respectively $2+\sqrt2$ and $2\sqrt3$. Neither proof requires the original shifted variable to be symmetric.

<a id="sec:obstruction"></a>
### Bounded paths exclude AUC renormings

The midpoint arguments depend on the multiplier distribution. Here we use only positivity, a nonconstant mean-one law, and a finite second moment to rule out AUC renormings. At every vertex the child increments are weakly null and have the same positive norm. An AUC norm would therefore grow by a fixed factor at each step of a suitably chosen branch, whereas all path products have inherited norm one.

For each fixed vertex $v$ and its children $v^\frown n$, put $$D_{v,n}=P_{v^\frown n}-P_v=P_v(W_{v^\frown n}-1).$$ Independence and <a href="#prob:product-moments" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="prob:product-moments">[prob:product-moments]</a> give <a id="prob:child-moments"></a>$$\label{prob:child-moments}
 \lVert D_{v,n}\rVert_1=\rho:=\mathbb E|W-1|>0,\qquad
 \mathbb ED_{v,n}^2=m_2^{|v|}(m_2-1).$$ For fixed $v$ the sequence $(D_{v,n})_n$ is pairwise orthogonal in $L^2$ and has constant finite $L^2$ norm. Bessel’s inequality therefore makes it weakly null in $L^2$. The inclusion $L^2\longrightarrow L^1$ is continuous on this probability space. Moreover, each continuous linear functional on $X_W$ extends to $L^1$ by Hahn–Banach and hence restricts to a continuous functional on $L^2$. Thus $(D_{v,n})_n$ is weakly null in $X_W$ itself.

<a id="eq:auc-zero"></a>
Suppose $N$ is equivalent to the inherited norm, with $a\lVert z\rVert_1\le N(z)\le b\lVert z\rVert_1$ for $0<a\le b<\infty$. We will show the precise obstruction $$\label{eq:auc-zero}
 \overline\delta_N\left(\frac{a\rho}{2b}\right)=0.$$ The usual one-sided modulus is nonnegative: for each unit center $x$, a norming functional gives a finite-codimensional kernel on which $N(x+ty)\ge1$. It remains to rule out positivity at the displayed scale.

Assume, to the contrary, that $$t_0=\frac{a\rho}{2b},\qquad
 \delta=\overline\delta_N(t_0)>0,\qquad \gamma=\delta/2.$$ At a vertex $v$, we have $a\le N(P_v)\le b$. The modulus at the unit center $P_v/N(P_v)$ supplies a closed finite-codimensional subspace $F$ such that $$N(P_v+z)\ge(1+\gamma)N(P_v)
 \quad(z\in F,\ N(z)\ge t_0N(P_v)).$$ Indeed, its definition first gives the estimate at equality of the radius. For $N(x)=N(w)=1$, with $w\in F$ and $r\ge t_0$, convexity yields $$N(x+rw)-1\ge\frac r{t_0}\bigl(N(x+t_0w)-1\bigr),$$ so the estimate extends to all larger radii.

Equivalent norms induce the same weak topology. The quotient images of the weakly null sequence $D_{v,n}$ in the finite-dimensional space $X_W/F$ therefore tend to zero in quotient norm. We may choose $z_n\in F$ with $N(D_{v,n}-z_n)\to0$. Eventually $$N(z_n)\ge a\rho/2=t_0b\ge t_0N(P_v).$$ Choose such an $n$ with approximation error at most $\gamma N(P_v)/2$. Then $$N(P_{v^\frown n})
 \ge N(P_v+z_n)-N(D_{v,n}-z_n)
 \ge(1+\gamma/2)N(P_v).$$ The same $\gamma$ works at every vertex, although $F$ and $n$ may change. Repeatedly selecting a child gives a depth-$k$ product with norm at least $a(1+\delta/4)^k$, contrary to the uniform upper bound $b$. This proves <a href="#eq:auc-zero" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:auc-zero">[eq:auc-zero]</a> and the renorming assertion of Theorem <a href="#thm:main" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:main">1</a>.

Finally $0<\rho<2$: nonconstancy gives positivity, and $\mathbb E|W-1|=2-2\mathbb E\min\{W,1\}<2$ because $W>0$ almost surely. Thus $0<t_0<1$, so positivity of the one-sided modulus on $(0,1)$ alone would already contradict the tree. For exponential multipliers $\rho=2/e$; indeed $\mathbb E|Z-1|=2\mathbb E(Z-1)_+=2\int_1^\infty e^{-s}\,ds$. The weak-null calculation requires only a finite $L^2$ bound at each fixed parent, not a bound uniform over the depth.
