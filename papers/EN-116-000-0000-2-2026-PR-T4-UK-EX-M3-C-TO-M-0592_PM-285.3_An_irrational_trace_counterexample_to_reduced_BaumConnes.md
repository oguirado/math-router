## 1. Abstract and result contract

> We construct a finitely generated discrete group whose reduced group <i>C</i><sup>∗</sup>-algebra contains a projection of irrational canonical trace. Its <i>K</i><sub>0</sub>-class lies outside the image of the coefficient-free reduced Baum–Connes assembly map. This disproves the coefficient-free reduced Baum–Connes conjecture for countable discrete groups.

- **claim-main-irrational-projection** (theorem, [source](#stmt-thm-main)): There exist a finitely generated discrete group $G_{\mathrm{sur}}$ and a projection $b_0\in C_r^*(G_{\mathrm{sur}})$ with irrational canonical trace. Moreover, $$[b_0]\notin\mu_0^{G_{\mathrm{sur}}}\bigl(K_0^{G_{\mathrm{sur}}}(\underline EG_{\mathrm{sur}})\bigr).$$ In particular, the coefficient-free reduced Baum–Connes conjecture for countable discrete groups is false.
  - Explanation: A finitely generated group is built whose reduced group C*-algebra has a projection of irrational canonical trace. Its K-theory class therefore lies outside the image of reduced assembly, so coefficient-free reduced Baum–Connes fails.
- **claim-trace-image-rational** (theorem, [source](#stmt-thm-trace-image)): For every countable discrete group $G$, $$\tau_{G*}\!\left(\mu_0^G(K_0^G(\underline EG))\right)\subseteq\mathbb Q.$$
  - Explanation: Lück's trace theorem: the canonical trace takes only rational values on classes in the degree-zero assembly image, for every countable discrete group.
- **claim-distinct-copies** (theorem, [source](#stmt-lem-distinct-copies)): Different indexed copies cannot share a simple edge path of length at least $\lambda\sigma(P)$ in either copy $P$. In particular, different index pairs give different labelled subgraphs.
  - Explanation: Label uniqueness of long simple paths implies that different indexed copies cannot overlap along a long simple path, so distinct index pairs give distinct labelled subgraphs.
- **claim-diagram-arc-count** (bound, [source](#stmt-lem-diagram-count)): In a diagram chosen as above, the following hold.* 1. *Every bounded face assigned to $P$ has perimeter at least $\sigma(P)$. Every arc incident to that face has original length less than $\lambda\sigma(P)$.* 2. *More generally, an arc with different copy assignments on its two incidences has length less than $\lambda\sigma(Q)$ for either assignment $Q$. An arc with a generator-side incidence has length one.* 3. *The compressed diagram satisfies $$\label{eq:diagram-count} (1/\lambda-6)F\le6m-6,\qquad b\le6m.$$
  - Explanation: In a minimal diagram with adjustable sides, faces have large perimeter, arcs between different assignments are short relative to the girth, and the number of faces and arcs is linearly bounded by the number of sides.
- **claim-cone-geometry** (theorem, [source](#stmt-item-simple-cayley)): There is an integer $D_0$, independent of all the above data, such that every vertex on a geodesic triangle in $\widehat X$ is within $D_0$ of a vertex on another side. Moreover:* 1. * If an edge path of length $L$ joins $x,a\in P$ while avoiding $c_P$, then $$\label{eq:avoidance} d_P(x,a)\le12(L+1)\lambda\sigma(P).$$* 2. *If $\lambda<1/12$, the graph $X$ is simple: no nonfreely-trivial closed label word in $X$ has length at most two.
  - Explanation: The coned graph has uniformly thin geodesic triangles with a universal constant. Paths avoiding a cone center have intrinsic endpoint distance linearly controlled, and the Cayley graph is simple for strict small cancellation.
- **claim-simultaneous-overlap** (bound, [source](#stmt-lem-overlap)): Let $R$ be a group vertex or a center of $\widehat X$, and let $A=c_P\ne R$. Choose one geodesic from $A$ to $R$, whose first group vertex is $a\in P$. Every vertex shared by $P$ and any *different* copy $Q$ with $$d_{\widehat X}(c_Q,R)\le d_{\widehat X}(c_P,R)$$ lies in the intrinsic induced ball $B_P$ about $a$ of radius $$\label{eq:overlap-radius} r_P=\left\lfloor12(L_0+1)\lambda\sigma(P)\right\rfloor.$$
  - Explanation: Relative to a root, every vertex a copy shares with any other copy whose center is no farther from the root lies in a single intrinsic ball about a point independent of the other copy.
- **claim-choice-of-data** (construction, [source](#stmt-prop-data)): There are labelled graphs $(\Theta_i)_{i\ge1}$ satisfying all the hypotheses of Section <a href="#sec:geometry" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:geometry">2</a>, with the following additional properties. Write $n_i=\|\operatorname{Vert}(\Theta_i)\|$, $k_i=\lfloor n_i/2\rfloor$, and let $\sigma_i$ be the girth. There are constants $\rho,\kappa>0$, independent of $i$, such that:* 1. *On the orthogonal complement of constants in $\ell^2(\operatorname{Vert}(\Theta_i))$, $dI-\mathop{\mathrm{Adj}}_{\Theta_i}\ge\rho I$, and for every vertex subset $W$, $$\label{eq:expansion} \|\partial W\|\ge\kappa\min\{\|W\|,n_i-\|W\|\}.$$ Here $\partial W$ is the unoriented edge boundary in $\Theta_i$.* 2. *With $$\label{eq:c-definition} c=\frac\eta2\min\{\kappa,1\},$$ we have $n_i>100$, $\sigma_i>\max\{16,20/c\}$, $\lambda\sigma_i>3$, and $1<\lfloor\lambda\sigma_i\rfloor<\lfloor\lambda\sigma_{i+1}\rfloor$.* 3. *Every induced intrinsic ball of radius at most $\sigma_i/16$ is a tree with fewer than $\eta n_i$ vertices.* 4. *The integers $k_i$ strictly increase and $k_{i+1}-k_i\to\infty$.* *Consequently every ball $B_P$ in Lemma <a href="#lem:overlap" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:overlap">6</a>, for every permitted root and chosen root geodesic, is an induced tree satisfying $$\label{eq:small-ball} r_P\le\sigma(P)/16,\qquad \|B_P\|<\eta\|P\|.$$
  - Explanation: A sequence of labelled expanders exists with uniform expansion, large girth, small induced tree balls and strictly increasing code dimensions with diverging gaps, so overlap balls are small induced trees.
- **claim-cycle-direct-sum** (theorem, [source](#stmt-lem-cycle-sum)): The inclusions of the copies give an internal algebraic direct sum $$\label{eq:cycle-sum} Z_1(X;\mathbb F_2)=\bigoplus_P Z_1(P;\mathbb F_2).$$
  - Explanation: Because simultaneous overlaps lie in induced trees, the finite-support cycle space of the Cayley graph is the internal direct sum of the cycle spaces of the copies.
- **claim-codes-both-distances** (construction, [source](#stmt-lem-codes)): For every $i$ there is a $k_i$-dimensional subspace $C_i\subseteq\mathbb F_p^{\operatorname{Vert}(\Theta_i)}$ such that $w_i\in C_i$ and $$\|\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}z\|>\eta n_i \qquad \text{for every nonzero }z\in C_i\cup C_i^\perp.$$
  - Explanation: A counting argument of Gilbert–Varshamov type yields a code containing the prototype pattern such that every nonzero word of the code and of its dual has large support.
- **claim-module-intersection** (theorem, [source](#stmt-lem-module-intersection)): For every copy $P$, $$\label{eq:module-intersection} W_0\cap\mathbb F_p^P=C_P^\perp.$$
  - Explanation: Imposing the dual-code relations on all translated copies creates exactly the intended relations on each single copy and no others.
- **claim-uniform-marginals** (theorem, [source](#stmt-lem-marginals)): For a copy $P=P_{g,i}$, the restriction map $$\operatorname{res}_P:\Omega\longrightarrow\mathbb F_p^P, \qquad \omega\longmapsto X_\omega\|_P$$ has image exactly $C_P$. Its pushforward of $m_\Omega$ is the uniform measure on $C_P$. In particular, for every $a\in C_P$, $$\label{eq:uniform-marginal} m_\Omega\{\omega:X_\omega\|_P=a\}=p^{-k_i},$$ and this applies to $a=w_P$.
  - Explanation: Restricting a character pattern to a copy is onto the code of that copy and pushes Haar measure to the uniform measure, so each codeword, including the prototype, has the same probability.
- **claim-local-deficit** (bound, [source](#stmt-lem-local-deficit)): If $P$ is invalid for $\omega$, then every subset $B\subseteq P$ satisfies $$d\|B\|-2e_{\omega,P}(B)\ge c\|B\|. \label{eq:local-deficit}$$
  - Explanation: Inside an invalid copy, every vertex subset has a degree deficit proportional to its size, from the primal distance and edge expansion.
- **claim-global-deficit** (bound, [source](#stmt-lem-global-deficit)): For every $\omega\in\Omega$ and every finite set $Y\subseteq U_\omega$, let $A$ be the graph on $Y$ consisting of all its internal enabled edges. Then $$d\|Y\|-2\|E(A)\|\ge(c/2)\|Y\|. \label{eq:global-deficit}$$
  - Explanation: The proportional deficit extends to every finite subset of the remaining vertex set, by bounding the cycle rank of the enabled graph through disjoint charged exterior vertex sets.
- **claim-uniform-spectral-gap** (bound, [source](#stmt-lem-spectral-gap)): For every $\omega\in\Omega$, the restriction of $\pi_\omega(T)$ to $\ell^2(U_\omega)$ satisfies $$\pi_\omega(T)\|_{\ell^2(U_\omega)}\ge\frac{c^2}{8d}I. \label{eq:remaining-spectral-bound}$$ Put $$\varepsilon=\min\{\rho,c^2/(8d)\}>0.$$ Then $$\mathop{\mathrm{spec}}(\pi_\omega(T))\subseteq\{0\}\cup[\varepsilon,2d], \label{eq:uniform-spectrum}$$ and its kernel is exactly the Hilbert-space direct sum of the one-dimensional spaces of constants on the valid copies.
  - Explanation: A coarea and Cauchy–Schwarz argument gives a uniform positive lower bound off the valid copies, so zero is isolated from the rest of the spectrum and the kernel is the span of constants on valid copies.
- **claim-projection-in-algebra** (theorem, [source](#stmt-prop-projection)): There exists a projection $b_0\in C_r^*(G_{\mathrm{sur}})$ such that, for every $\omega\in\Omega$, $\pi_\omega(b_0)$ is the orthogonal projection onto $\ker\pi_\omega(T)$. Explicitly, it is projection onto constants on each valid copy and is zero on $\ell^2(U_\omega)$. In particular, $$\langle\pi_\omega(b_0)\delta_1,\delta_1\rangle = \begin{cases} 1/n_i,&\text{if $1$ belongs to a valid copy of type $i$},\\ 0,&\text{if $1\in U_\omega$}. \end{cases} \label{eq:projection-diagonal}$$ The valid copy in the first case is unique.
  - Explanation: Continuous functional calculus on the operator with an isolated zero gives an honest projection in the reduced group C*-algebra, acting as the kernel projection in every evaluation representation.
- **claim-irrational-trace** (theorem, [source](#stmt-prop-irrational-trace)): The projection $b_0\in C_r^*(G_{\mathrm{sur}})$ satisfies $$\label{eq:irrational-trace} \tau_{G_{\mathrm{sur}}}(b_0)=\sum_{i\ge1}p^{-k_i}\notin\mathbb Q.$$
  - Explanation: The trace of the projection is a sum of Haar measures of mutually exclusive validity events, and its base-p expansion is not eventually periodic, hence the value is irrational.

## 2. Definitions, validity and bounds

- **module V and group G_sur** ([source](#stmt-auto-definition-1)): Define the discrete additive group and distinguished element $$V=\mathbb F_p^{(\Gamma)}/W_0,\qquad v_*=[\mathbf e_1]\in V,$$ with the induced $\Gamma$-action, and put $$G_{\mathrm{sur}}=V\rtimes\Gamma.$$ We use the multiplication $(v,g)(v',g')=(v+gv',gg')$ in $G_{\mathrm{sur}}$.
- **enabled graph, valid copy and remaining set** ([source](#stmt-auto-definition-2)): For $\omega\in\Omega$, its *enabled graph* $G_\omega$ has vertex set $\Gamma$. At $h\in\Gamma$ the allowed outgoing labels are $\mathcal J(X_\omega(h))$. The Cayley edge from $h$ to $hs$ is enabled precisely when $s$ is allowed at $h$ and $s^{-1}$ is allowed at $hs$. A copy $P$ is *valid for $\omega$* if $X_\omega\|_P=w_P$. Let $U_\omega$ be the complement in $\Gamma$ of the union of all valid copies.
- **canonical trace** ([source](#sec:introduction)): Our obstruction uses the canonical trace $\tau_G(\sum_g a_g g)=a_1$, normalized by $\tau_G(1)=1$.
- **port** ([source](#pm-h-13)): We call an outgoing generator label a *port*.
- **copy** ([source](#pm-h-4)): A *copy* is any left translate of an embedded $\Theta_i$.

- **step-codes** [claim](#stmt-lem-codes) / [proof](#proof-stmt-lem-codes): Random code containing the prototype word, Hamming ball count and union bound. -> Code with large primal and dual distance.

- **step-marginals** [claim](#stmt-lem-marginals) / [proof](#proof-stmt-lem-marginals): Duality of finite vector spaces, then uniqueness of Haar pushforward. -> Uniform copy marginals.
- **step-local-deficit** [claim](#stmt-lem-local-deficit) / [proof](#proof-stmt-lem-local-deficit): Primal distance gives many wrong vertices; edge expansion handles the large-complement case. -> Proportional deficit inside invalid copies.

- **step-spectral-gap** [claim](#stmt-lem-spectral-gap) / [proof](#proof-stmt-lem-spectral-gap): Layer-cake identity and Cauchy–Schwarz on level sets; expander gap on valid copies. -> Zero isolated from the rest of the spectrum.

- **step-trace-image** [claim](#stmt-thm-trace-image) / [proof](#proof-stmt-thm-trace-image): Deduction from Lück's trace theorem by extension of scalars. -> Rational trace on the assembly image.
- **step-main** [claim](#stmt-thm-main) / [proof](#proof-stmt-prop-irrational-trace-2): Irrational trace of an honest projection against the rational trace on the assembly image. -> Failure of surjectivity of reduced assembly.

## 4. Engineering Triptych

### Panel 1 - epistemic contract

- **inv-trace-rational-on-assembly-image**: For every countable discrete group, the canonical trace homomorphism takes only rational values on the image of degree-zero reduced assembly. Rationality of the trace is therefore a necessary condition for a K0 class to lie in the assembly image.
- **inv-uniform-spectral-gap-isolates-zero**: For every configuration the spectrum of the operator lies in zero together with a closed interval bounded away from zero by a uniform constant. The kernel is the direct sum of constants on the valid copies.
- **inv-projection-trace-irrational**: The kernel projection in the reduced group C*-algebra has canonical trace equal to a series of negative powers of the prime, which is not rational. Combined with rationality on the assembly image, its class lies outside that image.

### Panel 2 - negative knowledge

- **anti-assume-rational-projection-traces**: Assume that every projection in a reduced group C*-algebra of a finitely generated discrete group has rational canonical trace, or that assembly is surjective in degree zero. -> The constructed group carries a projection with irrational canonical trace, so surjectivity of the coefficient-free reduced assembly map fails for it.
- **anti-invert-assembly-for-any-projection**: Assume that every projection class in the reduced group C*-algebra is the analytic index of a geometric cycle on the classifying space for proper actions. -> Classes in the assembly image have rational trace, so a projection of irrational trace cannot be such an index.

### Panel 3 - reference implementation and oracle

Applicability: `not_applicable`. The results are existence statements about an infinite group, an infinite family of expanders, a compact dual and a continuous functional calculus in a C*-algebra. The codes are obtained by a probabilistic counting argument and the trace is an infinite series, so no finite procedure computes the stated objects.

## 6. Applicability limits and search

- **limit-no-finite-model** (formal_scope): The paper assumes no finite-dimensional or cocompact model for the classifying space of proper actions, and the result concerns the full proper classifying space only. It says nothing about restricted models or supports. Blocked use: ['Applying the failure of surjectivity to assembly maps defined on a finite-dimensional or cocompact model of the classifying space']
- **limit-coefficient-free-scope** (counterexample_scope): The counterexample refutes only the coefficient-free reduced conjecture, with the complex numbers carrying the trivial action. The authors state that earlier expander constructions with commutative coefficients do not give such a counterexample. Blocked use: ['Reading the result as a counterexample to the conjecture with coefficients in an arbitrary algebra or for the maximal group algebra', 'Citing it for hyperbolic groups, where the coefficient-free conjecture is proved']
- **limit-trace-only-necessary** (hypothesis): Rationality of the trace on the assembly image is a necessary condition only. It is stated for degree zero and for every countable discrete group, and it detects failure of surjectivity but not failure of injectivity or any degree-one phenomenon. Blocked use: ['Using the trace obstruction to disprove injectivity of assembly', 'Using rationality of a trace as proof that a class lies in the assembly image']
- **limit-trace-requires-c-star** (formal_scope): Projections in group von Neumann algebras with irrational kernel dimension do not obstruct assembly, because they need not lie in the reduced group C*-algebra. The construction needs a uniform spectral gap at zero so that functional calculus stays inside the C*-algebra. Blocked use: ['Treating irrational kernel dimensions of group-ring operators as obstructions to assembly without a spectral gap at zero']
- **limit-overlap-hypotheses** (hypothesis): The simultaneous overlap bound holds only for copies whose centers are no farther from the root than the given one, and for the coned graphical small-cancellation data with small girth-ratio parameters. Reduced labelling and uniqueness of long path words are required. Blocked use: ['Applying the overlap ball bound to copies farther from the root than the reference copy', 'Applying it to labellings that repeat long path words']
- **limit-code-existence** (nonconstructive): The codes with both primal and dual distance large are obtained by a probabilistic counting argument, so the paper gives no explicit code and no procedure to find one. The count needs the length to exceed a threshold. Blocked use: ['Presenting the construction as an explicit or effectively computable group', 'Using the codes where the block length is not above the stated threshold']
- **limit-no-independence** (edge_case): The marginal result gives the exact distribution on each single copy only. It makes no claim about joint behaviour of patterns on different copies, so the trace computation uses only exclusivity of events and countable additivity. Blocked use: ['Assuming that validity events on different copies are independent or computing joint probabilities from the marginals']
- **limit-irrationality-gaps** (hypothesis): Irrationality of the trace rests on strictly increasing code dimensions whose successive gaps diverge, and on a prime above two. A sequence with bounded gaps could have a periodic expansion and a rational sum. Blocked use: ['Concluding irrationality for a series of negative powers of the prime when the gaps between exponents do not tend to infinity']
- **limit-spectral-gap-uniform** (approximation): The spectral lower bound is an analytic bound with constants defined through non-explicit choices of the expansion and girth parameters. It is not a numerical value that a finite matrix computation can certify for the infinite operator. Blocked use: ['Certifying the gap of the infinite operator by computing spectra of finite truncations']

<a id="sec:introduction"></a>
### Introduction

For a countable discrete group $G$, let $C_r^*(G)$ be the norm closure of its complex group ring in the left regular representation. The reduced Baum–Connes assembly map is $$\mu_j^G:K_j^G(\underline EG)\longrightarrow K_j(C_r^*(G)),
\qquad j=0,1.$$ Here $\underline EG$ is the classifying space for proper actions: its fixed-point sets are contractible for finite subgroups and empty for infinite subgroups. The domain is equivariant $K$-homology with $G$-compact supports, and assembly takes geometric classes to their analytic indices. The conjecture asserts that $\mu_j^G$ is an isomorphism for $j=0,1$. We use integral complex $K$-theory, with coefficient algebra $\mathbb C$ carrying the trivial action; no finite-dimensional or cocompact model for $\underline EG$ is assumed.

Baum and Connes first formulated the conjecture in a manuscript circulated in 1982 and published in 2000 (Baum and Connes 2000) <a id="pm-cite-1"></a>[[BaumConnes2000](#ref-BaumConnes2000)]. Their geometric formulation connects elliptic indices with the $K$-theory of reduced crossed products. The formulation of Baum–Connes–Higson uses the universal proper space and equivariant Kasparov theory to state this index problem for second-countable locally compact groups (Baum, Connes, and Higson 1994, Conjecture 3.15) <a id="pm-cite-2"></a>[[BCH1994](#ref-BCH1994)]. The stronger formulation allows a separable $G$-$C^*$-algebra $A$ as coefficient, with target $K_j(A\rtimes_rG)$ (Baum, Connes, and Higson 1994, Conjecture 9.6) <a id="pm-cite-3"></a>[[BCH1994](#ref-BCH1994)].

For the discrete groups considered here, important positive results cover both flexible Hilbert-space geometry and hyperbolic geometry. Higson–Kasparov prove assembly with coefficients for groups acting properly by affine isometries on Hilbert space, including countable amenable groups (Higson and Kasparov 2001, Theorem 9.1 and Corollary 9.2) <a id="pm-cite-4"></a>[[HigsonKasparov2001](#ref-HigsonKasparov2001)]. Using Lafforgue’s Banach $KK$-theory, Mineyev–Yu prove the coefficient-free conjecture for hyperbolic groups and their subgroups (Mineyev and Yu 2002, Theorems 19 and 20) <a id="pm-cite-5"></a>[[MineyevYu2002](#ref-MineyevYu2002)]; Lafforgue later proves the conjecture with coefficients for hyperbolic groups (Lafforgue 2012, Theorem 0.4) <a id="pm-cite-6"></a>[[Lafforgue2012](#ref-Lafforgue2012)].

The distinction between trivial and general coefficients is central to this history. Higson–Lafforgue–Skandalis use expanders and spectral projections to detect failures of exactness in reduced crossed products, obtaining counterexamples for groupoids and for discrete groups with suitable commutative coefficients (Higson, Lafforgue, and Skandalis 2002, sec. 7 and Remark 12) <a id="pm-cite-7"></a>[[HLS2002](#ref-HLS2002)]. Those constructions do not give a counterexample to the coefficient-free group assertion above.

Our obstruction uses the canonical trace $\tau_G(\sum_g a_g g)=a_1$, normalized by $\tau_G(1)=1$. Lück’s trace theorem implies that its value on every degree-zero assembly class is rational. Thus a projection in $C_r^*(G)$ with irrational trace would obstruct surjectivity. We construct such a projection.

**Theorem 1**. *There exist a finitely generated discrete group $G_{\mathrm{sur}}$ and a projection $b_0\in C_r^*(G_{\mathrm{sur}})$ with irrational canonical trace. Moreover, $$[b_0]\notin\mu_0^{G_{\mathrm{sur}}}\bigl(K_0^{G_{\mathrm{sur}}}(\underline EG_{\mathrm{sur}})\bigr).$$ In particular, the coefficient-free reduced Baum–Connes conjecture for countable discrete groups is false.*

*Deduction from the trace theorem.* Put $\Lambda_G=\mathbb Z[1/|F|:F\le G\text{ finite}]\subseteq\mathbb Q$. Lück’s Theorem 0.3 (Lück 2002) <a id="pm-cite-8"></a>[[Luck2002](#ref-Luck2002)] states that the trace image of assembly after extension of scalars to $\Lambda_G$ is $\Lambda_G$. On $1\otimes x$, this composite has value $\tau_{G*}(\mu_0^G(x))$. Hence that value belongs to $\Lambda_G\subseteq\mathbb Q$ for every integral class $x$, without assuming surjectivity of assembly or injectivity of localization.

For precision, our domain is the direct limit of $KK_j^G(C_0(Z),\mathbb C)$ over closed invariant $G$-compact subspaces of a proper $G$-CW model. Assembly is reduced Kasparov descent followed by the product with a cutoff projection and this direct limit. Lück uses the same proper equivariant $KK$-homology and analytic index (Lück 2002, sec. 4 and 5) <a id="pm-cite-9"></a>[[Luck2002](#ref-Luck2002)]. His cocompact subcomplexes are cofinal among these supports: the quotient is a CW complex, and its compact subsets lie in finite subcomplexes. Comparisons with the Davis–Lück formulation are given in (Hambleton and Pedersen 2004, Corollary 8.4) <a id="pm-cite-10"></a>[[HambletonPedersen2004](#ref-HambletonPedersen2004)] and (Kranz 2021, Theorem 5.3) <a id="pm-cite-11"></a>[[Kranz2021](#ref-Kranz2021)]. ◻

<a id="pm-h-3"></a>
###### Kernel dimensions and the spectral gap.

The distinction between an individual kernel and a $K$-theory index already appears in Atiyah’s question about irrational $L^2$-Betti numbers of coverings (Atiyah 1976, sec. 6.4, question (iii)) <a id="pm-cite-12"></a>[[Atiyah1976](#ref-Atiyah1976)]. Grigorchuk and Żuk’s lamplighter spectral calculation led Grigorchuk–Linnell–Schick–Żuk to examples with kernel dimension $1/3$, although every finite subgroup had order a power of two (Grigorchuk et al. 2000) <a id="pm-cite-13"></a>[[GLSZ2000](#ref-GLSZ2000)]. Dicks–Schick gave an elementary spectral decomposition using finite path operators and projections (Dicks and Schick 2002) <a id="pm-cite-14"></a>[[DicksSchick2002](#ref-DicksSchick2002)]. Austin then obtained irrational kernel dimensions by varying translation-invariant quotients of finite-field lamp modules (Austin 2013, Theorem 1.1 and Corollary 1.2) <a id="pm-cite-15"></a>[[Austin2013](#ref-Austin2013)]. Fourier transformation turns these operators into local rules on the compact dual of the module; this viewpoint also underlies the present construction. Pichot–Schick–Żuk refined the method to compute explicit transcendental values (Pichot, Schick, and Żuk 2015) <a id="pm-cite-16"></a>[[PichotSchickZuk2015](#ref-PichotSchickZuk2015)].

These results concern projections in group von Neumann algebras, which need not belong to reduced group $C^*$-algebras. The difference is particularly clear in Grabowski’s construction of integral group-ring matrices with transcendental kernel dimension over the ordinary lamplighter groups $(\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z)\wr\mathbb Z$, for integers $m>1$ (Grabowski 2016, Theorem 2) <a id="pm-cite-17"></a>[[Grabowski2016](#ref-Grabowski2016)]. Those groups are amenable and satisfy Baum–Connes. By Theorem <a href="#thm:trace-image" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:trace-image">2</a>, their irrational kernel projections cannot lie in matrix algebras over their reduced group $C^*$-algebras.

A uniform spectral gap at zero supplies the required $C^*$-algebra property: the indicator of zero becomes continuous on the spectrum. Li–Nowak–Pooya use this mechanism for cohomological Laplacians and higher Kazhdan projections, relating gaps and trace restrictions to surjectivity of assembly (Li, Nowak, and Pooya 2024, Propositions 1, 5 and 7) <a id="pm-cite-18"></a>[[LiNowakPooya2024](#ref-LiNowakPooya2024)]. Here the operator comes from local finite-field rules. Its kernel consists of constants on designated expander components, and its trace is computed from their pattern probabilities. Beyond the gaps on those expanders, the construction must provide a uniform positive lower bound on the entire remaining graph.

We obtain that bound using labelled expanders as graphical relators, following the strategy of Gromov (Gromov 2003) <a id="pm-cite-19"></a>[[Gromov2003](#ref-Gromov2003)]. Osajda’s stronger labelling theorem provides one finite alphabet, globally distinguishable long paths, and isometrically embedded relators (Osajda 2020, Theorems 2.7 and 3.2) <a id="pm-cite-20"></a>[[Osajda2020](#ref-Osajda2020)]. To pass from separate expander estimates to the full operator, we prove a geometric bound on simultaneous intersections of translated relators and use it in a global cycle-rank estimate.

<a id="pm-h-4"></a>
###### Construction and proof.

The two ingredients that distinguish the construction are exact local module relations and a global spectral estimate. Both use the same geometric ordering, but neither follows from pairwise small overlaps alone. We first choose finite $d$-regular expanders $\Theta_i$ with large girth and label them by Osajda’s theorem so that they embed isometrically in a Cayley graph $X=\mathop{\mathrm{Cay}}(\Gamma,S)$ of a finitely generated group $\Gamma$. A *copy* is any left translate of an embedded $\Theta_i$. Coning off every copy gives a uniformly hyperbolic graph. An avoidance estimate then shows that, relative to any root, all intersections of a copy with other copies whose centers are no farther from the root lie in one small intrinsic tree ball.

This simultaneous intersection bound has two uses. It makes the finite cycle spaces of copies an internal direct sum, and it prevents new local linear relations when codes on copies are imposed on a lamp module. For one prime $p$, codes over $\mathbb F_p$ of large dual distance give exact finite-pattern distributions; large primal distance forces a positive proportion of discrepancies on every copy whose pattern is not the designated one.

The resulting group is $G_{\mathrm{sur}}=V\rtimes\Gamma$, with $V$ a finite-field module. On its compact dual, a coordinate value specifies allowed generator labels. The operator $T\in C_r^*(G_{\mathrm{sur}})$ is $d$ minus the adjacency operator of the enabled graph. Copies carrying the designated pattern are isolated expander components. To control everything else, we eliminate copy coordinates in finite cycle spaces and charge their ranks to disjoint exterior vertex sets. This yields a uniform spectral gap at zero and hence $b_0=\mathbf 1_{\{0\}}(T)\in C_r^*(G_{\mathrm{sur}})$ by continuous functional calculus. Its trace is a series $\sum_i p^{-k_i}$ whose base-$p$ digits are not eventually periodic; here $k_i$ are the code dimensions, selected to have increasingly large gaps.

The external geometric inputs are recalled where used. The diagram, module, and spectral arguments are given in full, including the simultaneous overlap bound needed to pass from individual copies to the whole enabled graph.

### Coned geometry of graphical presentations

This section establishes the geometric control needed by the module and spectral arguments: relative to one root, all intersections of a translated relator with relators no farther from the root lie in one short intrinsic ball. We first define the labelled graphs and their coned Cayley graph, then prove an avoidance estimate and uniform thinness. The existence of the graph data, with the required expansion properties, is established in Section <a href="#sec:data" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:data">3</a>.

Let $(\Theta_i)$ be finite connected simple $d$-regular graphs, $d\ge3$, of girths $\sigma_i$. Label their oriented edges by a finite alphabet $S^{\pm1}$, with reversal corresponding to formal inversion. Assume that the labelling is reduced, meaning locally injective into the bouquet with alphabet $S$, and that <a id="eq:geometric-parameters"></a>$$\label{eq:geometric-parameters}
0<\lambda\le\frac1{12},\qquad \lambda\sigma_i>3.$$ We require the following uniqueness property: the label word of a simple path in $\Theta_i$ of length at least $\lambda\sigma_i$ occurs on no other simple path in the disjoint family.

Define $$\Gamma=\langle S\mid\text{labels of all closed walks in all }\Theta_i\rangle,
\qquad X=\mathop{\mathrm{Cay}}(\Gamma,S).$$ Initially $X$ is the formal labelled Cayley graph: it has the specified edge from $h$ to $hs$ for each $s\in S$, and the reverse traversal has label $s^{-1}$. This graph is deterministic for reading words, even before loops or multiple edges have been excluded. Assume that the based label maps $\phi_i:\Theta_i\to X$ are isometric embeddings on vertices. They are well-defined by the presentation. For every $g\in\Gamma$, write $$P_{g,i}=g\phi_i(\Theta_i)$$ for the corresponding *copy*, with its source identification, and put $\sigma(P_{g,i})=\sigma_i$. Denote intrinsic distance in a copy by $d_P$.

<a id="stmt-lem-distinct-copies"></a>
<div id="lem:distinct-copies" class="lemma">

**Lemma 3**. *Different indexed copies cannot share a simple edge path of length at least $\lambda\sigma(P)$ in either copy $P$. In particular, different index pairs give different labelled subgraphs.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-distinct-copies"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-distinct-copies</summary>

*Proof.* Read a common path in the same direction in $X$. Its pullbacks are simple, since the vertex maps are injective. Label uniqueness identifies the source paths and hence their source graph. Equality at the first target vertex then identifies the translating elements. The graphs have simple paths longer than the threshold, by regularity and girth, so equality of whole copies also forces equality of their indices. ◻

</details>

Adjoin a new center $c_P$ for every copy and join it by an edge to every vertex of $P$. The resulting graph is denoted $\widehat X$; all edges have length one.

<a id="pm-h-7"></a>
#### Diagrams with adjustable sides

We shall use one boundary estimate twice: first to control paths avoiding a specified center, and then to obtain short fillings in $\widehat X$. For a passage through a center, we hold fixed its endpoints and assigned copy, while allowing the path inside that copy to change. A minimal filling will cut these paths into linearly many arcs, counted in terms of the length of the coned walk. An arc separating different assigned copies will be short relative to the girth of either copy.

Start a nonempty closed edge walk in $\widehat X$ at a group vertex and partition it into $m$ sides. Each side is a generator edge in $X$ or a two-edge passage through a center. Inflate a center passage to any walk between its endpoints in its assigned copy. Such a side is *adjustable*; an empty walk is allowed when the endpoints coincide. Generator sides remain fixed. The result is a closed walk in $X$.

For an inflated loop, use a finite connected plane multigraph diagram whose exterior contour maps to that loop and whose bounded-face contours are closed walks in assigned copies. Such diagrams exist by van Kampen’s lemma: the presentation allows all closed walks in the source graphs, and the group value at a face’s initial vertex selects its translated copy. Contours may repeat vertices and edges. This is essential for the surgeries below: deleting a common edge merges its incident regions, but other contacts can remain as repeated vertices or bridges. The merged bounded contour must remain a closed walk in its assigned copy; an adjusted exterior side must remain a walk with its prescribed endpoints and assignment.

An *incidence* is one occurrence of an edge on a contour; a bridge has two incidences on the same contour. The exterior contour retains its partition into side occurrences. Mark the positions where sides end, and also the corresponding plane-graph vertices. There are at most $m$ distinct marked vertices, although the contour may visit a marked vertex repeatedly. Only the marked positions are side breaks: another visit to the same vertex need not end the current side. If inflation gives the empty walk, an isolated marked vertex is permitted.

Minimize the number $F$ of bounded faces, then the number of edges, over diagrams and adjustable walks with the prescribed side data. Suppress all unmarked degree-two vertices; the resulting edges will be called *arcs*, and their number will be denoted $b$. An arc retains its original edge length. Loop arcs are allowed.

<a id="stmt-lem-diagram-count"></a>
<div id="lem:diagram-count" class="lemma">

**Lemma 4** (Short arcs and their number). *In a diagram chosen as above, the following hold.*

1.  *Every bounded face assigned to $P$ has perimeter at least $\sigma(P)$. Every arc incident to that face has original length less than $\lambda\sigma(P)$.*

2.  *More generally, an arc with different copy assignments on its two incidences has length less than $\lambda\sigma(Q)$ for either assignment $Q$. An arc with a generator-side incidence has length one.*

<a id="eq:diagram-count"></a>
3.  *The compressed diagram satisfies $$\label{eq:diagram-count}
    (1/\lambda-6)F\le6m-6,\qquad b\le6m.$$*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-diagram-count"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-diagram-count</summary>

*Proof.* We first record the consequences of minimality, with contour incidences understood throughout.

*Face words and mergers.* A bounded-face word cannot be freely trivial. Indeed, the diagram identity expresses the exterior word as a product of conjugates of the face words, each used once. Omitting a freely trivial factor and applying van Kampen’s lemma again gives a diagram with fewer faces for the same exterior word. Its prescribed exterior paths lift identically by determinism of $X$. A closed walk in $P$ shorter than $\sigma(P)$ cancels to the empty walk, so every face has the claimed lower perimeter bound.

Suppose two distinct bounded faces with the same copy assignment share an edge. That edge is not a bridge. Delete it and join the two contours along its former incidences. Every remaining step lies in the same copy, so the merged contour is an admissible closed walk there. Exactly one bounded face disappears. Other contacts between the old contours may produce repeated vertices or bridges, which our diagram category allows. Thus this configuration contradicts minimality of $F$.

The same operation excludes an edge shared by a bounded face and an adjustable exterior side with the same assignment. Replace this particular occurrence of the edge in the side by the rest of the bounded contour. The resulting side is still a walk in that copy with the same endpoints. Its prescribed position among the exterior sides is unchanged, and again one bounded face disappears.

*Leaves and bounded-face bridges.* There is no unmarked vertex of valence one. Its leaf edge gives an immediate backtrack; on the exterior this lies within one adjustable side, because a side break would be marked and a generator side has marked endpoints. Erasing the leaf preserves the assignments and lowers the edge count.

There is also no bridge whose two incidences belong to a bounded face. After its open edge is removed, the exterior contour lies wholly in one component. Keep that component and discard the other component together with the bridge. On the affected bounded contour this replaces $q e\gamma e^{-1}q'$ by $q q'$, where $e$ is the bridge and $\gamma$ is the entire excursion around the discarded component. The retained walk is still closed and still lies in the assigned copy; no free triviality of $\gamma$ is required. The exterior, including its side data, is unchanged. The face count cannot increase—also directly from $F=E-V+1$—and the edge count decreases.

*Short arcs.* The incidence type on either side of a compressed arc is constant: it is a bounded face or an exterior side of the prescribed decomposition. Changes of exterior side occur at marked vertices and are not suppressed. An arc with a generator-side incidence therefore has length one. By the preceding restrictions, an arc incident to a bounded face has either such an incidence on the other side or a different copy assignment there.

Consider an arc with different assignments and length greater than two. Its word is freely reduced. Otherwise a consecutive cancelling pair has an unmarked bivalent midpoint and two distinct endpoints in the diagram. The latter assertion holds even for a loop arc: its internal vertices are distinct and closure occurs only at the two ends of the entire arc. Contract the cancelling pair as a length-two tree, identifying its outer endpoints, which have the same group value. At each incident contour the two steps cancel, so assignments and side data are preserved. This non-loop contraction preserves $F$ and lowers the edge count, a contradiction.

If the arc had length at least $\lambda\sigma(Q)$ for one assignment $Q$, its first $\lceil\lambda\sigma(Q)\rceil$ edges would be a nonbacktracking path shorter than the girth. It is simple in $Q$, hence also in the other source by the vertex embeddings. This contradicts Lemma <a href="#lem:distinct-copies" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:distinct-copies">3</a>. Arcs of length at most two are already shorter than the threshold. This proves (a) and (b).

*Counting.* There is a marked vertex, so suppression cannot leave an exceptional unmarked circle component. Every unmarked surviving vertex has valence at least three. If $v_D$ is the compressed vertex count, then $$v_D-b+F=1,\qquad 2b\ge3(v_D-m),
\qquad b\le3F+3m-3.$$ By (a), every bounded face has at least $1/\lambda$ arc incidences, counted with multiplicity. Their total is at most $2b$, so $$F/\lambda\le2b\le6F+6m-6.$$ This is the first inequality in <a href="#eq:diagram-count" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:diagram-count">[eq:diagram-count]</a>. Since $\lambda\le1/12$, it gives $F\le m-1$ and then $b\le6m$. The same formulas cover trees and the isolated marked vertex. ◻

</details>

#### Avoidance and uniform thinness

The arc bound has two geometric consequences. Closing an avoiding path by a designated cone passage bounds its endpoints’ intrinsic distance. Replacing arcs by cone passages while retaining fixed generator edges produces a linear filling inequality with universal constants.

<a id="stmt-item-simple-cayley"></a>
<div id="item:simple-cayley" class="proposition">

**Proposition 5**. *<span id="prop:cone-geometry" label="prop:cone-geometry"></span> There is an integer $D_0$, independent of all the above data, such that every vertex on a geodesic triangle in $\widehat X$ is within $D_0$ of a vertex on another side. Moreover:*

<a id="eq:avoidance"></a>
1.  *<span id="item:avoidance" label="item:avoidance"></span> If an edge path of length $L$ joins $x,a\in P$ while avoiding $c_P$, then $$\label{eq:avoidance}
    d_P(x,a)\le12(L+1)\lambda\sigma(P).$$*

2.  *If $\lambda<1/12$, the graph $X$ is simple: no nonfreely-trivial closed label word in $X$ has length at most two.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-item-simple-cayley"><summary>Proof of stmt-item-simple-cayley</summary>

*Proof of Proposition <a href="#prop:cone-geometry" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:cone-geometry">[prop:cone-geometry]</a>.* *Avoidance.* For <a href="#eq:avoidance" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:avoidance">[eq:avoidance]</a>, close the given avoiding path by one designated side through $c_P$. Then $m\le L+1$, and no other side has center $c_P$. Use a minimal diagram. An arc incidence on the designated side is opposite a bounded face of different assignment or an exterior incidence across a bridge. In the latter case the other incidence cannot also lie in the designated side. If it did, the interval from that bridge’s first traversal to its retraversal in the *linear* side walk would contain no prescribed side break. Excise the visited component and the bridge. This shortens only the designated side, preserving its endpoints and copy assignment, and cannot increase $F$. It contradicts minimality. This argument concerns marked positions; additional visits to a marked vertex do not insert a side break.

Thus every designated-side incidence has length less than $\lambda\sigma(P)$, either by Lemma <a href="#lem:diagram-count" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:diagram-count">4</a>(b) or because its opposite incidence is a generator side. There are at most $2b$ such occurrences. The adjusted walk inside $P$ has length at most $12(L+1)\lambda\sigma(P)$, giving the distance bound. Empty walks, including the case $x=a$, satisfy it as well.

*Simplicity of the Cayley graph.* For part <a href="#item:simple-cayley" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="item:simple-cayley">5</a>, consider a closed word of one or two generator sides. Strict $\lambda<1/12$ in <a href="#eq:diagram-count" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:diagram-count">[eq:diagram-count]</a> forces $F=0$. The exterior contour of a tree is freely trivial. This excludes identity generators, unintended coincidences of generator edges, and generator involutions, as claimed.

*Short fillings.* To establish uniform thinness, we first show that every edge loop of length $n>0$ in $\widehat X$ has a filling with at most $12n$ relation faces of perimeter at most four. For its $m\le n$ sides, take the compressed minimal diagram. Choose one *standard path* for each arc, consistently under reversal. Keep the unit edge if the arc has a generator-side incidence. Otherwise an assignment at an incidence contains both endpoints, so use the two-edge path through that copy’s center.

Around a bounded face assigned to $P$, replace each standard path by the two-edge path through $c_P$. Each replacement uses one closed walk of length at most four. The resulting contour freely contracts through that one center. Thus the standard exterior walk can be filled at cost at most the number of bounded-face incidences. On an exterior adjustable side, make the same replacements through its prescribed center. The consecutive pairs reduce to the required single cone passage. If an inflated side was empty, insert its desired backtrack freely. Generator sides need no change. The total cost is at most the number of bounded-face and exterior adjustable-side incidences, hence at most $2b\le12m\le12n$.

This filling argument works for the singular diagrams being used. More explicitly, for directed edge paths with their endpoint values, the plane-diagram identity expresses the exterior contour as a product of conjugates of the bounded contours, one for each face, up to free edge cancellations and their inverses. Substituting the indicated fillings gives the claimed product of short relations. Conversely, the van Kampen construction from this product gives a plane diagram over the graph: inverse-edge folds identify only vertices with the same target value.

*From fillings to thin triangles.* The bound implies the coarse filling hypothesis in (Bridson and Haefliger 1999, 319:III.H.2.1 and III.H.2.9) <a id="pm-cite-21"></a>[[BH1999](#ref-BH1999)] with universal constants. Subdivide a rectifiable loop of length $\ell$ into $O(\ell+1)$ arcs of length at most one. Choose nearby vertices at distance at most one and join successive ones by paths of at most three edges. Fill this edge loop as above. In a resulting small-face diagram with $f$ faces and exterior length $n$, $$2E=n+\sum_F|\partial F|\le n+4f,\qquad V\le E+1.$$ Thicken its finite plane graph to a regular neighborhood, using vertex disks and edge strips, and cap the bounded contour components. The result is a disk. The vertex regions are mapped to vertices, the strips along their edges, and each cap has a boundary of bounded image perimeter. Triangulation has total cost $O(E+V+f)$; each triangle can be assigned a map of uniformly bounded image diameter. Coarse fillings do not require continuous maps on their interiors. An additional collar with $O(n)$ triangles restores the prescribed exterior parameterization: each block joins a unit-edge traversal to that traversal with its inserted vertex pauses, and its image lies in the same edge. The short paths to the original subdivision points give an annulus with $O(\ell+1)$ further triangles of bounded image diameter. Constant loops or empty approximating walks are filled separately by a bounded number of triangles.

The constants in this construction are independent of the graphs, alphabet, and $\lambda$. A connected unit-edge graph is geodesic, regardless of local finiteness. The cited theorem therefore gives a hyperbolicity constant depending only on these universal coarse filling constants. Enlarging it to an integer accounts for moving points on triangle sides to vertices and gives the required $D_0$. ◻

</details>

#### Simultaneous overlaps

<a id="eq:root-constants"></a>
Set, once and for all, $$\label{eq:root-constants}
t=D_0+3,\qquad L_0=2+2t+2D_0.$$ The next consequence is stronger than a pairwise intersection bound: one ball controls all the intersections in the specified distance order.

<a id="stmt-lem-overlap"></a>
<div id="lem:overlap" class="lemma">

<a id="eq:overlap-radius"></a>
**Lemma 6** (Simultaneous overlap bound). *Let $R$ be a group vertex or a center of $\widehat X$, and let $A=c_P\ne R$. Choose one geodesic from $A$ to $R$, whose first group vertex is $a\in P$. Every vertex shared by $P$ and any *different* copy $Q$ with $$d_{\widehat X}(c_Q,R)\le d_{\widehat X}(c_P,R)$$ lies in the intrinsic induced ball $B_P$ about $a$ of radius $$\label{eq:overlap-radius}
r_P=\left\lfloor12(L_0+1)\lambda\sigma(P)\right\rfloor.$$*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-overlap"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-overlap</summary>

*Proof.* Take such a $Q$ and a shared vertex $x$, and write $B=c_Q$. The path $A,x,B$ is a length-two geodesic. A geodesic from $B$ to $R$ avoids $A$, since passing through $A$ would give length $2+d(A,R)>d(A,R)$. All distances in the rest of this paragraph are in $\widehat X$.

If $d(A,R)\ge t$, let $z$ be the vertex at distance $t$ along the chosen $A$-to-$R$ geodesic. Its distance from every vertex of $A,x,B$ is at least $t-2>D_0$. Thinness supplies a vertex $y$ on the $B$-to-$R$ side with $d(y,z)\le D_0$. A shortest bridge between them avoids $A$, because $d(A,z)=t>D_0$. Follow $x,B,y,z,a$, using that bridge and the indicated geodesic segments. This walk avoids $A$, and its length is at most $$1+(2+t+D_0)+D_0+(t-1)=L_0.$$ If $d(A,R)<t$, use instead the walk from $x$ through $B$ to $R$ and back to $a$. It avoids $A$ and has length at most $1+d(B,R)+d(A,R)-1\le2d(A,R)\le L_0$.

Apply <a href="#eq:avoidance" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:avoidance">[eq:avoidance]</a> to obtain $d_P(x,a)\le12(L_0+1)\lambda\sigma(P)$. Intrinsic vertex distances are integers, so taking the floor gives the claimed radius. Crucially, the chosen $a$ does not depend on $Q$ or $x$. ◻

</details>

### Labelled expanders and their finite cycle spaces

<a id="eq:lambda-choice"></a>
Fix a positive $\lambda<1/24$ so small that $$\label{eq:lambda-choice}
12(L_0+1)\lambda\le\frac1{16},$$ where $L_0$ is the universal constant from <a href="#eq:root-constants" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:root-constants">[eq:root-constants]</a>. Put $\eta=1/100$.

<a id="stmt-prop-data"></a>
<div id="prop:data" class="proposition">

**Proposition 7** (Choice of data). *There are labelled graphs $(\Theta_i)_{i\ge1}$ satisfying all the hypotheses of Section <a href="#sec:geometry" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:geometry">2</a>, with the following additional properties. Write $n_i=|\operatorname{Vert}(\Theta_i)|$, $k_i=\lfloor n_i/2\rfloor$, and let $\sigma_i$ be the girth. There are constants $\rho,\kappa>0$, independent of $i$, such that:*

1.  *On the orthogonal complement of constants in $\ell^2(\operatorname{Vert}(\Theta_i))$, $dI-\mathop{\mathrm{Adj}}_{\Theta_i}\ge\rho I$, and for every vertex subset $W$, <a id="eq:expansion"></a>$$\label{eq:expansion}
    |\partial W|\ge\kappa\min\{|W|,n_i-|W|\}.$$ Here $\partial W$ is the unoriented edge boundary in $\Theta_i$.*

2.  *With <a id="eq:c-definition"></a>$$\label{eq:c-definition}
    c=\frac\eta2\min\{\kappa,1\},$$ we have $n_i>100$, $\sigma_i>\max\{16,20/c\}$, $\lambda\sigma_i>3$, and $1<\lfloor\lambda\sigma_i\rfloor<\lfloor\lambda\sigma_{i+1}\rfloor$.*

3.  *Every induced intrinsic ball of radius at most $\sigma_i/16$ is a tree with fewer than $\eta n_i$ vertices.*

4.  *The integers $k_i$ strictly increase and $k_{i+1}-k_i\to\infty$.*

<a id="eq:small-ball"></a>
*Consequently every ball $B_P$ in Lemma <a href="#lem:overlap" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:overlap">6</a>, for every permitted root and chosen root geodesic, is an induced tree satisfying $$\label{eq:small-ball}
r_P\le\sigma(P)/16,\qquad |B_P|<\eta|P|.$$*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-prop-data"><summary>Proof of stmt-prop-data</summary>

*Proof.* Start with a fixed-degree family of connected regular expanders of unbounded order and girth at least a positive constant times logarithmic order, as supplied by the Lubotzky–Phillips–Sarnak construction (Lubotzky, Phillips, and Sarnak 1988, Theorems 3.4 and 4.1) <a id="pm-cite-22"></a>[[LPS1988](#ref-LPS1988)]; see also (Osajda 2020, sec. 2.4) <a id="pm-cite-23"></a>[[Osajda2020](#ref-Osajda2020)]. Discarding small graphs leaves simple graphs. Their Laplacians have a common gap $\rho>0$ off the constants. Bipartiteness, if present, causes no problem: the adjacency eigenvalue $-d$ gives Laplacian eigenvalue $2d$, not another zero eigenvalue.

For $w=|W|$, the centered indicator of $W$ has squared norm $w(n-w)/n$ and Laplacian energy $|\partial W|$. Thus $$|\partial W|\ge\rho\frac{w(n-w)}n
\ge\frac\rho2\min\{w,n-w\},$$ so we may choose $\kappa=\rho/2$. A ball containing at most half the vertices has an outside vertex boundary of size at least $\kappa/d$ times its size. Balls grow by a fixed factor until they contain more than half the graph. Any two such balls intersect, giving a uniform $O(\log n)$ diameter bound. Hence the diameter/girth ratio is bounded.

An induced ball of integer radius $r$ with $2r+1<\sigma$ is a tree: an edge outside a shortest-path tree would create a cycle of length at most $2r+1$. Its order at $r\le\sigma/16$ is at most $$1+d\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor\sigma/16\rfloor-1}(d-1)^j.$$ Regularity and the same tree property at radius $\lfloor(\sigma-2)/2\rfloor$ give the lower bound $$n\ge1+d\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor(\sigma-2)/2\rfloor-1}(d-1)^j.$$ The ratio tends to zero with $\sigma$. A subsequence therefore satisfies (ii)–(iv), including (iii); growing order allows the gaps in (iv) to be chosen arbitrarily large.

The resulting sequence has bounded degree, growing girth, bounded diameter/girth ratio, and the required strictly increasing floors. Osajda’s labelling theorem (Osajda 2020, Theorem 2.7) <a id="pm-cite-24"></a>[[Osajda2020](#ref-Osajda2020)], applied to the already chosen $\lambda$, gives a reduced labelling over one finite alphabet with no repeated long path word. Its conclusion for nonbacktracking paths includes the simple-path condition used here. The associated graphical presentation has isometrically embedded relators by (Osajda 2020, Lemma 3.1 and Theorem 3.2) <a id="pm-cite-25"></a>[[Osajda2020](#ref-Osajda2020)]. This supplies the data of Section <a href="#sec:geometry" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:geometry">2</a>. Finally, <a href="#eq:lambda-choice" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:lambda-choice">[eq:lambda-choice]</a>, <a href="#eq:overlap-radius" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:overlap-radius">[eq:overlap-radius]</a>, and (iii) give <a href="#eq:small-ball" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:small-ball">[eq:small-ball]</a>. ◻

We identify a copy with its vertex set when discussing supports and specify its edges when discussing chains.

For a graph $Y$, let $Z_1(Y;\mathbb F_2)$ be its ordinary *finite-support* cycle space: its elements are finite sums of edges with coefficients in $\mathbb F_2$, with even incidence at every vertex. Thus no completed chain spaces are used in the next statement.

<a id="stmt-lem-cycle-sum"></a>
<div id="lem:cycle-sum" class="lemma">

**Lemma 8** (Direct sum of copy cycles). *The inclusions of the copies give an internal algebraic direct sum <a id="eq:cycle-sum"></a>$$\label{eq:cycle-sum}
Z_1(X;\mathbb F_2)=\bigoplus_P Z_1(P;\mathbb F_2).$$*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-cycle-sum"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-cycle-sum</summary>

*Proof.* Every finite cycle is a sum of closed walks. The label of any such walk is trivial in $\Gamma$, so a finite van Kampen diagram over the presentation expresses its edge chain, modulo two, as the sum of the chains of its bounded-face walks. Each lies in a copy. This proves spanning.

Suppose finitely many nonzero cycles in distinct copies sum to zero. Choose a group-vertex root $R$ and a participating copy $P$ whose center is farthest from $R$. By Lemma <a href="#lem:overlap" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:overlap">6</a>, all its intersections with the other participating copies lie in one ball $B_P$, which is an induced tree by <a href="#eq:small-ball" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:small-ball">[eq:small-ball]</a>. Every nonzero edge coefficient in its cycle must cancel against an edge of another participating copy. Both endpoints therefore lie in $B_P$, and inducedness puts the edge itself in that tree. A tree has no nonzero finite cycle, a contradiction. ◻

</details>

<a id="sec:module"></a>
### Codes and the module extension

Fix the labelled graphs and the group $\Gamma$ supplied by Proposition <a href="#prop:data" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:data">7</a>. Retain the notation $$n_i=|\operatorname{Vert}(\Theta_i)|,\qquad
  k_i=\lfloor n_i/2\rfloor,\qquad \eta=\frac1{100}.$$ In particular, $n_i>100$. A copy $P=P_{g,i}$ carries its prescribed identification with $\Theta_i$, and its vertex set is a subset of $\Gamma$. For every choice of root and copy to which Lemma <a href="#lem:overlap" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:overlap">6</a> applies, the ball furnished by that lemma is an induced tree with fewer than $\eta n_i$ vertices, by Proposition <a href="#prop:data" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:data">7</a>.

<a id="pm-h-13"></a>
#### Port patterns and codes

We call an outgoing generator label a *port*. At a vertex $v$ of $\Theta_i$, let $$J_i(v)\subseteq S^{\pm1}$$ be the set of outgoing labels of its incident edges. Reducedness of the labelling and $d$-regularity give $|J_i(v)|=d$. Choose a prime $p>2$ large enough that the $d$-element subsets of $S^{\pm1}$ admit an injection $$J\longmapsto a_J\in\mathbb F_p^\times.$$ Define the decoder $$\mathcal J(a)=
  \begin{cases}
    J,&a=a_J\text{ for a \(d\)-element subset }J\subseteq S^{\pm1},\\
    \varnothing,&\text{otherwise},
  \end{cases}
  \qquad a\in\mathbb F_p.$$ The prototype pattern on $\Theta_i$ is $$w_i(v)=a_{J_i(v)}.$$ Every coordinate of $w_i$ is nonzero. Write $J_P(h)$ and $w_P(h)$ for the corresponding data transported to a copy $P=P_{g,i}$, so that $\mathcal J(w_P(h))=J_P(h)$.

On a finite coordinate space $\mathbb F_p^E$, orthogonal complements will always refer to the nondegenerate bilinear form $$\langle z,z'\rangle=\sum_{v\in E}z(v)z'(v)\in\mathbb F_p.$$ For $z\in\mathbb F_p^E$, let $\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}z=\{v\in E:z(v)\ne0\}$.

We seek a linear code containing the prescribed word $w_i$ with two different support guarantees. Any other codeword must differ from $w_i$ at many vertices, forcing many missing prescribed edge incidences after decoding. Vectors in the dual code will instead supply the module relations. Their support must be larger than an overlap ball, so that a nonzero relation on a farthest copy cannot cancel against all the other relations. The following Gilbert–Varshamov type counting argument (Gilbert 1952; Varshamov 1957) <a id="pm-cite-26"></a>[[Gilbert1952](#ref-Gilbert1952), [Varshamov1957](#ref-Varshamov1957)] obtains both bounds while keeping $w_i$ in the code.

<a id="stmt-lem-codes"></a>
<div id="lem:codes" class="lemma">

**Lemma 9** (Codes with both distances). *For every $i$ there is a $k_i$-dimensional subspace $C_i\subseteq\mathbb F_p^{\operatorname{Vert}(\Theta_i)}$ such that $w_i\in C_i$ and $$|\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}z|>\eta n_i
  \qquad
  \text{for every nonzero }z\in C_i\cup C_i^\perp.$$*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-codes"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-codes</summary>

<a id="eq:primal-probability"></a>
*Proof.* Write $n=n_i$, $k=k_i$, and $w=w_i$, and choose $C$ uniformly among the $k$-dimensional subspaces containing $\langle w\rangle$. For a fixed $z\notin\langle w\rangle$, the quotient by $\langle w\rangle$ gives $$\label{eq:primal-probability}
  \Pr(z\in C)
  =\frac{p^{k-1}-1}{p^{n-1}-1}
  <p^{k-n}.$$ Indeed, a uniform $a$-dimensional subspace of an $N$-dimensional vector space contains a fixed nonzero vector with probability $(p^a-1)/(p^N-1)$, by counting nonzero vectors and symmetry. Nonzero vectors of $\langle w\rangle$ have full support.

Orthogonal complementation is a bijection from the sample space just used to the $(n-k)$-dimensional subspaces of the $(n-1)$-dimensional space $w^\perp$. Consequently, for a fixed nonzero $z$, <a id="eq:dual-probability"></a>$$\label{eq:dual-probability}
  \Pr(z\in C^\perp)=
  \begin{cases}
    \displaystyle\frac{p^{n-k}-1}{p^{n-1}-1}<p^{1-k},
       &z\in w^\perp,\\[6pt]
    0,&z\notin w^\perp.
  \end{cases}$$ This argument uses nondegeneracy of the form on the ambient coordinate space, not of its restriction to $w^\perp$; it remains valid when $\langle w,w\rangle=0$.

<a id="eq:hamming-ball"></a>
We bound the number of low-weight vectors. Put $r=\lfloor\eta n\rfloor$ and $q=\eta/(1-\eta)<1$. The binomial theorem gives $$q^r\sum_{j=0}^r\binom nj
  \le \sum_{j=0}^r\binom nj q^j
  \le (1+q)^n.$$ It follows that $$\sum_{j=0}^r\binom nj
  \le q^{-\eta n}(1+q)^n
  =2^{H_2(\eta)n},
  \qquad
  H_2(t)=-t\log_2t-(1-t)\log_2(1-t).$$ Here $H_2(1/100)<9/100$: use $\log_2 100<7$, $-\log(1-\eta)\le\eta/(1-\eta)$, and $\log2>1/2$. Since $p\ge2$, the Hamming ball therefore satisfies $$\label{eq:hamming-ball}
  \bigl|\{z:|\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}z|\le r\}\bigr|
  =\sum_{j=0}^r\binom nj(p-1)^j
  \le p^{\eta n}2^{H_2(\eta)n}
  \le p^{(\eta+H_2(\eta))n}
  <p^{n/4}.$$ Every nonzero vector in this ball lies outside $\langle w\rangle$. By <a href="#eq:primal-probability" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:primal-probability">[eq:primal-probability]</a>–<a href="#eq:hamming-ball" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:hamming-ball">[eq:hamming-ball]</a> and the union bound, the probability that either $C$ or $C^\perp$ contains such a vector is less than $$p^{n/4}\bigl(p^{k-n}+p^{1-k}\bigr)
  \le p^{-n/4}+p^{3/2-n/4}<1,$$ where $k=\lfloor n/2\rfloor$ and $n>100$. Thus a suitable $C$ exists. ◻

</details>

Choose a code $C_i$ as in Lemma <a href="#lem:codes" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:codes">9</a> for every $i$. Transporting coordinates defines subspaces $C_P$ and $C_P^\perp$ on every copy $P=P_{g,i}$. The transport preserves the bilinear form, so these subspaces are orthogonal complements in $\mathbb F_p^P$.

<a id="pm-h-14"></a>
#### The quotient module

Translation-invariant quotients of finite-field lamp modules also occur in Austin’s irrational-kernel construction (Austin 2013, secs. 1–2) <a id="pm-cite-27"></a>[[Austin2013](#ref-Austin2013)]. Here the relations are dual codewords on all translated expander copies. We must show that imposing them globally creates exactly the prescribed relations on each individual copy.

<a id="eq:relation-module"></a>
Let $\mathbb F_p^{(\Gamma)}$ denote the finite-support coordinate space with basis $(\mathbf e_h)_{h\in\Gamma}$, on which $\Gamma$ acts by $g\mathbf e_h=\mathbf e_{gh}$. Regard $\mathbb F_p^P$ as its coordinate subspace by extension by zero. Define the algebraic sum $$\label{eq:relation-module}
  W_0=\sum_{\substack{g\in\Gamma\\i\ge1}} C_{P_{g,i}}^\perp
  \subseteq\mathbb F_p^{(\Gamma)}.$$ Every vector in this sum is a sum of finitely many terms. Left translation carries $C_{P_{g,i}}^\perp$ to $C_{P_{hg,i}}^\perp$, so $W_0$ is $\Gamma$-invariant.

<a id="stmt-lem-module-intersection"></a>
<div id="lem:module-intersection" class="lemma">

<a id="eq:module-intersection"></a>
**Lemma 10** (Exact coordinate relations). *For every copy $P$, $$\label{eq:module-intersection}
  W_0\cap\mathbb F_p^P=C_P^\perp.$$*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-module-intersection"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-module-intersection</summary>

*Proof.* The inclusion from right to left follows from <a href="#eq:relation-module" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:relation-module">[eq:relation-module]</a>. For the converse, let $$z=\sum_{Q\in\mathcal F}z_Q\in W_0\cap\mathbb F_p^P,
  \qquad 0\ne z_Q\in C_Q^\perp,$$ where $\mathcal F$ is finite and its copies are distinct. Such an expression is obtained by combining terms on the same copy and discarding zero terms.

Suppose that $\mathcal F$ contains a copy different from $P$. Root the coned graph at $R=c_P$, and choose $Q\in\mathcal F\setminus\{P\}$ whose center is farthest from $R$. Every other copy in $\mathcal F$, and also $P$ itself, has center at distance from $R$ at most $d_{\widehat X}(c_Q,R)$. Lemma <a href="#lem:overlap" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:overlap">6</a> places all their vertex intersections with $Q$ in one intrinsic ball $B_Q\subseteq Q$. If $Q$ has type $j$, Proposition <a href="#prop:data" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:data">7</a> gives $$|B_Q|<\eta n_j<|\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}z_Q|.$$ Choose $h\in\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}z_Q\setminus B_Q$. Then $h\notin P$ and $h$ belongs to no other copy in $\mathcal F$. The $h$-coordinate of $z$ is therefore $z_Q(h)\ne0$, contradicting $z\in\mathbb F_p^P$. Hence the expression has no term away from $P$, and $z\in C_P^\perp$. ◻

</details>

<a id="stmt-auto-definition-1"></a>
<div class="definition">

**Definition 11**. Define the discrete additive group and distinguished element $$V=\mathbb F_p^{(\Gamma)}/W_0,\qquad v_*=[\mathbf e_1]\in V,$$ with the induced $\Gamma$-action, and put $$G_{\mathrm{sur}}=V\rtimes\Gamma.$$ We use the multiplication $(v,g)(v',g')=(v+gv',gg')$ in $G_{\mathrm{sur}}$.

</div>

For $h\in\Gamma$, one has $hv_*=[\mathbf e_h]$. The group $G_{\mathrm{sur}}$ is countable: $\Gamma$ is finitely generated, and the finite-support coordinate space over the finite field $\mathbb F_p$, its quotient $V$, and $V\times\Gamma$ are countable. In fact $G_{\mathrm{sur}}$ is finitely generated. The additive group of $V$ is generated by the $\Gamma$-orbit of $v_*$, since these are the images of the coordinate basis and multiplication by a scalar in $\mathbb F_p$ is repeated addition. In the semidirect product, $$(0,h)(v_*,1)(0,h)^{-1}=(hv_*,1).$$ It follows that $(v_*,1)$ together with the finitely many lifts $(0,s)$, $s\in S$, generates $G_{\mathrm{sur}}$.

<a id="pm-h-15"></a>
#### The compact dual and its finite marginals

Write the compact dual of $V$ as $$\Omega=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb F_p}(V,\mathbb F_p).$$ The corresponding complex character is $v\mapsto \exp(2\pi\mathrm i\,\omega(v)/p)$, where field elements are identified with residues modulo $p$. We equip $\Omega$ with the topology of pointwise convergence, equivalently the subspace topology from $\mathbb F_p^V$. It is a closed subgroup of this compact product, and hence a compact abelian group. Let $m_\Omega$ be its Haar probability measure. For $\omega\in\Omega$ and $h\in\Gamma$, define $$X_\omega(h)=\omega(hv_*).$$ The notation $X_\omega$ denotes a coordinate pattern, not the Cayley graph $X$.

<a id="stmt-lem-marginals"></a>
<div id="lem:marginals" class="lemma">

<a id="eq:uniform-marginal"></a>
**Lemma 12** (Uniform pattern marginals). *For a copy $P=P_{g,i}$, the restriction map $$\operatorname{res}_P:\Omega\longrightarrow\mathbb F_p^P,
  \qquad \omega\longmapsto X_\omega|_P$$ has image exactly $C_P$. Its pushforward of $m_\Omega$ is the uniform measure on $C_P$. In particular, for every $a\in C_P$, $$\label{eq:uniform-marginal}
  m_\Omega\{\omega:X_\omega|_P=a\}=p^{-k_i},$$ and this applies to $a=w_P$.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-marginals"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-marginals</summary>

*Proof.* Let $q_P:\mathbb F_p^P\to V$ be the restriction of the quotient map. Lemma <a href="#lem:module-intersection" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:module-intersection">10</a> gives $$\ker q_P=C_P^\perp.$$ For $z\in\mathbb F_p^P$ and $\omega\in\Omega$, $$\omega(q_P(z))
  =\sum_{h\in P}z(h)\omega(hv_*)
  =\langle z,X_\omega|_P\rangle.$$ Thus $X_\omega|_P$ annihilates $C_P^\perp$, and belongs to $(C_P^\perp)^\perp=C_P$.

Conversely, if $a\in C_P$, the rule $$q_P(z)\longmapsto\langle z,a\rangle$$ is a well-defined linear functional on $q_P(\mathbb F_p^P)$. Extend a basis of this subspace to a basis of $V$, and extend the functional linearly to obtain $\omega\in\Omega$. Then $X_\omega|_P=a$, proving surjectivity. No continuity condition is lost in this extension, since $V$ is discrete.

The map $\operatorname{res}_P$ is a continuous homomorphism of compact groups onto the finite group $C_P$. Its pushforward of Haar probability is translation-invariant and hence uniform on $C_P$. As $|C_P|=p^{k_i}$, formula <a href="#eq:uniform-marginal" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:uniform-marginal">[eq:uniform-marginal]</a> follows. ◻

</details>

<a id="stmt-auto-remark-1"></a>
<div class="remark">

*Remark 13*. Lemma <a href="#lem:marginals" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:marginals">12</a> specifies each individual copy marginal. It makes no independence assertion about patterns on different copies. The exact intersection <a href="#eq:module-intersection" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:module-intersection">[eq:module-intersection]</a>, which uses simultaneous containment of all relevant overlaps in one ball, is what guarantees these marginals despite the relations imposed on every translated copy.

</div>

<a id="sec:spectrum"></a>
### An operator with a uniform spectral gap

We use the data of Proposition <a href="#prop:data" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:data">7</a> and the module constructed above. In particular, the induced intrinsic balls supplied by Lemma <a href="#lem:overlap" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:overlap">6</a> have radius at most $\sigma(P)/16$, are trees, and contain fewer than $\eta|P|$ vertices. All copies below retain their indices, and $|P|=n_i$ for a copy of type $i$.

<a id="pm-h-17"></a>
#### The reduced algebra and its evaluation representations

<a id="eq:semidirect-fourier"></a>
The action of $\Gamma$ on the compact dual $\Omega$ of $V$ is $$(g\omega)(v)=\omega(g^{-1}v).$$ Write $\alpha_g(f)(\omega)=f(g^{-1}\omega)$ for the corresponding action on $C(\Omega)$. Fourier transformation on the discrete abelian group $V$ identifies $C_r^*(V)$ with $C(\Omega)$. The regular representation of the semidirect product, or equivalently regular induction followed by this Fourier transformation, gives $$C_r^*(G_{\mathrm{sur}})=C_r^*(V\rtimes\Gamma)
 \cong C(\Omega)\rtimes_{\alpha,r}\Gamma.
 \label{eq:semidirect-fourier}$$ We use this identification throughout, and write $u_g$ for the canonical unitary corresponding to $g\in\Gamma$. Thus $u_gfu_g^*=\alpha_g(f)$.

<a id="eq:regular-evaluations"></a>
For $\omega\in\Omega$, the regular representation induced by evaluation at $\omega$ acts on $\ell^2(\Gamma)$. After inverting the usual group index, its formulas are $$\begin{split}
 (\pi_\omega(f)\xi)(h)&=f(h^{-1}\omega)\xi(h),\qquad f\in C(\Omega),\\
 (\pi_\omega(u_g)\xi)(h)&=\xi(hg),\qquad g\in\Gamma.
 \end{split}
 \label{eq:regular-evaluations}$$ The direct sum of all evaluations is a faithful representation of $C(\Omega)$; inducing it regularly shows that $\bigoplus_{\omega\in\Omega}\pi_\omega$ is faithful for the reduced crossed product. In particular, the norm of an element of <a href="#eq:semidirect-fourier" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:semidirect-fourier">[eq:semidirect-fourier]</a> is the supremum of its norms in this family.

<a id="eq:trace-integral"></a>
Let $\tau_{G_{\mathrm{sur}}}$ denote the canonical trace, normalized by $\tau_{G_{\mathrm{sur}}}(1)=1$. For every $b\in C_r^*(G_{\mathrm{sur}})$, $$\tau_{G_{\mathrm{sur}}}(b)=\int_\Omega
 \langle\pi_\omega(b)\delta_1,\delta_1\rangle\,
 \mathrm{d}m_\Omega(\omega).
 \label{eq:trace-integral}$$ Indeed, for a finite crossed-product sum $b=\sum_g F_g u_g$ the diagonal coefficient at $1$ is $F_1(\omega)$, and its Haar integral is the canonical group trace under Fourier transformation. Such sums are dense. The diagonal coefficients converge uniformly under norm approximation, so the identity extends to every $b$. This also proves that the integrand in <a href="#eq:trace-integral" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:trace-integral">[eq:trace-integral]</a> is continuous.

For $s\in S^{\pm1}$, let $f_s\in C(\Omega)$ be the clopen-set projection $$f_s(\omega)=\mathbf 1_{\{s\in\mathcal J(\omega(v_*))\}}.$$ Define <a id="eq:operator"></a>$$T=dI-\sum_{s\in S^{\pm1}}f_su_sf_{s^{-1}}
 \quad\in C_r^*(G_{\mathrm{sur}}).
 \label{eq:operator}$$ This element is self-adjoint, since taking adjoints interchanges the terms indexed by $s$ and $s^{-1}$. Each $f_s$ is a function of a single finite-valued coordinate and hence is a finite Fourier sum of characters of $\Omega$ indexed by elements of $V$. Thus the construction takes place in the ordinary reduced group $C^*$-algebra in <a href="#eq:semidirect-fourier" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:semidirect-fourier">[eq:semidirect-fourier]</a>.

<a id="stmt-auto-definition-2"></a>
<div class="definition">

**Definition 14**. For $\omega\in\Omega$, its *enabled graph* $G_\omega$ has vertex set $\Gamma$. At $h\in\Gamma$ the allowed outgoing labels are $\mathcal J(X_\omega(h))$. The Cayley edge from $h$ to $hs$ is enabled precisely when $s$ is allowed at $h$ and $s^{-1}$ is allowed at $hs$. A copy $P$ is *valid for $\omega$* if $X_\omega|_P=w_P$. Let $U_\omega$ be the complement in $\Gamma$ of the union of all valid copies.

</div>

The graph $G_\omega$ is undirected and simple, and all its degrees are at most $d$, because the decoder returns either a $d$-element set or the empty set. Since $$(h^{-1}\omega)(v_*)=\omega(hv_*)=X_\omega(h),$$ the formulas <a href="#eq:regular-evaluations" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:regular-evaluations">[eq:regular-evaluations]</a> give <a id="eq:enabled-operator"></a>$$\pi_\omega(T)=dI-\mathop{\mathrm{Adj}}(G_\omega).
 \label{eq:enabled-operator}$$ The degree bound implies $\|\mathop{\mathrm{Adj}}(G_\omega)\|\le d$, so this operator is bounded, positive, and at most $2dI$.

On a valid copy $P$, the allowed port sets are exactly $J_P(h)$. All copy edges are therefore enabled, and their $d$ incidences at each vertex exhaust its allowed labels. As $P$ is connected, it is a whole isolated component of $G_\omega$, with precisely its copy edges. Moreover, distinct valid copies are disjoint. To see this, two valid copies meeting at a vertex would both be the entire same component. If their indices were distinct, root $\widehat X$ at a shared group vertex. Their centers would both have distance one from the root, so Lemma <a href="#lem:overlap" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:overlap">6</a> would place their entire common vertex set in an intrinsic ball of the first copy containing fewer than $\eta|P|<|P|$ vertices, a contradiction. It follows also that no enabled edge joins $U_\omega$ to its complement.

<a id="pm-h-18"></a>
#### Deficits on invalid copies and on arbitrary finite sets

For $B\subseteq P$, let $e_{\omega,P}(B)$ be the number of enabled copy edges with both endpoints in $B$.

<a id="stmt-lem-local-deficit"></a>
<div id="lem:local-deficit" class="lemma">

**Lemma 15** (Deficit inside an invalid copy). *If $P$ is invalid for $\omega$, then every subset $B\subseteq P$ satisfies <a id="eq:local-deficit"></a>$$d|B|-2e_{\omega,P}(B)\ge c|B|.
 \label{eq:local-deficit}$$*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-local-deficit"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-local-deficit</summary>

*Proof.* Write $n=|P|$, and let $M=\{h\in P:X_\omega(h)\ne w_P(h)\}$. By Lemma <a href="#lem:marginals" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:marginals">12</a>, the restriction $X_\omega|_P$ belongs to $C_P$, and $w_P\in C_P$ by Lemma <a href="#lem:codes" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:codes">9</a>. Their difference is nonzero, since $P$ is invalid. The primal-distance bound therefore gives $|M|>\eta n$.

At a vertex of $M$, the decoder returns either the empty set or a $d$-element set different from the prescribed $J_P(h)$. In either case at least one prescribed incidence is missing. Consequently, writing $b=|B|$, we have both $$db-2e_{\omega,P}(B)\ge |M\cap B|
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 db-2e_{\omega,P}(B)\ge |\partial_P B|.$$ If $n-b\ge\eta n/2$, then $\min(b,n-b)\ge(\eta/2)b$. The edge-expansion bound in Proposition <a href="#prop:data" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:data">7</a> yields $$|\partial_P B|\ge\kappa\min(b,n-b)
 \ge(\eta\kappa/2)b\ge cb.$$ Otherwise, $$|M\cap B|\ge |M|-(n-b)>\eta n/2
 \ge(\eta/2)b\ge cb.$$ Here we used $c=(\eta/2)\min(\kappa,1)$. The assertion for $B=\varnothing$ is included. ◻

</details>

<a id="stmt-lem-global-deficit"></a>
<div id="lem:global-deficit" class="lemma">

<a id="eq:global-deficit"></a>
**Lemma 16** (Deficit on the remaining graph). *For every $\omega\in\Omega$ and every finite set $Y\subseteq U_\omega$, let $A$ be the graph on $Y$ consisting of all its internal enabled edges. Then $$d|Y|-2|E(A)|\ge(c/2)|Y|.
 \label{eq:global-deficit}$$*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-global-deficit"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-global-deficit</summary>

*Proof.* The local deficits cannot simply be added over copies, since their vertices and edges can overlap. Instead, we bound the cycle rank $b_1(A)=\dim Z_1(A;\mathbb F_2)$. Euler’s identity $$|E(A)|=|Y|-\#\pi_0(A)+b_1(A)$$ holds also for isolated vertices and the empty graph. It shows that the desired deficit follows from $$b_1(A)\le\bigl(d/2-1-c/4\bigr)|Y|.$$ We will obtain this bound by charging the ranks of successive copy coordinates to pairwise disjoint subsets of $Y$.

*Eliminating cycle coordinates.* We regard the finite-dimensional space $\mathcal L=Z_1(A;\mathbb F_2)$ as a subspace of $Z_1(X;\mathbb F_2)$. Lemma <a href="#lem:cycle-sum" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:cycle-sum">8</a> provides the algebraic direct-sum decomposition $$Z_1(X;\mathbb F_2)=\bigoplus_P Z_1(P;\mathbb F_2),$$ with coordinate maps $\operatorname{pr}_P$. These are the coordinates in the algebraic decomposition, not restrictions of edge chains to $P$: a projected cycle can contain edges absent from the original cycle. Only finitely many coordinates can be nonzero on $\mathcal L$: choose a finite basis of $\mathcal L$ and take the union of its finite coordinate supports. Call these copies $P_1,\ldots,P_N$, ordered by nonincreasing distance of their centers from a fixed group-vertex root $R$ in $\widehat X$. Ties may be resolved arbitrarily.

<a id="eq:cycle-elimination"></a>
For $1\le j\le N+1$, set $$\mathcal L_j=
 \{z\in\mathcal L:\operatorname{pr}_{P_i}z=0\text{ for }i<j\}.$$ Then $\mathcal L_1=\mathcal L$, $\mathcal L_{N+1}=0$, and rank-nullity gives $$\dim\mathcal L=
 \sum_{j=1}^N\dim\operatorname{pr}_{P_j}(\mathcal L_j).
 \label{eq:cycle-elimination}$$ If $\mathcal L=0$, this is understood as an empty sum.

<a id="eq:projected-cycle-rank"></a>
*Localizing projected cycles.* Fix a step $j$, put $P=P_j$, and choose the induced intrinsic tree ball $B_P$ given by Lemma <a href="#lem:overlap" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:overlap">6</a> for this root. It contains every vertex of $P$ shared with any of the other remaining copies $P_{j+1},\ldots,P_N$. Let $$D_P=B_P\cup(A\cap P),$$ where $A\cap P$ has vertex set $Y\cap P$ and the edges common to $A$ and $P$. We claim that <a id="eq:projected-cycle-support"></a>$$\operatorname{pr}_P(\mathcal L_j)\subseteq Z_1(D_P;\mathbb F_2).
 \label{eq:projected-cycle-support}$$ Indeed, let an edge $e$ occur with nonzero coefficient in $\operatorname{pr}_P z$ for $z\in\mathcal L_j$. If $e$ does not belong to $A$, its coefficient must cancel with a coefficient from another remaining copy, because the total chain $z$ is supported in $A$. Both endpoints of $e$ are consequently shared with that copy and lie in $B_P$. As the ball is induced in $P$, the edge $e$ itself lies in $B_P$. All other edges of $\operatorname{pr}_P z$ lie in $A\cap P$. The projection is already a cycle in $P$, so the claimed inclusion follows. In particular, $$\dim\operatorname{pr}_P(\mathcal L_j)\le b_1(D_P).
 \label{eq:projected-cycle-rank}$$

The projected cycle may acquire edges in the overlap tree, but its other edges remain enabled edges on $Y$. The tree can contribute to a cycle only through multiple attachments of an exterior component.

*Bounding exterior cycles and attachments.* If $P$ is valid, $Y\cap P=\varnothing$, so $D_P=B_P$ is a tree and the right side is zero. Suppose from now on that $P$ is invalid, and write $\sigma=\sigma(P)$ and $r$ for the radius of $B_P$. Thus $r\le\sigma/16$ and $\sigma>\max(16,20/c)$.

<a id="eq:tree-attachment-rank"></a>
Consider the connected components $C$ of the graph obtained from $D_P$ by deleting the vertices of $B_P$. Their vertex sets partition $(Y\cap P)\setminus B_P$. Let $l_C$ be the number of edges joining $C$ to $B_P$. Since $B_P$ is a connected tree, the Euler characteristic formula for graph cycle rank gives $$b_1(D_P)=\sum_C\bigl(b_1(C)+\max(0,l_C-1)\bigr).
 \label{eq:tree-attachment-rank}$$ This formula includes components with no attachment: such a component contributes only its own cycle rank.

<a id="eq:exterior-cycle-rank"></a>
All edges of $C$ are enabled copy edges, and all enabled copy edges between its vertices occur in $C$, by the definition of $A$. If $C$ is cyclic, it has at least $\sigma$ vertices. Applying Lemma <a href="#lem:local-deficit" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:local-deficit">15</a> to its vertex set and using connectedness, we obtain $$\begin{split}
 b_1(C)&=|E(C)|-|C|+1\\
 &\le\bigl(d/2-1-c/2+1/\sigma\bigr)|C|.
 \end{split}
 \label{eq:exterior-cycle-rank}$$ The same bound holds for an acyclic $C$, because then $b_1(C)=0$ and the displayed coefficient is positive. Here $d\ge3$ and $0<c\le\eta/2=1/200$.

<a id="eq:attachment-separation"></a>
We next bound the extra rank caused by attachments to the tree. Choose two distinct attachment edges, and denote their endpoints in $C$ by $v,v'$ and their endpoints in $B_P$ by $b,b'$. A shortest path in $C$ from $v$ to $v'$, these two edges, and the tree path from $b'$ to $b$ form a simple cycle in $P$. This remains true if one pair of endpoints agrees, using the corresponding path of length zero; both pairs cannot agree because $P$ is simple. The tree path has length at most $2r$, since both its endpoints lie in the intrinsic ball of radius $r$. Girth therefore gives $$\mathop{\mathrm{dist}}_C(v,v')\ge\sigma-2r-2>\sigma/2.
 \label{eq:attachment-separation}$$ In particular, all the attachment endpoints in $C$ are distinct.

<a id="eq:attachment-packing"></a>
If $l_C\ge2$, the intrinsic balls in $C$ of radius $\lfloor\sigma/4\rfloor$ centered at these endpoints are pairwise disjoint, by <a href="#eq:attachment-separation" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:attachment-separation">[eq:attachment-separation]</a>. Each contains at least $\lfloor\sigma/4\rfloor+1>\sigma/4$ vertices: take an initial segment of a shortest path to any other attachment endpoint. Thus $$\max(0,l_C-1)\le(4/\sigma)|C|.
 \label{eq:attachment-packing}$$ For $l_C=0$ or $1$ the same inequality is immediate.

<a id="eq:exterior-rank-bound"></a>
Combining <a href="#eq:tree-attachment-rank" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:tree-attachment-rank">[eq:tree-attachment-rank]</a>, <a href="#eq:exterior-cycle-rank" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:exterior-cycle-rank">[eq:exterior-cycle-rank]</a>, and <a href="#eq:attachment-packing" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:attachment-packing">[eq:attachment-packing]</a>, and using $5/\sigma<c/4$, yields $$\begin{split}
 b_1(D_P)
 &\le\bigl(d/2-1-c/2+5/\sigma\bigr)
       |(Y\cap P)\setminus B_P|\\
 &\le\bigl(d/2-1-c/4\bigr)|(Y\cap P)\setminus B_P|.
 \end{split}
 \label{eq:exterior-rank-bound}$$ As observed above, this last bound holds also for a valid copy, since both sides then vanish.

*Summing over disjoint vertex sets.* The exterior sets charged in <a href="#eq:exterior-rank-bound" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:exterior-rank-bound">[eq:exterior-rank-bound]</a> are pairwise disjoint. Indeed, if $j<k$, the center of $P_k$ is no farther from $R$ than the center of $P_j$. Lemma <a href="#lem:overlap" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:overlap">6</a> therefore implies $$P_j\cap P_k\subseteq B_{P_j}.$$ Consequently $(Y\cap P_j)\setminus B_{P_j}$ misses all of $P_k$, including its charged exterior. The non-strict distance comparison also covers ties in the elimination order.

Summing <a href="#eq:projected-cycle-rank" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:projected-cycle-rank">[eq:projected-cycle-rank]</a> and <a href="#eq:exterior-rank-bound" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:exterior-rank-bound">[eq:exterior-rank-bound]</a> in <a href="#eq:cycle-elimination" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:cycle-elimination">[eq:cycle-elimination]</a> now gives $$\dim Z_1(A;\mathbb F_2)\le\bigl(d/2-1-c/4\bigr)|Y|.$$ The coefficient is positive, so this statement also follows from the empty sum when $\mathcal L=0$. Substituting this bound into Euler’s identity proves <a href="#eq:global-deficit" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:global-deficit">[eq:global-deficit]</a>. ◻

</details>

<a id="pm-h-19"></a>
#### The spectral gap and its projection

The passage from finite-set expansion to a spectral estimate belongs to the discrete Cheeger tradition (Dodziuk 1984; Alon and Milman 1985) <a id="pm-cite-28"></a>[[Dodziuk1984](#ref-Dodziuk1984), [AlonMilman1985](#ref-AlonMilman1985)]. We give the coarea and Cauchy–Schwarz calculation to account for the vertex potential $d-\deg_\omega(x)$ and the unnormalized Laplacian used here.

<a id="stmt-lem-spectral-gap"></a>
<div id="lem:spectral-gap" class="lemma">

<a id="eq:remaining-spectral-bound"></a>
<a id="eq:uniform-spectrum"></a>
**Lemma 17** (Uniform spectral gap). *For every $\omega\in\Omega$, the restriction of $\pi_\omega(T)$ to $\ell^2(U_\omega)$ satisfies $$\pi_\omega(T)|_{\ell^2(U_\omega)}\ge\frac{c^2}{8d}I.
 \label{eq:remaining-spectral-bound}$$ Put $$\varepsilon=\min\{\rho,c^2/(8d)\}>0.$$ Then $$\mathop{\mathrm{spec}}(\pi_\omega(T))\subseteq\{0\}\cup[\varepsilon,2d],
 \label{eq:uniform-spectrum}$$ and its kernel is exactly the Hilbert-space direct sum of the one-dimensional spaces of constants on the valid copies.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-spectral-gap"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-spectral-gap</summary>

*Proof.* On $U_\omega$ write $\deg_\omega(x)$ for the enabled degree and put $q(x)=d-\deg_\omega(x)\ge0$. There are no enabled edges to the removed set. Thus, for finitely supported complex $\xi$ on $U_\omega$, <a id="eq:remaining-energy"></a>$$\begin{split}
 \mathcal E(\xi)
 :=\langle\pi_\omega(T)\xi,\xi\rangle
 =\sum_{\{x,y\}\in E(G_\omega|_{U_\omega})}
       |\xi(x)-\xi(y)|^2
   +\sum_{x\in U_\omega}q(x)|\xi(x)|^2.
 \end{split}
 \label{eq:remaining-energy}$$ All edge sums in the rest of this proof are over enabled unoriented edges on $U_\omega$. For a finite set $F\subseteq U_\omega$, its deficit is exactly $$d|F|-2|E(G_\omega|_F)|
 =|\partial_{G_\omega|_{U_\omega}}F|+\sum_{x\in F}q(x).$$

Set $s(x)=|\xi(x)|$. Apply Lemma <a href="#lem:global-deficit" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:global-deficit">16</a> to the finite level sets $\{x:s(x)^2>t\}$ and integrate in $t\ge0$. The layer-cake identity gives $$(c/2)\|\xi\|^2
 \le\sum_{\{x,y\}}|s(x)^2-s(y)^2|+\sum_xq(x)s(x)^2.$$ Cauchy–Schwarz, applied together to the edge terms and the vertex-potential terms, then gives $$\begin{aligned}
 (c/2)\|\xi\|^2
 &\le
 \left(\sum_{\{x,y\}}|s(x)-s(y)|^2+\sum_xq(x)s(x)^2\right)^{1/2}
 \left(\sum_{\{x,y\}}(s(x)+s(y))^2+\sum_xq(x)s(x)^2\right)^{1/2}\\
 &\le\mathcal E(\xi)^{1/2}\sqrt{2d}\,\|\xi\|.
\end{aligned}$$ For the first factor we used $||\xi(x)|-|\xi(y)||\le|\xi(x)-\xi(y)|$. For the second we used $$\sum_{\{x,y\}}(s(x)+s(y))^2+\sum_xq(x)s(x)^2
 \le\sum_x\bigl(2\deg_\omega(x)+q(x)\bigr)s(x)^2
 \le2d\|\xi\|^2.$$ If $\xi\ne0$, division and squaring prove $\mathcal E(\xi)\ge(c^2/(8d))\|\xi\|^2$; the zero vector satisfies the same inequality. Finite-support vectors are dense and the operator is bounded, so <a href="#eq:remaining-spectral-bound" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:remaining-spectral-bound">[eq:remaining-spectral-bound]</a> holds on all of $\ell^2(U_\omega)$. If $U_\omega$ is empty, this assertion is vacuous.

On each valid copy $P$ of type $i$, the operator is its graph Laplacian $dI-\mathop{\mathrm{Adj}}(P)$. Its kernel consists of constants, and on the orthogonal complement of constants it is at least $\rho I$, by Proposition <a href="#prop:data" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:data">7</a>. The valid copies are disjoint isolated components. Decomposing $\ell^2(\Gamma)$ into their component spaces and $\ell^2(U_\omega)$ therefore shows that the full kernel is exactly $$\bigoplus_{P\text{ valid for }\omega}\mathbb C\mathbf 1_P,$$ and that the operator is at least $\varepsilon I$ on its orthogonal complement. These conclusions remain true for infinitely many valid components, because both lower bounds are uniform. The earlier bound $\pi_\omega(T)\le2dI$ proves <a href="#eq:uniform-spectrum" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:uniform-spectrum">[eq:uniform-spectrum]</a>. ◻

</details>

<a id="stmt-prop-projection"></a>
<div id="prop:projection" class="proposition">

<a id="eq:projection-diagonal"></a>
**Proposition 18** (A projection in the reduced group algebra). *There exists a projection $b_0\in C_r^*(G_{\mathrm{sur}})$ such that, for every $\omega\in\Omega$, $\pi_\omega(b_0)$ is the orthogonal projection onto $\ker\pi_\omega(T)$. Explicitly, it is projection onto constants on each valid copy and is zero on $\ell^2(U_\omega)$. In particular, $$\langle\pi_\omega(b_0)\delta_1,\delta_1\rangle
 =
 \begin{cases}
  1/n_i,&\text{if $1$ belongs to a valid copy of type $i$},\\
  0,&\text{if $1\in U_\omega$}.
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:projection-diagonal}$$ The valid copy in the first case is unique.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-prop-projection"><summary>Proof of stmt-prop-projection</summary>

*Proof.* Take the faithful direct sum of the representations $\pi_\omega$. Lemma <a href="#lem:spectral-gap" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:spectral-gap">17</a> gives the same positive constant $\varepsilon$ in every summand, so its spectrum is contained in the closed set $\{0\}\cup[\varepsilon,2d]$. Faithfulness preserves the spectrum of the self-adjoint element $T$; hence $$\mathop{\mathrm{spec}}_{C_r^*(G_{\mathrm{sur}})}(T)\subseteq\{0\}\cup[\varepsilon,2d].$$ Choose a continuous real-valued function $\chi$ on $[0,2d]$ with $\chi(0)=1$ and $\chi=0$ on $[\varepsilon,2d]$, and set $b_0=\chi(T)$ by continuous functional calculus. Since $\chi^2=\chi$ on the spectrum, $b_0$ is a projection in $C_r^*(G_{\mathrm{sur}})$. This uses the ordinary C\*-algebra functional calculus, with no passage to a von Neumann algebra.

Functional calculus commutes with each $\pi_\omega$. The kernel description in Lemma <a href="#lem:spectral-gap" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:spectral-gap">17</a> thus identifies $\pi_\omega(b_0)$ as claimed, also when there are no valid copies or infinitely many of them. The normalized constant vector on a valid copy of type $i$ is $n_i^{-1/2}\mathbf 1_P$, so the diagonal of its rank-one projection at any vertex of $P$ is $1/n_i$. At vertices of $U_\omega$ the diagonal is zero. Disjointness of the valid copies proves uniqueness and <a href="#eq:projection-diagonal" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:projection-diagonal">[eq:projection-diagonal]</a>. ◻

</details>

<a id="sec:trace"></a>
### An irrational trace and failure of assembly

We now compute the trace of the projection constructed in Proposition <a href="#prop:projection" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:projection">18</a>.

<a id="stmt-prop-irrational-trace"></a>
<div id="prop:irrational-trace" class="proposition">

<a id="eq:irrational-trace"></a>
**Proposition 19**. *The projection $b_0\in C_r^*(G_{\mathrm{sur}})$ satisfies $$\label{eq:irrational-trace}
\tau_{G_{\mathrm{sur}}}(b_0)=\sum_{i\ge1}p^{-k_i}\notin\mathbb Q.$$*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-prop-irrational-trace"><summary>Proof of stmt-prop-irrational-trace</summary>

*Proof.* For a fixed $\omega$, the diagonal entry of $\pi_\omega(b_0)$ at the identity is zero unless that vertex belongs to a valid copy. If it does, the copy is unique and has some type $i$; projection onto the constants on that component has diagonal entry $1/n_i$.

Exactly $n_i$ indexed copies of type $i$ contain the identity. Indeed, their translating elements are precisely $g=\phi_i(v)^{-1}$ for the $n_i$ distinct embedded vertices $v\in\operatorname{Vert}(\Theta_i)$. Lemma <a href="#lem:distinct-copies" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:distinct-copies">3</a> ensures that these indices give distinct copies. By Lemma <a href="#lem:marginals" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:marginals">12</a>, each is valid on an event of Haar measure $p^{-k_i}$. Validity events for copies containing the identity are mutually exclusive, including across types. The trace formula <a href="#eq:trace-integral" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:trace-integral">[eq:trace-integral]</a> and nonnegative countable summation therefore give $$\tau_{G_{\mathrm{sur}}}(b_0)=\sum_{i\ge1}n_i\frac1{n_i}p^{-k_i}
=\sum_{i\ge1}p^{-k_i}.$$ No independence of these events is needed.

Since $p>2$ and the positive integers $k_i$ strictly increase, this number lies in $[0,1)$ and has base-$p$ digits equal to one exactly at positions $k_i$, and zero elsewhere. There is no eventually $(p-1)$ expansion ambiguity. The digits contain infinitely many ones, but their successive gaps tend to infinity by Proposition <a href="#prop:data" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:data">7</a>(iv). They cannot be eventually periodic. Rational numbers have eventually periodic base-$p$ expansions, so <a href="#eq:irrational-trace" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:irrational-trace">[eq:irrational-trace]</a> is irrational. ◻

</details>

<details id="proof-stmt-prop-irrational-trace-2"><summary>Proof of stmt-prop-irrational-trace</summary>

*Proof of Theorem <a href="#thm:main" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:main">1</a>.* The module construction gives a finitely generated discrete group $G_{\mathrm{sur}}$, and Proposition <a href="#prop:projection" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:projection">18</a> gives an actual projection $b_0\in C_r^*(G_{\mathrm{sur}})$. Its trace is positive and irrational by Proposition <a href="#prop:irrational-trace" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:irrational-trace">19</a>; in particular its integral $K_0$-class is nonzero. Every class in the image of the proper-action assembly map has rational trace by Theorem <a href="#thm:trace-image" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:trace-image">2</a>. Thus $[b_0]$ is outside that image.

The algebra $C(\Omega)\rtimes_r\Gamma$ was used to represent $C_r^*(V\rtimes\Gamma)$ and to analyze the operator. The assembly map just obstructed is the coefficient-free reduced map for $G_{\mathrm{sur}}$ itself, with the full proper classifying space. Its failure of surjectivity in degree zero refutes the universal Baum–Connes assertion. ◻

</details>

The obstruction persists after rationalization. Extend the canonical trace to $K_0(C_r^*(G_{\mathrm{sur}}))\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathbb Q$ by $x\otimes q\mapsto q\tau_{G_{\mathrm{sur}}*}(x)$. Every element in the image of $\mu_0^{G_{\mathrm{sur}}}\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\operatorname{id}_{\mathbb Q}$ has rational trace, since it is a finite rational linear combination of assembly classes. The trace of $[b_0]\otimes1$ remains irrational, so this class is outside the image of rationalized assembly as well.
