## 1. Abstract and result contract

> We prove the subcritical Hénon–Lane–Emden conjecture: for every dimension <i>n</i> ≥ 2, positive powers <i>p</i>, <i>q</i>, and real weights <i>A</i>, <i>B</i>, the system has no strictly positive entire solution when $`(n+A)/(p+1)+(n+B)/(q+1)\gt n-2`$. Solutions need only be continuous at the origin and classical elsewhere, without a condition at infinity. For <i>n</i> ≥ 3 and $`A,B\gt -2`$, combining this result with the radial existence theorem of Bidaut-Véron and Giacomini proves Phan's Conjecture C in full: a positive radial entire solution exists whenever the strict inequality fails.

- **claim-subcritical-nonexistence** (theorem, [source](#stmt-thm-main)): Let $n\ge2$ be an integer, let $p,q>0$, and let $A,B\in\mathbb R$. Suppose that $$\label{eq:subcritical} \frac{n+A}{p+1}+\frac{n+B}{q+1}>n-2.$$ There are no functions $$u,v\in C^2(\mathbb R^n\setminus\{0\})\cap C(\mathbb R^n), \qquad u(x)>0,\quad v(x)>0\quad(x\in\mathbb R^n),$$ satisfying Equation <a href="#eq:system" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:system">[eq:system]</a> at every $x\ne0$.
  - Explanation: The weighted subcritical inequality excludes strictly positive entire solutions that are continuous at the origin and classical away from it. The argument requires no symmetry or condition at infinity.
- **claim-unweighted-nonexistence** (theorem, [source](#stmt-cor-lane-emden)): For every integer $n\ge3$ and $p,q>0$ satisfying $$\frac{1}{p+1}+\frac{1}{q+1}>\frac{n-2}{n},$$ there is no strictly positive $C^2(\mathbb R^n)$ solution of $$-\Delta u=v^p,\qquad -\Delta v=u^q\qquad\text{in }\mathbb R^n.$$
  - Explanation: The unweighted Lane–Emden nonexistence assertion follows by setting both spatial weights to zero.
- **claim-origin-reductions** (conditional_result, [source](#stmt-lem-reductions)): The existence of a solution implies $n\ge3$ and $A,B>-2$.
  - Explanation: Positivity and continuity at the origin force the admissible dimension and weight range through spherical flux integration.
- **claim-distributional-newton-representation** (conditional_result, [source](#stmt-lem-potential)): For every solution with $n\ge3$ and $A,B>-2$, the sources $f,g$ belong to $L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb R^n)$ and $$-\Delta u=g,\qquad -\Delta v=f \quad\hbox{in distributions on }\mathbb R^n.$$ The Newton potentials are finite everywhere and satisfy $$\label{eq:potential-lower} K*g\le u,\qquad K*f\le v \quad\hbox{on }\mathbb R^n.$$ There are constants $c_u,c_v\ge0$ such that $$\label{eq:potential-constants} u=c_u+K*g,\qquad v=c_v+K*f$$ almost everywhere on $\mathbb R^n$ and pointwise on $\mathbb R^n\setminus\{0\}$. On the latter set the potentials are continuously differentiable, and $$\label{eq:potential-gradients} \begin{split} \nabla u(x)&=\int_{\mathbb R^n}\nabla K(x-y)g(y)\,\,\mathrm dy,\\ \nabla v(x)&=\int_{\mathbb R^n}\nabla K(x-y)f(y)\,\,\mathrm dy,\\ \nabla K(z)&=-(n-2)\frac{z}{\|z\|^2}K(z). \end{split}$$ Both gradient integrals converge absolutely at every $x\ne0$.
  - Explanation: The punctured equations extend distributionally through the origin. Each component is its Newton potential plus a nonnegative constant, and its gradient has an absolutely convergent kernel representation away from the origin.
- **claim-exponent-product-exclusion** (conditional_result, [source](#stmt-lem-low-exponents)): A solution with $n\ge3$ and $A,B>-2$ necessarily has $pq>1$.
  - Explanation: Centered potential lower bounds force growth of the component infima that is incompatible with a small exponent product.
- **claim-universal-centered-estimates** (bound, [source](#stmt-prop-uniform)): Fix $n\ge3$, $p,q>0$, $A,B>-2$, and $pq>1$. There is a universal constant $C$ such that every solution satisfies, for all $R>0$, $$\label{eq:source-bounds} \int_{B_R}f\le CR^{n-2-\beta},\qquad \int_{B_R}g\le CR^{n-2-\alpha}.$$ The constants in <a href="#eq:potential-constants" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:potential-constants">[eq:potential-constants]</a> vanish, so that $$\label{eq:potentials} u=K*g,\qquad v=K*f\quad\hbox{on }\mathbb R^n\setminus\{0\}.$$ Moreover, $$\label{eq:uniform} \begin{split} &\int_{B_4}(f+g+u+v)\le C,\\ &\int_{\|x-y\|\ge\ell}K(x-y)(f(y)+g(y))\,\,\mathrm dy \le C\ell^{2-n} \qquad (x\in B_2,\ 0<\ell\le1). \end{split}$$
  - Explanation: Dilation gives solution-independent source mass bounds on origin-centered balls. These bounds eliminate the harmonic constants and control local masses and truncated potentials.
- **claim-localized-virial-identity** (conditional_result, [source](#stmt-auto-lemma-1)): The following identity holds, with an absolutely convergent integral: $$\label{eq:virial} \begin{split} a(n+B)E_f+b(n+A)E_g-aW_f-bW_g &=(n-2)\int_{\mathbb R^{2n}}\Phi(x,y)\,\mathrm d\pi(x,y),\\ \Phi(x,y)&= \frac{(X(x)-X(y))\cdot(x-y)}{\|x-y\|^2}. \end{split}$$
  - Explanation: Compactly supported integration by parts and the differentiated Newton potentials give an exact weighted virial identity. Absolute convergence is established before combining the kernel-gradient terms.
- **claim-energy-relative-localization** (bound, [source](#stmt-lem-localization)): For every $\varepsilon>0$, there is a constant $C_\varepsilon$, independent of the solution, such that $$\label{eq:localization} \|E_f-E_g\|+ \int_{\mathbb R^{2n}}\|\Phi(x,y)-\eta(x)+w(x)D(x,y)\|\,\mathrm d\pi(x,y) \le\varepsilon E+C_\varepsilon.$$
  - Explanation: A close-pair Taylor estimate and a far-pair truncated-potential estimate control both marginal imbalance and the virial approximation by a small relative energy error plus a universal additive term.
- **claim-weighted-interval-comparison** (bound, [source](#stmt-lem-interval-pair)): Let $n\ge3$, $\lambda>0$, and let $I,J\subset\mathbb R$ be nonempty open intervals, possibly unbounded. Let $c_I,c_J\ge0$, with $I$ bounded whenever $c_I>0$ and $J$ bounded whenever $c_J>0$. Then $$\label{eq:interval-pair} \begin{split} \int_{I\times J}(c_I+c_J)\mathcal K\,\,\mathrm ds\,\,\mathrm ds' \le \int_{I\times J}\Bigl[ &c_I\overline{\mathcal K}_I+c_J\overline{\mathcal K}_J\\ &+\bigl((n-2)\min(c_I,c_J)\mathcal D +\|c_I-c_J\|\bigr)\mathcal K\Bigr]\,\,\mathrm ds\,\,\mathrm ds'. \end{split}$$ Every term is nonnegative and the inequality is understood for extended integrals. An endpoint term with coefficient zero is defined to be zero, without evaluating the endpoint average.
  - Explanation: Midpoint integration by parts and the monotone overlap density compare interval kernel mass with endpoint averages, a directional interaction term, and coefficient mismatch. The nonnegative formulation accommodates unbounded zero-weight components.
- **claim-localized-pressure** (bound, [source](#stmt-prop-pressure)): Let $(u,v)$ be a positive solution pair as in Theorem <a href="#thm:main" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:main">1</a>, with $n\ge3$, $A,B>-2$, and $pq>1$. For every $\varepsilon>0$ there is a constant $C_\varepsilon$, depending only on the equation parameters and $\varepsilon$, such that $$\label{eq:pressure} aW_f+bW_g \le(n-2)\int_{\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^n}w(x)D(x,y)\,\,\mathrm d\pi(x,y) +\varepsilon E+C_\varepsilon.$$
  - Explanation: Weighted source-layer comparisons recover the cutoff pressure with a small energy-relative error. Angular averaging and short retained intervals control directional and endpoint-weight variations.
- **claim-averaged-layer-loss** (bound, [source](#stmt-lem-layer-loss)): For the cap weights <a href="#eq:cap-weight" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:cap-weight">[eq:cap-weight]</a> and component weights <a href="#eq:component-weight" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:component-weight">[eq:component-weight]</a>, under the hypotheses of Proposition <a href="#prop:pressure" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:pressure">10</a>, every $P\ge1$ satisfies $$\label{eq:layer-loss} \mathbb E_e\bigl(L_f(e)+L_g(e)\bigr) \le\bigl(C_\kappa\delta+C_{\kappa,\delta}P^{-1}\bigr)E+CP.$$ The constants in the relative coefficient are independent of $P$, and the last constant is independent of $\kappa,\delta,P$.
  - Explanation: Short components lose little cap weight. Polar source-mass estimates bound the probability of long components at high levels, while exact cap normalization bounds the low-level contribution.
- **claim-uniform-local-energy** (bound, [source](#stmt-prop-energy)): For the fixed equation parameters there is a constant $C$, independent of the solution pair, such that $$\label{eq:energy-bound} \int_{B_1}\bigl(\|x\|^B u^{q+1}+\|x\|^A v^{p+1}\bigr)\,\mathrm dx \le E\le C.$$
  - Explanation: The positive subcritical gap absorbs the relative localization and pressure errors, yielding a uniform local energy bound without assuming a bound on the energy beforehand.
- **claim-weighted-existence-classification** (classification, [source](#stmt-cor-phan-classification)): Let $n\ge3$ be an integer, let $p,q>0$, and let $A,B>-2$. There is no pair $$u,v\in C^2(\mathbb R^n\setminus\{0\})\cap C(\mathbb R^n), \qquad u(x)>0,\quad v(x)>0\quad(x\in\mathbb R^n),$$ satisfying Equation <a href="#eq:system" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:system">[eq:system]</a> at every $x\ne0$ if and only if $$\frac{n+A}{p+1}+\frac{n+B}{q+1}>n-2.$$ When the reverse weak inequality holds, such a pair exists and can be chosen radial.
  - Explanation: The nonexistence theorem and the cited radial ground-state theorem yield an exact existence–nonexistence classification in the admissible weight range, including radial existence on the critical hyperbola.

## 2. Definitions, validity and bounds

- **Hénon–Lane–Emden system** ([source](#eq:system)): The Hénon–Lane–Emden system is $$\label{eq:system} -\Delta u=\|x\|^A v^p,\qquad -\Delta v=\|x\|^B u^q \qquad\text{in }\mathbb R^n\setminus\{0\}.$$
- **Admissible solution** ([source](#sec:preliminaries)): Throughout this section, a solution means a pair $$u,v\in C^2(\mathbb R^n\setminus\{0\})\cap C(\mathbb R^n),\qquad u,v>0 \quad\hbox{on }\mathbb R^n,$$ where $n\ge2$, $p,q>0$, and $A,B\in\mathbb R$, satisfying $$-\Delta u=\|x\|^A v^p,\qquad -\Delta v=\|x\|^B u^q \quad\hbox{on }\mathbb R^n\setminus\{0\}.$$
- **Centered and translated balls** ([source](#sec:preliminaries)): We write $B_R(z)=\{x:\|x-z\|<R\}$ and $B_R=B_R(0)$.
- **Universal constant** ([source](#eq:sources-kernel)): A constant called *universal* may depend on $n,p,q,A,B$ and on explicitly indicated auxiliary parameters, but not on the particular solution.
- **Solution-dependent constants** ([source](#eq:sources-kernel)): In contrast, constants denoted by $C_{u,v}$ are allowed to depend on the solution.
- **Interaction measure and marginals** ([source](#eq:interaction)): Define the nonnegative interaction measure by $$\label{eq:interaction} \begin{gathered} \,\mathrm d\pi(x,y)=f(x)g(y)K(x-y)\,\mathrm dx\,\mathrm dy,\\ \pi(\,\mathrm dx,\mathbb R^n)=f(x)u(x)\,\mathrm dx, \quad \pi(\mathbb R^n,\,\mathrm dy)=g(y)v(y)\,\mathrm dy. \end{gathered}$$
- **Directional interaction factors** ([source](#eq:interaction)): For a unit vector $e\in\mathbb S^{n-1}$, put $$D_e(x,y)=\frac{((x-y)\cdot e)^2}{\|x-y\|^2}, \qquad \omega_x=\frac{x}{\|x\|}, \qquad D(x,y)=D_{\omega_x}(x,y).$$
- **Localized energies and cutoff pressures** ([source](#eq:cutoff-bounds)): Set $$\begin{gathered} E_f=\int_{\mathbb R^n}\eta fu\,\mathrm dx,\qquad E_g=\int_{\mathbb R^n}\eta gv\,\mathrm dx,\qquad E=E_f+E_g, \\ W_f=\int_{\mathbb R^n}wfu\,\mathrm dx,\qquad W_g=\int_{\mathbb R^n}wgv\,\mathrm dx. \end{gathered}$$
- **Cap weight** ([source](#eq:cap-weight)): Define the cap weight by $$\label{eq:cap-weight} h_e(x)=w(x)\frac{\psi(\|e-\omega_x\|/(\kappa d(x)))}{Z(\kappa d(x))} \quad(1<\|x\|<2),\qquad h_e(x)=0\quad\hbox{otherwise}.$$
- **Source-layer component weight** ([source](#pm-h-10)): Assign it the weight $$\label{eq:component-weight} \chi_I= \begin{cases} \displaystyle\inf_I h_e, &I\text{ bounded and }\|I\|\le2\delta\inf_I d,\\ 0,&\text{otherwise}. \end{cases}$$

## 3. Proof roadmap

- **step-origin-flux-reductions** [claim](#stmt-lem-reductions) / [proof](#proof-stmt-lem-reductions): Integrate spherical fluxes on annuli, force their origin limits to vanish by continuity, and use positivity to exclude the inadmissible dimension and singular weights. -> The necessary dimension and weight restrictions for any solution.
- **step-distributional-potentials** [claim](#stmt-lem-potential) / [proof](#proof-stmt-lem-potential): Remove the puncture by cutoff testing, derive potential domination by spherical averaging, identify the harmonic remainder, and differentiate the potentials locally away from the origin. -> Distributional equations, finite Newton potentials, nonnegative constant remainders, and absolute gradient convergence.
- **step-exclude-small-products** [claim](#stmt-lem-low-exponents) / [proof](#proof-stmt-lem-low-exponents): Use centered kernel lower bounds to relate the component infima, then compare the resulting radius growth with the fixed origin value. -> The exponent product must lie in the superlinear range.
- **step-universal-centered-bounds** [claim](#stmt-prop-uniform) / [proof](#proof-stmt-prop-uniform): Bound source masses on a fixed centered ball, transport the bounds by dilation, eliminate positive constant remainders, and sum exterior annular potential contributions. -> Universal centered source bounds, exact potentials, local integral bounds, and truncated-potential bounds.
- **step-localized-virial** [claim](#stmt-auto-lemma-1) / [proof](#proof-stmt-auto-lemma-1): Establish absolute kernel-gradient integrability, integrate by parts outside a shrinking origin ball, and combine the differentiated potential terms. -> The exact localized weighted virial identity.
- **step-localization-errors** [claim](#stmt-lem-localization) / [proof](#proof-stmt-lem-localization): Split endpoint pairs into close and far interactions, use cutoff Taylor bounds on close pairs, and use source masses with truncated potentials on far pairs. -> Small energy-relative virial error and marginal imbalance, with a uniform additive constant.
- **step-interval-midpoint-comparison** [claim](#stmt-lem-interval-pair) / [proof](#proof-stmt-lem-interval-pair): Treat zero coefficients pointwise; for positive coefficients integrate by parts about interval midpoints and pair opposite longitudinal differences using the monotone interval-overlap density. -> A nonnegative interval comparison valid for extended integrals.
- **step-averaged-layer-loss** [claim](#stmt-lem-layer-loss) / [proof](#proof-stmt-lem-layer-loss): Control short-component oscillation, estimate the probability of long source-layer segments by polar source integration, and split source levels into high and low ranges. -> An averaged layer-loss bound with separately controlled relative and additive terms.
- **step-recover-localized-pressure** [claim](#stmt-prop-pressure) / [proof](#proof-stmt-lem-layer-loss-2): Apply the interval comparison to source-layer components, integrate nonnegative terms before subtraction, control weighted endpoint variation, recover the exact layer coefficients, and average over angular caps. -> The localized pressure inequality with an arbitrarily small relative energy error.
- **step-absorb-energy-errors** [claim](#stmt-prop-energy) / [proof](#proof-stmt-prop-energy): Combine the virial identity with localization and pressure estimates, replace unequal component energies by their sum and imbalance, and absorb the relative errors through the positive subcritical gap. -> A universal local weighted-energy bound.
- **step-dilation-contradiction** [claim](#stmt-thm-main) / [proof](#proof-stmt-prop-energy-2): Apply the same universal bound to every origin-centered dilation and compare its growing energy factor with the fixed positive energy on the unit ball. -> Nonexistence throughout the strict weighted subcritical regime.
- **step-unweighted-specialization** [claim](#stmt-cor-lane-emden) / [proof](#sec:introduction): Set both spatial weights to zero in the main theorem and restrict to entire classical solutions. -> The unweighted Lane–Emden corollary.
- **step-complementary-radial-existence** [claim](#stmt-cor-phan-classification) / [proof](#proof-stmt-cor-phan-classification): Derive the superlinear exponent product under the reverse weak inequality, match the cited radial ground-state theorem to the same equation parameters, and use radial flux monotonicity to verify strict positivity at the origin. -> The full admissible-weight existence–nonexistence classification, including radial existence at equality.

## 4. Engineering Triptych

### Panel 1 - epistemic contract

- **invariant-subcritical-nonexistence**: Strict weighted subcriticality excludes strictly positive entire solutions that are continuous at the origin and classical elsewhere.
- **invariant-admissible-solution-reductions**: Existence forces the admissible dimension and weight range and excludes small exponent products before the subcritical gap is used.
- **invariant-uniform-local-energy**: The localized energy bound is independent of the solution pair and therefore applies with the same constant to every admissible origin-centered dilation.
- **invariant-complementary-radial-existence**: Within the admissible dimension and weight range, the reverse weak inequality permits a strictly positive radial entire solution, including the critical case.

### Panel 2 - negative knowledge

- **anti-origin-positivity-omission**: Apply nonexistence to functions that vanish at the origin or have an origin singularity. -> Such functions fail the entire strictly positive solution contract, so the theorem does not establish their nonexistence.
- **anti-weighted-translation**: Translate a weighted solution and reuse the centered source estimates with an arbitrary new center. -> Translation changes the radial spatial weights and does not preserve the equation used by the estimates.
- **anti-constant-weighted-endpoints**: Replace weighted source-layer endpoint values with the unweighted constant level value. -> The endpoint value depends on its radial position, so the pressure recovery loses the spatial-weight correction.
- **anti-infinite-interval-subtraction**: Subtract endpoint defects on unbounded partner intervals before establishing finiteness. -> The kernel integral can diverge, making the subtraction undefined.
- **anti-critical-nonexistence-extension**: Extend strict subcritical nonexistence to the critical hyperbola or the reverse weak inequality. -> The positive gap needed for the scaling contradiction is lost, and the classification supplies radial existence in the complementary regime.
- **anti-finite-sampling-certificate**: Treat numerical agreement on a bounded sampling region as a certificate of the entire nonexistence theorem. -> Finite sampling cannot establish the full solution contract or the limiting contradiction over arbitrarily large origin-centered dilations.

### Panel 3 - reference implementation and oracle

Applicability: `not_applicable`. The paper proves nonexistence for entire continuous functions through an analytic energy bound and a limiting dilation contradiction, not a finite constructive procedure. ## 7. Complete mathematical paper


We prove the subcritical Hénon–Lane–Emden conjecture: for every dimension <i>n</i> ≥ 2, positive powers <i>p</i>, <i>q</i>, and real weights <i>A</i>, <i>B</i>, the system has no strictly positive entire solution when $`(n+A)/(p+1)+(n+B)/(q+1)\gt n-2`$. Solutions need only be continuous at the origin and classical elsewhere, without a condition at infinity. For <i>n</i> ≥ 3 and $`A,B\gt -2`$, combining this result with the radial existence theorem of Bidaut-Véron and Giacomini proves Phan's Conjecture C in full: a positive radial entire solution exists whenever the strict inequality fails.

<a id="sec:introduction"></a>
### Introduction

<a id="eq:system"></a>
The Hénon–Lane–Emden system is $$\label{eq:system}
 -\Delta u=|x|^A v^p,\qquad
 -\Delta v=|x|^B u^q
 \qquad\text{in }\mathbb R^n\setminus\{0\}.$$ Its subcritical nonexistence conjecture asks whether the weighted hyperbola alone rules out positive solutions, without symmetry or conditions at infinity. The unweighted case $A=B=0$ is the classical Lane–Emden system.

The scalar subcritical Liouville theorem of Gidas and Spruck is a starting point for this question (Gidas and Spruck 1981b) <a id="pm-cite-1"></a>[[GidasSpruck1981](#ref-GidasSpruck1981)]. For systems, Mitidieri developed a Rellich identity and early nonexistence regions (Mitidieri 1993, 1996) <a id="pm-cite-2"></a>[[Mitidieri1993](#ref-Mitidieri1993), [Mitidieri1996](#ref-Mitidieri1996)]. Serrin and Zou proved the unweighted three-dimensional assertion under a growth condition; Poláčik, Quittner, and Souplet removed that condition, and Souplet settled dimension four (Serrin and Zou 1996; Poláčik, Quittner, and Souplet 2007; Souplet 2009) <a id="pm-cite-3"></a>[[SerrinZou1996](#ref-SerrinZou1996), [PolacikQuittnerSouplet2007](#ref-PolacikQuittnerSouplet2007), [Souplet2009](#ref-Souplet2009)]. Li, Li, and Wei obtained a further unweighted nonexistence region for $p,q\ge1$ in every dimension $n\ge5$ (K. Li, Li, and Wei 2025, Theorem 1.1) <a id="pm-cite-4"></a>[[LiLiWei2025](#ref-LiLiWei2025)]. Liouville theorems also serve as inputs to rescaling arguments for a priori bounds and singularity estimates (Gidas and Spruck 1981a; Poláčik, Quittner, and Souplet 2007) <a id="pm-cite-5"></a>[[GidasSpruckBounds1981](#ref-GidasSpruckBounds1981), [PolacikQuittnerSouplet2007](#ref-PolacikQuittnerSouplet2007)].

For the weighted system, Bidaut-Véron and Giacomini identified the critical hyperbola in the regular radial theory under admissible weights (Bidaut-Véron and Giacomini 2010) <a id="pm-cite-6"></a>[[BidautVeronGiacomini2010](#ref-BidautVeronGiacomini2010)]. Phan formulated the unrestricted weighted question for $A,B>-2$ as Conjecture C and established bounded, low-dimensional, and conditional cases (Phan 2012) <a id="pm-cite-7"></a>[[Phan](#ref-Phan)]. Fazly and Ghoussoub obtained bounded and stability-based results for nonnegative weights (Fazly and Ghoussoub 2014) <a id="pm-cite-8"></a>[[FazlyGhoussoub2014](#ref-FazlyGhoussoub2014)]; Li and Zhang subsequently proved the weighted conjecture in dimension three without a boundedness assumption (K. Li and Zhang 2019) <a id="pm-cite-9"></a>[[LiZhang2019](#ref-LiZhang2019)]. Results in restricted exponent ranges extend to dimensions four and five (H. Li 2022) <a id="pm-cite-10"></a>[[Li2022](#ref-Li2022)]. More recently, Huang and Zou stated the all-real-weight, origin-continuous assertion as Conjecture B and proved cases with stability, decay, or energy hypotheses (Huang and Zou 2026) <a id="pm-cite-11"></a>[[HuangZou](#ref-HuangZou)]. Xu and Luo obtained a higher-order weighted theorem over the full exponent range for radial solutions subject to regularity and growth conditions (Xu and Luo 2026) <a id="pm-cite-12"></a>[[XuLuo2026](#ref-XuLuo2026)]. The full nonradial question in higher dimensions is the subject of this paper.

<a id="stmt-thm-main"></a>
<div id="thm:main" class="theorem">

<a id="eq:subcritical"></a>
**Theorem 1**. *Let $n\ge2$ be an integer, let $p,q>0$, and let $A,B\in\mathbb R$. Suppose that $$\label{eq:subcritical}
 \frac{n+A}{p+1}+\frac{n+B}{q+1}>n-2.$$ There are no functions $$u,v\in C^2(\mathbb R^n\setminus\{0\})\cap C(\mathbb R^n),
 \qquad u(x)>0,\quad v(x)>0\quad(x\in\mathbb R^n),$$ satisfying Equation <a href="#eq:system" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:system">[eq:system]</a> at every $x\ne0$.*

</div>

<a id="stmt-cor-lane-emden"></a>
<div id="cor:lane-emden" class="corollary">

**Corollary 2** (Unweighted Lane–Emden conjecture). *For every integer $n\ge3$ and $p,q>0$ satisfying $$\frac{1}{p+1}+\frac{1}{q+1}>\frac{n-2}{n},$$ there is no strictly positive $C^2(\mathbb R^n)$ solution of $$-\Delta u=v^p,\qquad -\Delta v=u^q\qquad\text{in }\mathbb R^n.$$*

</div>

In particular, positivity at the origin is part of the hypothesis, but differentiability there is not. No radial symmetry, boundedness, integrability, finite energy, stability, or growth or decay condition at infinity is required.

Theorem <a href="#thm:main" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:main">1</a> resolves Huang and Zou’s Conjecture B positively and yields Corollary <a href="#cor:lane-emden" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="cor:lane-emden">2</a> by setting $A=B=0$. For $n\ge3$ and $A,B>-2$, its nonexistence conclusion joins the radial existence theorem of Bidaut-Véron and Giacomini (Bidaut-Véron and Giacomini 2010, Theorem 1.4(i)) <a id="pm-cite-13"></a>[[BidautVeronGiacomini2010](#ref-BidautVeronGiacomini2010)] to prove Phan’s Conjecture C in full, including existence on the critical hyperbola. Corollary <a href="#cor:phan-classification" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="cor:phan-classification">13</a> records this derived classification. The weak regularity at the origin is genuine: positivity and continuity, rather than a prescribed order of vanishing or smoothness there, are all that is assumed.

The weighted assertion cannot be inferred merely by applying the unweighted assertion at the same exponents. For instance, $n=5$, $p=q=4$, $A=B=3$ satisfies Equation <a href="#eq:subcritical" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:subcritical">[eq:subcritical]</a>, whereas $1/(p+1)+1/(q+1)=2/5<3/5$. Likewise, the unweighted input in Phan’s transfer argument must remain within its actual scope; weighted subcriticality does not imply that input (Phan 2012) <a id="pm-cite-14"></a>[[Phan](#ref-Phan)].

<a id="pm-h-3"></a>
###### The weighted argument.

Rellich–Pohozaev identities and spherical estimates have long been central to system Liouville proofs (Mitidieri 1993; Souplet 2009; Phan 2012) <a id="pm-cite-15"></a>[[Mitidieri1993](#ref-Mitidieri1993), [Souplet2009](#ref-Souplet2009), [Phan](#ref-Phan)]. Chen and Li used Newtonian integral representations with moving planes under integrability hypotheses (Chen and Li 2009) <a id="pm-cite-16"></a>[[ChenLi2009](#ref-ChenLi2009)]; Cheng and Huang later formulated an energy-estimate criterion for the unweighted conjecture (Cheng and Huang 2019) <a id="pm-cite-17"></a>[[ChengHuang2019](#ref-ChengHuang2019)]. The passage from a uniform local weighted-energy bound to nonexistence by origin-centered dilation also appears in Li (H. Li 2022, sec. 2) <a id="pm-cite-18"></a>[[Li2022](#ref-Li2022)]. Here a localized potential-kernel virial identity and an energy-relative interval-pressure estimate supply that bound throughout the subcritical range.

There are two difficulties in passing from unweighted source layers to Equation <a href="#eq:system" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:system">[eq:system]</a>. Translations do not preserve the equation, and an endpoint of a layer $\{|x|^B u(x)^q>t\}$ has value $(t/|x|^B)^{1/q}$, not the constant $t^{1/q}$. We address both directly.

First, origin-centered dilations give uniform source masses and truncated-potential bounds on a fixed ball. A compactly supported virial identity then retains the exact spatial-weight coefficients. Second, the interval comparison is applied with directions in a small cap around the radial direction and with weights constant on each retained interval. Retained intervals are short enough that their endpoint weight variation is small. The discarded intervals are controlled by polar integration of source masses and a high/low layer decomposition.

All errors are arbitrarily small relative to a fixed localized energy, up to a universal additive constant. No bound on that energy is assumed in obtaining the error estimates. The weighted subcritical gap then supplies such a bound, and dilation gives a contradiction. This combination of spatially varying directional cutoffs, intervalwise infima, and energy-relative layer errors is the part of the method that may be useful for other weighted potential systems. The elementary interval comparison itself is reproved below.

### Reductions and Newton potentials

We begin with consequences of positivity that do not require the subcritical inequality. Throughout this section, a solution means a pair $$u,v\in C^2(\mathbb R^n\setminus\{0\})\cap C(\mathbb R^n),\qquad u,v>0
 \quad\hbox{on }\mathbb R^n,$$ where $n\ge2$, $p,q>0$, and $A,B\in\mathbb R$, satisfying $$-\Delta u=|x|^A v^p,\qquad -\Delta v=|x|^B u^q
 \quad\hbox{on }\mathbb R^n\setminus\{0\}.$$ We write $B_R(z)=\{x:|x-z|<R\}$ and $B_R=B_R(0)$.

<a id="stmt-lem-reductions"></a>
<div id="lem:reductions" class="lemma">

**Lemma 3** (Origin reductions). *The existence of a solution implies $n\ge3$ and $A,B>-2$.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-reductions"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-reductions</summary>

<a id="eq:spherical-flux"></a>
*Proof.* For a function $h$ continuous on each sphere about the origin, write $$\overline h(r)=\frac1{|\mathbb S^{n-1}|}\int_{\mathbb S^{n-1}}h(r\theta)\,\,\mathrm d\theta.$$ Integration of the equations on annuli gives, for $r>0$, $$\label{eq:spherical-flux}
 \begin{split}
  \bigl(r^{n-1}\overline u'(r)\bigr)'
     &=-r^{n-1+A}\overline{v^p}(r),\\
  \bigl(r^{n-1}\overline v'(r)\bigr)'
     &=-r^{n-1+B}\overline{u^q}(r).
 \end{split}$$ In particular, each flux on the left is strictly decreasing. Its extended limit as $r\downarrow0$ exists. If that limit were nonzero or infinite, the flux would have a fixed sign and absolute value bounded below by a positive constant for all sufficiently small $r$. Since $\int_0^1 r^{1-n}\,\,\mathrm dr=\infty$, integration would contradict boundedness of the corresponding spherical mean at zero. Thus both limits are zero, and both fluxes are strictly negative for every $r>0$.

If $n=2$, then $r\overline u'(r)\le\overline u'(1)<0$ for $r\ge1$. Consequently $\overline u(r)\le\overline u(1)+\overline u'(1)\log r$, contradicting positivity for large $r$.

Continuity and $v(0)>0$ give $v^p\ge c>0$ near zero. Integrating the first equation in <a href="#eq:spherical-flux" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:spherical-flux">[eq:spherical-flux]</a> over $[r/2,r]$ and using the negative flux at $r/2$, we obtain $$-\overline u'(r)
 \ge c r^{1-n}\int_{r/2}^r s^{n-1+A}\,\,\mathrm ds
 =c_A r^{1+A},\qquad c_A>0.$$ The equality remains valid when $n+A=0$, with the corresponding constant containing $\log2$. If $A\le-2$, integrating this inequality towards zero contradicts continuity of $u$ there. Interchanging the two equations proves $B>-2$. ◻

</details>

Henceforth assume $n\ge3$ and $A,B>-2$. Define, off zero, <a id="eq:sources-kernel"></a>$$\label{eq:sources-kernel}
 \begin{gathered}
 f(x)=|x|^B u(x)^q,\qquad g(x)=|x|^A v(x)^p,\\
 K(x)=c_n|x|^{2-n},\qquad c_n=\frac1{(n-2)|\mathbb S^{n-1}|}.
 \end{gathered}$$ We set $f(0)=g(0)=0$, which has no effect on any integral. All unspecified integrals below are Lebesgue integrals. A constant called *universal* may depend on $n,p,q,A,B$ and on explicitly indicated auxiliary parameters, but not on the particular solution. In contrast, constants denoted by $C_{u,v}$ are allowed to depend on the solution.

<a id="stmt-lem-potential"></a>
<div id="lem:potential" class="lemma">

<a id="eq:potential-lower"></a>
<a id="eq:potential-constants"></a>
<a id="eq:potential-gradients"></a>
**Lemma 4** (Distributional extension and potentials). *For every solution with $n\ge3$ and $A,B>-2$, the sources $f,g$ belong to $L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb R^n)$ and $$-\Delta u=g,\qquad -\Delta v=f
 \quad\hbox{in distributions on }\mathbb R^n.$$ The Newton potentials are finite everywhere and satisfy $$\label{eq:potential-lower}
 K*g\le u,\qquad K*f\le v \quad\hbox{on }\mathbb R^n.$$ There are constants $c_u,c_v\ge0$ such that $$\label{eq:potential-constants}
 u=c_u+K*g,\qquad v=c_v+K*f$$ almost everywhere on $\mathbb R^n$ and pointwise on $\mathbb R^n\setminus\{0\}$. On the latter set the potentials are continuously differentiable, and $$\label{eq:potential-gradients}
 \begin{split}
 \nabla u(x)&=\int_{\mathbb R^n}\nabla K(x-y)g(y)\,\,\mathrm dy,\\
 \nabla v(x)&=\int_{\mathbb R^n}\nabla K(x-y)f(y)\,\,\mathrm dy,\\
 \nabla K(z)&=-(n-2)\frac{z}{|z|^2}K(z).
 \end{split}$$ Both gradient integrals converge absolutely at every $x\ne0$.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-potential"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-potential</summary>

*Proof.* Local boundedness of $u,v$ gives $g(x)\le C_{u,v}|x|^A$ and $f(x)\le C_{u,v}|x|^B$ near zero. Since $n+A,n+B>0$, both sources are locally integrable. Let $\varphi\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R^n)$ and choose $\xi\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R^n)$ equal to one near zero. Test the equation for $u$ away from zero against $\varphi(x)(1-\xi(x/\rho))$. After expanding its Laplacian, the terms involving first and second derivatives of $\xi(x/\rho)$ have absolute values bounded respectively by $C_{u,v,\varphi,\xi}\rho^{n-1}$ and $C_{u,v,\varphi,\xi}\rho^{n-2}$. They tend to zero. The remaining terms pass to the limit by local integrability, proving the first distributional equation. The second follows in the same way.

Let $\zeta_\rho$ be a nonnegative, compactly supported smooth approximate identity, and put $u_\rho=u*\zeta_\rho$ and $g_\rho=g*\zeta_\rho$. Then $u_\rho>0$ and $-\Delta u_\rho=g_\rho\ge0$ on all of $\mathbb R^n$. Two integrations of the spherical flux formula, now about an arbitrary point $x$, give $$u_\rho(x)-\frac1{|\mathbb S^{n-1}|}\int_{\mathbb S^{n-1}}u_\rho(x+R\theta)\,\,\mathrm d\theta
 =c_n\int_{|x-y|<R}
       \bigl(|x-y|^{2-n}-R^{2-n}\bigr)g_\rho(y)\,\,\mathrm dy.$$ The spherical average is positive. Monotone convergence as $R\to\infty$ therefore yields $K*g_\rho\le u_\rho$. As $\rho\downarrow0$, $u_\rho(x)\to u(x)$ for every $x$, while $g_\rho\to g$ almost everywhere. Fatou’s Lemma proves $K*g(x)\le u(x)$ at every point. The same argument proves $K*f\le v$.

The normalization of $K$ gives $-\Delta K=\delta_0$ in distributions: $K$ is harmonic off zero, $-\int_{\partial B_r}\partial_rK=1$, and $\int_{\partial B_r}K=O(r)$, so integration by parts outside $B_r$ and passage to $r=0$ gives the assertion. To apply this to $K*g$, note that for every compactly supported smooth $\varphi$, $$\int_{\mathbb R^n}g(y)\int_{\mathbb R^n}K(x-y)|\Delta\varphi(x)|\,\,\mathrm dx\,\,\mathrm dy
 =\int_{\mathbb R^n}(K*g)(x)|\Delta\varphi(x)|\,\,\mathrm dx
 \le\int_{\mathbb R^n}u(x)|\Delta\varphi(x)|\,\,\mathrm dx<\infty.$$ Fubini’s Theorem thus gives $-\Delta(K*g)=g$. The difference $h=u-K*g$ is a nonnegative locally integrable harmonic distribution. Every $h*\zeta_\rho$ is a nonnegative entire harmonic function. Such a function $H$ is constant: for $b=|x-z|$, the mean-value property and $B_R(x)\subset B_{R+b}(z)$ imply $$H(x)\le\left(\frac{R+b}{R}\right)^n H(z).$$ Letting $R\to\infty$ and then interchanging $x,z$ proves equality. Passing back to distributions shows that $h$ itself is a nonnegative constant. The argument for $v-K*f$ is identical.

Fix $x_0\ne0$. The sources are locally $C^1$ near $x_0$, because $u,v$ are strictly positive there and $p,q>0$. Split $g$ by a smooth cutoff supported near $x_0$ and equal to one on a smaller neighborhood. For the compactly supported part, local integrability of $K$ and $\nabla K$ gives a continuously differentiable convolution with the asserted derivative. For the other part, $|x-y|$ stays bounded away from zero as $x$ varies in a sufficiently small neighborhood of $x_0$. There both $K(x-y)$ and $|\nabla K(x-y)|$ are bounded by a fixed multiple of $K(x_0-y)$. The latter is integrable against $g$ by <a href="#eq:potential-lower" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:potential-lower">[eq:potential-lower]</a>. Dominated convergence proves continuity and differentiation of this part as well. The almost-everywhere identity in <a href="#eq:potential-constants" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:potential-constants">[eq:potential-constants]</a> is therefore pointwise off zero, and <a href="#eq:potential-gradients" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:potential-gradients">[eq:potential-gradients]</a> follows. The proof for $f$ is the same. ◻

</details>

<a id="eq:ball-lower"></a>
For $x,y\in B_R$, the kernel satisfies $K(x-y)\ge cR^{2-n}$. Thus Lemma <a href="#lem:potential" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:potential">4</a> gives the centered lower bounds $$\label{eq:ball-lower}
 u(x)\ge cR^{2-n}\int_{B_R}g,\qquad
 v(x)\ge cR^{2-n}\int_{B_R}f\qquad(x\in B_R),$$ where $c>0$ depends only on $n$.

<a id="stmt-lem-low-exponents"></a>
<div id="lem:low-exponents" class="lemma">

**Lemma 5** (Exclusion of small products). *A solution with $n\ge3$ and $A,B>-2$ necessarily has $pq>1$.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-low-exponents"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-low-exponents</summary>

*Proof.* Set $m_u(R)=\inf_{B_R}u$ and $m_v(R)=\inf_{B_R}v$. Both are positive by positivity and continuity on $\overline{B_R}$. Integrating the weights in <a href="#eq:ball-lower" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:ball-lower">[eq:ball-lower]</a> yields $$m_u(R)\ge cR^{2+A}m_v(R)^p,\qquad
 m_v(R)\ge cR^{2+B}m_u(R)^q.$$ Combining them and dividing by $m_u(R)^{pq}>0$ gives $$m_u(R)^{1-pq}\ge cR^{2+A+p(2+B)}.$$ The exponent on the right is positive. If $pq<1$, the left side is at most $u(0)^{1-pq}$, and if $pq=1$, it is one. Either case contradicts the inequality as $R\to\infty$. ◻

</details>

<a id="eq:scaling"></a>
We may consequently assume $n\ge3$, $A,B>-2$, and $pq>1$. Introduce <a id="eq:parameters"></a>$$\label{eq:parameters}
 \begin{gathered}
 \alpha=\frac{2+A+p(2+B)}{pq-1},\qquad
 \beta=\frac{2+B+q(2+A)}{pq-1},\\
 a=\frac1{q+1},\qquad b=\frac1{p+1}.
 \end{gathered}$$ Both $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are positive, and $$p\beta=\alpha+2+A,\qquad q\alpha=\beta+2+B.$$ For every $R>0$, the pair $$\label{eq:scaling}
 \begin{split}
 u_R(x)&=R^\alpha u(Rx),\qquad v_R(x)=R^\beta v(Rx),\\
 f_R(x)&=|x|^B u_R(x)^q=R^{\beta+2}f(Rx),\\
 g_R(x)&=|x|^A v_R(x)^p=R^{\alpha+2}g(Rx)
 \end{split}$$ again solves the same system, with the same parameters and regularity. These are dilations about the origin, where the weights are centered.

<a id="stmt-prop-uniform"></a>
<div id="prop:uniform" class="proposition">

<a id="eq:source-bounds"></a>
<a id="eq:potentials"></a>
<a id="eq:uniform"></a>
**Proposition 6** (Universal centered estimates). *Fix $n\ge3$, $p,q>0$, $A,B>-2$, and $pq>1$. There is a universal constant $C$ such that every solution satisfies, for all $R>0$, $$\label{eq:source-bounds}
 \int_{B_R}f\le CR^{n-2-\beta},\qquad
 \int_{B_R}g\le CR^{n-2-\alpha}.$$ The constants in <a href="#eq:potential-constants" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:potential-constants">[eq:potential-constants]</a> vanish, so that $$\label{eq:potentials}
 u=K*g,\qquad v=K*f\quad\hbox{on }\mathbb R^n\setminus\{0\}.$$ Moreover, $$\label{eq:uniform}
 \begin{split}
 &\int_{B_4}(f+g+u+v)\le C,\\
 &\int_{|x-y|\ge\ell}K(x-y)(f(y)+g(y))\,\,\mathrm dy
       \le C\ell^{2-n}
       \qquad (x\in B_2,\ 0<\ell\le1).
 \end{split}$$*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-prop-uniform"><summary>Proof of stmt-prop-uniform</summary>

*Proof.* Write $M_f=\int_{B_1}f$ and $M_g=\int_{B_1}g$. The lower bounds <a href="#eq:ball-lower" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:ball-lower">[eq:ball-lower]</a>, followed by integration of $|x|^B$ and $|x|^A$ on $B_1$, give $$M_f\ge c_fM_g^q,\qquad M_g\ge c_gM_f^p,$$ where $c_f,c_g>0$ are universal. In particular, $M_f\ge c_fc_g^qM_f^{pq}$, and $M_f>0$ and $pq>1$ give a universal upper bound for $M_f$. The interchanged argument bounds $M_g$. Apply these bounds to the scaled pair <a href="#eq:scaling" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:scaling">[eq:scaling]</a>; changing variables proves <a href="#eq:source-bounds" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:source-bounds">[eq:source-bounds]</a>. The argument uses only positive powers, and requires neither $p\ge1$ nor $q\ge1$.

If $c_u>0$ in <a href="#eq:potential-constants" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:potential-constants">[eq:potential-constants]</a>, then $u\ge c_u$ almost everywhere and $$\int_{B_R}f\ge c_u^q\int_{B_R}|x|^B\,\,\mathrm dx
 =c_u^q\frac{|\mathbb S^{n-1}|}{n+B}R^{n+B}.$$ This contradicts <a href="#eq:source-bounds" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:source-bounds">[eq:source-bounds]</a> as $R\to\infty$, since $n+B-(n-2-\beta)=B+2+\beta=q\alpha>0$. Likewise $c_v=0$, proving <a href="#eq:potentials" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:potentials">[eq:potentials]</a>.

For $x\in B_4$ and $j\ge3$, the annulus $2^j\le|y|<2^{j+1}$ has $|x-y|\ge2^{j-1}$. Its contribution to the $f$ potential is at most $$C2^{j(2-n)}\int_{B_{2^{j+1}}}f\le C2^{-j\beta},$$ and its contribution to the $g$ potential is at most $C2^{-j\alpha}$. The two series converge because $\alpha,\beta>0$. Thus the potentials of the sources restricted to $\mathbb R^n\setminus B_8$ are uniformly bounded on $B_4$. On the other hand, $$\sup_{y\in B_8}\int_{B_4}K(x-y)\,\,\mathrm dx
 \le\int_{B_{12}}K(z)\,\,\mathrm dz<\infty.$$ Tonelli’s Theorem, the source bounds on $B_8$, and <a href="#eq:potentials" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:potentials">[eq:potentials]</a> now bound $\int_{B_4}(u+v)$. The source part of the first estimate in <a href="#eq:uniform" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:uniform">[eq:uniform]</a> follows directly from <a href="#eq:source-bounds" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:source-bounds">[eq:source-bounds]</a>.

Finally, on $|x-y|\ge\ell$ the kernel is at most $c_n\ell^{2-n}$. This bounds the contribution from $y\in B_8$ by $C\ell^{2-n}$. The contribution from $y\notin B_8$ has already been bounded by $C$; since $0<\ell\le1$ and $n\ge3$, this proves the second estimate in <a href="#eq:uniform" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:uniform">[eq:uniform]</a>. ◻

</details>

The bounds <a href="#eq:source-bounds" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:source-bounds">[eq:source-bounds]</a> are centered at the origin. For example, when a later argument uses a ball $B_\ell(x)\subset B_4$, its source mass is bounded by the mass of $B_4$ in <a href="#eq:uniform" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:uniform">[eq:uniform]</a>. No translation of the weighted equation or source estimate is needed. Also, local energy is finite for each solution: $$fu=|x|^Bu^{q+1},\qquad gv=|x|^Av^{p+1}
 \quad\hbox{belong to }L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb R^n).$$ This statement follows from local boundedness and $A,B>-2$; it is not yet a bound uniform over solutions. We assume no finiteness of either energy integral on all of $\mathbb R^n$.

<a id="eq:gap"></a>
<a id="eq:energy-scaling"></a>
To connect the centered dilation with the subcritical hypothesis, define $$\label{eq:gap}
 \begin{split}
 \gamma
 &:=a(n+B)+b(n+A)-(n-2)\\
 &=(1-a-b)(\alpha+\beta+2-n),\\
 1-a-b&=\frac{pq-1}{(p+1)(q+1)}>0.
 \end{split}$$ Indeed, the numerator of $\alpha+\beta$ is $(q+1)(2+A)+(p+1)(2+B)$, and substitution gives the identity. The strict inequality in Theorem <a href="#thm:main" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:main">1</a> is precisely $\gamma>0$, and hence implies $\alpha+\beta+2-n>0$. Finally, changing variables in <a href="#eq:scaling" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:scaling">[eq:scaling]</a> gives the local energy identity $$\label{eq:energy-scaling}
 \int_{B_1}(f_Ru_R+g_Rv_R)
 =R^{\alpha+\beta+2-n}\int_{B_R}(fu+gv).$$ We shall obtain a universal upper bound for the left side, valid for every scaled solution. Positivity of the exponent in <a href="#eq:energy-scaling" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:energy-scaling">[eq:energy-scaling]</a> will then yield the final contradiction.

### A localized virial identity

System Rellich–Pohozaev identities have a long history (Mitidieri 1993; Souplet 2009) <a id="pm-cite-19"></a>[[Mitidieri1993](#ref-Mitidieri1993), [Souplet2009](#ref-Souplet2009)]; Phan gave a weighted version (Phan 2012) <a id="pm-cite-20"></a>[[Phan](#ref-Phan)]. We use a potential-kernel form that retains the spatial weights in a compactly supported interaction identity.

<a id="eq:interaction"></a>
We retain the parameters and exact potentials of the preceding section. Define the nonnegative interaction measure by $$\label{eq:interaction}
\begin{gathered}
  \,\mathrm d\pi(x,y)=f(x)g(y)K(x-y)\,\mathrm dx\,\mathrm dy,\\
  \pi(\,\mathrm dx,\mathbb R^n)=f(x)u(x)\,\mathrm dx,
  \quad
  \pi(\mathbb R^n,\,\mathrm dy)=g(y)v(y)\,\mathrm dy.
\end{gathered}$$ The marginal identities follow from Tonelli’s Theorem and <a href="#eq:potentials" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:potentials">[eq:potentials]</a>. This measure is locally finite: on each compact set the sources are integrable and the potentials are bounded. Its total mass is not assumed finite. Values of the density or of the quotients below on the diagonal may be set to zero. For a unit vector $e\in\mathbb S^{n-1}$, put $$D_e(x,y)=\frac{((x-y)\cdot e)^2}{|x-y|^2},
  \qquad \omega_x=\frac{x}{|x|},
  \qquad D(x,y)=D_{\omega_x}(x,y).$$ Choose any unit vector as $\omega_0$; this convention does not affect any integral.

<a id="eq:cutoff-bounds"></a>
The cutoff will vanish to a sufficiently high order at its outer boundary. Writing $t_+=\max\{t,0\}$, fix <a id="eq:cutoff"></a>$$\label{eq:cutoff}
\begin{gathered}
  d(x)=\min\{1,(2-|x|)_+\},\qquad
  m=n+3,\qquad k=n(m+1),\qquad N=k+n+2,\\
  \eta(x)=\bigl[\bigl(1-(|x|-1)_+^3\bigr)_+\bigr]^N,
  \qquad w(x)=-x\cdot\nabla\eta(x),\qquad X(x)=x\eta(x).
\end{gathered}$$ Thus $\eta\in C_c^2(\mathbb R^n)$ equals one on $B_1$, and $w\ge0$ vanishes unless $1<|x|<2$. The cubic term makes the first two derivatives match at radius one, and the power $N$ makes them vanish at radius two. On the intervening annulus, with $s=|x|-1$, $1-s^3=d(x)(1+s+s^2)$. Differentiation therefore gives $$\label{eq:cutoff-bounds}
\begin{gathered}
  d^N\le\eta\le C d^N,\qquad
  |\nabla\eta|+w\le C d^{N-1},\\
  |\nabla^2\eta|+|\nabla w|+|\nabla^2X|\le C d^{N-2},
  \qquad dw\le C\eta.
\end{gathered}$$ Set $$\begin{gathered}
  E_f=\int_{\mathbb R^n}\eta fu\,\mathrm dx,\qquad
  E_g=\int_{\mathbb R^n}\eta gv\,\mathrm dx,\qquad E=E_f+E_g,
  \\
  W_f=\int_{\mathbb R^n}wfu\,\mathrm dx,\qquad
  W_g=\int_{\mathbb R^n}wgv\,\mathrm dx.
\end{gathered}$$ All these quantities are finite for each solution: $u,v$ are bounded on $\overline B_2$, and $|x|^A,|x|^B$ are integrable there because $A,B>-2$ and $n\ge3$. This asserts finiteness only; a bound independent of the solution remains to be proved.

<a id="stmt-auto-lemma-1"></a>
<div class="lemma">

<a id="eq:virial"></a>
**Lemma 7** (Localized virial identity). *The following identity holds, with an absolutely convergent integral: $$\label{eq:virial}
\begin{split}
  a(n+B)E_f+b(n+A)E_g-aW_f-bW_g
    &=(n-2)\int_{\mathbb R^{2n}}\Phi(x,y)\,\mathrm d\pi(x,y),\\
  \Phi(x,y)&=
    \frac{(X(x)-X(y))\cdot(x-y)}{|x-y|^2}.
\end{split}$$*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-auto-lemma-1"><summary>Proof of stmt-auto-lemma-1</summary>

*Proof.* We first justify absolute convergence of the separate kernel-gradient integrals, before combining them. For $r=|x|$ sufficiently small, local boundedness of $v$ gives $g(y)\le C_{u,v}r^A$ when $|x-y|\le r/2$. Hence $$|X(x)|\int_{|x-y|\le r/2}|x-y|^{1-n}g(y)\,\mathrm dy
       \le C_{u,v}r\,r^A\int_0^{r/2}\,\mathrm ds
       \le C_{u,v}r^{2+A}.$$ On the complementary region, $|X(x)|\le r\le2|x-y|$, so the same expression with that region in place of the ball is at most $C(K*g)(x)=Cu(x)$. Both bounds remain bounded as $x\to0$. On compact subsets away from zero, the nearby source is bounded and $|\nabla K|$ is locally integrable; the remaining part is bounded by a constant times the finite potential. Since $X$ is supported in $\overline B_2$ and $f$ is locally integrable, this proves $$\int_{\mathbb R^n}f(x)|X(x)|
       \int_{\mathbb R^n}|\nabla K(x-y)|g(y)\,\mathrm dy\,\mathrm dx<\infty.$$ Interchanging $(f,u,B)$ and $(g,v,A)$ proves the other assertion. In particular, $fX\cdot\nabla u$ and $gX\cdot\nabla v$ are absolutely integrable.

Since $a=1/(q+1)$, integration by parts outside $B_\rho$, with $0<\rho<1$, gives $$\begin{split}
  -\int_{|x|>\rho} fX\cdot\nabla u\,\mathrm dx
   ={}&a\int_{|x|>\rho}|x|^B u^{q+1}
             \bigl((n+B)\eta-w\bigr)\,\mathrm dx\\
     &+a\rho^{B+1}\int_{\partial B_\rho}u^{q+1}\,\mathrm dS.
\end{split}$$ Here $\operatorname{div}(|x|^BX)=|x|^B((n+B)\eta-w)$, and the boundary term has the displayed positive sign because the outward normal of the punctured domain on $\partial B_\rho$ is $-\omega_x$. Its magnitude is $O_{u,v}(\rho^{n+B})$, which tends to zero. The corresponding term for $gX\cdot\nabla v$ is positive and $O_{u,v}(\rho^{n+A})$, and also tends to zero. There is no outer boundary term because $X$ is compactly supported. Consequently, $$-\int fX\cdot\nabla u\,\mathrm dx-\int gX\cdot\nabla v\,\mathrm dx
       =a(n+B)E_f+b(n+A)E_g-aW_f-bW_g.$$ Finally substitute the differentiated potential identities and $\nabla K(z)=-(n-2)zK(z)/|z|^2$. Absolute convergence permits Fubini’s Theorem and interchanging the variables in the second integral; their sum is exactly the right side of <a href="#eq:virial" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:virial">[eq:virial]</a>. ◻

</details>

<a id="stmt-lem-localization"></a>
<div id="lem:localization" class="lemma">

<a id="eq:localization"></a>
**Lemma 8** (Localization). *For every $\varepsilon>0$, there is a constant $C_\varepsilon$, independent of the solution, such that $$\label{eq:localization}
  |E_f-E_g|+
  \int_{\mathbb R^{2n}}|\Phi(x,y)-\eta(x)+w(x)D(x,y)|\,\mathrm d\pi(x,y)
       \le\varepsilon E+C_\varepsilon.$$*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-localization"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-localization</summary>

*Proof.* Fix $0<\delta<1/4$, and write $\ell(z)=\delta d(z)^m$ whenever $d(z)>0$. Call a pair $(x,y)$ close if $|x-y|\le\ell(z)$ for at least one endpoint $z\in\{x,y\}$ with $d(z)>0$, and far otherwise. Since $d$ is 1-Lipschitz, for a close pair every point $\xi$ of the segment $[x,y]$ satisfies $$(1-\delta)d(z)\le d(\xi)\le(1+\delta)d(z).$$ In particular, the entire segment is inside $B_2$. The derivative matrix of $X$ is $$\nabla X(x)=\eta(x)\operatorname{Id}
                   -w(x)\omega_x\otimes\omega_x,$$ with its second term zero on $B_1$. Taylor’s formula and <a href="#eq:cutoff-bounds" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:cutoff-bounds">[eq:cutoff-bounds]</a> give, on close pairs with $x\ne y$, $$\begin{aligned}
  |\Phi-\eta(x)+w(x)D|
       &\le C\delta d(z)^{N-2+m}
        =C\delta d(z)^{N+n+1},\\
  |\eta(x)-\eta(y)|
       &\le C\delta d(z)^{N-1+m}
        =C\delta d(z)^{N+n+2}.
\end{aligned}$$ Both are at most $C\delta d(z)^N$. Since $d(z)^N\le\eta(z)\le\eta(x)+\eta(y)$, the marginal identities in <a href="#eq:interaction" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:interaction">[eq:interaction]</a> bound the integral of their sum over close pairs by $C\delta E$.

For a far pair, every endpoint $z$ in $B_2$ satisfies $|x-y|>\ell(z)$. Using $|\Phi|\le(|X(x)|+|X(y)|)/|x-y|$ and $0\le D\le1$, we obtain $$|\Phi-\eta(x)+w(x)D|+|\eta(x)-\eta(y)|
   \le C\sum_{\substack{z\in\{x,y\}\\d(z)>0}}
          \left(d(z)^{N-1}+\frac{d(z)^N}{\ell(z)}\right).$$ Pairs with both endpoints outside $B_2$ contribute zero. For the terms associated with $x$, the tail estimate in Proposition <a href="#prop:uniform" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:uniform">6</a>, <a href="#eq:uniform" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:uniform">[eq:uniform]</a>, bounds the integral by $$\begin{split}
  &C\int_{B_2}f(x)
       \left(d(x)^{N-1}+\frac{d(x)^N}{\ell(x)}\right)
       \ell(x)^{2-n}\,\mathrm dx\\
  &\quad\le C\int_{B_2}f(x)
      \left(\delta^{2-n}d(x)^{4n+7}
                 +\delta^{1-n}d(x)^{3n+5}\right)\,\mathrm dx
       \le C_\delta.
\end{split}$$ Indeed the two exponents are $$N-1-m(n-2)=4n+7>0,\qquad
  N-m(n-1)=3n+5>0,$$ and $\int_{B_2}f\le C$ by <a href="#eq:uniform" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:uniform">[eq:uniform]</a>. The terms associated with $y$ satisfy the same bound with $g$. All these constants are independent of the solution.

Finally, both cutoff marginals are finite, so $$|E_f-E_g|
    =\left|\int_{\mathbb R^{2n}}(\eta(x)-\eta(y))\,\mathrm d\pi(x,y)\right|
    \le\int_{\mathbb R^{2n}}|\eta(x)-\eta(y)|\,\mathrm d\pi(x,y).$$ The preceding estimates bound the left side of <a href="#eq:localization" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:localization">[eq:localization]</a> by $C\delta E+C_\delta$. Choosing $\delta$ sufficiently small proves the result. ◻

</details>

To obtain a uniform bound for $E$, it remains to prove that, for every $\varepsilon>0$, $$aW_f+bW_g-(n-2)\int w(x)D(x,y)\,\mathrm d\pi(x,y)
 \le\varepsilon E+C_\varepsilon.$$ The next two sections establish this comparison from source-layer intervals. In Section <a href="#sec:conclusion" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:conclusion">6</a>, it will combine with the marginal estimate above and the positive subcritical gap $\gamma$ to give the energy bound.

### An interval-pair inequality

We first isolate the geometric comparison used in the pressure estimate. It is a statement about two parallel lines and does not involve the equations. The self-contained midpoint argument below gives the comparison; its nonnegative formulation also permits unbounded intervals of weight zero.

Fix a unit vector $e$ and distinct $z,z'\in e^\perp$. Write $$x=z+se,\qquad y=z'+s'e,\qquad
 \lambda=|z-z'|>0,\qquad h=s-s'.$$ The restricted Newton kernel and its directional factor are $$\mathcal K(s,s')
 =\frac{(\lambda^2+h^2)^{(2-n)/2}}{(n-2)|\mathbb S^{n-1}|},
 \qquad \mathcal D(s,s')=\frac{h^2}{\lambda^2+h^2}.$$ For a bounded interval $I=(s_-,s_+)$, let $$\overline{\mathcal K}_I(s')
 =\frac{\mathcal K(s_-,s')+\mathcal K(s_+,s')}{2}.$$ For a bounded interval $J=(s'_-,s'_+)$, define $\overline{\mathcal K}_J(s)$ by averaging in the second variable. When $I$ and $J$ are bounded, write $m_I,m_J$ for their midpoints and $d_0=m_I-m_J$ for their midpoint separation.

<a id="stmt-lem-interval-pair"></a>
<div id="lem:interval-pair" class="lemma">

<a id="eq:interval-pair"></a>
**Lemma 9** (Weighted interval comparison). *Let $n\ge3$, $\lambda>0$, and let $I,J\subset\mathbb R$ be nonempty open intervals, possibly unbounded. Let $c_I,c_J\ge0$, with $I$ bounded whenever $c_I>0$ and $J$ bounded whenever $c_J>0$. Then $$\label{eq:interval-pair}
\begin{split}
 \int_{I\times J}(c_I+c_J)\mathcal K\,\,\mathrm ds\,\,\mathrm ds'
 \le \int_{I\times J}\Bigl[
 &c_I\overline{\mathcal K}_I+c_J\overline{\mathcal K}_J\\
 &+\bigl((n-2)\min(c_I,c_J)\mathcal D
                  +|c_I-c_J|\bigr)\mathcal K\Bigr]\,\,\mathrm ds\,\,\mathrm ds'.
\end{split}$$ Every term is nonnegative and the inequality is understood for extended integrals. An endpoint term with coefficient zero is defined to be zero, without evaluating the endpoint average.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-interval-pair"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-interval-pair</summary>

*Proof.* If at most one coefficient is positive, then $|c_I-c_J|=c_I+c_J$, so the inequality holds pointwise before integration. Suppose now that both coefficients are positive. Both intervals are bounded and $\lambda>0$, so all integrals in the following calculation are finite.

<a id="eq:midpoint-identity"></a>
Integration by parts in the first variable gives $$\int_I(\mathcal K-\overline{\mathcal K}_I)\,\,\mathrm ds
 =-\int_I(s-m_I)\partial_s\mathcal K\,\,\mathrm ds.$$ The corresponding identity in the second variable, together with $$\partial_s\mathcal K=-(n-2)\frac{h}{\lambda^2+h^2}\mathcal K,
 \qquad
 \partial_{s'}\mathcal K=(n-2)\frac{h}{\lambda^2+h^2}\mathcal K,$$ yields $$\label{eq:midpoint-identity}
 \int_{I\times J}
  (2\mathcal K-\overline{\mathcal K}_I-\overline{\mathcal K}_J)
   \,\,\mathrm ds\,\,\mathrm ds'
 =(n-2)\int_{I\times J}
    \frac{h^2-d_0h}{\lambda^2+h^2}\mathcal K\,\,\mathrm ds\,\,\mathrm ds'.$$

The sign of the midpoint term follows directly from interval overlap. If $L=|I|$ and $M=|J|$, the pushforward of $\,\mathrm ds\,\,\mathrm ds'$ under $(s,s')\mapsto h=s-s'$ has density $$\rho(h)=F(|h-d_0|),\qquad
 F(r)=\max\left\{0,\min\left\{L,M,\frac{L+M}{2}-r\right\}\right\}
 \quad(r\ge0).$$ Indeed, $\rho(h)=|I\cap(J+h)|$, the overlap length of two intervals whose centers are separated by $|h-d_0|$. The function $F$ is nonincreasing. If $d_0\ge0$ and $h>0$, then $|h-d_0|\le|h+d_0|$, hence $\rho(h)\ge\rho(-h)$; for $d_0\le0$ the inequality is reversed. Pairing positive and negative $h$ against the odd function $h\mathcal K(h)/(\lambda^2+h^2)$ therefore proves $$d_0\int_{I\times J}\frac{h}{\lambda^2+h^2}
                 \mathcal K\,\,\mathrm ds\,\,\mathrm ds'\ge0.$$ Consequently the left side of <a href="#eq:midpoint-identity" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:midpoint-identity">[eq:midpoint-identity]</a> is at most $(n-2)\int_{I\times J}\mathcal D\mathcal K$.

Multiply this last estimate by $c=\min(c_I,c_J)$. Each individual endpoint defect is bounded above by $\int_{I\times J}\mathcal K$, because its subtracted endpoint average is nonnegative. The remaining coefficients are $c_I-c$ and $c_J-c$, whose sum is $|c_I-c_J|$. Adding these estimates and moving the finite endpoint terms to the right proves <a href="#eq:interval-pair" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:interval-pair">[eq:interval-pair]</a>. ◻

</details>

The nonnegative formulation is essential when $n=3$: even for $\lambda>0$, an integral of $\mathcal K$ over an unbounded partner interval can diverge. Lemma <a href="#lem:interval-pair" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:interval-pair">9</a> makes no subtraction on such an interval.

<a id="sec:pressure"></a>
### Pressure from weighted source layers

We retain the sources, potentials, interaction measure, and cutoffs defined above. In particular, $n\ge3$, $A,B>-2$, $p,q>0$, and $pq>1$. All constants in this section are uniform over admissible solution pairs. A subscript records permitted dependence on the auxiliary parameters; constants are always independent of the direction $e$.

<a id="stmt-prop-pressure"></a>
<div id="prop:pressure" class="proposition">

<a id="eq:pressure"></a>
**Proposition 10** (Localized pressure). *Let $(u,v)$ be a positive solution pair as in Theorem <a href="#thm:main" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:main">1</a>, with $n\ge3$, $A,B>-2$, and $pq>1$. For every $\varepsilon>0$ there is a constant $C_\varepsilon$, depending only on the equation parameters and $\varepsilon$, such that $$\label{eq:pressure}
 aW_f+bW_g
 \le(n-2)\int_{\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^n}w(x)D(x,y)\,\,\mathrm d\pi(x,y)
       +\varepsilon E+C_\varepsilon.$$*

</div>

The proof must accommodate the spatial factors at source-layer endpoints. We localize to short intervals, where those factors vary by at most $C\delta d(x)$, and use caps of angular radius proportional to $d(x)$. The factor $d$ converts both errors into multiples of $E$, by $dw\le C\eta$ from <a href="#eq:cutoff-bounds" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:cutoff-bounds">[eq:cutoff-bounds]</a>.

<a id="pm-h-9"></a>
#### Angular localization

Write $\mathbb E_e$ and $\mathbb P_e$ for expectation and probability under normalized surface measure on $\mathbb S^{n-1}$. Fix $0<\kappa<1$ and $0<\delta<1/4$, and put $$\ell(x)=\delta d(x)^m\qquad(d(x)>0).$$ Choose a fixed nonnegative smooth function $\psi$ on $[0,\infty)$, equal to one near zero, supported in $[0,1]$, and bounded by one. For $0<l\le1$ and any $\theta\in\mathbb S^{n-1}$, set $$Z(l)=\mathbb E_e\psi(|e-\theta|/l).$$ Rotational invariance makes this independent of $\theta$. Chordal polar coordinates give $$Z(l)=\frac{|\mathbb S^{n-2}|}{|\mathbb S^{n-1}|}
       l^{n-1}\int_0^1\psi(t)t^{n-2}
                  (1-l^2t^2/4)^{(n-3)/2}\,\,\mathrm dt$$ and therefore $c l^{n-1}\le Z(l)\le C l^{n-1}$ and $|Z'(l)|\le C l^{n-2}$.

<a id="eq:cap-weight"></a>
<a id="eq:cap-bounds"></a>
Define the cap weight by $$\label{eq:cap-weight}
 h_e(x)=w(x)\frac{\psi(|e-\omega_x|/(\kappa d(x)))}{Z(\kappa d(x))}
 \quad(1<|x|<2),\qquad h_e(x)=0\quad\hbox{otherwise}.$$ Its exact normalization is $\mathbb E_e h_e(x)=w(x)$. Differentiation, using <a href="#eq:cutoff-bounds" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:cutoff-bounds">[eq:cutoff-bounds]</a>, gives $$\label{eq:cap-bounds}
 0\le h_e(x)\le C_\kappa d(x)^{N-n},\qquad
 |\nabla h_e(x)|\le C_\kappa d(x)^{N-n-1}
 \quad(1<|x|<2).$$ To check the second estimate, derivatives of the cap profile cost at most $C_\kappa/d$, and differentiation of $Z(\kappa d)^{-1}$ costs the same relative factor. The profile is constant near its center, so the norm $|e-\omega_x|$ causes no differentiability problem there. The weight extends continuously by zero across both boundary spheres: $w$ vanishes quadratically at $|x|=1$, and $N-n>1$ at $|x|=2$.

<a id="eq:cap-oscillation"></a>
<a id="eq:cap-mismatch"></a>
<a id="eq:cap-pressure"></a>
As in Lemma <a href="#lem:localization" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:localization">8</a>, call a pair close if $|x-y|\le\ell(z)$ for at least one endpoint $z\in\{x,y\}$ with $d(z)>0$. On its segment, $d$ is comparable to $d(z)$ because $d$ is $1$-Lipschitz and $\ell(z)\le\delta d(z)$. Integrating the derivative bound across the pieces of this segment proves $$\label{eq:cap-oscillation}
 |h_e(x)-h_e(y)|
 \le C_\kappa\delta d(z)^{N-n-1+m}
 \le C_\kappa\delta d(z)^N,$$ where $N-n-1+m=N+2$. The interaction marginals bound the integral over close pairs by $C_\kappa\delta E$. On the complementary pairs, <a href="#eq:cap-bounds" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:cap-bounds">[eq:cap-bounds]</a> and the tail bound <a href="#eq:uniform" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:uniform">[eq:uniform]</a> give, for example, the bound $$C_\kappa\int_{B_2}f(x)d(x)^{N-n}\ell(x)^{2-n}\,\,\mathrm dx
 \le C_{\kappa,\delta},$$ since $N-n-m(n-2)=3n+8>0$; the other endpoint is identical with $g$ in place of $f$. We have proved $$\label{eq:cap-mismatch}
 \int|h_e(x)-h_e(y)|\,\,\mathrm d\pi(x,y)
 \le C_\kappa\delta E+C_{\kappa,\delta}.$$ Moreover, on the support of $h_e(x)$, $$|D_e(x,y)-D(x,y)|\le2|e-\omega_x|\le2\kappa d(x).$$ Averaging and using $dw\le C\eta$ gives $$\label{eq:cap-pressure}
 \mathbb E_e\int h_e(x)D_e(x,y)\,\,\mathrm d\pi(x,y)
 \le\int w(x)D(x,y)\,\,\mathrm d\pi(x,y)+C\kappa E.$$ Here the constant $C$ is independent of $\kappa,\delta$.

<a id="pm-h-10"></a>
#### Measurable intervals and their loss

<a id="eq:component-weight"></a>
For $0<t<f(x)$, let $I$ be the connected component containing $x$ of the source layer $\{f>t\}$ on the line $x+\mathbb Re$. Assign it the weight $$\label{eq:component-weight}
 \chi_I=
 \begin{cases}
  \displaystyle\inf_I h_e,
   &I\text{ bounded and }|I|\le2\delta\inf_I d,\\
  0,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}$$ Set $\chi_f(e,x,t)=\chi_I$. Define $\chi_g(e,y,t')=\chi_J$ in the same way from $\{g>t'\}$. In particular, $0\le\chi_f(e,x,t)\le h_e(x)$ and $0\le\chi_g(e,y,t')\le h_e(y)$.

All these functions, as well as finite endpoints, are jointly measurable. Indeed, with the convention $f(0)=0$, the set $\{(x,t):x\ne0,\ 0<t<f(x)\}$ is open. The forward exit distance of its line component exceeds $r\ge0$ exactly when the compact segment $\{x+se:0\le s\le r\}$ is contained in the layer. This is an open condition in $(e,x,t)$; the backward exit distance is treated in the same way. Their sum, including the value $+\infty$, is the measurable component length. The infima in <a href="#eq:component-weight" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:component-weight">[eq:component-weight]</a> can be taken over rational offsets between these two exits, since $d$ and $h_e$ are continuous. This proves the assertion, including measurability in $e$, without a choice of coordinates on the varying spaces $e^\perp$.

For each direction define the nonnegative losses $$\begin{aligned}
 L_f(e)&=\int_{\mathbb R^n}u(x)\int_0^{f(x)}
                 (h_e(x)-\chi_f(e,x,t))\,\,\mathrm dt\,\,\mathrm dx,\\
 L_g(e)&=\int_{\mathbb R^n}v(y)\int_0^{g(y)}
                 (h_e(y)-\chi_g(e,y,t'))\,\,\mathrm dt'\,\,\mathrm dy.
\end{aligned}$$ They are finite for each fixed solution and direction, since $h_e$ is bounded and supported on the compact annulus $1\le|x|\le2$. This finiteness does not assert a uniform bound on those marginals.

<a id="stmt-lem-layer-loss"></a>
<div id="lem:layer-loss" class="lemma">

<a id="eq:layer-loss"></a>
**Lemma 11** (Averaged layer loss). *For the cap weights <a href="#eq:cap-weight" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:cap-weight">[eq:cap-weight]</a> and component weights <a href="#eq:component-weight" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:component-weight">[eq:component-weight]</a>, under the hypotheses of Proposition <a href="#prop:pressure" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:pressure">10</a>, every $P\ge1$ satisfies $$\label{eq:layer-loss}
 \mathbb E_e\bigl(L_f(e)+L_g(e)\bigr)
 \le\bigl(C_\kappa\delta+C_{\kappa,\delta}P^{-1}\bigr)E+CP.$$ The constants in the relative coefficient are independent of $P$, and the last constant is independent of $\kappa,\delta,P$.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-layer-loss"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-layer-loss</summary>

*Proof.* We prove the assertion for $f,u$; the same argument applies to $g,v$. Only $1<|x|<2$ contributes. Fix such an $x$ and write $d=d(x)$ and $\ell=\delta d^m$. If the component $I$ at level $t$ has $|I|\le\ell$, then $$\inf_I d\ge d-\ell\ge(1-\delta)d,
 \qquad |I|\le\delta d\le2\delta\inf_I d.$$ It therefore passes the length condition in <a href="#eq:component-weight" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:component-weight">[eq:component-weight]</a>. Estimate <a href="#eq:cap-oscillation" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:cap-oscillation">[eq:cap-oscillation]</a> shows that its entire loss of weight is at most $C_\kappa\delta d^N$.

<a id="eq:long-interval"></a>
If $|I|>\ell$, at least one of its two portions from $x$ contains a segment of length $\ell/2$. Let $F$ be the set of directions whose forward segment of that length lies in $\{f>t\}$. Polar integration centered at $x$ gives $$\int_{B_{\ell/2}(x)}f
 \ge \frac{|\mathbb S^{n-1}|}{n}\,t(\ell/2)^n\mathbb P_e(F).$$ The long-interval event is contained in $F\cup(-F)$. Antipodal symmetry, followed by the source bound in <a href="#eq:uniform" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:uniform">[eq:uniform]</a>, yields $$\label{eq:long-interval}
 \mathbb P_e\{|I|>\ell\}
 \le \frac{C}{t\ell^n}\int_{B_\ell(x)}f
 \le\frac{C}{t\ell^n}.$$ Here $B_\ell(x)\subset B_4$; only its containment in a fixed centered ball is used.

For $t\ge P d^{-k}$, the expected loss on long intervals is bounded using the supremum of $h_e(x)$ times the probability in <a href="#eq:long-interval" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:long-interval">[eq:long-interval]</a>. No independence is needed. The result is $$C_\kappa d^{N-n}\,C\delta^{-n}P^{-1}d^{k-mn}
   =C_{\kappa,\delta}P^{-1}d^N,$$ because $k=n(m+1)$. Integrating the short- and long-interval estimates over these high levels, and then against $u(x)\,\,\mathrm dx$, gives at most $\bigl(C_\kappa\delta+C_{\kappa,\delta}P^{-1}\bigr)E_f$.

For the remaining levels use the exact cap normalization: $$\int_0^{\min\{f(x),Pd^{-k}\}}
        \mathbb E_e(h_e(x)-\chi_f(e,x,t))\,\,\mathrm dt
 \le Pd^{-k}w(x)\le CPd^{N-1-k}\le CP.$$ The last exponent equals $n+1$. Integration against $u$ is bounded by $CP$ by <a href="#eq:uniform" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:uniform">[eq:uniform]</a>. Repeating the proof for $g,v$ proves <a href="#eq:layer-loss" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:layer-loss">[eq:layer-loss]</a>. ◻

</details>

<a id="pm-h-11"></a>
#### Weighted endpoints and nonnegative integration

<a id="eq:weighted-endpoints"></a>
Suppose $\chi_I>0$ for an $f$-layer at level $t$. Then $I$ is bounded and $h_e\ge\chi_I>0$ on it. Continuity gives the same inequality at its two endpoints $x_-,x_+$, so they lie in the annulus $1<|x|<2$. Continuity of $f$ there and maximality of the component imply $$f(x_-)=f(x_+)=t,\qquad
 u(x_\pm)=t^{1/q}|x_\pm|^{-B/q}.$$ For any $x\in I$, <a href="#eq:component-weight" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:component-weight">[eq:component-weight]</a> gives $|x_\pm-x|\le2\delta d(x)$. The positive radial function $|x|^{-B/q}$ has a bounded relative Lipschitz constant on $1\le|x|\le2$. Thus, with $\overline u_I=(u(x_-)+u(x_+))/2$, we have $$\label{eq:weighted-endpoints}
 \left|\overline u_I-(t/|x|^B)^{1/q}\right|
 \le C\delta d(x)(t/|x|^B)^{1/q}
 \le C\delta d(x)u(x).$$ The same statement holds for a positive-weight $g$-component $J$, with endpoint average $\overline v_J$ and ideal value $(t'/|y|^A)^{1/p}$. When a component weight is zero, every product involving its endpoint average is defined as zero without selecting or evaluating endpoints.

<a id="eq:layer-pair"></a>
Fix a direction $e$ and levels $t,t'>0$. On two distinct parallel lines $x=z+se$ and $y=z'+s'e$, apply Lemma <a href="#lem:interval-pair" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:interval-pair">9</a> to each pair of layer components $I,J$ with $c_I=\chi_I$ and $c_J=\chi_J$. Since $\min(\chi_I,\chi_J)\le h_e(x)$, it gives $$\label{eq:layer-pair}
\begin{split}
 \int_{I\times J}(\chi_I+\chi_J)\mathcal K
 \le\int_{I\times J}\Bigl[
  &\chi_I\overline{\mathcal K}_I+\chi_J\overline{\mathcal K}_J\\
  &+\bigl((n-2)h_e(x)D_e(x,y)+|\chi_I-\chi_J|\bigr)
       \mathcal K\Bigr],
\end{split}$$ where the measures are $\,\mathrm ds\,\,\mathrm ds'$. At this stage all terms are nonnegative extended integrals.

Each open line layer has at most countably many components. Sum <a href="#eq:layer-pair" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:layer-pair">[eq:layer-pair]</a> over the two sets of components, and integrate in $z,z'\in e^\perp$ and $t,t'>0$. The summed functions are the jointly measurable functions described above; hence Tonelli’s Theorem applies. The coincident-line set $z=z'$ is null and may be omitted. By the potential identities <a href="#eq:potentials" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:potentials">[eq:potentials]</a>, the two terms on the left become $$\begin{aligned}
 U_f(e)&=\int\int_0^{f(x)}\chi_f(e,x,t)u(x)\,\,\mathrm dt\,\,\mathrm dx,\\
 U_g(e)&=\int\int_0^{g(y)}\chi_g(e,y,t')v(y)\,\,\mathrm dt'\,\,\mathrm dy.
\end{aligned}$$ Similarly, the endpoint terms become $$\begin{aligned}
 V_f(e)&=\int\int_0^{f(x)}\chi_f(e,x,t)\overline u_I\,\,\mathrm dt\,\,\mathrm dx,\\
 V_g(e)&=\int\int_0^{g(y)}\chi_g(e,y,t')\overline v_J\,\,\mathrm dt'\,\,\mathrm dy.
\end{aligned}$$ For example, integrating $\overline{\mathcal K}_I$ over all $(y,t')$ layers recovers the average of $K*g$ at $x_-,x_+$. For each selected endpoint the omitted $y$-points comprise one affine line, which is Lebesgue null in $\mathbb R^n$, so the recovered potential is exactly $\overline u_I$.

We now verify finiteness before any subtraction. Set $$H_f(e)=\int h_e(x)f(x)u(x)\,\,\mathrm dx,
 \qquad H_g(e)=\int h_e(y)g(y)v(y)\,\,\mathrm dy.$$ Both are finite for every fixed solution and direction because the cap weights are bounded and supported on a compact annulus. We have $U_f\le H_f$, $U_g\le H_g$, while <a href="#eq:weighted-endpoints" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:weighted-endpoints">[eq:weighted-endpoints]</a> gives $V_f\le C H_f$ and $V_g\le C H_g$. The integrated pressure term is at most $(n-2)H_f$. Finally, $$|\chi_f(e,x,t)-\chi_g(e,y,t')|\le h_e(x)+h_e(y),$$ so its integrated contribution is at most $H_f+H_g$, again by Tonelli and the two potential marginals. All the integrated terms are therefore finite, even when $n=3$. In particular, we have never subtracted the possibly infinite integrals over an unbounded line component.

<a id="eq:integrated-pressure"></a>
It is now legitimate to subtract $V_f+V_g$. The sharper mismatch bound $$\begin{aligned}
 |\chi_f(e,x,t)-\chi_g(e,y,t')|
 \le{}&|h_e(x)-h_e(y)|\\
      &+(h_e(x)-\chi_f(e,x,t))
        +(h_e(y)-\chi_g(e,y,t'))
\end{aligned}$$ shows that the resulting finite quantity $$\begin{split}
 S_e={}&\int\int_0^{f(x)}\chi_f(e,x,t)(u(x)-\overline u_I)\,\,\mathrm dt\,\,\mathrm dx\\
   &+\int\int_0^{g(y)}\chi_g(e,y,t')(v(y)-\overline v_J)\,\,\mathrm dt'\,\,\mathrm dy
\end{split}$$ satisfies $$\label{eq:integrated-pressure}
 S_e\le(n-2)\int h_e(x)D_e(x,y)\,\,\mathrm d\pi(x,y)
       +\int|h_e(x)-h_e(y)|\,\,\mathrm d\pi(x,y)+L_f(e)+L_g(e).$$ The quantity $S_e$ need not be nonnegative; only the preceding unsubtracted expressions were treated with Tonelli’s Theorem.

<a id="pm-h-12"></a>
#### Recovery of the cutoff pressure

<a id="eq:weighted-layer-identity"></a>
<a id="eq:pressure-recovery"></a>
The exact weighted layer identity is $$\label{eq:weighted-layer-identity}
 \int_0^{f(x)}\left[u(x)-(t/|x|^B)^{1/q}\right]\,\,\mathrm dt
 =\frac{f(x)u(x)}{q+1}=a f(x)u(x)\qquad(x\ne0).$$ Indeed, the integral of $(t/|x|^B)^{1/q}$ equals $qf(x)u(x)/(q+1)$. The analogous identity for $g$ has coefficient $b=1/(p+1)$. To compare <a href="#eq:weighted-layer-identity" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:weighted-layer-identity">[eq:weighted-layer-identity]</a> with $S_e$, set $\tau=(t/|x|^B)^{1/q}$. Since $0\le\tau\le u(x)$, $$\begin{aligned}
 &h_e(x)(u(x)-\tau)-\chi_f(e,x,t)(u(x)-\overline u_I)\\
 &\quad=(h_e(x)-\chi_f(e,x,t))(u(x)-\tau)
                  +\chi_f(e,x,t)(\overline u_I-\tau)\\
 &\quad\le(h_e(x)-\chi_f(e,x,t))u(x)
                  +C\delta d(x)h_e(x)u(x),
\end{aligned}$$ by <a href="#eq:weighted-endpoints" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:weighted-endpoints">[eq:weighted-endpoints]</a>; the zero-weight case has the same bound under the convention already stated. Integrating and treating the $g$-layer in the same way gives $$\label{eq:pressure-recovery}
\begin{split}
 aH_f(e)+bH_g(e)
 \le{}&S_e+L_f(e)+L_g(e)\\
       &+C\delta\int d(x)h_e(x)f(x)u(x)\,\,\mathrm dx\\
       &+C\delta\int d(y)h_e(y)g(y)v(y)\,\,\mathrm dy.
\end{split}$$

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-layer-loss-2"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-layer-loss</summary>

*Proof of Proposition <a href="#prop:pressure" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:pressure">10</a>.* Combine <a href="#eq:integrated-pressure" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:integrated-pressure">[eq:integrated-pressure]</a> and <a href="#eq:pressure-recovery" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:pressure-recovery">[eq:pressure-recovery]</a>, and average over $e$. This incurs two copies of $L_f+L_g$. The identities $\mathbb E_e h_e=w$ and $dw\le C\eta$, together with <a href="#eq:cap-mismatch" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:cap-mismatch">[eq:cap-mismatch]</a>, <a href="#eq:cap-pressure" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:cap-pressure">[eq:cap-pressure]</a>, and Lemma <a href="#lem:layer-loss" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:layer-loss">11</a>, give $$\begin{split}
 aW_f+bW_g\le{}&(n-2)\int w(x)D(x,y)\,\,\mathrm d\pi(x,y)\\
 &+\bigl(C\kappa+C_\kappa\delta
                    +C_{\kappa,\delta}P^{-1}\bigr)E
   +C_{\kappa,\delta,P}.
\end{split}$$ The constant multiplying $\kappa$ is independent of all three auxiliary parameters; the one multiplying $\delta$ is independent of $\delta,P$; and the one multiplying $P^{-1}$ is independent of $P$. First choose $\kappa$ sufficiently small, next $\delta$ sufficiently small, and finally $P$ sufficiently large, so that the relative coefficient is at most $\varepsilon$. The remaining additive constant is finite and uniform over the solution pair. This proves <a href="#eq:pressure" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:pressure">[eq:pressure]</a>. ◻

</details>

<a id="sec:conclusion"></a>
### The energy bound and the scaling contradiction

We now close the argument. Throughout this section the reductions of Section <a href="#sec:preliminaries" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:preliminaries">2</a> have been made, so $n\ge3$, $A,B>-2$, $pq>1$, and the gap $\gamma$ in Equation <a href="#eq:gap" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:gap">[eq:gap]</a> is positive.

<a id="stmt-prop-energy"></a>
<div id="prop:energy" class="proposition">

<a id="eq:energy-bound"></a>
**Proposition 12** (Uniform local energy bound). *For the fixed equation parameters there is a constant $C$, independent of the solution pair, such that $$\label{eq:energy-bound}
 \int_{B_1}\bigl(|x|^B u^{q+1}+|x|^A v^{p+1}\bigr)\,\mathrm dx
 \le E\le C.$$*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-prop-energy"><summary>Proof of stmt-prop-energy</summary>

<a id="eq:closing-errors"></a>
*Proof.* Write $$T=\int w(x)D(x,y)\,\mathrm d\pi(x,y),\qquad
 Q=\int\bigl(\Phi(x,y)-\eta(x)+w(x)D(x,y)\bigr)\,\mathrm d\pi(x,y).$$ Lemma <a href="#lem:localization" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:localization">8</a> gives, for every $\varepsilon>0$, $$\label{eq:closing-errors}
 |Q|+|E_f-E_g|\le\varepsilon E+C_\varepsilon.$$ The virial identity, Equation <a href="#eq:virial" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:virial">[eq:virial]</a>, is therefore exactly <a id="eq:closing-identity"></a>$$\label{eq:closing-identity}
\begin{gathered}
 c_1E_f+c_2E_g
   =aW_f+bW_g-(n-2)T+(n-2)Q,\\
 c_1=a(n+B)-(n-2),\quad c_2=b(n+A).
\end{gathered}$$ Proposition <a href="#prop:pressure" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:pressure">10</a> bounds the first three terms on the right from above by $\varepsilon E+C_\varepsilon$. Consequently $$c_1E_f+c_2E_g\le(n-1)\varepsilon E+C_\varepsilon.$$ Here and below the additive constant may change, but remains independent of the solution. Since $c_1+c_2=\gamma$, $$c_1E_f+c_2E_g
   =\frac{\gamma}{2}E+\frac{c_1-c_2}{2}(E_f-E_g).$$ Using Equation <a href="#eq:closing-errors" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:closing-errors">[eq:closing-errors]</a>, we obtain $$\frac{\gamma}{2}E
 \le\left(n-1+\frac{|c_1-c_2|}{2}\right)\varepsilon E+C_\varepsilon.$$ Choose $\varepsilon$ so that the coefficient of $E$ on the right is at most $\gamma/4$. This proves the universal bound on $E$. The first inequality in Equation <a href="#eq:energy-bound" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:energy-bound">[eq:energy-bound]</a> follows from $\eta=1$ on $B_1$ and the nonnegativity of the energy densities. ◻

</details>

<details id="proof-stmt-prop-energy-2"><summary>Proof of stmt-prop-energy</summary>

*Proof of Theorem <a href="#thm:main" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:main">1</a>.* The elementary reductions in Section <a href="#sec:preliminaries" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:preliminaries">2</a> already exclude $n=2$, weights at most $-2$, and $pq\le1$. Suppose a solution exists in the remaining range. For every $R>0$, the pair $$u_R(x)=R^\alpha u(Rx),\qquad v_R(x)=R^\beta v(Rx)$$ is an admissible solution with the same equation parameters. Proposition <a href="#prop:energy" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:energy">12</a> applies to every such pair with the same constant. Both energy terms have the same dilation exponent: $$\begin{aligned}
 &\int_{B_1}
   \bigl(|x|^B u_R(x)^{q+1}+|x|^A v_R(x)^{p+1}\bigr)\,\mathrm dx\\
 &\hspace{2em}
 =R^{\alpha+\beta+2-n}
   \int_{B_R}\bigl(|x|^B u(x)^{q+1}+|x|^A v(x)^{p+1}\bigr)\,\mathrm dx.
\end{aligned}$$ For $R\ge1$, the last integral is at least its fixed, strictly positive value on $B_1$. It is finite on every bounded ball. Equation <a href="#eq:gap" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:gap">[eq:gap]</a> and $1-a-b>0$ give $\alpha+\beta+2-n>0$, so the displayed expression tends to infinity as $R\to\infty$. This contradicts the uniform bound. ◻

</details>

<a id="stmt-cor-phan-classification"></a>
<div id="cor:phan-classification" class="corollary">

**Corollary 13** (Hénon–Lane–Emden classification; Phan’s Conjecture C). *Let $n\ge3$ be an integer, let $p,q>0$, and let $A,B>-2$. There is no pair $$u,v\in C^2(\mathbb R^n\setminus\{0\})\cap C(\mathbb R^n),
 \qquad u(x)>0,\quad v(x)>0\quad(x\in\mathbb R^n),$$ satisfying Equation <a href="#eq:system" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:system">[eq:system]</a> at every $x\ne0$ if and only if $$\frac{n+A}{p+1}+\frac{n+B}{q+1}>n-2.$$ When the reverse weak inequality holds, such a pair exists and can be chosen radial.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-cor-phan-classification"><summary>Proof of stmt-cor-phan-classification</summary>

*Proof.* The strict inequality gives nonexistence by Theorem <a href="#thm:main" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:main">1</a>. Suppose instead that $$\frac{n+A}{p+1}+\frac{n+B}{q+1}\le n-2.$$ Since $n+A,n+B>n-2>0$, $$\frac1{p+1}+\frac1{q+1}
 <\frac1{n-2}\left(\frac{n+A}{p+1}+\frac{n+B}{q+1}\right)
 \le1.$$ Thus $pq>1$, because $1-1/(p+1)-1/(q+1)=(pq-1)/((p+1)(q+1))$.

We now apply Proposition B of Phan (Phan 2012) <a id="pm-cite-21"></a>[[Phan](#ref-Phan)], which states the radial ground-state theorem of Bidaut-Véron and Giacomini (Bidaut-Véron and Giacomini 2010, Theorem 1.4(i)) <a id="pm-cite-22"></a>[[BidautVeronGiacomini2010](#ref-BidautVeronGiacomini2010)] for the same equation and solution class, with $n\ge3$, $A,B>-2$, and $p,q>0$. Its radial existence region is exactly the displayed reverse weak inequality, including equality on the critical hyperbola. In the original Hamiltonian notation, the substitution is $N=n$, $a=A$, $b=B$, $\delta=p$, and $\mu=q$, with both differential-operator exponents equal to $2$; the preceding calculation verifies the standing superlinearity condition $D=\delta\mu-1>0$.

The original ground states have positive radial profiles $U,V$ on $(0,\infty)$, classical there and continuous at zero. This origin regularity gives the strict positivity at zero required here: the radial equation makes $r^{n-1}U'(r)$ strictly decreasing. Its limit as $r\downarrow0$ must be zero: a nonzero or infinite limit would contradict continuity of $U$ at zero after integration against $r^{1-n}$. Hence $U'(r)<0$, and $U(0)\ge U(r)>0$ for every $r>0$. The same argument applies to $V$. Thus $u(x)=U(|x|)$ and $v(x)=V(|x|)$ belong to the stated solution class and are strictly positive on all of $\mathbb R^n$. Radial existence in this class rules out unrestricted nonexistence under the reverse weak inequality, completing the equivalence. ◻

</details>

This classification is a consequence of Theorem <a href="#thm:main" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:main">1</a> joined to the cited radial existence theorem, rather than a new construction of ground states. The complementary existence assertion is restricted to $n\ge3$ and $A,B>-2$; the broader nonexistence statement of Theorem <a href="#thm:main" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:main">1</a> is unchanged.
