## 1. Abstract and result contract

> We construct an explicit degree-five polynomial over $`\mathbf C`$ that is not a coordinate in four variables but becomes one after adjoining a single variable. This gives a counterexample to the stable coordinate conjecture in four variables. Every fiber is isomorphic to affine three-space, yet its embedding in affine four-space is not rectifiable. Thus the example also disproves the Abhyankar–Sathaye embedding conjecture in ambient dimension four.

- **claim-1** (counterexample, [source](#stmt-thm-main)): In $R=\mathbf C[x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4]$, put $$\label{eq:explicit-counterexample} Q=x_2^2-x_4^2+x_1x_3, \qquad f=x_1-2Q\bigl(Q(x_2+x_4)+x_1x_4\bigr).$$ There is a $\mathbf C$-algebra automorphism of $R[w]$ sending $f$ to $x_1$, but there is no such automorphism of $R$.
  - Explanation: The stated polynomial becomes a coordinate after adjoining an independent variable, while its original ambient polynomial ring admits no such coordinate change.
- **claim-2** (theorem, [source](#stmt-cor-fibers)): For every $\lambda\in\mathbf C$, the hypersurface $f^{-1}(\lambda)\subset\mathbb A^4$ is isomorphic to $\mathbb A^3$ and its embedding in $\mathbb A^4$ is not rectifiable.
  - Explanation: Affine-space fibers do not imply that their embeddings can be carried to coordinate hyperplanes by ambient automorphisms.
- **claim-3** (construction, [source](#stmt-prop-polynomial)): There are polynomial coordinates $p',s',F',J$ on $A$ such that $$\label{con:candidate} x=(s')^2-J^2+p'F',\qquad p=p'-2x\bigl(x(s'+J)+p'J\bigr).$$ Thus, under the identification $(p',s',F',J)=(x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4)$, the element $p$ is the polynomial $f$ in Theorem <a href="#thm:main" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:main">1</a>.
  - Explanation: A determinant-preserving substitution supplies polynomial coordinates and identifies the distinguished element with the counterexample.
- **claim-4** (construction, [source](#stmt-prop-stable)): For an independent variable $w$, the ring $A[w]$ is a polynomial ring in five variables with $p$ as one of its coordinates.
  - Explanation: An exponential substitution followed by elimination exhibits polynomial coordinates after adjoining the independent variable.
- **claim-5** (classification, [source](#stmt-prop-fibers)): For every $\lambda\in k$, the quotient $A/(p-\lambda)$ is a polynomial ring in three variables over $k$.
  - Explanation: Every scalar specialization of the distinguished element has a polynomial quotient ring.
- **claim-6** (construction, [source](#stmt-prop-graded)): The filtration on $A$ is exhaustive and separated. Its associated graded ring is the domain $$\label{gr:presentation} G:=\mathop{\mathrm{gr}}A \simeq k[p,s,u,F,J]/(H_*), \qquad H_*=x^2F-(1+2sx)J-pJ^2, \quad x=s^2-u^2+pF,$$ with the grading induced by <a href="#gr:weights" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="gr:weights">[gr:weights]</a>. The filtration degrees of $p,s,u,F,J$ in $A$ are respectively $-1,0,0,1,1$.
  - Explanation: The quotient filtration has well-defined initial forms and an integral associated graded presentation.
- **claim-7** (theorem, [source](#stmt-gr-positive-piece)): For each integer $\ell>0$, the degree-$\ell$ piece of $G$ is $$G_\ell=\tau^{-\ell}I^\ell.$$
  - Explanation: Positive homogeneous pieces are expressed using powers of the distinguished ideal.
- **claim-8** (conditional_result, [source](#stmt-prop-coordinate-obstruction)): If $p$ is a coordinate of $A$, then there exist a nonzero locally nilpotent derivation $E$ of the ring $B$ in <a href="#gr:quadric" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="gr:quadric">[gr:quadric]</a> and an element $0\ne h\in I$ such that $E(h)=0$.
  - Explanation: A hypothetical coordinate system would induce a nonzero locally nilpotent derivation on the quadric ring fixing a nonzero ideal element.
- **claim-9** (construction, [source](#stmt-bun-charts)): The map $B\longrightarrow C$ is injective. The morphism $\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}C\longrightarrow Y$ is the complement of the zero section in a line bundle $\mathcal L\longrightarrow Y$. It is trivial over the open cover $$U_0=D(z+1),\qquad U_1=D(z)$$ of $Y$, with respective invertible fiber coordinates $a$ and $d$.
  - Explanation: Explicit charts identify the larger ring with the complement of a line-bundle zero section and establish injectivity.
- **claim-10** (theorem, [source](#stmt-bun-principalization)): The extended ideal $IC$ is the principal ideal $(v)$.
  - Explanation: Pulling the distinguished ideal to the bundle complement makes it principal.
- **claim-11** (theorem, [source](#stmt-bun-homotopy)): Every line bundle on $\mathbb A^1\times Y$ is isomorphic to the pullback of its restriction to $\{0\}\times Y$.
  - Explanation: Line bundles on the affine-line product are pulled back from their restriction at zero.
- **claim-12** (conditional_result, [source](#stmt-bun-action-lift)): Let $X$ be an affine integral $k$-variety such that every line bundle on $\mathbb A^1\times X$ is the pullback of its restriction at zero. Every $\mathbb G_a$-action on $X$ lifts to any line bundle on $X$ by maps linear on fibers, commuting with fiber scaling.
  - Explanation: Under the stated line-bundle pullback condition, additive actions admit lifts that commute with fiber scaling.
- **claim-13** (conditional_result, [source](#stmt-bun-factorial-closure)): Let $R$ be a domain over a field of characteristic zero and let $\delta$ be a locally nilpotent derivation of $R$. If $r,s\in R$ are nonzero and $\delta(rs)=0$, then $\delta(r)=\delta(s)=0$.
  - Explanation: A fixed nonzero product under a locally nilpotent derivation forces both factors to be fixed.
- **claim-14** (conditional_result, [source](#stmt-prop-lift)): Let $E$ be a nonzero locally nilpotent derivation of $B$ such that $I\cap\ker E\ne0$. There is a nonzero locally nilpotent derivation $\widetilde E$ of $C$ that extends $E$, preserves fiber weights, and satisfies $\widetilde E(v)=0$.
  - Explanation: An invariant in the distinguished ideal forces a lifted weight-preserving derivation to fix its principal generator.
- **claim-15** (theorem, [source](#stmt-prop-rigidity)): There is no nonzero locally nilpotent derivation of $C=k[a,d,b,c,u]/(ac-bd-1)$ that preserves the fiber weights <a href="#bun:fiber-weights" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="bun:fiber-weights">[bun:fiber-weights]</a> and annihilates $v=a^3b+a^2u^2-d^2$.
  - Explanation: An auxiliary grading and factorial closure force an impossible negative fiber weight for a polynomial coefficient.

## 2. Definitions, validity and bounds

- **coordinate** ([source](#sec:introduction)): An element $f\in k[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$ is a *coordinate* if there are elements $f_2,\ldots,f_n$ such that $$k[x_1,\ldots,x_n]=k[f,f_2,\ldots,f_n]$$ as a polynomial ring.
- **one-stable coordinate** ([source](#sec:introduction)): It is a *one-stable coordinate* if it is a coordinate in $k[x_1,\ldots,x_n,w]$, where $w$ is independent.
- **rectifiable embedding** ([source](#sec:introduction)): A closed embedding $\mathbb A^{n-1}\hookrightarrow\mathbb A^n$ is *rectifiable* if an automorphism of the ambient affine space takes its image to a coordinate hyperplane.
- **hypersurface presentation** ([source](#sec:construction)): Let $$\label{con:presentation} \begin{gathered} P=k[p,s,u,F,J],\qquad x=s^2-u^2+pF,\\ H=x^2F-(1+2sx)J-pJ^2-u,\qquad A=P/(H). \end{gathered}$$
- **generator weights** ([source](#gr:weights)): Give the generators of $P=k[p,s,u,F,J]$ the weights $$\label{gr:weights} \begin{array}{c\|rrrrr} &p&s&u&F&J\\ \hline \text{weight}&-1&0&0&1&1. \end{array}$$
- **distinguished ideal** ([source](#gr:modification)): Define elements and an ideal of $B$ by $$\label{gr:ideal} \begin{aligned} s_B&=y-x(x+u^2),\\ f_0&=x-s_B^2+u^2,\qquad g_0=z-s_Bx,\qquad I=(f_0,g_0). \end{aligned}$$

## 3. Proof roadmap

- **stmt-prop-polynomial** [claim](#stmt-prop-polynomial) / [proof](#proof-stmt-prop-polynomial): A determinant-preserving substitution supplies polynomial coordinates and identifies the distinguished element with the counterexample. -> There are polynomial coordinates $p',s',F',J$ on $A$ such that $$\label{con:candidate} x=(s')^2-J^2+p'F',\qquad p=p'-2x\bigl(x(s'+J)+p'J\bigr).$$ Thus, under the identification $(p',s',F',J)=(x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4)$, the element $p$ is the polynomial $f$ in Theorem <a href="#thm:main" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:main">1</a>.
- **stmt-prop-stable** [claim](#stmt-prop-stable) / [proof](#proof-stmt-prop-stable): An exponential substitution followed by elimination exhibits polynomial coordinates after adjoining the independent variable. -> For an independent variable $w$, the ring $A[w]$ is a polynomial ring in five variables with $p$ as one of its coordinates.
- **stmt-prop-fibers** [claim](#stmt-prop-fibers) / [proof](#proof-stmt-prop-fibers): Every scalar specialization of the distinguished element has a polynomial quotient ring. -> For every $\lambda\in k$, the quotient $A/(p-\lambda)$ is a polynomial ring in three variables over $k$.
- **stmt-prop-graded** [claim](#stmt-prop-graded) / [proof](#proof-stmt-prop-graded): The quotient filtration has well-defined initial forms and an integral associated graded presentation. -> The filtration on $A$ is exhaustive and separated. Its associated graded ring is the domain $$\label{gr:presentation} G:=\mathop{\mathrm{gr}}A \simeq k[p,s,u,F,J]/(H_*), \qquad H_*=x^2F-(1+2sx)J-pJ^2, \quad x=s^2-u^2+pF,$$ with the grading induced by <a href="#gr:weights" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="gr:weights">[gr:weights]</a>. The filtration degrees of $p,s,u,F,J$ in $A$ are respectively $-1,0,0,1,1$.
- **stmt-gr-positive-piece** [claim](#stmt-gr-positive-piece) / [proof](#proof-stmt-gr-positive-piece): Positive homogeneous pieces are expressed using powers of the distinguished ideal. -> For each integer $\ell>0$, the degree-$\ell$ piece of $G$ is $$G_\ell=\tau^{-\ell}I^\ell.$$
- **stmt-prop-coordinate-obstruction** [claim](#stmt-prop-coordinate-obstruction) / [proof](#proof-stmt-prop-coordinate-obstruction): A hypothetical coordinate system would induce a nonzero locally nilpotent derivation on the quadric ring fixing a nonzero ideal element. -> If $p$ is a coordinate of $A$, then there exist a nonzero locally nilpotent derivation $E$ of the ring $B$ in <a href="#gr:quadric" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="gr:quadric">[gr:quadric]</a> and an element $0\ne h\in I$ such that $E(h)=0$.
- **stmt-bun-charts** [claim](#stmt-bun-charts) / [proof](#proof-stmt-bun-charts): Explicit charts identify the larger ring with the complement of a line-bundle zero section and establish injectivity. -> The map $B\longrightarrow C$ is injective. The morphism $\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}C\longrightarrow Y$ is the complement of the zero section in a line bundle $\mathcal L\longrightarrow Y$. It is trivial over the open cover $$U_0=D(z+1),\qquad U_1=D(z)$$ of $Y$, with respective invertible fiber coordinates $a$ and $d$.
- **stmt-bun-principalization** [claim](#stmt-bun-principalization) / [proof](#proof-stmt-bun-principalization): Pulling the distinguished ideal to the bundle complement makes it principal. -> The extended ideal $IC$ is the principal ideal $(v)$.
- **stmt-bun-homotopy** [claim](#stmt-bun-homotopy) / [proof](#proof-stmt-bun-homotopy): Line bundles on the affine-line product are pulled back from their restriction at zero. -> Every line bundle on $\mathbb A^1\times Y$ is isomorphic to the pullback of its restriction to $\{0\}\times Y$.
- **stmt-bun-action-lift** [claim](#stmt-bun-action-lift) / [proof](#proof-stmt-bun-action-lift): Under the stated line-bundle pullback condition, additive actions admit lifts that commute with fiber scaling. -> Let $X$ be an affine integral $k$-variety such that every line bundle on $\mathbb A^1\times X$ is the pullback of its restriction at zero. Every $\mathbb G_a$-action on $X$ lifts to any line bundle on $X$ by maps linear on fibers, commuting with fiber scaling.
- **stmt-bun-factorial-closure** [claim](#stmt-bun-factorial-closure) / [proof](#proof-stmt-bun-factorial-closure): A fixed nonzero product under a locally nilpotent derivation forces both factors to be fixed. -> Let $R$ be a domain over a field of characteristic zero and let $\delta$ be a locally nilpotent derivation of $R$. If $r,s\in R$ are nonzero and $\delta(rs)=0$, then $\delta(r)=\delta(s)=0$.
- **stmt-prop-lift** [claim](#stmt-prop-lift) / [proof](#proof-stmt-prop-lift): An invariant in the distinguished ideal forces a lifted weight-preserving derivation to fix its principal generator. -> Let $E$ be a nonzero locally nilpotent derivation of $B$ such that $I\cap\ker E\ne0$. There is a nonzero locally nilpotent derivation $\widetilde E$ of $C$ that extends $E$, preserves fiber weights, and satisfies $\widetilde E(v)=0$.
- **stmt-prop-rigidity** [claim](#stmt-prop-rigidity) / [proof](#proof-stmt-prop-rigidity): An auxiliary grading and factorial closure force an impossible negative fiber weight for a polynomial coefficient. -> There is no nonzero locally nilpotent derivation of $C=k[a,d,b,c,u]/(ac-bd-1)$ that preserves the fiber weights <a href="#bun:fiber-weights" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="bun:fiber-weights">[bun:fiber-weights]</a> and annihilates $v=a^3b+a^2u^2-d^2$.
- **stmt-thm-main** [claim](#stmt-thm-main) / [proof](#proof-stmt-prop-rigidity-2): The stated polynomial becomes a coordinate after adjoining an independent variable, while its original ambient polynomial ring admits no such coordinate change. -> In $R=\mathbf C[x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4]$, put $$\label{eq:explicit-counterexample} Q=x_2^2-x_4^2+x_1x_3, \qquad f=x_1-2Q\bigl(Q(x_2+x_4)+x_1x_4\bigr).$$ There is a $\mathbf C$-algebra automorphism of $R[w]$ sending $f$ to $x_1$, but there is no such automorphism of $R$.
- **stmt-cor-fibers** [claim](#stmt-cor-fibers) / [proof](#proof-stmt-cor-fibers): Affine-space fibers do not imply that their embeddings can be carried to coordinate hyperplanes by ambient automorphisms. -> For every $\lambda\in\mathbf C$, the hypersurface $f^{-1}(\lambda)\subset\mathbb A^4$ is isomorphic to $\mathbb A^3$ and its embedding in $\mathbb A^4$ is not rectifiable.

## 4. Engineering Triptych

### Panel 1 - epistemic contract

- **matrix-change**: The matrix substitution preserves the determinant and has a polynomial inverse.
- **stable-distinction**: The distinguished polynomial becomes a coordinate after adjoining an independent variable and is not a coordinate in the original ring.
- **fiber-distinction**: Affine-space fibers can have nonrectifiable embeddings in the ambient affine space.

### Panel 2 - negative knowledge

- **stable**: Infer original coordinate status from a coordinate system after stabilization. -> The distinguished polynomial is stable but not a coordinate in the original ring.
- **fibers**: Infer rectifiability from fibers isomorphic to affine space. -> Every fiber in the example is affine space while its embedding is not rectifiable.
- **finite**: Use substitution checks as a certificate of the full noncoordinate theorem. -> The coordinate obstruction needs the graded derivation and line-bundle rigidity argument.

### Panel 3 - reference implementation and oracle

Applicability: `constructive`. The determinant-preserving substitution and its polynomial inverse are explicit finite constructions. This check concerns their identities, not the obstruction to coordinateness.

```python
# INPUTS: JSON array of at most 300 integer tuples (p,s,u,F,J).
# PRECONDITIONS: Each tuple has length five; entries have absolute value at most 100.
# OUTPUTS: JSON records of transformed coordinates and inverse-restored coordinates.
# TIME: Linear in tuple count; fixed polynomial arithmetic per tuple, with integer bit costs.
# SPACE: Linear in tuple count and exact output bit length.
# ARITHMETIC: Exact rational arithmetic with python-flint fmpq.
# FAILURE: Invalid input raises an exception; no approximate fallback.
import json, sys
from flint import fmpq

def change(row, direction):
    p,s,u,F,J = map(fmpq, row)
    x = s*s-u*u+p*F
    aa = 1+direction*2*J*x
    bb = -direction*2*J*J
    cc = direction*2*x*x
    dd = 1-direction*2*x*J
    top = aa*F+bb*(s+u)
    right = aa*(s-u)-bb*p
    left = cc*F+dd*(s+u)
    bottom = cc*(s-u)-dd*p
    return [-bottom,(right+left)/2,(left-right)/2,top,J]

rows = json.load(sys.stdin)
assert isinstance(rows,list) and 1 <= len(rows) <= 300
answer=[]
for row in rows:
    assert len(row)==5 and all(type(v) is int and abs(v)<=100 for v in row)
    prime = change(row, -1)
    restored = change(prime, 1)
    answer.append([[str(v) for v in prime],[str(v) for v in restored]])
print(json.dumps(answer))
```

> ## Scope
>
> The stable coordinate conjecture predicts that a polynomial that becomes a coordinate after adjoining variables was already a coordinate. The formalization gives an explicit degree-five polynomial in four complex variables that becomes a coordinate after adjoining one variable but is not a coordinate in four variables. Every fiber is isomorphic to affine three-space, yet no fiber can be carried to a coordinate hyperplane by an ambient polynomial automorphism.
>
> The Abhyankar–Sathaye conjecture predicts that a polynomial defining an affine-space quotient must be an ambient coordinate. namespace OAI

namespace StableCoordinate

open MvPolynomial

abbrev R (n : ℕ) := MvPolynomial (Fin n) ℂ

noncomputable def Q : R 4 :=
  X 1 ^ 2 - X 3 ^ 2 + X 0 * X 2

noncomputable def f : R 4 :=
  X 0 - 2 * Q * (Q * (X 1 + X 3) + X 0 * X 3)

def IsCoordinate {n : ℕ} (hn : 0 < n) (g : R n) : Prop :=
  ∃ φ : R n ≃ₐ[ℂ] R n, φ g = X ⟨0, hn⟩

noncomputable def stabilize : R 4 →ₐ[ℂ] R 5 :=
  rename (Fin.castSucc : Fin 4 → Fin 5)

def IsOneStableCoordinate (g : R 4) : Prop :=
  ∃ φ : R 5 ≃ₐ[ℂ] R 5, φ (stabilize g) = X 0

noncomputable def fiberIdeal (t : ℂ) : Ideal (R 4) :=
  Ideal.span ({f - C t} : Set (R 4))

abbrev FiberRing (t : ℂ) := R 4 ⧸ fiberIdeal t

def IsAffineThreeSpaceFiber (t : ℂ) : Prop :=
  Nonempty (FiberRing t ≃ₐ[ℂ] R 3)

def IsRectifiableFiber (t : ℂ) : Prop :=
  ∃ φ : R 4 ≃ₐ[ℂ] R 4,
    Ideal.map φ.toRingHom (fiberIdeal t) = Ideal.span ({X 0} : Set (R 4))

def PolynomialPackage : Prop :=
  f.totalDegree = 5 ∧
  IsOneStableCoordinate f ∧
  ¬ IsCoordinate (by decide : 0 < 4) f

def FiberPackage : Prop :=
  ∀ t : ℂ, IsAffineThreeSpaceFiber t ∧ ¬ IsRectifiableFiber t

def PrincipalPackage : Prop := PolynomialPackage ∧ FiberPackage

## 7. Complete mathematical paper


We construct an explicit degree-five polynomial over $`\mathbf C`$ that is not a coordinate in four variables but becomes one after adjoining a single variable. This gives a counterexample to the stable coordinate conjecture in four variables. Every fiber is isomorphic to affine three-space, yet its embedding in affine four-space is not rectifiable. Thus the example also disproves the Abhyankar–Sathaye embedding conjecture in ambient dimension four.

<a id="sec:introduction"></a>
### Introduction

Let $k=\mathbf C$. An element $f\in k[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$ is a *coordinate* if there are elements $f_2,\ldots,f_n$ such that $$k[x_1,\ldots,x_n]=k[f,f_2,\ldots,f_n]$$ as a polynomial ring. Equivalently, a polynomial automorphism sends $f$ to $x_1$. It is a *one-stable coordinate* if it is a coordinate in $k[x_1,\ldots,x_n,w]$, where $w$ is independent. The stable coordinate conjecture asks whether an element that becomes a coordinate after adjoining finitely many variables was already a coordinate. The one-variable formulation appears in (Shpilrain and Yu 2002, Conjecture 3) <a id="pm-cite-1"></a>[[ShpilrainYu2002](#ref-ShpilrainYu2002)]; successive applications give the formulation with finitely many added variables. Over $\mathbf C$, the answer is affirmative in two and three variables by (Shpilrain and Yu 2002, Proposition 1.1(c)) <a id="pm-cite-2"></a>[[ShpilrainYu2002](#ref-ShpilrainYu2002)]. The case of one original variable is elementary. Thus four variables are the first possible dimension for a counterexample, and one added variable already suffices.

<a id="stmt-thm-main"></a>
<div id="thm:main" class="theorem">

<a id="eq:explicit-counterexample"></a>
**Theorem 1**. *In $R=\mathbf C[x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4]$, put $$\label{eq:explicit-counterexample}
 Q=x_2^2-x_4^2+x_1x_3,
 \qquad
 f=x_1-2Q\bigl(Q(x_2+x_4)+x_1x_4\bigr).$$ There is a $\mathbf C$-algebra automorphism of $R[w]$ sending $f$ to $x_1$, but there is no such automorphism of $R$.*

</div>

The example also has a geometric consequence. A closed embedding $\mathbb A^{n-1}\hookrightarrow\mathbb A^n$ is *rectifiable* if an automorphism of the ambient affine space takes its image to a coordinate hyperplane. The Abhyankar–Sathaye embedding conjecture asserts, in characteristic zero, that a polynomial $g$ with $k[x_1,\ldots,x_n]/(g)\simeq k^{[n-1]}$ is a coordinate; see (Blanc and Santen 2019, 8429) <a id="pm-cite-3"></a>[[BlancVanSanten2019](#ref-BlancVanSanten2019)]. Here $k^{[r]}$ denotes a polynomial ring in $r$ variables. Corollary <a href="#cor:fibers" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="cor:fibers">2</a> gives a negative answer to this conjecture in ambient dimension four. In fact, every fiber is affine three-space and its inclusion in $\mathbb A^4$ is nonrectifiable.

<a id="stmt-cor-fibers"></a>
<div id="cor:fibers" class="corollary">

**Corollary 2**. *For every $\lambda\in\mathbf C$, the hypersurface $f^{-1}(\lambda)\subset\mathbb A^4$ is isomorphic to $\mathbb A^3$ and its embedding in $\mathbb A^4$ is not rectifiable.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-cor-fibers"><summary>Proof of stmt-cor-fibers</summary>

*Proof.* Proposition <a href="#prop:fibers" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:fibers">5</a> identifies each fiber with $\mathbb A^3$; in particular, $(f-\lambda)$ is its prime defining ideal. If an ambient automorphism takes $f^{-1}(\lambda)$ to a coordinate hyperplane, it takes its defining principal ideal to the ideal of a coordinate. The two generators therefore differ by a nonzero constant. This would make $f-\lambda$, and hence $f$, a coordinate, contrary to Theorem <a href="#thm:main" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:main">1</a>. ◻

</details>

<a id="pm-h-3"></a>
#### History and related methods

The coordinate question retains the embedding in affine space: it asks whether a given function can be made into a coordinate projection by an ambient automorphism. Cancellation asks instead whether an affine variety whose product with an affine line is an affine space must itself be an affine space. Makar-Limanov, van Rossum, Shpilrain, and Yu established an implication from the stable coordinate conjecture to cancellation in (Makar-Limanov et al. 2004, Theorem 1.2) <a id="pm-cite-4"></a>[[MakarLimanovRossumShpilrainYu2004](#ref-MakarLimanovRossumShpilrainYu2004)]. In our example the ambient variety is already $\mathbb A^4$, and the obstruction concerns its distinguished polynomial.

The low-dimensional proof in (Shpilrain and Yu 2002, proof of Proposition 1.1(c)) <a id="pm-cite-5"></a>[[ShpilrainYu2002](#ref-ShpilrainYu2002)] uses curve cancellation and the Abhyankar–Moh embedding theorem in two variables. In three variables it combines affine-surface cancellation, using work of Fujita and Miyanishi–Sugie, with Kaliman’s theorem that a polynomial on $\mathbf C^3$ with general fiber $\mathbf C^2$ is a coordinate (Kaliman 2002, Main Theorem) <a id="pm-cite-6"></a>[[Kaliman2002](#ref-Kaliman2002)].

The four-variable Vénéreau polynomials have been an important testing ground for these coordinate questions. Lewis proved that the second Vénéreau polynomial is a coordinate (Lewis 2013) <a id="pm-cite-7"></a>[[Lewis2013](#ref-Lewis2013)]. Blanc and Poloni developed a description in terms of affine bundles and bivariables, revisited that result, and proved one-stability for a broader family of related polynomials (Blanc and Poloni 2022, Proposition 2.3) <a id="pm-cite-8"></a>[[BlancPoloni2022](#ref-BlancPoloni2022)]. These results illustrate the distinction between exhibiting polynomial coordinates after stabilization and proving that a polynomial cannot occur in any original coordinate system. For the polynomial in Theorem <a href="#thm:main" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:main">1</a>, the first problem is settled by explicit substitutions; the second requires a derivation obstruction.

<a id="pm-h-4"></a>
#### The construction and the obstruction

The proof uses two presentations of the same four-variable polynomial ring. We first write it as a hypersurface $A=P/(H)$ in a five-variable polynomial ring $P$, with a distinguished element $p$. A determinant-preserving matrix transformation makes $H$ linear in one new coordinate. It identifies $A$ with $R$ and carries $p$ to the polynomial in <a href="#eq:explicit-counterexample" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:explicit-counterexample">[eq:explicit-counterexample]</a>. A second construction, using the exponential of a locally nilpotent derivation, shows that $p$ belongs to polynomial coordinates on $A[w]$. These explicit changes of coordinates occupy Section <a href="#sec:construction" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:construction">2</a>.

To show that $p$ is not a coordinate, we put an integer-indexed filtration on $A$. Its associated graded ring has the form $$G=B[\tau,I/\tau]\subset B[\tau,\tau^{-1}],
 \qquad B=k[x,y,z,u]/(xy-z(z+1)),$$ where $I$ is an explicitly defined two-generated ideal of $B$ and $\tau$ is the initial form of $p$. The notation $B[\tau,I/\tau]$ means the subalgebra generated by $B$, $\tau$, and all elements $h/\tau$ with $h\in I$. If $p$ were a coordinate, differentiation in a suitable other coordinate would induce a nonzero locally nilpotent derivation $E$ on $B$ whose kernel contains a nonzero element of $I$. Section <a href="#sec:filtration" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:filtration">3</a> proves this implication, including the care needed for negative filtration degrees.

The last two sections exclude that invariant. Section <a href="#sec:bundle" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:bundle">4</a> passes from $\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}B$ to the complement of the zero section in a line bundle. In its coordinate ring $$C=k[a,d,b,c,u]/(ac-bd-1),$$ the ideal $I$ becomes principal, generated by $v=a^3b+a^2u^2-d^2$. The additive action defined by $E$ lifts to this bundle and commutes with fiber scaling. A nonzero invariant in $I$ would force the lifted derivation to annihilate $v$. In Section <a href="#sec:rigidity" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:rigidity">5</a>, a second grading reduces this to a derivation fixing $a,d,u$. Such a derivation has a coefficient that would simultaneously have to be a polynomial in $a,d,u$ and have negative fiber weight. This is impossible.

The graded-ring and line-bundle strategy adapts the construction in (OpenAI 2026, secs. 2–6) <a id="pm-cite-9"></a>[[Cancellation2026](#ref-Cancellation2026)]. That paper studies an affine-space cancellation example. Here the quadratic choice in the defining equation has two different uses: it permits the determinant-preserving change that makes $A$ polynomial, and it makes the highest auxiliary part of $v$ factor as $(au-d)(au+d)$. The latter factorization gives the final rigidity argument without a polynomial $abc$ estimate. The passage to leading derivations follows the filtered methods of Kaliman and Makar-Limanov (Kaliman and Makar-Limanov 2007, secs. 3–5) <a id="pm-cite-10"></a>[[KalimanMakarLimanov2007](#ref-KalimanMakarLimanov2007)], and the lift of an additive action uses the line-bundle normalization argument of Brion (Brion 2015, Lemma 2.9) <a id="pm-cite-11"></a>[[Brion2015](#ref-Brion2015)]. Every construction and obstruction needed for Theorem <a href="#thm:main" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:main">1</a> is proved below.

<a id="sec:construction"></a>
### Polynomial presentation and stabilization

We construct the counterexample through a hypersurface presentation of a four-variable polynomial ring. This presentation makes stabilization explicit and will also supply the filtration used to obstruct coordinateness. It adapts the construction of (OpenAI 2026, sec. 2) <a id="pm-cite-12"></a>[[Cancellation2026](#ref-Cancellation2026)], with quadratic terms that permit the polynomial change of coordinates below.

Let <a id="con:presentation"></a>$$\label{con:presentation}
 \begin{gathered}
 P=k[p,s,u,F,J],\qquad x=s^2-u^2+pF,\\
 H=x^2F-(1+2sx)J-pJ^2-u,\qquad A=P/(H).
 \end{gathered}$$ The five generators of $P$ are independent. We use the same letters for their images in $A$; the distinguished element is $p\in A$.

<a id="pm-h-6"></a>
#### A polynomial change of coordinates

<a id="stmt-prop-polynomial"></a>
<div id="prop:polynomial" class="proposition">

<a id="con:candidate"></a>
**Proposition 3**. *There are polynomial coordinates $p',s',F',J$ on $A$ such that $$\label{con:candidate}
 x=(s')^2-J^2+p'F',\qquad
 p=p'-2x\bigl(x(s'+J)+p'J\bigr).$$ Thus, under the identification $(p',s',F',J)=(x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4)$, the element $p$ is the polynomial $f$ in Theorem <a href="#thm:main" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:main">1</a>.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-prop-polynomial"><summary>Proof of stmt-prop-polynomial</summary>

<a id="con:matrix-change"></a>
*Proof.* The relation $H$ becomes linear after a change of coordinates preserving $x$. To exhibit that change, write $$M=\begin{pmatrix}F&s-u\\s+u&-p\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 e=\binom{x}{-J},\qquad n=\binom{J}{x}.$$ Then $x=-\det M$, $e^{\mathsf t}n=0$, and $$H=e^{\mathsf t}Me-J-u.$$ Keep $J$ fixed and define $F',s',u',p'$ by $$\label{con:matrix-change}
 M'=(I_2-2ne^{\mathsf t})M
   =\begin{pmatrix}F'&s'-u'\\s'+u'&-p'\end{pmatrix}.$$ Since $(ne^{\mathsf t})^2=0$ and $\det(I_2-2ne^{\mathsf t})=1$, we have $-\det M'=x$. Consequently $x$ and $J$, and hence $e$ and $n$, are unchanged by the substitution. Its inverse is the polynomial substitution $$M=(I_2+2ne^{\mathsf t})M',\qquad x=-\det M',$$ so $p',s',u',F',J$ form a coordinate system on $P$.

Taking half the difference of the off-diagonal entries in Equation <a href="#con:matrix-change" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="con:matrix-change">[con:matrix-change]</a> gives $$u'=u-e^{\mathsf t}Me,\qquad H=-J-u'.$$ Eliminating $u'$ therefore identifies $A$ with $k[p',s',F',J]$. In this quotient $u'=-J$, so the determinant identity gives the first formula in Equation <a href="#con:candidate" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="con:candidate">[con:candidate]</a>. The lower-right entry of the inverse matrix identity gives $$p=p'-2x\bigl(x(s'-u')+p'J\bigr),$$ which becomes the second formula after substituting $u'=-J$. ◻

</details>

The expression for $p$ in Equation <a href="#con:candidate" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="con:candidate">[con:candidate]</a> takes the values $0$ and $1$ at $(p',s',F',J)=(0,0,0,0)$ and $(1,0,0,0)$, respectively. In particular, $p$ is a nonzero nonunit of $A$.

<a id="pm-h-7"></a>
#### One-variable stabilization

<a id="con:quadric-identity"></a>
<a id="con:localized-coordinates"></a>
<a id="con:localized-inverse"></a>
We next change the defining equation after adjoining one variable, while keeping $p$ fixed. For this purpose set <a id="con:xyz"></a>$$\label{con:xyz}
 y=s+x(x+u^2),\qquad z=sx+pJ.$$ Expansion gives the identity $$\label{con:quadric-identity}
 xy-z(z+1)=p(H+u).$$ Moreover, $$\label{con:localized-coordinates}
 P[p^{-1}]=k[p,p^{-1}][x,y,z,u],$$ with inverse coordinate formulas $$\label{con:localized-inverse}
 s=y-x(x+u^2),\qquad
 F=\frac{x-s^2+u^2}{p},\qquad
 J=\frac{z-sx}{p}.$$

<a id="stmt-prop-stable"></a>
<div id="prop:stable" class="proposition">

**Proposition 4**. *For an independent variable $w$, the ring $A[w]$ is a polynomial ring in five variables with $p$ as one of its coordinates.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-prop-stable"><summary>Proof of stmt-prop-stable</summary>

*Proof.* A derivation $\delta$ of a $k$-algebra is *locally nilpotent* if, for every element $g$, some iterate $\delta^r(g)$ is zero. We use such a derivation to replace the equation $H=0$ by $H+pw=0$.

<a id="con:stabilized-hypersurface"></a>
In the coordinates of Equation <a href="#con:localized-coordinates" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="con:localized-coordinates">[con:localized-coordinates]</a>, take $$\Delta=-p\frac{\partial}{\partial u},
 \qquad \Delta(p)=\Delta(x)=\Delta(y)=\Delta(z)=0.$$ This locally nilpotent derivation of $P[p^{-1}]$ preserves $P$, because Equation <a href="#con:localized-inverse" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="con:localized-inverse">[con:localized-inverse]</a> yields <a id="con:delta"></a>$$\label{con:delta}
 \begin{gathered}
 \Delta(p)=0,\qquad \Delta(u)=-p,\qquad \Delta(s)=2pxu,\\
 \Delta(F)=-4sxu-2u,\qquad \Delta(J)=-2x^2u.
 \end{gathered}$$ Its restriction to $P$ is therefore locally nilpotent as well. Applying $\Delta$ to Equation <a href="#con:quadric-identity" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="con:quadric-identity">[con:quadric-identity]</a> gives $\Delta(H)=p$. Extend $\Delta$ to $P[w]$ by $\Delta(w)=0$. The exponential $$\exp(w\Delta)(g)=\sum_{r\geq0}\frac{w^r\Delta^r(g)}{r!}$$ is a polynomial automorphism: each sum is finite, and its inverse is $\exp(-w\Delta)$. It fixes $p$ and sends $H$ to $H+pw$, inducing a $k[p]$-algebra isomorphism $$\label{con:stabilized-hypersurface}
 A[w]\simeq P[w]/(H+pw).$$

<a id="con:linear-coordinates"></a>
<a id="con:H-expansion"></a>
It remains to eliminate one variable from the equation on the right. Put $x_0=s^2-u^2$ and introduce $$\label{con:linear-coordinates}
 \begin{aligned}
 L&=x_0^2F-(1+2sx_0)J,\\
 N&=(1-2sx_0)F+4s^2J.
 \end{aligned}$$ The coefficient matrix of this substitution has determinant $4s^2x_0^2+(1+2sx_0)(1-2sx_0)=1$; explicitly, <a id="con:linear-inverse"></a>$$\label{con:linear-inverse}
 F=4s^2L+(1+2sx_0)N,\qquad
 J=-(1-2sx_0)L+x_0^2N.$$ Thus $p,s,u,L,N$ are polynomial coordinates on $P$. Expanding $x=x_0+pF$ in $H$ gives $$\label{con:H-expansion}
 H=L-u+pQ_0,\qquad Q_0=2x_0F^2+pF^3-2sFJ-J^2.$$ Since $Q_0\in P$, the substitution $w'=w+Q_0$ is another polynomial change of coordinates, and $$P[w]/(H+pw)
 =k[p,s,u,L,N,w']/(L-u+pw')
 \simeq k[p,s,u,N,w'].$$ Together with Equation <a href="#con:stabilized-hypersurface" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="con:stabilized-hypersurface">[con:stabilized-hypersurface]</a>, this proves the assertion with $p$ fixed throughout. Composing with the identification in Proposition <a href="#prop:polynomial" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:polynomial">3</a> and relabeling the target variables so that $p$ maps to $x_1$ gives an automorphism of $R[w]$ sending $f$ to $x_1$. ◻

</details>

The two steps explicitly realize the exponential shift of (Dutta and Lahiri 2021, Lemma 3.3) <a id="pm-cite-13"></a>[[DuttaLahiri2021](#ref-DuttaLahiri2021)] and the case of the transfer construction in (Edo and Vénéreau 2001, Theorem 7) <a id="pm-cite-14"></a>[[EdoVenereau2001](#ref-EdoVenereau2001)] in which the perturbation $pQ_0$ is divisible by $p$.

#### The fibers

The same coordinate computations describe every fiber of $p$. This will give a geometric consequence once noncoordinateness has been proved.

<a id="stmt-prop-fibers"></a>
<div id="prop:fibers" class="proposition">

**Proposition 5**. *For every $\lambda\in k$, the quotient $A/(p-\lambda)$ is a polynomial ring in three variables over $k$.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-prop-fibers"><summary>Proof of stmt-prop-fibers</summary>

*Proof.* At $\lambda=0$, Equations <a href="#con:linear-coordinates" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="con:linear-coordinates">[con:linear-coordinates]</a>–<a href="#con:H-expansion" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="con:H-expansion">[con:H-expansion]</a> give $$A/(p)=k[s,u,L,N]/(L-u)=k[s,u,N].$$ For $\lambda\ne0$, Equations <a href="#con:localized-coordinates" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="con:localized-coordinates">[con:localized-coordinates]</a> and <a href="#con:quadric-identity" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="con:quadric-identity">[con:quadric-identity]</a> give $$A/(p-\lambda)
 =k[x,y,z,u]/\bigl(xy-z(z+1)-\lambda u\bigr)
 \simeq k[x,y,z],$$ by elimination of $u$. ◻

</details>

Propositions <a href="#prop:polynomial" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:polynomial">3</a> and <a href="#prop:stable" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:stable">4</a> establish the polynomial presentation and the stable-coordinate assertion of Theorem <a href="#thm:main" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:main">1</a>. The remaining task is to show that $p$ cannot be a coordinate of $A$ itself.

<a id="sec:filtration"></a>
### A graded obstruction to coordinateness

We now turn to the assertion that $p$ is not a coordinate of $A$. The first step is to pass to a weighted degeneration of $A$. If $p$ were a coordinate, differentiation in another coordinate would then produce a nonzero locally nilpotent derivation of a simpler quadric ring, with a nonzero invariant in an explicit ideal. The rest of the proof will exclude that possibility. The use of leading derivations follows the filtration methods of (Kaliman and Makar-Limanov 2007, secs. 3–5) <a id="pm-cite-15"></a>[[KalimanMakarLimanov2007](#ref-KalimanMakarLimanov2007)]; the particular quadric construction is adapted from (OpenAI 2026, secs. 3–5) <a id="pm-cite-16"></a>[[Cancellation2026](#ref-Cancellation2026)].

<a id="pm-h-10"></a>
#### The associated graded ring

<a id="gr:weights"></a>
Give the generators of $P=k[p,s,u,F,J]$ the weights $$\label{gr:weights}
\begin{array}{c|rrrrr}
  &p&s&u&F&J\\ \hline
 \text{weight}&-1&0&0&1&1.
\end{array}$$ For $d\in\mathbb Z$, let $\mathcal F_dP$ be the span of the monomials of weight at most $d$, and give $A=P/(H)$ the quotient filtration $\mathcal F_dA=\operatorname{im}(\mathcal F_dP\to A)$. These are increasing multiplicative filtrations, indexed by all integers. Their negative indices are essential: $p$ has weight $-1$.

<a id="stmt-prop-graded"></a>
<div id="prop:graded" class="proposition">

<a id="gr:presentation"></a>
**Proposition 6**. *The filtration on $A$ is exhaustive and separated. Its associated graded ring is the domain $$\label{gr:presentation}
 G:=\mathop{\mathrm{gr}}A
 \simeq k[p,s,u,F,J]/(H_*),
 \qquad
 H_*=x^2F-(1+2sx)J-pJ^2,
 \quad x=s^2-u^2+pF,$$ with the grading induced by <a href="#gr:weights" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="gr:weights">[gr:weights]</a>. The filtration degrees of $p,s,u,F,J$ in $A$ are respectively $-1,0,0,1,1$.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-prop-graded"><summary>Proof of stmt-prop-graded</summary>

*Proof.* Exhaustiveness follows because every element of $A$ has a polynomial representative. For $r>0$, every monomial of weight at most $-r$ is divisible by $p^r$, so $$\mathcal F_{-r}A\subseteq p^rA.$$ By Proposition <a href="#prop:polynomial" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:polynomial">3</a>, $A$ is a polynomial ring, and Equation <a href="#con:candidate" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="con:candidate">[con:candidate]</a> shows that $p$ is a nonzero nonunit. Therefore $\bigcap_{r>0}p^rA=0$: in a unique factorization domain, a nonzero element cannot be divisible by arbitrarily high powers of an irreducible factor of $p$. This proves separation. In particular, every nonzero $a\in A$ has a well-defined degree $$\deg a=\min\{d\in\mathbb Z:a\in\mathcal F_dA\}$$ and a nonzero initial form in $\mathcal F_dA/\mathcal F_{d-1}A$.

The element $x$ is homogeneous of weight zero in $P$, and $H=H_*-u$ has highest weight part $H_*$, of weight one. For every nonzero $q\in P$, the highest weight part of $qH$ is $(\operatorname{in}q)H_*$: the polynomial ring is a graded domain, so the product of the highest parts does not vanish. Consequently the ideal of initial relations is exactly $(H_*)$. For completeness, the natural graded map $P\to\mathop{\mathrm{gr}}A$ sends a homogeneous polynomial of weight $d$ to its class in $\mathcal F_dA/\mathcal F_{d-1}A$, which may at this stage be zero. This map is surjective. A homogeneous polynomial of weight $d$ lies in its kernel exactly when its image in $A$ has a representative of weight at most $d-1$; subtracting that representative gives a relation with the original polynomial as its highest part. Thus the kernel is the ideal of initial relations, proving <a href="#gr:presentation" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="gr:presentation">[gr:presentation]</a>.

To prove that this quotient is a domain, use the polynomial coordinates $x,y,z,u$ of $P[p^{-1}]$ from the preceding section. The identity $xy-z(z+1)=p(H+u)$ becomes $$pH_*=xy-z(z+1).$$ The polynomial on the right is irreducible over $k[p,p^{-1},x,z,u]$: as a polynomial in $y$, it is primitive, because $x$ and $z(z+1)$ are coprime, and it has degree one over the fraction field. Thus $H_*$ is irreducible after inverting $p$. It is not divisible by $p$ before localization, since its reduction modulo $p$ is $$(s^2-u^2)^2F-\bigl(1+2s(s^2-u^2)\bigr)J\ne0.$$ Any factorization of $H_*$ in $P$ has a factor that becomes a unit after inverting $p$, hence is a scalar times a power of $p$. Since $p\nmid H_*$, this factor is already a unit in $P$. Thus $H_*$ is irreducible in $P$, and hence prime by unique factorization. Finally, $H_*$ is not associated to any of the five variables, so their classes in $G$ are nonzero. These classes are the initial forms of the corresponding elements of $A$, proving the asserted degrees. ◻

</details>

<a id="pm-h-11"></a>
#### A quadric and its distinguished ideal

<a id="gr:quadric"></a>
<a id="gr:localization"></a>
From now on write $\tau$ for the class of $p$ in $G$, to distinguish the graded ring from $A$. Consider the quadric ring $$\label{gr:quadric}
 B=k[x,y,z,u]/\bigl(xy-z(z+1)\bigr).$$ The formulas $$x=s^2-u^2+\tau F,\qquad
 y=s+x(x+u^2),\qquad z=sx+\tau J$$ define elements of degree zero in $G$. They satisfy the relation in <a href="#gr:quadric" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="gr:quadric">[gr:quadric]</a>; after inverting $\tau$, the inverse coordinate formulas from the preceding section give an isomorphism $$\label{gr:localization}
 G[\tau^{-1}]=B[\tau,\tau^{-1}],
 \qquad \deg B=0,\quad \deg\tau=-1.$$ In particular, the induced map $B\to G$ is injective: its composition with localization is the inclusion of $B$ in this Laurent polynomial ring. We henceforth regard $B$ as a subring of $G$.

<a id="gr:modification"></a>
Define elements and an ideal of $B$ by <a id="gr:ideal"></a>$$\label{gr:ideal}
 \begin{aligned}
 s_B&=y-x(x+u^2),\\
 f_0&=x-s_B^2+u^2,\qquad
 g_0=z-s_Bx,\qquad I=(f_0,g_0).
 \end{aligned}$$ In <a href="#gr:localization" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="gr:localization">[gr:localization]</a> we have $s=s_B$, $F=f_0/\tau$, and $J=g_0/\tau$. These identities give the more precise description $$\label{gr:modification}
 G=B[\tau,I/\tau]
   =B[\tau,f_0/\tau,g_0/\tau]
   \subset B[\tau,\tau^{-1}].$$ This is the algebraic construction of an affine modification, with divisor $\tau=0$ and center $(\tau,I)$ in $B[\tau]$; see (Kaliman and Zaidenberg 1999, Definition 1.1 and Proposition 1.1(b)) <a id="pm-cite-17"></a>[[KalimanZaidenberg1999](#ref-KalimanZaidenberg1999)]. We will only need its explicit homogeneous pieces.

<a id="stmt-gr-positive-piece"></a>
<div id="gr:positive-piece" class="lemma">

**Lemma 7**. *For each integer $\ell>0$, the degree-$\ell$ piece of $G$ is $$G_\ell=\tau^{-\ell}I^\ell.$$*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-gr-positive-piece"><summary>Proof of stmt-gr-positive-piece</summary>

*Proof.* A monomial in the generators in <a href="#gr:modification" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="gr:modification">[gr:modification]</a>, with coefficient in $B$, has the form $$b\,\tau^r(f_0/\tau)^i(g_0/\tau)^j,
 \qquad r,i,j\ge0.$$ It has degree $\ell$ exactly when $i+j-r=\ell$. In that case it equals $\tau^{-\ell}b f_0^i g_0^j$, whose numerator belongs to $I^{\ell+r}\subseteq I^\ell$. Since the presentation is homogeneous, such monomials span $G_\ell$. Conversely, every element of $I^\ell$ is a $B$-linear combination of the products $f_0^i g_0^j$ with $i+j=\ell$, and dividing these products by $\tau^\ell$ gives elements of $G_\ell$. ◻

</details>

<a id="pm-h-12"></a>
#### What a coordinate would force

The ideal $I$ records the positive part of the grading. We now show why a hypothetical coordinate system containing $p$ supplies a nonzero locally nilpotent derivation of $B$ fixing a nonzero element of $I$.

<a id="stmt-prop-coordinate-obstruction"></a>
<div id="prop:coordinate-obstruction" class="proposition">

**Proposition 8**. *If $p$ is a coordinate of $A$, then there exist a nonzero locally nilpotent derivation $E$ of the ring $B$ in <a href="#gr:quadric" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="gr:quadric">[gr:quadric]</a> and an element $0\ne h\in I$ such that $E(h)=0$.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-prop-coordinate-obstruction"><summary>Proof of stmt-prop-coordinate-obstruction</summary>

*Proof.* Suppose $A=k[p,q_1,q_2,q_3]$ for a polynomial coordinate system. At least one $q_i$ has strictly positive filtration degree. Indeed, if all three had degree at most zero, then so would every polynomial in $p,q_1,q_2,q_3$, contrary to $\deg F=1$. Relabel so that $\deg q_1>0$, and take $$D=\frac{\partial}{\partial q_2}.$$ This is a nonzero locally nilpotent derivation of $A$ satisfying $D(p)=D(q_1)=0$.

<a id="gr:shift"></a>
<a id="gr:iterate"></a>
We give the details of its passage to $G$, because the filtration has both positive and negative indices. Write $\omega(t)$ for the assigned weight of a generator $t\in\{p,s,u,F,J\}$, and put $$\label{gr:shift}
 m=\max_{D(t)\ne0}\bigl(\deg D(t)-\omega(t)\bigr).$$ This maximum is defined: a nonzero derivation cannot kill all algebra generators. The product rule shows that a monomial of weight at most $d$ has derivative in $\mathcal F_{d+m}A$. Every element of $\mathcal F_dA$ has a representative that is a sum of such monomials, by the definition of the quotient filtration. Thus $$D(\mathcal F_dA)\subseteq\mathcal F_{d+m}A
 \qquad(d\in\mathbb Z).$$ It follows that $D$ induces a homogeneous derivation $D_0$ of $G$ of degree $m$. For $a\in\mathcal F_dA$, write $[a]_d$ for its class in $\mathcal F_dA/\mathcal F_{d-1}A$. Explicitly, $$\label{gr:iterate}
 D_0([a]_d)=[D(a)]_{d+m},\qquad
 D_0^r([a]_d)=[D^r(a)]_{d+rm}\quad(r\ge0).$$ The first formula is well-defined because $D$ also sends $\mathcal F_{d-1}A$ into $\mathcal F_{d+m-1}A$; the second follows by induction. It proves that $D_0$ is locally nilpotent, first on homogeneous elements and then on finite sums of them. Moreover, equality is attained in <a href="#gr:shift" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="gr:shift">[gr:shift]</a> for some generator $t$, whose initial form has degree $\omega(t)$ by Proposition <a href="#prop:graded" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:graded">6</a>. Its image under $D_0$ is the nonzero initial form of $D(t)$, so $D_0\ne0$. Finally, <a href="#gr:iterate" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="gr:iterate">[gr:iterate]</a> shows that $D_0$ kills both $\tau$ and the nonzero initial form $\eta$ of $q_1$. The latter is homogeneous of degree $\ell=\deg q_1>0$.

<a id="gr:descent"></a>
Because $D_0(\tau)=0$, extending $D_0$ to $G[\tau^{-1}]=B[\tau,\tau^{-1}]$ preserves local nilpotence. Homogeneity implies that there is a derivation $E:B\to B$ with $$\label{gr:descent}
 D_0(b)=\tau^{-m}E(b)\qquad(b\in B).$$ Indeed, every degree-$m$ element of the Laurent ring is uniquely $\tau^{-m}$ times an element of $B$. The derivation $E$ is nonzero: otherwise $D_0$ would kill both $B$ and $\tau$, hence the whole Laurent ring and its subring $G$. It is locally nilpotent because $$E^r(b)=\tau^{rm}D_0^r(b)
 \qquad(b\in B, r\ge0).$$ Here, as in <a href="#gr:iterate" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="gr:iterate">[gr:iterate]</a>, the formula follows by induction, using that $D_0$ fixes $\tau$.

By Lemma <a href="#gr:positive-piece" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="gr:positive-piece">7</a>, write $\eta=\tau^{-\ell}h$ with $0\ne h\in I^\ell$. Equations <a href="#gr:descent" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="gr:descent">[gr:descent]</a> and $D_0(\eta)=0$ give $$0=D_0(\eta)=\tau^{-m-\ell}E(h).$$ Thus $E(h)=0$, and $I^\ell\subseteq I$ because $\ell>0$. ◻

</details>

Proposition <a href="#prop:coordinate-obstruction" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:coordinate-obstruction">8</a> has removed the original coordinate system from the problem. It remains to show that no nonzero locally nilpotent derivation of $B$ can have a nonzero invariant in $I$. We will make this ideal principal after pulling it back to the complement of the zero section in a line bundle, where a second grading gives the contradiction.

<a id="sec:bundle"></a>
### Lifting the obstruction to a line bundle

Proposition <a href="#prop:coordinate-obstruction" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:coordinate-obstruction">8</a> reduces noncoordinateness to excluding a nonzero locally nilpotent derivation $E$ of $B$ with a nonzero invariant in $I=(f_0,g_0)$. We pull $I$ back to the complement of the zero section in a line bundle over $\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}B$; on this complement the ideal becomes principal. Every additive action on the base lifts linearly to the line bundle and preserves this complement. Consequently, an invariant in $I$ will force the lifted action to fix one specific polynomial. This use of the quadric and its line bundle adapts the construction of (OpenAI 2026, secs. 4–6) <a id="pm-cite-18"></a>[[Cancellation2026](#ref-Cancellation2026)]; all the lifting and principalization arguments needed here are proved below.

<a id="bun:base-map"></a>
<a id="bun:fiber-weights"></a>
Put $Y=\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}B$ and define <a id="bun:C"></a>$$\label{bun:C}
 C=k[a,d,b,c,u]/(ac-bd-1).$$ The assignment $$\label{bun:base-map}
 x=ab,\qquad y=dc,\qquad z=db,\qquad u=u$$ defines a homomorphism $B\longrightarrow C$, since $xy=abdc=(db)(ac)=z(z+1)$. The ring $C$ is a domain: the polynomial $ac-bd-1$, viewed as a primitive linear polynomial in $c$ over $k[a,d,b,u]$, is irreducible. We give $C$ the *fiber weights* $$\label{bun:fiber-weights}
 \operatorname{wt}(a,d,b,c,u)=(1,1,-1,-1,0).$$ The relation is homogeneous of weight zero, and the image of $B$ has weight zero. These weights describe the action of $\mathbb G_m$ that scales the fibers of the following bundle. Geometrically this is the familiar quotient of $\mathrm{SL}_2$ by a diagonal torus, times the affine $u$-line; see (Blanc and Santen 2019, 8431) <a id="pm-cite-19"></a>[[BlancVanSanten2019](#ref-BlancVanSanten2019)]. We use explicit charts throughout.

<a id="stmt-bun-charts"></a>
<div id="bun:charts" class="lemma">

**Lemma 9**. *The map $B\longrightarrow C$ is injective. The morphism $\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}C\longrightarrow Y$ is the complement of the zero section in a line bundle $\mathcal L\longrightarrow Y$. It is trivial over the open cover $$U_0=D(z+1),\qquad U_1=D(z)$$ of $Y$, with respective invertible fiber coordinates $a$ and $d$.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-bun-charts"><summary>Proof of stmt-bun-charts</summary>

*Proof.* Over $U_0$, the identity $ac=z+1$ makes $a$ invertible. The formulas <a id="bun:chart-a"></a>$$\label{bun:chart-a}
 c=\frac{z+1}{a},\qquad b=\frac{x}{a},\qquad
 d=\frac{ay}{z+1}$$ give an isomorphism $C[(z+1)^{-1}]=B[(z+1)^{-1}][a,a^{-1}]$. In particular the localized map from $B$ is injective, and so is the original map because $B$ is a domain. Over $U_1$, the corresponding formulas are <a id="bun:chart-d"></a>$$\label{bun:chart-d}
 b=\frac{z}{d},\qquad c=\frac{y}{d},\qquad a=\frac{xd}{z},$$ and give $C[z^{-1}]=B[z^{-1}][d,d^{-1}]$. The opens cover $Y$ because $z$ and $z+1$ generate the unit ideal. On their overlap the fiber coordinates satisfy <a id="bun:transition"></a>$$\label{bun:transition}
 d=\frac{y}{z+1}\,a,$$ where $y/(z+1)$ is a unit with inverse $x/z$. Gluing the trivial line bundles by this transition gives $\mathcal L$; removing their zero sections gives precisely the two charts of $\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}C$ above. Both fiber coordinates have weight one, so the scaling agrees with <a href="#bun:fiber-weights" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="bun:fiber-weights">[bun:fiber-weights]</a>. ◻

</details>

The two generators of $I$ acquire a common factor in $C$. Define <a id="bun:v"></a>$$\label{bun:v}
 v=a^2(x+u^2)-d^2=a^3b+a^2u^2-d^2\in C.$$

<a id="stmt-bun-principalization"></a>
<div id="bun:principalization" class="lemma">

**Lemma 10**. *The extended ideal $IC$ is the principal ideal $(v)$.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-bun-principalization"><summary>Proof of stmt-bun-principalization</summary>

*Proof.* In $C[a^{-1}]$, put $\xi=d/a$ and $j=x+u^2-\xi^2$. The determinant relation and <a href="#bun:base-map" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="bun:base-map">[bun:base-map]</a> give $$z=\xi x,\qquad y=\xi+\xi^2x,\qquad
 s_B=y-x(x+u^2)=\xi-xj.$$ Consequently <a id="bun:ideal-chart"></a>$$\label{bun:ideal-chart}
 f_0=(1+2\xi x-x^2j)j,\qquad g_0=x^2j,\qquad v=a^2j.$$ The two coefficients of $j$ generate the unit ideal, as the identity $$(1-2\xi x)(1+2\xi x-x^2j)
   +x^2(4\xi^2+j-2\xi xj)=1$$ shows. Thus $IC[a^{-1}]=(j)=(v)$.

It remains to check primes of $C$ containing $a$. Modulo such a prime, $bd=-1$, while $x=0$ and $z=-1$. Hence $g_0=z-s_Bx$ has nonzero residue $-1$, and $v$ has nonzero residue $-d^2$. Both are units in the corresponding local ring. The ideals $IC$ and $(v)$ therefore agree at every prime, which proves the assertion. ◻

</details>

We next lift additive actions from $Y$ to $\mathcal L$. The elementary reason that a lift exists is that adjoining an affine-line parameter does not change line bundles on $Y$.

<a id="stmt-bun-homotopy"></a>
<div id="bun:homotopy" class="lemma">

**Lemma 11**. *Every line bundle on $\mathbb A^1\times Y$ is isomorphic to the pullback of its restriction to $\{0\}\times Y$.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-bun-homotopy"><summary>Proof of stmt-bun-homotopy</summary>

*Proof.* The two charts of Lemma <a href="#bun:charts" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="bun:charts">9</a> have coordinate rings $$\begin{aligned}
 B[(z+1)^{-1}]&=k[x,t,u,(xt+1)^{-1}],
       &t&=\frac{y}{z+1},\quad z=xt,\\
 B[z^{-1}]&=k[x,t,u,(xt-1)^{-1}],
       &t&=\frac{y}{z},\quad z=xt-1.
\end{aligned}$$ Here $t$ denotes a separate coordinate on each chart. Each ring, and its polynomial extension by the affine-line coordinate $T$, is a noetherian unique factorization domain. A line bundle on the spectrum of such a ring is trivial: its divisor class is represented by a Cartier divisor, and unique factorization makes every divisor principal.

Let $\mathcal M$ be a line bundle on $\mathbb A^1\times Y$. Choose trivializations on $\mathbb A^1\times U_0$ and $\mathbb A^1\times U_1$. Its transition function belongs to $B[z^{-1},(z+1)^{-1}][T]^*$. A polynomial over a domain is a unit only when it is a constant unit, so this transition function is independent of $T$. Its specialization at $T=0$ is therefore the same transition function. The chosen trivializations identify $\mathcal M$ with the pullback of its restriction at zero. ◻

</details>

The lifting argument has a useful general form. Its normalization step is the standard one for linearizing a line bundle under an additive action; compare (Brion 2015, Lemma 2.9) <a id="pm-cite-20"></a>[[Brion2015](#ref-Brion2015)].

<a id="stmt-bun-action-lift"></a>
<div id="bun:action-lift" class="lemma">

**Lemma 12**. *Let $X$ be an affine integral $k$-variety such that every line bundle on $\mathbb A^1\times X$ is the pullback of its restriction at zero. Every $\mathbb G_a$-action on $X$ lifts to any line bundle on $X$ by maps linear on fibers, commuting with fiber scaling.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-bun-action-lift"><summary>Proof of stmt-bun-action-lift</summary>

*Proof.* Write $R=k[X]$, let $\mathcal M$ be the line bundle, and let $\alpha:\mathbb A^1\times X\longrightarrow X$ be the action. If $\pi:\mathbb A^1\times X\longrightarrow X$ is projection, then $\alpha^*\mathcal M$ and $\pi^*\mathcal M$ restrict to $\mathcal M$ at parameter zero. The hypothesis therefore supplies an isomorphism $$\eta:\pi^*\mathcal M\longrightarrow\alpha^*\mathcal M.$$ At zero, $\eta$ is an automorphism of $\mathcal M$, hence multiplication by a unit of $R$. Multiplying $\eta$ by the inverse pullback of that unit normalizes it to the identity at zero.

The isomorphism $\eta$ gives a fiberwise linear lift of $\alpha$. To check the group law, compare, over $\mathbb A^2\times X$, the map on fibers obtained by applying parameters $S$ and then $T$ with the map obtained by applying $T+S$. Both are global isomorphisms of the same source and target line bundles, with fiber maps from $\mathcal M_x$ to $\mathcal M_{\alpha(T+S,x)}$. Their ratio is an automorphism of the source line bundle. Its endomorphism sheaf is canonically $\mathcal O$, so this automorphism is multiplication by a global unit in $R[T,S]$. Since $R$ is a domain, this unit is independent of $T,S$. At $(T,S)=(0,0)$ it equals one by the normalization of $\eta$. Thus the group law holds. The resulting action is linear on fibers, so it commutes with fiber scaling and preserves the complement of the zero section. ◻

</details>

We will also use the following elementary property of locally nilpotent derivations; see (Kaliman and Makar-Limanov 2007, Proposition 2.1(3)) <a id="pm-cite-21"></a>[[KalimanMakarLimanov2007](#ref-KalimanMakarLimanov2007)].

<a id="stmt-bun-factorial-closure"></a>
<div id="bun:factorial-closure" class="lemma">

**Lemma 13**. *Let $R$ be a domain over a field of characteristic zero and let $\delta$ be a locally nilpotent derivation of $R$. If $r,s\in R$ are nonzero and $\delta(rs)=0$, then $\delta(r)=\delta(s)=0$.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-bun-factorial-closure"><summary>Proof of stmt-bun-factorial-closure</summary>

*Proof.* The exponential $\exp(T\delta)$ takes values in $R[T]$. If $rs$ is fixed, then $$\exp(T\delta)(r)\,\exp(T\delta)(s)=rs.$$ Degrees in $T$ add under multiplication of nonzero polynomials over a domain. Both factors on the left therefore have degree zero, and their coefficients of $T$ give the assertion. ◻

</details>

<a id="stmt-prop-lift"></a>
<div id="prop:lift" class="proposition">

**Proposition 14**. *Let $E$ be a nonzero locally nilpotent derivation of $B$ such that $I\cap\ker E\ne0$. There is a nonzero locally nilpotent derivation $\widetilde E$ of $C$ that extends $E$, preserves fiber weights, and satisfies $\widetilde E(v)=0$.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-prop-lift"><summary>Proof of stmt-prop-lift</summary>

*Proof.* The exponential of $E$ defines an additive-group action on $Y$. By Lemmas <a href="#bun:homotopy" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="bun:homotopy">11</a> and <a href="#bun:action-lift" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="bun:action-lift">12</a>, it lifts to $\mathcal L$ by fiberwise linear maps. Restrict to the complement of the zero section, namely $\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}C$. Differentiating this action at the identity gives a derivation $\widetilde E$ of $C$. The action law expresses its pullback on each function as $\sum_{r\ge0}T^r\widetilde E^{\,r}/r!$; because the action is algebraic, the sum is finite for each function. Hence $\widetilde E$ is locally nilpotent. It extends $E$ and is nonzero, and commutation with fiber scaling says exactly that it preserves the weights <a href="#bun:fiber-weights" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="bun:fiber-weights">[bun:fiber-weights]</a>.

Finally choose $0\ne h\in I\cap\ker E$. The inclusion $B\subset C$ and Lemma <a href="#bun:principalization" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="bun:principalization">10</a> give a factorization $h=vq$ in $C$, with both factors nonzero. Since $\widetilde E(h)=0$, Lemma <a href="#bun:factorial-closure" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="bun:factorial-closure">13</a> yields $\widetilde E(v)=0$. ◻

</details>

<a id="sec:rigidity"></a>
### Rigidity and the coordinate obstruction

Proposition <a href="#prop:lift" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:lift">14</a> converts the hypothetical invariant in $I$ into a locally nilpotent derivation of $C$ that fixes $v$ and preserves fiber weights. We now rule out such a derivation. An auxiliary grading isolates the difference of squares $a^2u^2-d^2$ inside $v$. Its two factors force three generators to be invariant, leaving a coefficient whose required fiber weight is impossible.

<a id="stmt-prop-rigidity"></a>
<div id="prop:rigidity" class="proposition">

**Proposition 15**. *There is no nonzero locally nilpotent derivation of $C=k[a,d,b,c,u]/(ac-bd-1)$ that preserves the fiber weights <a href="#bun:fiber-weights" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="bun:fiber-weights">[bun:fiber-weights]</a> and annihilates $v=a^3b+a^2u^2-d^2$.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-prop-rigidity"><summary>Proof of stmt-prop-rigidity</summary>

<a id="rig:gradings"></a>
*Proof.* Suppose that $\widetilde E$ is such a derivation. Give $C$ the auxiliary grading in the second row of the following table: $$\label{rig:gradings}
\begin{array}{c|rrrrr}
 &a&d&b&c&u\\ \hline
 \text{fiber weight}&1&1&-1&-1&0\\
 \text{auxiliary degree}&0&1&-1&0&1
\end{array}$$ The two gradings are compatible because $ac-bd-1$ is homogeneous of degree zero for each. The auxiliary degrees of the two parts of $v$ are $$\deg_{\mathrm{aux}}(a^3b)=-1,\qquad
 \deg_{\mathrm{aux}}(a^2u^2-d^2)=2.$$

Decompose $\widetilde E$ into its homogeneous components for auxiliary degree shift, and let $E'$ be the nonzero component with largest shift, say $m$. There are only finitely many components: a derivation is determined by its values on the five homogeneous generators, and each value is a finite sum of homogeneous terms. Each component is a derivation by the product rule. Moreover, $E'$ is locally nilpotent. Indeed, for homogeneous $r\in C$ of auxiliary degree $e$, the component of auxiliary degree $e+nm$ in $\widetilde E^{\,n}(r)$ is $(E')^n(r)$. For sufficiently large $n$ the former expression vanishes, so the latter vanishes as well. Homogeneous elements span $C$, proving local nilpotence on every element. Since the gradings in <a href="#rig:gradings" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="rig:gradings">[rig:gradings]</a> are compatible, $E'$ still preserves fiber weights.

<a id="rig:fixed"></a>
<a id="rig:a-chart"></a>
Taking the component of degree $m+2$ in $\widetilde E(v)=0$ gives $$E'(a^2u^2-d^2)=0.$$ The factorization $$a^2u^2-d^2=(au-d)(au+d)$$ and Lemma <a href="#bun:factorial-closure" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="bun:factorial-closure">13</a> imply that $E'$ annihilates both $au-d$ and $au+d$. Thus $E'(d)=E'(au)=0$, and another application of that lemma gives $$\label{rig:fixed}
 E'(a)=E'(d)=E'(u)=0.$$ All the factors used here are nonzero; for example, after inverting $a$ one has $$\label{rig:a-chart}
 C[a^{-1}]=k[a,a^{-1},d,u][b],\qquad c=\frac{1+bd}{a},$$ where their nonvanishing is immediate.

<a id="rig:q"></a>
It remains to control the possible values of $E'$ on $b,c$. Differentiating $ac-bd=1$ and using <a href="#rig:fixed" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="rig:fixed">[rig:fixed]</a> yields $aE'(c)=dE'(b)$. Define $$q=cE'(b)-bE'(c)\in C.$$ Multiplication by $a$ and $d$, respectively, then gives $$\label{rig:q}
 E'(b)=aq,\qquad E'(c)=dq.$$ The element $q$ is nonzero, since otherwise $E'$ would kill every generator. We claim that $q$ belongs to the polynomial subring $k[a,d,u]$.

The elements $a,d,u$ are algebraically independent by <a href="#rig:a-chart" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="rig:a-chart">[rig:a-chart]</a>. Put $K=k(a,d,u)$ and localize $C$ at the nonzero elements of $k[a,d,u]$. Because these elements lie in $\ker E'$, the localized derivation remains locally nilpotent. The resulting algebra is $K[b]$, with $c=(1+bd)/a$. A locally nilpotent $K$-derivation of $K[b]$ sends $b$ into $K$: if its value $P(b)$ had degree at least one, iterating $P(b)\,d/db$ on $b$ would never give zero. More explicitly, differentiation of a nonconstant polynomial multiplies its nonzero leading coefficient by its positive degree, and multiplication by $P$ then leaves the result nonconstant. Equation <a href="#rig:q" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="rig:q">[rig:q]</a> therefore gives $q\in K$.

To compute $C\cap K$ inside the fraction field of $C$, use both coordinate charts: $$C[a^{-1}]=k[a,a^{-1},d,u][b],\qquad
 C[d^{-1}]=k[a,d,d^{-1},u][c].$$ An element of the first polynomial ring lying in $K$ has no $b$ terms, and an element of the second lying in $K$ has no $c$ terms. Hence $$C\cap K\subset
 k[a,a^{-1},d,u]\cap k[a,d,d^{-1},u]=k[a,d,u].$$ The last equality follows from unique factorization in $k[a,d,u]$: a reduced denominator that is both a power of $a$ and a power of $d$ is a unit. The reverse inclusion is immediate, proving the claim.

Finally, preservation of fiber weights makes $E'(b)$ have weight $-1$. By <a href="#rig:q" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="rig:q">[rig:q]</a>, the nonzero polynomial $q$ has weight $-2$. This is impossible in $k[a,d,u]$, whose generators have weights $1,1,0$. The contradiction proves the proposition. ◻

</details>

<details id="proof-stmt-prop-rigidity-2"><summary>Proof of stmt-prop-rigidity</summary>

*Proof of Theorem <a href="#thm:main" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:main">1</a>.* By Proposition <a href="#prop:polynomial" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:polynomial">3</a>, the algebra $A$ is a polynomial ring in four variables and its element $p$ becomes the polynomial $f$ stated in the theorem. Proposition <a href="#prop:stable" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:stable">4</a> makes $p$ a coordinate of $A[w]$. If $p$ were a coordinate of $A$, Proposition <a href="#prop:coordinate-obstruction" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:coordinate-obstruction">8</a> would produce a nonzero locally nilpotent derivation $E$ of $B$ with $I\cap\ker E\ne0$. Proposition <a href="#prop:lift" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:lift">14</a> would then supply a derivation forbidden by Proposition <a href="#prop:rigidity" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:rigidity">15</a>. Thus $p$ is not a coordinate of $A$. Transporting these two properties through the polynomial presentation proves the assertion for $f$. ◻

</details>
