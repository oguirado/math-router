## 1. Abstract and result contract

> For every odd positive integer <i>N</i>, every irreducible component of the full two-adic Hecke algebra of level $`\Gamma_1(N)`$ has Krull dimension four. This proves the <i>p</i> = 2 case of Emerton's dimension conjecture, including all residual components.

- **C1** (theorem, [source](#stmt-thm-main)): For every odd positive integer $N$, every irreducible component of $\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}T_2(N)$ has dimension exactly $4$.
  - Explanation: Every component of the full Hecke algebra has Krull dimension four, including components with reducible or scalar residual systems.
- **C2** (classification, [source](#stmt-lem-density)): The ring $A$ is compact, Hausdorff, and reduced. Its classical points are Zariski dense, and the operators $T_\ell,\ell S_\ell$, $\ell\nmid2N$, topologically generate it over $\mathbb Z_2$.
  - Explanation: Finite-stage embeddings establish reducedness and density; the inverse-limit topology supplies compactness and topological generation.
- **C3** (construction, [source](#stmt-lem-determinant)): There is a continuous two-dimensional determinant over $A$ whose trace and determinant satisfy $$t(\mathop{\mathrm{Frob}}_\ell)=T_\ell,\qquad d(\mathop{\mathrm{Frob}}_\ell)=\ell S_\ell\qquad(\ell\nmid2N).$$
  - Explanation: Classical specializations supply a determinant law over the integral algebra without division by two.
- **C4** (classification, [source](#stmt-prop-noetherian)): The ring $A$ is a finite product of complete Noetherian local rings with finite residue fields. Its given topology on each factor is the maximal-ideal-adic topology.
  - Explanation: Finiteness of residual systems and universal determinant deformation rings give complete Noetherian local factors and their adic topologies.
- **C5** (bound, [source](#stmt-thm-lower)): For the algebra $A$ in <a href="#eq:algebra" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:algebra">[eq:algebra]</a>, every irreducible component of $\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}A$ has dimension at least $4$.
  - Explanation: The infinite fern supplies the lower dimension bound for the full algebra of modular forms.
- **C6** (bound, [source](#stmt-prop-exceptional)): The closure of the classical points of bounded weight has dimension at most $1$. The closure of all Eisenstein points has dimension at most $2$.
  - Explanation: Finite-stage quotients bound the bounded-weight locus; Eisenstein interpolation into a power series ring bounds the Eisenstein locus.
- **C7** (construction, [source](#stmt-cor-classical-point)): For every minimal prime $P$ of $A$, there is a classical cuspidal map $\lambda:A\to\mathcal O_E$ of weight at least $3$ such that $P\subseteq\mathfrak x:=\ker\lambda$ and $\mathfrak x$ belongs to no other irreducible component of $\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}A$.
  - Explanation: Density outside the exceptional loci provides a cuspidal point belonging to the chosen component and to no other component.
- **C8** (bound, [source](#stmt-prop-tangent)): Let $R$ be a compact Hausdorff Noetherian topological $\mathbb Z_p$-algebra, with continuous structure map $\mathbb Z_p\to R$. Suppose a profinite group $H$ has a continuous two-dimensional determinant over $R$, with trace $t$ and determinant $d$, and that their values topologically generate $R$ over $\mathbb Z_p$. Let $\lambda:R\to\mathcal O_E$ be a continuous $\mathbb Z_p$-algebra map, where $E/\mathbb Q_p$ is finite, and suppose its specialized determinant is that of an absolutely irreducible continuous representation $r:H\to\mathop{\mathrm{GL}}_2(E)$. For $\mathfrak x=\ker\lambda$, $$\label{eq:tangent-bound} \dim R_{\mathfrak x}\leq\mathop{\mathrm{edim}}R_{\mathfrak x} \leq\dim_E H^1(H,\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}r).$$
  - Explanation: Continuous characteristic-zero derivations and determinant lifts bound local embedding dimension by full adjoint cohomology.
- **C9** (theorem, [source](#stmt-lem-global-selmer)): For the representation <a href="#eq:r" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:r">[eq:r]</a>, $H^1_f(\mathbb Q,\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}r)=0$.
  - Explanation: Global finite adjoint Selmer vanishing applies to the cuspidal representation; odd level excludes the forbidden cyclotomic CM fields.
- **C10** (theorem, [source](#stmt-lem-local-invariants)): For every finite place $v$, $H^0(\mathbb Q_v,V^*(1))=0$, where $V=\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}r$.
  - Explanation: Hodge–Tate weight separation and local genericity rule out twisted local invariants.
- **C11** (bound, [source](#stmt-prop-cohomology)): For the representation $r$ of a cuspidal newform of weight at least $3$ and odd level dividing $N$, $$\dim_E H^1(G_{\mathbb Q,S},\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}r)\leq3.$$
  - Explanation: Global Selmer vanishing makes restriction injective, and the local quotient at two bounds the global cohomology dimension.

## 2. Definitions, validity and bounds

- **Modular form spaces** ([source](#eq:algebra)): For $i\geq1$, let $M_i(N)$ be the complex vector space of modular forms of weight $i$ and level $\Gamma_1(N)$, including Eisenstein series.
- **Hecke and diamond operators** ([source](#eq:algebra)): For every prime $\ell\nmid N$, write $T_\ell$ for the usual Hecke operator and let $S_\ell$ act on $M_i(N)$ as $\ell^{i-2}\langle\ell\rangle$, where $\langle\ell\rangle$ is the diamond operator.
- **Finite-stage Hecke algebra** ([source](#eq:algebra)): Let $$T_{\leq k}^{(2)}(N) \subseteq \mathop{\mathrm{End}}_{\mathbb C}\!\left(\bigoplus_{i=1}^k M_i(N)\right)$$ be the $\mathbb Z$-algebra generated by $T_\ell$ and $\ell S_\ell$ for $\ell\nmid2N$.
- **Full two-adic Hecke algebra** ([source](#eq:algebra)): Set $$\label{eq:algebra} A_k=\mathbb Z_2\otimes_{\mathbb Z}T_{\leq k}^{(2)}(N), \qquad A=T_2(N)=\varprojlim_k A_k,$$ with transition maps given by restriction.
- **Inverse-limit topology** ([source](#eq:algebra)): The topology on $A$ is the inverse-limit topology, with the $2$-adic topology on each $A_k$.
- **Classical point** ([source](#pm-h-6)): A *classical point* of $\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}A$ means the kernel of a classical eigenvalue map $A\to\mathcal O_E$ obtained from some finite stage.
- **Trace and determinant of a determinant law** ([source](#pm-h-7)): For a determinant $D$, its trace and determinant on a group element $g$ are defined by $D(Z-g)=Z^2-t(g)Z+d(g)$.
- **Full adjoint representation** ([source](#sec:tangent)): For a representation $r$, the notation $\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}r$ means $\mathop{\mathrm{End}}_E(E^2)$ with the conjugation action, not its trace-zero subspace.
- **Global finite Selmer group** ([source](#sec:selmer)): The global group $H^1_f(\mathbb Q,V)$ consists of classes satisfying these conditions at every finite place.

## 3. Proof roadmap

- **R1** [claim](#stmt-lem-density) / [proof](#proof-stmt-lem-density): Finite-stage embeddings and inverse limits -> Finite-stage embeddings establish reducedness and density; the inverse-limit topology supplies compactness and topological generation.
- **R2** [claim](#stmt-lem-determinant) / [proof](#proof-stmt-lem-determinant): Classical determinants and polynomial identities -> Classical specializations supply a determinant law over the integral algebra without division by two.
- **R3** [claim](#stmt-prop-noetherian) / [proof](#proof-stmt-prop-noetherian): Universal determinant deformation rings -> Finiteness of residual systems and universal determinant deformation rings give complete Noetherian local factors and their adic topologies.
- **R4** [claim](#stmt-thm-lower) / [proof](#stmt-thm-lower): Infinite fern lower bound -> The infinite fern supplies the lower dimension bound for the full algebra of modular forms.
- **R5** [claim](#stmt-prop-exceptional) / [proof](#proof-stmt-prop-exceptional): Compact graph interpolation and Strassmann’s theorem -> Finite-stage quotients bound the bounded-weight locus; Eisenstein interpolation into a power series ring bounds the Eisenstein locus.
- **R6** [claim](#stmt-cor-classical-point) / [proof](#proof-stmt-cor-classical-point): Density outside exceptional loci and other components -> Density outside the exceptional loci provides a cuspidal point belonging to the chosen component and to no other component.
- **R7** [claim](#stmt-prop-tangent) / [proof](#proof-stmt-prop-tangent): Continuous derivations and characteristic-zero determinant lifting -> Continuous characteristic-zero derivations and determinant lifts bound local embedding dimension by full adjoint cohomology.
- **R8** [claim](#stmt-lem-global-selmer) / [proof](#proof-stmt-lem-global-selmer): Global finite Selmer vanishing -> Global finite adjoint Selmer vanishing applies to the cuspidal representation; odd level excludes the forbidden cyclotomic CM fields.
- **R9** [claim](#stmt-lem-local-invariants) / [proof](#proof-stmt-lem-local-invariants): Hodge–Tate weights and generic Weil–Deligne parameters -> Hodge–Tate weight separation and local genericity rule out twisted local invariants.
- **R10** [claim](#stmt-prop-cohomology) / [proof](#proof-stmt-prop-cohomology): Local Euler characteristics and injective global restriction -> Global Selmer vanishing makes restriction injective, and the local quotient at two bounds the global cohomology dimension.
- **R11** [claim](#stmt-thm-main) / [proof](#proof-stmt-prop-cohomology-2): Catenary dimension formula and matching dimension bounds -> Every component of the full Hecke algebra has Krull dimension four, including components with reducible or scalar residual systems.

## 4. Engineering Triptych

### Panel 1 - epistemic contract

- **I-level**: Every irreducible component of the full two-adic Hecke algebra at odd positive level has Krull dimension four.
- **I-adjoint**: The adjoint cohomology bound uses the full endomorphism representation with conjugation action.
- **I-point**: Every component admits a classical cuspidal point of weight at least three lying on no other component.
- **I-tangent**: The tangent estimate requires an absolutely irreducible characteristic-zero specialization of the determinant.
- **I-arithmetic**: The component dimension argument includes the arithmetic dimension of the classical quotient.

### Panel 2 - negative knowledge

- **A-level**: Apply the component-dimension conclusion to even level. -> Odd level is used to ensure the CM field does not lie in the cyclotomic extension required by Selmer vanishing.
- **A-adjoint**: Replace the full adjoint representation by its trace-zero subspace while retaining the same cohomology bound. -> Changing the coefficient representation changes the deformation problem and the local dimension calculation.
- **A-tangent**: Use the tangent estimate at a reducible characteristic-zero specialization. -> The lifting and trace-detection argument requires absolute irreducibility at the specialized point.
- **A-integral**: Recover the integral determinant by dividing the trace identity by two. -> Two need not be invertible in the integral coefficient ring.
- **A-dimension**: Identify the localized tangent bound with the dimension of the whole component. -> The dimension formula also includes the classical arithmetic quotient.

### Panel 3 - reference implementation and oracle

Applicability: `not_applicable`. The result concerns every irreducible component of an inverse-limit Hecke algebra across all weights. Its proof uses density of classical points, a cuspidal point on each component, external Selmer vanishing, and a catenary dimension formula. These do not supply a finite constructive procedure for computing the components or certifying their dimensions from finite input. The finite-stage embeddings and quadratic determinant formula support the proof but do not turn the component theorem into such a procedure.

<a id="sec:introduction"></a>
### Introduction

The congruences between classical modular forms of different weights assemble their Hecke eigenvalues into a single $p$-adic algebra. Its spectrum is larger than an individual family of eigenforms: many such families can pass through the same classical point. The dimension of this spectrum measures how much variation is present when no slope or residual irreducibility condition is imposed.

<a id="eq:algebra"></a>
Fix an odd positive integer $N$. For $i\geq1$, let $M_i(N)$ be the complex vector space of modular forms of weight $i$ and level $\Gamma_1(N)$, including Eisenstein series. For every prime $\ell\nmid N$, write $T_\ell$ for the usual Hecke operator and let $S_\ell$ act on $M_i(N)$ as $\ell^{i-2}\langle\ell\rangle$, where $\langle\ell\rangle$ is the diamond operator. Let $$T_{\leq k}^{(2)}(N)
 \subseteq \mathop{\mathrm{End}}_{\mathbb C}\!\left(\bigoplus_{i=1}^k M_i(N)\right)$$ be the $\mathbb Z$-algebra generated by $T_\ell$ and $\ell S_\ell$ for $\ell\nmid2N$. Set $$\label{eq:algebra}
 A_k=\mathbb Z_2\otimes_{\mathbb Z}T_{\leq k}^{(2)}(N),
 \qquad A=T_2(N)=\varprojlim_k A_k,$$ with transition maps given by restriction. The topology on $A$ is the inverse-limit topology, with the $2$-adic topology on each $A_k$. All dimensions below are Krull dimensions.

<a id="stmt-thm-main"></a>
<div id="thm:main" class="theorem">

**Theorem 1**. *For every odd positive integer $N$, every irreducible component of $\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}T_2(N)$ has dimension exactly $4$.*

</div>

This is the $p=2$ case of Emerton’s Conjecture 2.9 (Emerton 2011) <a id="pm-cite-1"></a>[[Emerton11](#ref-Emerton11)]. The algebra in <a href="#eq:algebra" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:algebra">[eq:algebra]</a> retains all weights, all Eisenstein systems, and all residual systems of eigenvalues. In particular, the theorem does not require a residual Galois representation to be irreducible, nonscalar, or distinguished at $2$.

<a id="pm-h-3"></a>
#### History and the main input

Hida’s ordinary families (Hida 1986) <a id="pm-cite-2"></a>[[Hida86](#ref-Hida86)] and the eigencurve of Coleman and Mazur (Coleman and Mazur 1998) <a id="pm-cite-3"></a>[[ColemanMazur98](#ref-ColemanMazur98)] give fundamental constructions of $p$-adic families of modular eigenforms. Gouvêa and Mazur’s infinite fern (Gouvêa and Mazur 1998) <a id="pm-cite-4"></a>[[GouveaMazur98](#ref-GouveaMazur98)] explains how families intersect and produce higher-dimensional Zariski closures. Emerton’s account (Emerton 2011) <a id="pm-cite-5"></a>[[Emerton11](#ref-Emerton11)] formulates the dimension question for the full prime-to-$Np$ Hecke algebra, without restricting to one residual deformation problem. His Corollary 2.28 gives the lower bound needed here: every irreducible component has dimension at least $4$.

The complementary upper bound is a deformation-theoretic question. Mazur’s deformation theory (Mazur 1989) <a id="pm-cite-6"></a>[[Mazur89](#ref-Mazur89)] relates infinitesimal deformations of a Galois representation to its adjoint cohomology. For automorphic representations, the finite local conditions of Bloch and Kato (Bloch and Kato 1990) <a id="pm-cite-7"></a>[[BlochKato90](#ref-BlochKato90)] isolate a Selmer group within this cohomology. Under suitable hypotheses, Kisin (Kisin 2004) <a id="pm-cite-8"></a>[[Kisin04](#ref-Kisin04)] proved geometric adjoint Selmer vanishing and applied it to local deformation and eigencurve geometry. Allen (Allen 2016) <a id="pm-cite-9"></a>[[Allen16](#ref-Allen16)] established adjoint Selmer vanishing under residual hypotheses and related local genericity to unobstructed deformations. Newton and Thorne (Newton and Thorne 2023) <a id="pm-cite-10"></a>[[NewtonThorne23](#ref-NewtonThorne23)] subsequently proved the vanishing theorem used here without the residual restrictions that would obstruct its application to all components at $2$.

We use their Theorem 5.4 for classical cuspidal forms over $\mathbb Q$. The theorem includes $p=2$ and also includes a CM form provided its CM field is not contained in the cyclotomic $2$-power extension. Odd level guarantees this last condition. The result concerns the *full* adjoint representation, of dimension four, which is the one required when the determinant is allowed to vary.

<a id="pm-h-4"></a>
#### Method and organization

The proof works at characteristic-zero points, even on components whose residual systems are reducible or scalar. There are three steps. First, the integral algebra is a finite product of complete Noetherian local rings, and its classical points are Zariski dense. At $2$ we justify Noetherianity using Chenevier’s determinant laws (Chenevier 2014) <a id="pm-cite-11"></a>[[Chenevier14](#ref-Chenevier14)], avoiding division by $2$ in the integral pseudorepresentation. Second, the Eisenstein locus has dimension at most $2$. Together with Emerton’s lower bound, this supplies a classical cuspidal point of weight at least $3$ on every component. Third, the tangent space at such a point has dimension at most $3$: the Newton–Thorne theorem annihilates the global finite Selmer group, and the only nonzero local quotient has dimension $3$ at $2$. A dimension formula then adds the one-dimensional arithmetic quotient.

Two passages in this argument are useful beyond the immediate application. Section <a href="#sec:eisenstein" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:eisenstein">3</a> constructs the Eisenstein interpolation maps directly from the inverse-limit Hecke algebra by a compact graph argument. Section <a href="#sec:tangent" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:tangent">4</a> gives a characteristic-zero tangent bound for a compact Noetherian algebra carrying a two-dimensional determinant. In particular, it proves the continuity of the derivations that occur in its algebraic tangent space. Neither passage assumes residual irreducibility.

We supply these arguments in detail. The deep external inputs are the classical attachment and local compatibility of Galois representations, Emerton’s lower bound, Chenevier’s determinant theorems, and Newton–Thorne’s Selmer vanishing. Their relevant forms and hypotheses are stated where they are used.

<a id="sec:integral"></a>
### The integral algebra and its Galois determinant

Our first goal is to put the inverse limit <a href="#eq:algebra" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:algebra">[eq:algebra]</a> in the category of Noetherian rings while retaining all of its classical systems. Write $S$ for the set of finite primes dividing $2N$, and let $G=G_{\mathbb Q,S}$ be the Galois group of the maximal extension of $\mathbb Q$ unramified at finite primes outside $S$. Ramification at infinity is allowed. All Galois cohomology will be continuous cohomology.

<a id="pm-h-6"></a>
#### Finite stages and classical systems

We use the classical rationality, integrality, and simultaneous semisimplicity of the prime-to-level Hecke action; a convenient statement including weight one and Eisenstein forms is (Emerton 2011, Proposition 1.12 and Remark 1.13) <a id="pm-cite-12"></a>[[Emerton11](#ref-Emerton11)]. For cusp forms, old copies have the same good-prime eigenvalues as their associated newforms. On the Eisenstein subspace the eigenvalues are sums and products of Dirichlet character values and powers of primes.

<a id="eq:finite-embedding"></a>
Consequently, for every nonzero finite stage there is an embedding $$\label{eq:finite-embedding}
 A_k\lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow
 \prod_{j=1}^{m_k}\mathcal O_{E_{k,j}},$$ where each $E_{k,j}/\mathbb Q_2$ is finite and the coordinate maps are classical eigenvalue systems, with all necessary $2$-adic embeddings included. Indeed, before tensoring with $\mathbb Z_2$ the algebra embeds into a finite product of rings of integers of number fields. It is therefore finite and torsion-free over $\mathbb Z$; flatness of $\mathbb Z_2$ preserves the embedding. Thus $A_k$ is finite free over $\mathbb Z_2$, and its image in <a href="#eq:finite-embedding" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:finite-embedding">[eq:finite-embedding]</a> is closed. Zero initial stages, if present, can be discarded.

A *classical point* of $\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}A$ means the kernel of a classical eigenvalue map $A\to\mathcal O_E$ obtained from some finite stage. The following consequences will be used repeatedly.

<a id="stmt-lem-density"></a>
<div id="lem:density" class="lemma">

**Lemma 2**. *The ring $A$ is compact, Hausdorff, and reduced. Its classical points are Zariski dense, and the operators $T_\ell,\ell S_\ell$, $\ell\nmid2N$, topologically generate it over $\mathbb Z_2$.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-density"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-density</summary>

*Proof.* The restriction maps between finite-stage algebras are surjective: the generators at the smaller stage are restrictions of the same generators at the larger stage. Compactness and Hausdorffness follow from <a href="#eq:algebra" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:algebra">[eq:algebra]</a>. Reducedness follows from the injections <a href="#eq:finite-embedding" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:finite-embedding">[eq:finite-embedding]</a>. Those injections also show that the intersection of all classical kernels in $A$ is zero, which is equivalent to the asserted density. Finally, the $\mathbb Z_2$-algebra of polynomials in the indicated generators surjects onto each $A_k$, and is therefore dense in the inverse limit. ◻

</details>

<a id="eq:frobenius"></a>
For every classical system, the theorems of Deligne and Deligne–Serre (Deligne 1971; Deligne and Serre 1974) <a id="pm-cite-13"></a>[[Deligne71](#ref-Deligne71), [DeligneSerre74](#ref-DeligneSerre74)] give a continuous two-dimensional representation of $G$ over a finite extension of $\mathbb Q_2$; in the Eisenstein case one uses a direct sum of characters. Choose the Frobenius convention so that its characteristic polynomial at $\ell\nmid2N$ is $$\label{eq:frobenius}
 Z^2-T_\ell Z+\ell S_\ell$$ after specialization. A stable lattice ensures integral traces and determinants at every element of $G$.

<a id="pm-h-7"></a>
#### An integral determinant law

A two-dimensional *determinant* over a commutative ring $R$ is a unital multiplicative homogeneous polynomial law of degree two $R[G]\to R$, compatible with extension of scalars; see (Chenevier 2014, sec. 1) <a id="pm-cite-14"></a>[[Chenevier14](#ref-Chenevier14)]. For a determinant $D$, its trace and determinant on a group element $g$ are defined by $D(Z-g)=Z^2-t(g)Z+d(g)$. This notion works over rings in which $2$ is not invertible.

<a id="stmt-lem-determinant"></a>
<div id="lem:determinant" class="lemma">

**Lemma 3**. *There is a continuous two-dimensional determinant over $A$ whose trace and determinant satisfy $$t(\mathop{\mathrm{Frob}}_\ell)=T_\ell,\qquad
 d(\mathop{\mathrm{Frob}}_\ell)=\ell S_\ell\qquad(\ell\nmid2N).$$*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-determinant"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-determinant</summary>

*Proof.* At a fixed finite stage, take the tuple of traces and determinants of its classical Galois representations. The tuple belongs to the image of <a href="#eq:finite-embedding" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:finite-embedding">[eq:finite-embedding]</a> on Frobenius conjugacy classes, by <a href="#eq:frobenius" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:frobenius">[eq:frobenius]</a>. The union of these classes is dense in $G$ by Chebotarev. The tuples are continuous class functions, and the image of <a href="#eq:finite-embedding" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:finite-embedding">[eq:finite-embedding]</a> is closed. Hence the tuples belong to $A_k$ at every $g\in G$. Uniqueness on the dense union of Frobenius classes makes these functions compatible as $k$ varies. They give continuous functions $t,d$ with values in $A$.

<a id="eq:quadratic-law"></a>
For completeness, the determinant law itself can be constructed without recovering it by division by $2$. On a finite formal sum $\sum_g x_g g$, prescribe the quadratic polynomial $$\label{eq:quadratic-law}
 \sum_g d(g)x_g^2+
 \sum_{g<h}\bigl(t(g)t(h)-t(gh)\bigr)x_gx_h,$$ where any ordering of the finite support may be used. In every classical representation this is the determinant of $\sum_g x_g r(g)$; the formula is symmetric since $t(gh)=t(hg)$. All polynomial identities expressing normalization and multiplicativity hold coefficientwise in every classical specialization. Their coefficients therefore vanish in $A$ by Lemma <a href="#lem:density" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:density">2</a>. Formula <a href="#eq:quadratic-law" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:quadratic-law">[eq:quadratic-law]</a> defines the required polynomial law over arbitrary $A$-algebras, with trace $t$ and determinant $d$. ◻

</details>

<a id="pm-h-8"></a>
#### Noetherianity at two

<a id="stmt-prop-noetherian"></a>
<div id="prop:noetherian" class="proposition">

**Proposition 4**. *The ring $A$ is a finite product of complete Noetherian local rings with finite residue fields. Its given topology on each factor is the maximal-ideal-adic topology.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-prop-noetherian"><summary>Proof of stmt-prop-noetherian</summary>

*Proof.* Jochnowitz’s finiteness theorem (Jochnowitz 1982) <a id="pm-cite-15"></a>[[Jochnowitz82](#ref-Jochnowitz82)], in the form (Emerton 2011, Proposition 2.8) <a id="pm-cite-16"></a>[[Emerton11](#ref-Emerton11)], says that the reductions of the classical systems at fixed prime-to-$2$ level form a finite set. Every maximal ideal of $A_k$ is detected by such a reduction: the product in <a href="#eq:finite-embedding" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:finite-embedding">[eq:finite-embedding]</a> is finite integral over $A_k$, so one can apply lying over. Each $A_k$ is a finite product of complete local rings with finite residue fields. A surjective transition map $A_{k+1}\to A_k$ injects the set of maximal ideals of $A_k$ into that of $A_{k+1}$. These increasing sets have bounded cardinality, so stabilize on a tail. The corresponding local-factor maps are surjective, and identify their residue fields. We obtain $$A=\prod_{\alpha=1}^s A^{(\alpha)},$$ where each $A^{(\alpha)}$ is a profinite local ring with finite residue field $k_\alpha$.

The canonical Witt coefficient maps $W(k_\alpha)$ into the finite-stage local rings are the unique unramified lifts of the residue-field identifications. Their uniqueness makes them compatible, and gives $A^{(\alpha)}$ its $W(k_\alpha)$-algebra structure. Project the determinant of Lemma <a href="#lem:determinant" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:determinant">3</a> to this factor, and let $\overline D_\alpha$ be its reduction. Chenevier’s determinant deformation theorem (Chenevier 2014, Propositions 3.3 and 3.7) <a id="pm-cite-17"></a>[[Chenevier14](#ref-Chenevier14)] provides a complete Noetherian local universal ring $R_{\overline D_\alpha}$ with finite residue field, representing continuous deformations of $\overline D_\alpha$. Here the relevant finiteness hypothesis is Mazur’s condition: every open subgroup $H\subseteq G$ has only finitely many continuous homomorphisms to $\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z$. For $G_{\mathbb Q,S}$ this follows from number-field finiteness for quadratic extensions with ramification restricted to a fixed finite set; see also (Chenevier 2014, Example 3.6) <a id="pm-cite-18"></a>[[Chenevier14](#ref-Chenevier14)].

Apply the universal property to finite quotients and pass to the inverse limit. This gives a continuous map $$R_{\overline D_\alpha}\longrightarrow A^{(\alpha)}.$$ Its image contains the projections of every $T_\ell$ and $\ell S_\ell$. The image is compact and therefore closed, so Lemma <a href="#lem:density" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:density">2</a> makes the map surjective. Its kernel is closed; the quotient is a complete Noetherian local ring. The continuous bijection from this compact quotient to the Hausdorff ring $A^{(\alpha)}$ is a homeomorphism. This proves the topology assertion as well. ◻

</details>

The determinant argument supplies the $p=2$ justification for the pseudodeformation step in (Emerton 2011, Theorem 2.7) <a id="pm-cite-19"></a>[[Emerton11](#ref-Emerton11)], whose original references included an odd-prime restriction. No division by $2$ has been used in Proposition <a href="#prop:noetherian" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:noetherian">4</a>.

We now record the geometric input from the infinite fern.

<a id="stmt-thm-lower"></a>
<div id="thm:lower" class="theorem">

**Theorem 5** (Emerton). *For the algebra $A$ in <a href="#eq:algebra" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:algebra">[eq:algebra]</a>, every irreducible component of $\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}A$ has dimension at least $4$.*

</div>

This is (Emerton 2011, Corollary 2.28) <a id="pm-cite-20"></a>[[Emerton11](#ref-Emerton11)], with the definitions in Sections 2.1 and 2.5 of that paper and with $p=2$. It concerns the Hecke algebra of all modular forms used here, rather than a universal deformation ring for an irreducible residual representation.

<a id="sec:eisenstein"></a>
### Finding cuspidal points on every component

The lower bound in Theorem <a href="#thm:lower" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:lower">5</a> lets us exclude small classical loci. We show that Eisenstein systems cannot fill a component, even when all weights are allowed.

<a id="stmt-prop-exceptional"></a>
<div id="prop:exceptional" class="proposition">

**Proposition 6**. *The closure of the classical points of bounded weight has dimension at most $1$. The closure of all Eisenstein points has dimension at most $2$.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-prop-exceptional"><summary>Proof of stmt-prop-exceptional</summary>

*Proof.* Systems of weights at most $k$ factor through $A_k$, which is finite over $\mathbb Z_2$. Their closure therefore lies in the one-dimensional closed subset defined by $\ker(A\to A_k)$. In particular, we may discard weights one and two when considering the Eisenstein locus.

<a id="eq:eisenstein-values"></a>
In weight $i\geq3$, an Eisenstein system has eigenvalues $$\label{eq:eisenstein-values}
 T_\ell\longmapsto\psi(\ell)+\phi(\ell)\ell^{i-1},
 \qquad
 \ell S_\ell\longmapsto\psi(\ell)\phi(\ell)\ell^{i-1},$$ where $\psi,\phi$ are Dirichlet characters of conductors dividing $N$. The standard Eisenstein description gives these formulas; oldforms do not change the good-prime system. There are finitely many possible pairs, including their $2$-adic embeddings. Partition the systems by pair and by the parity of $i$. A family with finitely many weights has already been dealt with, so fix an infinite family, indexed by a set $I$ of weights, and a finite extension $E/\mathbb Q_2$ containing the character values. Put $\mathcal O=\mathcal O_E$.

Every odd prime has a unique expression $$\ell=s_\ell5^{b_\ell},\qquad
 s_\ell\in\{1,-1\},\quad b_\ell\in\mathbb Z_2.$$ On the fixed parity class, $s_\ell^{i-1}$ is constant. In <a href="#eq:eisenstein-values" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:eisenstein-values">[eq:eisenstein-values]</a>, replace $\ell^{i-1}$ by $s_\ell^{i-1}(1+X)^{b_\ell}\in\mathcal O[[X]]$. The resulting series specialize correctly at <a id="eq:weight-specialization"></a>$$\label{eq:weight-specialization}
 X=x_i:=5^{i-1}-1\in4\mathbb Z_2,\qquad i\in I.$$ We next verify that this prescription extends from generators to the inverse-limit algebra.

<a id="eq:graph-evaluation"></a>
Give $\mathcal O[[X]]$ its maximal-ideal-adic topology. Let $C$ be the closure in $A\times\mathcal O[[X]]$ of the $\mathbb Z_2$-algebra generated by the paired Hecke generators and their prescribed series. The projection $C\to A$ is surjective: its image is closed by compactness and contains a dense subalgebra. For every $(a,F)\in C$ and $i\in I$, continuity gives $$\label{eq:graph-evaluation}
 \lambda_i(a)=F(x_i),$$ where $\lambda_i$ is the corresponding classical system. If $(0,F)\in C$, then $F$ vanishes at all the distinct $x_i$. The series $F(4Y)$ is a restricted power series, since its coefficients tend to zero. Strassmann’s theorem therefore implies $F(4Y)=0$, and hence $F=0$. Thus $C\to A$ is also injective. It is a homeomorphism, and the second projection defines a continuous map <a id="eq:eisenstein-map"></a>$$\label{eq:eisenstein-map}
 \Phi:A\longrightarrow\mathcal O[[X]].$$

Let $B=\Phi(A)$, a compact, hence closed, subring of $\mathcal O[[X]]$. Choose a prime $\ell\nmid2N$ with $$\ell\equiv1\pmod N,\qquad \ell\equiv5\pmod8.$$ Such primes exist by the Chinese remainder theorem and Dirichlet’s theorem. For this prime $\psi(\ell)=\phi(\ell)=1$, $s_\ell=1$, and $b_\ell\in\mathbb Z_2^\times$. It follows from the image of $\ell S_\ell$ that $u=(1+X)^{b_\ell}\in B$. The series $$u^{b_\ell^{-1}}=
 \sum_{n\geq0}\binom{b_\ell^{-1}}{n}(u-1)^n=1+X$$ converges in $\mathcal O[[X]]$, and its partial sums belong to $B$. Closedness gives $X\in B$ and then $\mathbb Z_2[[X]]\subseteq B$. A finite $\mathbb Z_2$-basis of $\mathcal O$ consequently generates $\mathcal O[[X]]$ as a $B$-module. This is a finite integral extension, so $\dim B=\dim\mathcal O[[X]]=2$. All the points in this family factor through $B=A/\ker\Phi$ by <a href="#eq:graph-evaluation" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:graph-evaluation">[eq:graph-evaluation]</a>; their closure has dimension at most $2$. Taking the finite union over the families, and adding weights one and two, proves the claim. ◻

</details>

<a id="stmt-cor-classical-point"></a>
<div id="cor:classical-point" class="corollary">

**Corollary 7**. *For every minimal prime $P$ of $A$, there is a classical cuspidal map $\lambda:A\to\mathcal O_E$ of weight at least $3$ such that $P\subseteq\mathfrak x:=\ker\lambda$ and $\mathfrak x$ belongs to no other irreducible component of $\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}A$.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-cor-classical-point"><summary>Proof of stmt-cor-classical-point</summary>

*Proof.* Let $Z$ be the closure of the Eisenstein points and of the points of weights one and two. By Proposition <a href="#prop:exceptional" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:exceptional">6</a>, $\dim Z\leq2$, whereas $\dim V(P)\geq4$ by Theorem <a href="#thm:lower" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:lower">5</a>. Since $A$ is Noetherian, it has finitely many minimal primes. Removing $Z$ and every component other than $V(P)$ leaves an open subset of $\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}A$ containing the point $P$. This open subset is nonempty and lies in $V(P)$. Lemma <a href="#lem:density" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:density">2</a> supplies a classical point in it, which has the asserted properties. ◻

</details>

<a id="eq:r"></a>
Fix a point furnished by Corollary <a href="#cor:classical-point" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="cor:classical-point">7</a>. Its system comes from a cuspidal newform $f$ of weight $k\geq3$ and level dividing $N$. Enlarging $E$ if necessary, let $$\label{eq:r}
 r:G\longrightarrow\mathop{\mathrm{GL}}_2(E)$$ be its absolutely irreducible Galois representation. Absolute irreducibility here is a characteristic-zero theorem for cuspidal newforms (Ribet 1977, Theorem 2.3) <a id="pm-cite-21"></a>[[Ribet77](#ref-Ribet77)]; nothing is asserted about its reduction. The specializations $\lambda(t),\lambda(d)$ are the trace and determinant of $r$.

<a id="sec:tangent"></a>
### A tangent bound in characteristic zero

We prove the tangent estimate in a form that separates it from the particular Hecke algebra. For a representation $r$, the notation $\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}r$ means $\mathop{\mathrm{End}}_E(E^2)$ with the conjugation action, not its trace-zero subspace.

<a id="stmt-prop-tangent"></a>
<div id="prop:tangent" class="proposition">

<a id="eq:tangent-bound"></a>
**Proposition 8**. *Let $R$ be a compact Hausdorff Noetherian topological $\mathbb Z_p$-algebra, with continuous structure map $\mathbb Z_p\to R$. Suppose a profinite group $H$ has a continuous two-dimensional determinant over $R$, with trace $t$ and determinant $d$, and that their values topologically generate $R$ over $\mathbb Z_p$. Let $\lambda:R\to\mathcal O_E$ be a continuous $\mathbb Z_p$-algebra map, where $E/\mathbb Q_p$ is finite, and suppose its specialized determinant is that of an absolutely irreducible continuous representation $r:H\to\mathop{\mathrm{GL}}_2(E)$. For $\mathfrak x=\ker\lambda$, $$\label{eq:tangent-bound}
 \dim R_{\mathfrak x}\leq\mathop{\mathrm{edim}}R_{\mathfrak x}
 \leq\dim_E H^1(H,\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}r).$$*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-prop-tangent"><summary>Proof of stmt-prop-tangent</summary>

*Proof.* The first inequality is the usual dimension bound for a Noetherian local ring. We prove the second by relating derivations to first-order Galois deformations.

<a id="eq:derivations"></a>
*The algebraic tangent space consists of continuous derivations.* The image $R/\mathfrak x$ is a $\mathbb Z_p$-submodule of $\mathcal O_E$, so is finite over $\mathbb Z_p$. Its fraction field $E_0=\mathop{\mathrm{Frac}}(R/\mathfrak x)$ is the residue field of $R_{\mathfrak x}$ and is finite over $\mathbb Q_p$. Since $p\notin\mathfrak x$, $R_{\mathfrak x}$ is a $\mathbb Q_p$-algebra. Put $\mathfrak m=\mathfrak xR_{\mathfrak x}$. Separability of $E_0/\mathbb Q_p$ gives a coefficient field in $R_{\mathfrak x}/\mathfrak m^2$, and every $\mathbb Q_p$-derivation kills this field. It follows that $$\label{eq:derivations}
 \mathop{\mathrm{Der}}_{\mathbb Q_p}(R_{\mathfrak x},E)
 \simeq\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}_{E_0}(\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2,E),$$ so the left side has $E$-dimension $\mathop{\mathrm{edim}}R_{\mathfrak x}$. The coefficient field can also be obtained by lifting a primitive element of the finite separable extension across the square-zero ideal; its minimal polynomial has invertible derivative.

Let $D$ be such a derivation, restricted to $R$. It is $\mathbb Z_p$-linear and vanishes on $\mathfrak x^2$. Noetherianity makes $\mathfrak x/\mathfrak x^2$ finite over $R/\mathfrak x$, and the exact sequence $$0\longrightarrow\mathfrak x/\mathfrak x^2\longrightarrow
 R/\mathfrak x^2\longrightarrow R/\mathfrak x\longrightarrow0$$ shows that $R/\mathfrak x^2$ is finite over $\mathbb Z_p$. Every finitely generated ideal of $R$ is closed: it is the image of a continuous map $R^m\to R$, hence compact. The quotient topology on $R/\mathfrak x^2$ is its usual finite-module topology, since a continuous surjection from a finite free $\mathbb Z_p$-module to it is a quotient map between compact Hausdorff spaces. Any $\mathbb Z_p$-linear map from a finite $\mathbb Z_p$-module to $E$ is continuous. In particular, $D:R\to E$ is continuous.

*First-order determinants lift to continuous representations.* Write $E[\varepsilon]$ for the dual-number $E$-algebra, with $\varepsilon^2=0$. The map $\lambda+\varepsilon D:R\to E[\varepsilon]$ specializes the determinant to a continuous determinant lifting that of $r$. Passing to the canonical Cayley–Hamilton quotient of $E[\varepsilon][H]$ (the quotient imposing the characteristic-polynomial identities of this determinant), the absolutely irreducible lifting theorem for determinants (Chenevier 2014, Theorem 2.22(i) and Corollary 2.23) <a id="pm-cite-22"></a>[[Chenevier14](#ref-Chenevier14)] gives a representation $$r_D:H\longrightarrow\mathop{\mathrm{GL}}_2(E[\varepsilon])$$ with this determinant, whose reduction can be identified with $r$. Its applicability is over the Henselian local ring $E[\varepsilon]$: the residual representation at this point is the split absolutely irreducible representation $r$ over the characteristic-zero field $E$.

Here continuity of $r_D$ follows from continuity of its trace. Choose $g_1,\ldots,g_4\in H$ such that the matrices $r(g_j)$ form an $E$-basis of $M_2(E)$; absolute irreducibility guarantees such a choice. The lifts $r_D(g_j)$ form a basis over $E[\varepsilon]$. The trace pairing on this matrix algebra is nondegenerate, so the coordinates of $r_D(g)$ in that basis are obtained by inverting a fixed Gram matrix and using the four functions $$g\longmapsto\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(r_D(g)r_D(g_j))
       =\lambda(t(gg_j))+\varepsilon D(t(gg_j)).$$ These functions are continuous. Hence $r_D$ is continuous.

<a id="eq:cohom-traces"></a>
*Traces detect tangent vectors.* Write $r_D(g)=(1+\varepsilon c(g))r(g)$. The homomorphism identity is exactly the cocycle identity for a continuous $c\in Z^1(H,\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}r)$. Comparing traces gives $$D(t(g))=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(c(g)r(g)).$$ Coboundaries have zero image under the linear map $$\label{eq:cohom-traces}
 H^1(H,\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}r)\longrightarrow\mathop{\mathrm{Maps}}(H,E),\qquad
 [c]\longmapsto\bigl(g\mapsto\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(c(g)r(g))\bigr),$$ where $\mathop{\mathrm{Maps}}(H,E)$ is the $E$-vector space of all functions $H\to E$. Thus the image of the linear map $D\mapsto D\circ t$ lies in the image of <a href="#eq:cohom-traces" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:cohom-traces">[eq:cohom-traces]</a>.

This map on derivations is injective. Indeed the degree-two trace identity gives $$2d(g)=t(g)^2-t(g^2).$$ If $D(t(g))=0$ for every $g$, applying $D$ and working in the characteristic-zero field $E$ gives $D(d(g))=0$ for every $g$. The derivation then vanishes on the algebra of generators, and on its closure by continuity. Its restriction to $R$ determines it on $R_{\mathfrak x}$ by the quotient rule. The injection and <a href="#eq:derivations" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:derivations">[eq:derivations]</a> now prove <a href="#eq:tangent-bound" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:tangent-bound">[eq:tangent-bound]</a>. ◻

</details>

<a id="eq:hecke-tangent"></a>
Apply Proposition <a href="#prop:tangent" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:tangent">8</a> to $R=A$, $p=2$, $H=G$, and the classical point chosen above. Propositions and lemmas in Section <a href="#sec:integral" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:integral">2</a> verify its hypotheses. With $V=\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}r$, we obtain $$\label{eq:hecke-tangent}
 \dim A_{\mathfrak x}\leq\mathop{\mathrm{edim}}A_{\mathfrak x}
 \leq \dim_E H^1(G,V).$$ The use of characteristic zero in the last proof does not impose any condition on the residual system of the component.

<a id="sec:selmer"></a>
### The adjoint cohomology bound

We now show that the right side of <a href="#eq:hecke-tangent" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:hecke-tangent">[eq:hecke-tangent]</a> is at most $3$. For a finite place $v$, write $H^j(\mathbb Q_v,V)$ for the cohomology of its absolute Galois group. The Bloch–Kato finite subspace is $$H^1_f(\mathbb Q_v,V)=
 \begin{cases}
 \ker\bigl(H^1(\mathbb Q_v,V)\to H^1(I_v,V)\bigr),&v\ne2,\\
 \ker\bigl(H^1(\mathbb Q_2,V)\to
 H^1(\mathbb Q_2,V\otimes_{\mathbb Q_2}B_{\mathrm{cris}})\bigr),&v=2,
 \end{cases}$$ where $I_v$ is inertia and $B_{\mathrm{cris}}$ is Fontaine’s crystalline period ring. The global group $H^1_f(\mathbb Q,V)$ consists of classes satisfying these conditions at every finite place. Conditions at infinity impose nothing here: the positive-degree cohomology of the real Galois group with $E$-coefficients vanishes, since $2$ is invertible in $E$.

<a id="pm-h-12"></a>
#### Global Selmer vanishing

The input from (Newton and Thorne 2023, Theorem 5.4) <a id="pm-cite-23"></a>[[NewtonThorne23](#ref-NewtonThorne23)], specialized to $\mathop{\mathrm{GL}}_2/\mathbb Q$, is the following: if a regular algebraic cuspidal automorphic representation is non-CM, or is CM by a quadratic field not contained in $\mathbb Q(\zeta_{2^\infty})$, then the finite Selmer group of its full $2$-adic adjoint representation vanishes. There is no hypothesis of residual irreducibility or an odd-prime restriction in this statement.

<a id="stmt-lem-global-selmer"></a>
<div id="lem:global-selmer" class="lemma">

**Lemma 9**. *For the representation <a href="#eq:r" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:r">[eq:r]</a>, $H^1_f(\mathbb Q,\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}r)=0$.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-global-selmer"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-global-selmer</summary>

*Proof.* The form $f$ has weight at least $3$, so its associated cuspidal automorphic representation is regular algebraic, in the usual cohomological normalization. Twists introduced by normalization do not change its adjoint representation.

Only the CM case requires a check. If $f$ has CM by $K$, its automorphic representation has the quadratic self-twist $\chi_{K/\mathbb Q}$. Since the level of $f$ divides the odd integer $N$, its local representation at $2$ is unramified. The local Langlands parameter of an unramified representation has trivial inertia; tensoring it with a ramified character cannot leave it unchanged. Thus $\chi_{K/\mathbb Q}$ is unramified at $2$. The three quadratic subfields of $\mathbb Q(\zeta_{2^\infty})$ are $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-1})$, $\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)$, and $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-2})$, all ramified at $2$. Therefore $K$ is not contained in that cyclotomic extension. Newton–Thorne’s theorem applies in both cases and gives the result. We may enlarge the finite coefficient field to apply the theorem; vanishing descends to $E$ under finite extension of scalars. ◻

</details>

<a id="pm-h-13"></a>
#### The local quotients

Global Selmer vanishing will make restriction to the sum of the local quotients injective. To compute these quotients, we first use local Tate duality to eliminate the $H^2$ terms from the local Euler characteristic formulas.

<a id="stmt-lem-local-invariants"></a>
<div id="lem:local-invariants" class="lemma">

**Lemma 10**. *For every finite place $v$, $H^0(\mathbb Q_v,V^*(1))=0$, where $V=\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}r$.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-local-invariants"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-local-invariants</summary>

*Proof.* The trace pairing identifies $V^*$ with $V$. The asserted group is therefore $\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}_{G_{\mathbb Q_v}}(r,r(1))$.

At $v=2$, the representation $r$ is de Rham, hence Hodge–Tate, with two weights whose difference is $k-1\geq2$. These are the classical comparison and filtration statements for modular Galois representations; see (Kato 2004, sec. 11.3, especially (11.3.3)–(11.3.4)) <a id="pm-cite-24"></a>[[Kato04](#ref-Kato04)]. One may pass to an auxiliary fine level when applying that cohomological description; the representation attached to $f$ is unchanged. A Tate twist shifts both weights by one, so the weight sets of $r$ and $r(1)$ are disjoint. Subrepresentations and quotients of Hodge–Tate representations are Hodge–Tate, with their weights among those of the original representation. A nonzero image of a map $r\to r(1)$ would have weights in both sets, which is impossible.

Let $v\ne2$. Local–global compatibility for cuspidal newforms (Carayol 1986, Theorem A) <a id="pm-cite-25"></a>[[Carayol86](#ref-Carayol86)] identifies the Frobenius-semisimplified Weil–Deligne parameter of $r|_{G_{\mathbb Q_v}}$ with that of the local automorphic representation, up to the conventional normalizing twist. The local automorphic representation is generic. The genericity criterion (Allen 2016, Lemma 1.1.3) <a id="pm-cite-26"></a>[[Allen16](#ref-Allen16)] says that its Frobenius-semisimple Weil–Deligne parameter $W$ has $\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathrm{WD}}(W,W(1))=0$. Here the Tate twist is normalized consistently with geometric Frobenius; normalizing character twists do not affect the criterion.

A nonzero Galois morphism $r\to r(1)$ would give a nonzero morphism of Weil–Deligne representations. It would remain a morphism after Frobenius semisimplification: an intertwiner of Frobenius also intertwines its semisimple part, and the inertia and monodromy conditions are unchanged. This contradicts the genericity criterion. ◻

</details>

<a id="stmt-prop-cohomology"></a>
<div id="prop:cohomology" class="proposition">

**Proposition 11**. *For the representation $r$ of a cuspidal newform of weight at least $3$ and odd level dividing $N$, $$\dim_E H^1(G_{\mathbb Q,S},\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}r)\leq3.$$*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-prop-cohomology"><summary>Proof of stmt-prop-cohomology</summary>

<a id="eq:away-two"></a>
*Proof.* Write $h^j$ for the $E$-dimension of a cohomology group. Local Tate duality and the local Euler characteristic formulas are used in their characteristic-zero form; see (Neukirch, Schmidt, and Wingberg 2008, vol. 323, chap. 7) <a id="pm-cite-27"></a>[[NeukirchSchmidtWingberg08](#ref-NeukirchSchmidtWingberg08)]. Duality and Lemma <a href="#lem:local-invariants" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:local-invariants">10</a> give $h^2(\mathbb Q_v,V)=0$ at every finite place. For $v\ne2$, the local Euler characteristic formula yields $h^1(\mathbb Q_v,V)=h^0(\mathbb Q_v,V)$. The unramified cohomology is $$H^1_f(\mathbb Q_v,V)=
 V^{I_v}/(\mathop{\mathrm{Frob}}_v-1)V^{I_v}.$$ The cokernel and kernel of an endomorphism of a finite-dimensional vector space have the same dimension. Consequently this group has dimension $h^0(\mathbb Q_v,V)$, and $$\label{eq:away-two}
 H^1(\mathbb Q_v,V)/H^1_f(\mathbb Q_v,V)=0\qquad(v\ne2).$$

<a id="eq:at-two"></a>
At $2$, the Euler characteristic formula instead gives $$h^1(\mathbb Q_2,V)=h^0(\mathbb Q_2,V)+\dim_E V
            =h^0(\mathbb Q_2,V)+4.$$ Write $D_{\mathrm{dR}}(V)=(V\otimes_{\mathbb Q_2}B_{\mathrm{dR}})^{G_{\mathbb Q_2}}$ for the filtered de Rham module, where $B_{\mathrm{dR}}$ is Fontaine’s de Rham period field. The Bloch–Kato dimension formula (Bloch and Kato 1990, Corollary 3.8.4) <a id="pm-cite-28"></a>[[BlochKato90](#ref-BlochKato90)] gives $$\begin{aligned}
 h^1_f(\mathbb Q_2,V)
 &=h^0(\mathbb Q_2,V)+
   \dim_E\bigl(D_{\mathrm{dR}}(V)/\mathop{\mathrm{Fil}}^0D_{\mathrm{dR}}(V)\bigr)\\
 &=h^0(\mathbb Q_2,V)+1.
\end{aligned}$$ Indeed the filtration degrees of the adjoint consist of two zeros and two nonzero opposite integers, so exactly one degree contributes to the indicated quotient. We have proved $$\label{eq:at-two}
 \dim_E H^1(\mathbb Q_2,V)/H^1_f(\mathbb Q_2,V)=3.$$

<a id="eq:selmer-injection"></a>
Finally consider restriction followed by passage to these local quotients: $$\label{eq:selmer-injection}
 H^1(G_{\mathbb Q,S},V)\longrightarrow
 \bigoplus_{v\in S}H^1(\mathbb Q_v,V)/H^1_f(\mathbb Q_v,V).$$ Inflation identifies the source with a subspace of $H^1(G_{\mathbb Q},V)$. Its classes are already unramified outside $S$, so the kernel of <a href="#eq:selmer-injection" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:selmer-injection">[eq:selmer-injection]</a> lies in $H^1_f(\mathbb Q,V)$, which vanishes by Lemma <a href="#lem:global-selmer" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:global-selmer">9</a>. Thus <a href="#eq:selmer-injection" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:selmer-injection">[eq:selmer-injection]</a> is injective. Equations <a href="#eq:away-two" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:away-two">[eq:away-two]</a> and <a href="#eq:at-two" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:at-two">[eq:at-two]</a> give a target of dimension $3$. ◻

</details>

<a id="sec:conclusion"></a>
### Dimension of the components

<details id="proof-stmt-prop-cohomology-2"><summary>Proof of stmt-prop-cohomology</summary>

*Proof of Theorem <a href="#thm:main" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:main">1</a>.* Let $P$ be an arbitrary minimal prime of $A$. By Corollary <a href="#cor:classical-point" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="cor:classical-point">7</a>, choose a classical cuspidal point $\mathfrak x=\ker\lambda$ of weight at least $3$ with $P\subseteq\mathfrak x$. Equation <a href="#eq:hecke-tangent" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:hecke-tangent">[eq:hecke-tangent]</a> and Proposition <a href="#prop:cohomology" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:cohomology">11</a> give $$\dim A_{\mathfrak x}\leq3.$$ The quotient $A/P$ is a complete Noetherian local domain by Proposition <a href="#prop:noetherian" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:noetherian">4</a>, and is therefore catenary. The classical quotient $A/\mathfrak x$ is finite over $\mathbb Z_2$ and contains $\mathbb Z_2$, so has dimension one. The dimension formula for a catenary local domain yields $$\dim A/P
 =\mathop{\mathrm{ht}}(\mathfrak x/P)+\dim A/\mathfrak x
 \leq\dim A_{\mathfrak x}+1
 \leq4.$$ Theorem <a href="#thm:lower" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:lower">5</a> gives the opposite inequality. Since $P$ was arbitrary, all irreducible components have dimension $4$. ◻

</details>
