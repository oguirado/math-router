## 1. Abstract and result contract

> We prove that Thompson's group <i>F</i> is nonamenable. This confirms Geoghegan's conjecture and resolves the amenability problem for <i>F</i>.

- **C1** (theorem, [source](#stmt-thm-main)): Thompson’s group $F$ is not amenable.
  - Explanation: The paper claims nonamenability of Thompson’s group through a uniform obstruction to finite translation invariance.
- **C2** (construction, [source](#stmt-lem-displacement)): There are a real Hilbert space $H$, a Lipschitz map $f:B\to B$ on its closed unit ball, and a constant $\delta>0$ such that $$\lVert f(x)-x\rVert\ge\delta\qquad(x\in B).$$
  - Explanation: An infinite-dimensional Hilbert ball admits a Lipschitz self-map whose displacement is uniformly positive.
- **C3** (theorem, [source](#stmt-dyad-eventual)): For each $g\in F$, the partitions $gT^{(n)}$ are basic for all sufficiently large $n$, and their meshes tend to zero. In particular, given a finite set $K\subset F$ and a finite family $\mathcal J$ of basic intervals, there is an $n_0$ such that $gT^{(n)}$ is basic and respects every member of $\mathcal J$ whenever $g\in K$ and $n\geq n_0$.
  - Explanation: A common sufficiently fine partition level accommodates a finite family of transformations and basic intervals.
- **C4** (construction, [source](#stmt-dyad-transport)): If $I<J$ and $I'<J'$ are pairs of internal basic intervals, there is an $h\in F$ whose restrictions carry $I$ affinely onto $I'$ and $J$ affinely onto $J'$.
  - Explanation: Separated internal dyadic pairs can be simultaneously transported by affine restrictions of a group element.
- **C5** (bound, [source](#stmt-avg-boundary)): For every nonempty finite set $A\subset F$, $$\label{avg:boundary-bound} \max_{h\in S}\frac{\|hA\mathbin{\triangle}A\|}{\|A\|} \geq \frac{\delta^2/L^2-4/D}{4(1-1/D)}>0.$$
  - Explanation: A fixed finite family of transports forces a positive relative boundary for every nonempty finite subset.
- **C6** (theorem, [source](#stmt-cor-nonunitarizable)): For every $\varepsilon>0$, there are a separable complex Hilbert space $H$ and a representation $\pi:F\to\mathrm{GL}(H)$ such that $\sup_{g\in F}\lVert \pi(g)\rVert\le1+\varepsilon$, but no bounded invertible operator $S$ on $H$ makes every $S\pi(g)S^{-1}$ unitary.
  - Explanation: The claimed nonamenability supplies uniformly bounded representations that cannot be made unitary by conjugation.
- **C7** (theorem, [source](#stmt-cor-percolation)): For every such generating set $S$, $$\lVert T_{p_c}\rVert_{2\to2}<\infty,\qquad p_c<p_{2\to2}\le p_u.$$ For each fixed $p\in(p_c,p_u)$, there are almost surely infinitely many infinite open clusters. Moreover, there are deterministic $p_c<p_1<p_2<1$, depending on $S$, such that in the coupling with independent uniform $[0,1]$ edge labels $U_e$, opening $e$ when $U_e\le p$, there are almost surely infinitely many infinite open clusters simultaneously for every $p\in[p_1,p_2]$.
  - Explanation: The companion percolation theorem supplies nonuniqueness and a simultaneous interval of nonuniqueness on the specified Cayley graphs.
- **C8** (construction, [source](#stmt-aux-map)): Let $H=L^2([0,1];\mathbb R^2)$ be a real Hilbert space and let $B=\{x\in H:\lVert x\rVert\le1\}$. There is a globally Lipschitz map $f:B\to B$ such that $$\lVert f(x)\rVert=1, \qquad \lVert f(x)-x\rVert\ge\frac12 \quad(x\in B).$$
  - Explanation: The auxiliary construction maps the Hilbert ball onto its sphere while keeping displacement uniformly positive.
- **C9** (theorem, [source](#stmt-aux-tube)): The curve $\gamma$ is injective, has a bounded Lipschitz derivative, and $$c:=\inf_{t\in\mathbb R}\lVert \gamma'(t)\rVert=\frac18.$$ Its unit tangent $u(t)=\gamma'(t)/\lVert \gamma'(t)\rVert$ is Lipschitz, $\langle u(t),v(t)\rangle\ge0$, and $u(t)=v(t)$ for $t\le1$. There is a radius $0<\rho<1/32$ such that every point in $$U=\{x\in H:\operatorname{dist}(x,\gamma(\mathbb R))<\rho\}$$ has a unique nearest point $\gamma(t(x))$. Writing $e(x)=x-\gamma(t(x))$, we have $e(x)\perp u(t(x))$, and $$\label{aux:parameter} \|t(x)-t(y)\|\le16\lVert x-y\rVert \quad\text{if }x,y\in U\text{ and }\lVert x-y\rVert<\rho.$$
  - Explanation: Uniform separation and tangent control give a tube with unique nearest points and local parameter control.
- **C10** (construction, [source](#stmt-aux-rotation)): There is a family of orthogonal operators $R_t:H\to H$, Lipschitz in operator norm, such that $R_tu(t)=v(t)$ and $R_t$ is the identity for $t\le1$.
  - Explanation: An explicit orthogonal rotation carries the curve’s unit tangent to its angular direction with Lipschitz dependence.

## 2. Definitions, validity and bounds

- **Thompson’s group** ([source](#sec:introduction)): Thompson’s group $F$ consists of the increasing piecewise linear homeomorphisms of $[0,1]$ with finitely many pieces, dyadic rational breakpoints, and slopes in $2^{\mathbb Z}$.
- **Amenability** ([source](#eq:folner)): A discrete group $G$ is *amenable* if its bounded real-valued functions admit a positive normalized left-invariant mean: a linear functional $M:\ell^\infty(G;\mathbb R)\to\mathbb R$ satisfying $M(1)=1$, $M(\varphi)\ge0$ whenever $\varphi\ge0$, and $M(g\mapsto\varphi(hg))=M(\varphi)$ for every $h\in G$.
- **Basic dyadic interval** ([source](#sec:dyadic)): A *basic dyadic interval* is an interval $$[k2^{-r},(k+1)2^{-r}]\subseteq[0,1], \qquad r\in\mathbb N\cup\{0\},\quad 0\leq k<2^r.$$
- **Basic partition** ([source](#sec:dyadic)): A *basic partition* is a finite partition of $[0,1]$ into such intervals; cells may share endpoints.
- **Mesh** ([source](#sec:dyadic)): Its mesh is the greatest length of a cell.
- **Respecting an interval** ([source](#sec:dyadic)): A partition $T$ *respects* an interval $I$ if $I$ is a union of cells of $T$.
- **Normalized restriction** ([source](#dyad:restriction)): If $T$ respects $I$, define its normalized restriction by $$T_I=\{s_I^{-1}(K):K\in T,\ K\subseteq I\}.$$
- **Recursive color** ([source](#sec:coloring)): Define a color $p(T)\in B$ for every basic partition $T$ by induction on its number of cells: $$\label{color:recursion} p(T)= \begin{cases} \displaystyle f\left(\frac1D\sum_{j=1}^D p(T_{I_j})\right), &\text{if $T$ respects every $I_j$},\\[6pt] 0, &\text{otherwise}. \end{cases}$$
- **Relative boundary defect** ([source](#avg:defect)): Fix such a set $A$ and put $$\label{avg:defect} \eta=\max_{h\in S}\frac{\|hA\mathbin{\triangle}A\|}{\|A\|}.$$
- **Connection threshold** ([source](#pm-h-13)): The connection kernel $T_p(x,y)=\mathbb P_p(x\leftrightarrow y)$ acts on counting-measure $\ell^2(F)$, initially on finitely supported functions by summation over $y$; put $p_{2\to2}=\sup\{p\in[0,1]:T_p\text{ is bounded on }\ell^2(F)\}$.

## 4. Engineering Triptych

### Panel 1 - epistemic contract

- **affine-transport**: Separated internal basic interval pairs admit simultaneous affine transport by an element of Thompson’s group.
- **fixed-transports**: The transport family is fixed before the finite set being tested.
- **separation-scope**: The common scalar correlation comparison applies to strictly separated intervals and imposes no condition on nested pairs.
- **hilbert-displacement**: The displacement obstruction uses a Lipschitz self-map of a real Hilbert ball with a uniform positive lower displacement.

### Panel 2 - negative knowledge

- **arbitrary-intervals**: Transport arbitrary interval pairs with the dyadic construction. -> The resulting map need not have dyadic breakpoints and slopes that are powers of two.
- **moving-transports**: Choose a new transport family for each tested finite set. -> A boundary bound for changing transport families does not supply the fixed family used in the contradiction.
- **nested-correlations**: Apply the separated correlation estimate to a descendant and its own parent. -> The covariance comparison does not supply the common correlation estimate for nested pairs.
- **finite-displacement**: Use a finite-dimensional discretization as a certificate of the displacement lemma. -> A finite approximation does not establish uniform displacement on the whole Hilbert ball.
- **network-guarantee**: Use the percolation corollary as a reliability guarantee for an arbitrary network. -> An arbitrary network need not be the specified Cayley graph with independent bond percolation.

### Panel 3 - reference implementation and oracle

Applicability: `constructive`. The pair-transport lemma gives a finite constructive procedure: subdivide complementary dyadic gaps, equalize cell counts by bisection, and map corresponding cells increasingly and affinely. The implementation covers that auxiliary transport; the Hilbert displacement argument and the nonamenability conclusion remain outside its certificate.

```python
# INPUTS: JSON list of transport cases, each four [k,r] basic dyadic interval descriptors.
# PRECONDITIONS: At most 4096 cases; integer 1 <= r <= 4; internal source and target pairs separated by positive gaps.
# OUTPUTS: JSON list of piecewise affine maps as ordered [source,target] rational breakpoint pairs.
# TIME: O(N * 4^R) exact rational operations, N cases and largest interval exponent R <= 4.
# SPACE: O(N * 2^R) exact rational breakpoint storage.
# ARITHMETIC: Certified exact python-flint fmpq arithmetic; no floating point.
# FAILURE: Invalid input raises an exception and returns a nonzero exit.
import json
import sys
from flint import fmpq

def transport(case):
    if not isinstance(case, list) or len(case) != 4:
        raise ValueError('four intervals required')
    intervals = []
    for desc in case:
        if not isinstance(desc, list) or len(desc) != 2:
            raise ValueError('descriptor')
        k, r = desc
        if type(k) != int or type(r) != int or not 1 <= r <= 4 or not 0 < k < 2**r - 1:
            raise ValueError('internal basic interval required')
        intervals.append((fmpq(k, 2**r), fmpq(k+1, 2**r)))
    if not (intervals[0][1] < intervals[1][0] and intervals[2][1] < intervals[3][0]):
        raise ValueError('positive gaps required')
    level = max(d[1] for d in case)
    step = fmpq(1, 2**level)
    def parts(left, right):
        return [(left + j*step, left + (j+1)*step) for j in range(int((right-left)/step))]
    def gaps(pair):
        a, b = pair
        return [parts(fmpq(0), a[0]), parts(a[1], b[0]), parts(b[1], fmpq(1))]
    source = gaps(intervals[:2])
    target = gaps(intervals[2:])
    def split(cells):
        l, u = cells.pop(0)
        mid = (l+u)/2
        cells[:0] = [(l, mid), (mid, u)]
    for s, t in zip(source, target):
        while len(s) < len(t):
            split(s)
        while len(t) < len(s):
            split(t)
    sc = source[0] + [intervals[0]] + source[1] + [intervals[1]] + source[2]
    tc = target[0] + [intervals[2]] + target[1] + [intervals[3]] + target[2]
    points = [(fmpq(0), fmpq(0))] + [(s[1], t[1]) for s, t in zip(sc, tc)]
    return [[str(a), str(b)] for a, b in points]

cases = json.load(sys.stdin)
if not isinstance(cases, list) or not 1 <= len(cases) <= 4096:
    raise ValueError('bounded nonempty case batch required')
print(json.dumps([transport(c) for c in cases]))
```

## 6. Applicability limits and search

- **group-scope** (formal_scope): The nonamenability conclusion concerns Thompson’s group with its specified dyadic piecewise linear structure and multiplication convention. Blocked use: ['Transfer the conclusion to arbitrary transformation groups without establishing the relevant group structure.']
- **structural-noncriteria** (counterexample_scope): Absence of a nonabelian free subgroup and failure of elementary amenability do not decide ordinary amenability. Blocked use: ['Use either structural property alone as a decision rule for amenability.']
- **displacement-dimensionality** (formal_scope): The supplied displacement construction is on an infinite-dimensional Hilbert ball; the paper does not supply a finite-dimensional replacement with the same guarantee. Blocked use: ['Treat a finite-dimensional truncation or a finite sample as a certificate of uniform displacement on the stated Hilbert ball.']
- **transport-domain** (hypothesis): Affine pair transport requires ordered, strictly separated internal basic dyadic intervals in both the source and target. Blocked use: ['Apply the transport guarantee to overlapping intervals, touching intervals, or intervals meeting the boundary.']
- **refinement-dependence** (edge_case): A common sufficiently fine partition level exists for a finite family of group elements and intervals; the level used in the boundary proof may depend on the tested finite set. Blocked use: ['Assume a single preset partition level is admissible for every finite subset of the group.']
- **fixed-transports** (hypothesis): The finite transport family must be fixed independently of the finite set being tested. Blocked use: ['Choose a new transport family after seeing each test set and call the resulting bound a uniform obstruction.']
- **finite-average-domain** (hypothesis): The cancellation estimate applies to a nonempty finite group subset and a globally bounded scalar function. Blocked use: ['Use an empty-set denominator, an unbounded observable, or an uncontrolled sampled average as the stated finite-average comparison.']
- **nested-correlations** (edge_case): Only strictly separated interval colors receive the common correlation comparison; nested pairs require their separate unit-ball estimate. Blocked use: ['Apply the separated-pair correlation estimate to a descendant and its own parent.']
- **source-target-admissibility** (hypothesis): Covariance in the proof requires admissibility at both the source and translated group elements; the transport need not preserve the remaining intervals. Blocked use: ['Infer admissibility of the translated partition merely from admissibility of the source partition.']
- **strict-positivity-regime** (hypothesis): The positive boundary lower bound requires the stated strict size condition on the parent family, together with a positive Lipschitz constant and positive displacement. ### Introduction

Thompson’s group $F$ consists of the increasing piecewise linear homeomorphisms of $[0,1]$ with finitely many pieces, dyadic rational breakpoints, and slopes in $2^{\mathbb Z}$. We use the multiplication convention $hg=h\circ g$ and regard $F$ as a discrete group.

<a id="eq:folner"></a>
A discrete group $G$ is *amenable* if its bounded real-valued functions admit a positive normalized left-invariant mean: a linear functional $M:\ell^\infty(G;\mathbb R)\to\mathbb R$ satisfying $M(1)=1$, $M(\varphi)\ge0$ whenever $\varphi\ge0$, and $M(g\mapsto\varphi(hg))=M(\varphi)$ for every $h\in G$. This invariant-mean viewpoint belongs to the classical theory developed by von Neumann and Day (Neumann 1929; Day 1957) <a id="pm-cite-1"></a>[[vonNeumann1929](#ref-vonNeumann1929), [Day1957](#ref-Day1957)]. We use the following forward implication of the Følner criterion (Følner 1955) <a id="pm-cite-2"></a>[[Folner1955](#ref-Folner1955)]: if $G$ is amenable, then for every finite $S\subset G$ and every $\varepsilon>0$, there is a nonempty finite $A\subset G$ such that $$\label{eq:folner}
  \frac{|hA\mathbin{\triangle}A|}{|A|}<\varepsilon
  \qquad(h\in S).$$ The modern left-translation formulation in (Moore 2013a, Theorem 1.2) <a id="pm-cite-3"></a>[[Moore2013](#ref-Moore2013)] bounds the sum of these ratios and hence implies each strict individual bound by taking a smaller tolerance. Thus amenability requires finite sets whose relative boundaries are simultaneously small under any fixed finite family of translations.

Thompson introduced $F$ in 1965; an early published construction appears in McKenzie and Thompson (McKenzie and Thompson 1973) <a id="pm-cite-4"></a>[[McKenzieThompson1973](#ref-McKenzieThompson1973)], with the identification explained by Cannon, Floyd, and Parry (Cannon, Floyd, and Parry 1996, 215–16) <a id="pm-cite-5"></a>[[CannonFloydParry1996](#ref-CannonFloydParry1996)]. The latter authors record Geoghegan’s 1979 conjecture that $F$ is nonamenable (Cannon, Floyd, and Parry 1996, 227) <a id="pm-cite-6"></a>[[CannonFloydParry1996](#ref-CannonFloydParry1996)]. We prove this conjecture.

<a id="stmt-thm-main"></a>
<div id="thm:main" class="theorem">

**Theorem 1**. *Thompson’s group $F$ is not amenable.*

</div>

<a id="pm-h-3"></a>
###### Context and prior work.

Two classical structural facts help explain the difficulty of the amenability problem. Brin and Squier show that $F$ contains no nonabelian free subgroup (Brin and Squier 1985, Theorem 3.1) <a id="pm-cite-7"></a>[[BrinSquier1985](#ref-BrinSquier1985)], so the familiar free-subgroup obstruction does not apply. Yet $F$ is not elementary amenable (Cannon, Floyd, and Parry 1996, Theorem 4.10) <a id="pm-cite-8"></a>[[CannonFloydParry1996](#ref-CannonFloydParry1996)]: it lies outside the class generated by finite and abelian groups under subgroups, quotients, extensions, and directed unions. Neither fact decides ordinary amenability. The group also has strong topological finiteness properties: Brown and Geoghegan construct a classifying space with finitely many cells in each dimension (Brown and Geoghegan 1984) <a id="pm-cite-9"></a>[[BrownGeoghegan1984](#ref-BrownGeoghegan1984)].

Finite approximations have provided several ways to study the remaining question. Moore proves tower lower bounds on the sizes of possible Følner sets (Moore 2013b, Theorem 1.1) <a id="pm-cite-10"></a>[[MooreFolner2013](#ref-MooreFolner2013)] and characterizes amenability of $F$ by a scalar convex Ramsey property for finite rooted ordered binary trees (Moore 2013a, Theorem 3.1) <a id="pm-cite-11"></a>[[Moore2013](#ref-Moore2013)]. Guba improves densities of finite Cayley subgraphs (Guba 2025) <a id="pm-cite-12"></a>[[Guba2025Density](#ref-Guba2025Density)] and derives restrictions on right-invariant means from a partition into seven diagram-defined classes (Guba 2026) <a id="pm-cite-13"></a>[[Guba2026](#ref-Guba2026)]. From an operator-algebraic direction, Haagerup and Olesen prove that simplicity of the reduced group $C^*$-algebra of Thompson’s group $T$ would imply nonamenability of $F$ (Haagerup and Olesen 2017, Theorem 5.5) <a id="pm-cite-14"></a>[[HaagerupOlesen2017](#ref-HaagerupOlesen2017)]. These results expose constraints on amenability through finite sets, trees, diagrams, and representations. The proof below uses finite dyadic partitions and scalar correlations of Hilbert-valued colors.

There have also been claims of a complete resolution in both directions. Akhmedov’s 2021 version claims nonamenability through a height-function criterion (Akhmedov 2021) <a id="pm-cite-15"></a>[[Akhmedov2021](#ref-Akhmedov2021)]. Shavgulidze claimed amenability (Shavgulidze 2009) <a id="pm-cite-16"></a>[[Shavgulidze2009](#ref-Shavgulidze2009)]; Moore identified errors in that approach (Moore 2011) <a id="pm-cite-17"></a>[[Moore2011Critique](#ref-Moore2011Critique)]. Moore separately withdrew an amenability claim after Akhmedov identified an error in Lemma 4.13 of that manuscript (Moore 2012) <a id="pm-cite-18"></a>[[Moore2012Withdrawn](#ref-Moore2012Withdrawn)]. That withdrawal concerns a different work from his published Ramsey characterization cited above.

<a id="pm-h-4"></a>
###### Proof strategy.

The proof turns approximate translation invariance of finite averages of scalar functions on $F$ into an approximate fixed point of a Lipschitz map on a Hilbert ball. The following infinite-dimensional phenomenon supplies a map for which that conclusion is impossible.

<a id="stmt-lem-displacement"></a>
<div id="lem:displacement" class="lemma">

**Lemma 2** (Benyamini–Sternfeld). *There are a real Hilbert space $H$, a Lipschitz map $f:B\to B$ on its closed unit ball, and a constant $\delta>0$ such that $$\lVert f(x)-x\rVert\ge\delta\qquad(x\in B).$$*

</div>

Benyamini and Sternfeld prove this assertion for every infinite-dimensional normed space (Benyamini and Sternfeld 1983, Theorem, part (3), p. 439) <a id="pm-cite-19"></a>[[BenyaminiSternfeld1983](#ref-BenyaminiSternfeld1983)]. For completeness, Appendix <a href="#app:auxiliary" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="app:auxiliary">4</a> constructs such a map on $L^2([0,1];\mathbb R^2)$ with $\delta=1/2$, including all the needed Lipschitz estimates.

Fix this map $f$, a positive Lipschitz constant $L$, and a large integer $D$. A basic dyadic interval is a cell of a uniform dyadic partition, and a basic partition is a finite partition into such cells. Choose $D$ basic intervals with positive gaps between them and endpoints in $(0,1)$, called parents. Put an affine copy of this family inside each parent, calling the copies descendants. When a parent is a union of partition cells, restricting to it and stretching back to $[0,1]$ gives its normalized restriction. Color each basic partition recursively by applying $f$ to the mean of its parent-restriction colors when all these restrictions are defined, and by zero otherwise. Proper restriction decreases the number of cells, so the recursion is well founded. For a sufficiently fine image partition under $g\in F$, its parent colors $X_i$ and descendant colors $Y_{ij}$ therefore satisfy $$m=\frac1D\sum_{i=1}^D X_i,\qquad
 z_i=\frac1D\sum_{j=1}^D Y_{ij},\qquad
 X_i=f(z_i)\quad(1\le i\le D).$$

The group $F$ can carry any ordered pair of separated basic intervals with endpoints in $(0,1)$ affinely onto any other such pair. Choose a finite set $S$ of these transports comparing every separated pair among the parents and descendants with one reference pair. The set $S$ is fixed before the arbitrary nonempty finite set $A\subset F$; only the common partition level, chosen fine enough for $A\cup SA$, may depend on $A$. Write $\eta$ for the largest relative $S$-boundary of $A$. Exact covariance of normalized restrictions and finite-average cancellation make all averaged scalar correlations of separated interval colors differ from one common value by at most $\eta$.

Expand the average over $A$ of $\lVert z_i-m\rVert^2$. The common correlation cancels; diagonal and nested pairs occupy only a fraction $1/D$ of each relevant sum, as Figure <a href="#fig:mixed-pairs" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="fig:mixed-pairs">1</a> illustrates. Unit-ball bounds control those exceptions, giving a universal constant times $D^{-1}+\eta$. Since $m$ is also the mean of the $f(z_i)$, convexity and Lipschitz continuity bound the average squared displacement $\lVert m-f(m)\rVert^2$ by a constant times $L^2(D^{-1}+\eta)$. For large $D$ and small $\eta$, this contradicts the uniform lower bound $\delta^2$. Section <a href="#sec:proof" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:proof">2</a> makes the argument precise and proves a uniform positive boundary bound in Proposition <a href="#avg:boundary" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="avg:boundary">5</a>, contradicting <a href="#eq:folner" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:folner">[eq:folner]</a>.

Section <a href="#sec:consequences" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:consequences">3</a> records consequences for uniformly bounded representations and percolation, using separate companion theorems.

<a id="sec:proof"></a>
### Recursive colors and finite averages

We prove Theorem <a href="#thm:main" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:main">1</a> using the map supplied by Lemma <a href="#lem:displacement" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:displacement">2</a>. The argument uses a single finite average over the group. We first record the dyadic facts that make its correlations comparable.

<a id="sec:dyadic"></a>
#### Dyadic partitions and affine transport

We use the standard dyadic-partition model of $F$; see (Cannon, Floyd, and Parry 1996, sec. 2, Lemma 2.2) <a id="pm-cite-20"></a>[[CannonFloydParry1996](#ref-CannonFloydParry1996)]. We record the restriction and pair-transport facts needed for the finite averages.

A *basic dyadic interval* is an interval $$[k2^{-r},(k+1)2^{-r}]\subseteq[0,1],
  \qquad r\in\mathbb N\cup\{0\},\quad 0\leq k<2^r.$$ A *basic partition* is a finite partition of $[0,1]$ into such intervals; cells may share endpoints. Its mesh is the greatest length of a cell. A partition $T$ *respects* an interval $I$ if $I$ is a union of cells of $T$.

<a id="dyad:restriction"></a>
For a basic interval $I$, let $s_I:[0,1]\to I$ be the increasing affine map, and write $I\cdot J=s_I(J)$. If $T$ respects $I$, define its normalized restriction by $$T_I=\{s_I^{-1}(K):K\in T,\ K\subseteq I\}.$$ Both $I\cdot J$ and the cells of $T_I$ are basic dyadic intervals. Indeed, if $I$ has length $2^{-r}$, a basic cell contained in $I$ has length $2^{-s}$ with $s\geq r$; applying $s_I^{-1}$ gives a cell of length $2^{-(s-r)}$ whose left endpoint is a multiple of that length. The affine charts satisfy $s_{I\cdot J}=s_I\circ s_J$. Consequently, whenever $T$ respects both $I$ and $I\cdot J$, the partition $T_I$ respects $J$ and $$\label{dyad:restriction}
  (T_I)_J=T_{I\cdot J}.$$

Let $T^{(n)}$ be the uniform partition of $[0,1]$ into intervals of length $2^{-n}$. For $g\in F$, the notation $gT^{(n)}$ denotes the partition formed by the images of these cells.

<a id="stmt-dyad-eventual"></a>
<div id="dyad:eventual" class="lemma">

**Lemma 3**. *For each $g\in F$, the partitions $gT^{(n)}$ are basic for all sufficiently large $n$, and their meshes tend to zero. In particular, given a finite set $K\subset F$ and a finite family $\mathcal J$ of basic intervals, there is an $n_0$ such that $gT^{(n)}$ is basic and respects every member of $\mathcal J$ whenever $g\in K$ and $n\geq n_0$.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-dyad-eventual"><summary>Proof of stmt-dyad-eventual</summary>

*Proof.* The image under $g$ of each breakpoint is dyadic: starting from $g(0)=0$, sum the increments on the preceding linear pieces, each a power of two times a dyadic length. Thus every linear piece has the form $x\mapsto 2^q x+b$ with $q\in\mathbb Z$ and $b$ dyadic. Choose $n$ large enough to resolve all breakpoints, to have $n\geq q$ on every piece, and to make each corresponding $b$ an integer multiple of $2^{q-n}$. The image of each uniform cell is then a basic interval of length $2^{q-n}$. There are finitely many slopes, so the mesh tends to zero.

Two basic intervals with intersecting interiors are nested. Hence a basic partition of mesh smaller than the length of a basic interval $I$ respects $I$: every cell whose interior meets that of $I$ must be contained in $I$. Apply this observation to the finitely many intervals in $\mathcal J$, and then take the maximum of the finitely many thresholds for $g\in K$. ◻

</details>

Write $I<J$ when $\max I<\min J$; in particular this notation requires a positive gap. An interval is *internal* if both its endpoints lie in $(0,1)$.

<a id="stmt-dyad-transport"></a>
<div id="dyad:transport" class="lemma">

**Lemma 4**. *If $I<J$ and $I'<J'$ are pairs of internal basic intervals, there is an $h\in F$ whose restrictions carry $I$ affinely onto $I'$ and $J$ affinely onto $J'$.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-dyad-transport"><summary>Proof of stmt-dyad-transport</summary>

*Proof.* The two selected intervals leave three complementary gaps, all of positive length and with dyadic endpoints. Partition each gap into basic cells, for example by a sufficiently fine uniform dyadic grid. Do this for both pairs. Within each corresponding pair of gaps, equalize the numbers of cells by successively bisecting cells on the side with fewer cells. Each bisection increases the number by one. The source and target partitions now have the same number of cells, with the two selected intervals in matching positions. Map corresponding cells increasingly and affinely. All breakpoints are dyadic and all slopes are ratios of basic lengths, hence powers of two. This defines the required element of $F$. ◻

</details>

<a id="dyad:covariance"></a>
The relevant covariance is exact. If $h$ carries $I$ affinely onto $I'$, then $h\circ s_I=s_{I'}$. Thus, if $gT^{(n)}$ and $(hg)T^{(n)}$ are basic and respect $I$ and $I'$, respectively, $$\label{dyad:covariance}
  \bigl((hg)T^{(n)}\bigr)_{I'}=(gT^{(n)})_I.$$ Here, as throughout, $hg=h\circ g$. To verify the equality, match each source cell $C$ with its image $h(C)$ and apply $s_{I'}^{-1}\circ h=s_I^{-1}$ on $I$.

<a id="sec:coloring"></a>
#### The recursive coloring

<a id="color:parameters"></a>
Fix a real Hilbert space $H$, its closed unit ball $B$, and a Lipschitz map $f:B\to B$ as in Lemma <a href="#lem:displacement" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:displacement">2</a>. Fix $L>0$ and $\delta>0$ such that <a id="color:map"></a>$$\label{color:map}
  \lVert f(x)-f(y)\rVert\leq L\lVert x-y\rVert,\qquad
  \lVert f(x)-x\rVert\geq\delta
  \quad(x,y\in B).$$ Choose an integer $D\geq2$ so that $$\label{color:parameters}
  D>\frac{4L^2}{\delta^2}.$$ Choose internal basic intervals $I_1<\cdots<I_D$. Such a family exists for every finite $D$: take $2^r>2D$ and use $I_j=[(2j-1)2^{-r},2j2^{-r}]$.

Define a color $p(T)\in B$ for every basic partition $T$ by induction on its number of cells: <a id="color:recursion"></a>$$\label{color:recursion}
  p(T)=
  \begin{cases}
  \displaystyle f\left(\frac1D\sum_{j=1}^D p(T_{I_j})\right),
       &\text{if $T$ respects every $I_j$},\\[6pt]
  0,   &\text{otherwise}.
  \end{cases}$$ This induction is well founded. In the first case each $T_{I_j}$ has strictly fewer cells than $T$, since $I_j$ is internal and $T$ has cells outside it. The average of the previously defined colors belongs to the convex ball $B$, so the argument of $f$ is always in its domain, and $p(T)\in B$.

For the displayed choice $|I_j|=2^{-r}$, the one-cell partition $T^{(0)}$ has color zero, while $$p(T^{(r)})=f(0),\qquad
  p(T^{(2r)})=f(f(0)).$$ Indeed, normalizing a restriction to any $I_j$ changes these uniform partition levels from $r$ to $0$ and from $2r$ to $r$, respectively.

We use the finite family of parent intervals and their descendants $$\mathcal I=\{I_i:1\leq i\leq D\}
       \cup\{I_i\cdot I_j:1\leq i,j\leq D\}.$$ All these intervals are internal. Call $(n,g)$ *admissible* if $gT^{(n)}$ is a basic partition respecting every member of $\mathcal I$. For every $n$, every $g\in F$, and every $I\in\mathcal I$, define <a id="color:global"></a>$$\label{color:global}
  X_I^{(n)}(g)=
  \begin{cases}
    p\bigl((gT^{(n)})_I\bigr),&\text{if $(n,g)$ is admissible},\\
    0,&\text{otherwise}.
  \end{cases}$$ In particular these are globally defined $B$-valued functions. Their scalar correlations $$\varphi_{I,J}^{(n)}(g)
     =\langle X_I^{(n)}(g),X_J^{(n)}(g)\rangle$$ are defined on all of $F$ and have absolute value at most one. By Lemma <a href="#dyad:eventual" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="dyad:eventual">3</a>, every finite set of group elements is admissible at one common sufficiently large level $n$.

<a id="sec:boundary"></a>
#### A uniform boundary bound

For every ordered pair $I<J$ in $\mathcal I$, choose, using Lemma <a href="#dyad:transport" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="dyad:transport">4</a>, an element $h_{I,J}\in F$ carrying $I,J$ affinely onto $I_1,I_2$, respectively. Let $S$ be the finite set of these elements. All choices so far, in particular $S$, are independent of the finite set to be tested.

<a id="eq:finite-translation"></a>
For any nonempty finite $A\subset F$, any $h\in F$, and any $\varphi:F\to[-1,1]$, cancellation over $hA\cap A$ gives $$\label{eq:finite-translation}
 \left|\frac1{|A|}\sum_{g\in A}\varphi(hg)
       -\frac1{|A|}\sum_{g\in A}\varphi(g)\right|
 \le\frac{|hA\mathbin{\triangle}A|}{|A|}.$$ This is the finite-average comparison we will apply to the scalar correlations.

<a id="stmt-avg-boundary"></a>
<div id="avg:boundary" class="proposition">

<a id="avg:boundary-bound"></a>
**Proposition 5**. *For every nonempty finite set $A\subset F$, $$\label{avg:boundary-bound}
  \max_{h\in S}\frac{|hA\mathbin{\triangle}A|}{|A|}
  \geq \frac{\delta^2/L^2-4/D}{4(1-1/D)}>0.$$*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-avg-boundary"><summary>Proof of stmt-avg-boundary</summary>

<a id="avg:defect"></a>
*Proof.* Fix such a set $A$ and put $$\label{avg:defect}
  \eta=\max_{h\in S}\frac{|hA\mathbin{\triangle}A|}{|A|}.$$ Choose a single $n$ for which $(n,g)$ is admissible for all $$g\in A\cup SA,
  \qquad SA=\bigcup_{h\in S}hA.$$ This choice is possible because that union is finite; the level $n$ may depend on $A$. We now fix this $n$, omit its superscript, and write $$\mathbb E_A\psi=\frac1{|A|}\sum_{g\in A}\psi(g),
  \qquad
  \alpha=\mathbb E_A\varphi_{I_1,I_2}.$$

<a id="pm-h-9"></a>
###### Comparing correlations.

<a id="avg:correlation"></a>
For $I<J$ in $\mathcal I$ and $g\in A$, admissibility of both $g$ and $h_{I,J}g$, together with <a href="#dyad:covariance" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="dyad:covariance">[dyad:covariance]</a>, gives $$X_I(g)=X_{I_1}(h_{I,J}g),\qquad
  X_J(g)=X_{I_2}(h_{I,J}g).$$ It follows that $\varphi_{I,J}(g)
=\varphi_{I_1,I_2}(h_{I,J}g)$. Apply <a href="#eq:finite-translation" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:finite-translation">[eq:finite-translation]</a> to the globally bounded function $\varphi_{I_1,I_2}$ and use <a href="#avg:defect" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="avg:defect">[avg:defect]</a>. We obtain $$\label{avg:correlation}
  \left|\mathbb E_A\langle X_I,X_J\rangle-\alpha\right|
       \leq\eta
  \qquad\text{whenever $I,J\in\mathcal I$ are strictly separated}.$$ The reversed order follows from symmetry of the real inner product. No condition is imposed on nested pairs. Also, a transport $h_{I,J}$ need not preserve the other intervals in $\mathcal I$: admissibility was required separately at its source and target group elements.

<a id="pm-h-10"></a>
###### The variance estimate.

<a id="avg:displacement"></a>
For $g\in A$, put $$m(g)=\frac1D\sum_{k=1}^D X_{I_k}(g),\qquad
  z_i(g)=\frac1D\sum_{j=1}^D X_{I_i\cdot I_j}(g)
       \quad(1\leq i\leq D).$$ These vectors lie in $B$. Admissibility ensures that the partition $(gT^{(n)})_{I_i}$ respects each $I_j$. Applying the recursion and <a href="#dyad:restriction" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="dyad:restriction">[dyad:restriction]</a> therefore yields the exact identities <a id="avg:recursive-identity"></a>$$\label{avg:recursive-identity}
  X_{I_i}(g)=f(z_i(g))\qquad(g\in A, 1\leq i\leq D).$$ Consequently, for every $g\in A$, the displacement bound, the convexity of the squared norm, and the Lipschitz bound give $$\begin{aligned}
  \delta^2
  &\leq\lVert m(g)-f(m(g))\rVert^2\notag\\
  &=\left\|\frac1D\sum_{i=1}^D
                   \bigl(f(z_i(g))-f(m(g))\bigr)\right\|^2\notag\\
  &\leq\frac1D\sum_{i=1}^D
                   \lVert f(z_i(g))-f(m(g))\rVert^2\notag\\
  &\leq\frac{L^2}{D}\sum_{i=1}^D\lVert z_i(g)-m(g)\rVert^2.
       \label{avg:displacement}
\end{aligned}$$

<a id="avg:parent-bound"></a>
<a id="avg:mixed-bound"></a>
We bound the finite average of each squared distance in the last line. In the expansion of $\lVert m\rVert^2$, the $D(D-1)$ off-diagonal terms pair strictly separated parents, so <a href="#avg:correlation" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="avg:correlation">[avg:correlation]</a> applies. Each of the $D$ diagonal terms is at most one. The same statements hold for the siblings in $\lVert z_i\rVert^2$. Hence <a id="avg:child-bound"></a>$$\begin{aligned}
  \mathbb E_A\lVert m\rVert^2
       &\leq\left(1-\frac1D\right)(\alpha+\eta)+\frac1D,
       \label{avg:parent-bound}\\
  \mathbb E_A\lVert z_i\rVert^2
       &\leq\left(1-\frac1D\right)(\alpha+\eta)+\frac1D.
       \label{avg:child-bound}
\end{aligned}$$ In the mixed term $$\langle z_i,m\rangle
    =\frac1{D^2}\sum_{j=1}^D\sum_{k=1}^D
                  \langle X_{I_i\cdot I_j},X_{I_k}\rangle,$$ the $D(D-1)$ terms with $k\ne i$ pair strictly separated intervals. Each has average at least $\alpha-\eta$. The remaining $D$ terms pair a descendant with its own parent; each is at least $-1$ by the unit-ball bound. Figure <a href="#fig:mixed-pairs" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="fig:mixed-pairs">1</a> shows this division of the mixed pairs. Thus $$\label{avg:mixed-bound}
  \mathbb E_A\langle z_i,m\rangle
    \geq\left(1-\frac1D\right)(\alpha-\eta)-\frac1D.$$

Expanding the squared distance and using <a href="#avg:parent-bound" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="avg:parent-bound">[avg:parent-bound]</a>–<a href="#avg:mixed-bound" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="avg:mixed-bound">[avg:mixed-bound]</a>, the coefficients of $\alpha$ cancel and give $$\label{avg:variance}
  \mathbb E_A\lVert z_i-m\rVert^2
    \leq\frac4D+4\left(1-\frac1D\right)\eta
    \qquad(1\leq i\leq D).$$ This estimate does not require any sign assumption on $\alpha$.

Average <a href="#avg:displacement" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="avg:displacement">[avg:displacement]</a> over $A$ and apply <a href="#avg:variance" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="avg:variance">[avg:variance]</a>: $$\begin{aligned}
  \delta^2
  &\leq\frac{L^2}{D}\sum_{i=1}^D
                 \mathbb E_A\lVert z_i-m\rVert^2\\
  &\leq L^2\left(\frac4D+4\left(1-\frac1D\right)\eta\right).
\end{aligned}$$ Rearranging proves <a href="#avg:boundary-bound" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="avg:boundary-bound">[avg:boundary-bound]</a>; its right-hand side is positive by <a href="#color:parameters" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="color:parameters">[color:parameters]</a> and does not depend on $A$. ◻

</details>

<details id="proof-stmt-avg-boundary-2"><summary>Proof of stmt-avg-boundary</summary>

*Proof of Theorem <a href="#thm:main" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:main">1</a>.* The fixed finite set $S$ and the uniform positive lower bound in Proposition <a href="#avg:boundary" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="avg:boundary">5</a> violate the Følner criterion <a href="#eq:folner" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:folner">[eq:folner]</a>. Hence $F$ is not amenable. ◻

</details>

<a id="sec:consequences"></a>
### Consequences

Theorem <a href="#thm:main" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:main">1</a> allows us to apply two companion results for nonamenable groups. The first concerns representations that are uniformly close to being isometric but cannot be made unitary by an equivalent Hilbert norm. The second concerns percolation on the simple Cayley graphs of $F$ associated with finite symmetric generating sets. Neither companion result is used in the proof of nonamenability.

<a id="pm-h-12"></a>
#### Uniformly bounded representations

<a id="stmt-cor-nonunitarizable"></a>
<div id="cor:nonunitarizable" class="corollary">

**Corollary 6**. *For every $\varepsilon>0$, there are a separable complex Hilbert space $H$ and a representation $\pi:F\to\mathrm{GL}(H)$ such that $\sup_{g\in F}\lVert \pi(g)\rVert\le1+\varepsilon$, but no bounded invertible operator $S$ on $H$ makes every $S\pi(g)S^{-1}$ unitary.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-cor-nonunitarizable"><summary>Proof of stmt-cor-nonunitarizable</summary>

*Proof.* The group $F$ is countable, by its finite dyadic piecewise linear description, and nonamenable by Theorem <a href="#thm:main" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:main">1</a>, so the conclusion follows from the companion unitarizability theorem (OpenAI 2026b, Theorem 1.1) <a id="pm-cite-21"></a>[[OpenAIUnitarizability2026](#ref-OpenAIUnitarizability2026)]. ◻

</details>

<a id="pm-h-13"></a>
#### Percolation on Cayley graphs

For a finite symmetric generating set $S\subset F\setminus\{\mathrm{id}\}$, let $G_S$ be the simple undirected Cayley graph with edges $\{x,xs\}$. In Bernoulli bond percolation on this fixed graph, each edge is independently open with probability $p$. Write $p_c$ for the threshold for existence of an infinite open cluster and $p_u$ for the threshold for almost-sure uniqueness of the infinite open cluster. The connection kernel $T_p(x,y)=\mathbb P_p(x\leftrightarrow y)$ acts on counting-measure $\ell^2(F)$, initially on finitely supported functions by summation over $y$; put $p_{2\to2}=\sup\{p\in[0,1]:T_p\text{ is bounded on }\ell^2(F)\}$.

<a id="stmt-cor-percolation"></a>
<div id="cor:percolation" class="corollary">

**Corollary 7**. *For every such generating set $S$, $$\lVert T_{p_c}\rVert_{2\to2}<\infty,\qquad p_c<p_{2\to2}\le p_u.$$ For each fixed $p\in(p_c,p_u)$, there are almost surely infinitely many infinite open clusters. Moreover, there are deterministic $p_c<p_1<p_2<1$, depending on $S$, such that in the coupling with independent uniform $[0,1]$ edge labels $U_e$, opening $e$ when $U_e\le p$, there are almost surely infinitely many infinite open clusters simultaneously for every $p\in[p_1,p_2]$.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-cor-percolation"><summary>Proof of stmt-cor-percolation</summary>

*Proof.* Apply the companion percolation result (OpenAI 2026a, Corollary 1.2) <a id="pm-cite-22"></a>[[OpenAIPercolation2026](#ref-OpenAIPercolation2026)] to $F$, which is nonamenable by Theorem <a href="#thm:main" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:main">1</a>. ◻

</details>

### An explicit map with positive displacement

We give a direct construction of the map needed in Lemma <a href="#lem:displacement" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:displacement">2</a>. This proves the Hilbert-space instance of the Benyamini–Sternfeld theorem (Benyamini and Sternfeld 1983) <a id="pm-cite-23"></a>[[BenyaminiSternfeld1983](#ref-BenyaminiSternfeld1983)] directly. The aim is to construct a Lipschitz map $G:B\to H$ bounded away from zero and equal to the identity when $\lVert x\rVert\ge1/2$. Then $f(x)=-G(x)/\lVert G(x)\rVert$ has the required displacement. A tube about a curve with a bounded tail supplies $G$.

<a id="stmt-aux-map"></a>
<div id="aux:map" class="proposition">

**Proposition 8**. *Let $H=L^2([0,1];\mathbb R^2)$ be a real Hilbert space and let $B=\{x\in H:\lVert x\rVert\le1\}$. There is a globally Lipschitz map $f:B\to B$ such that $$\lVert f(x)\rVert=1,
  \qquad \lVert f(x)-x\rVert\ge\frac12
  \quad(x\in B).$$*

</div>

Define functions on $\mathbb R$ by $$a(t)=
 \begin{cases}
 t/8,&t\le2,\\
 5/16-(3-t)^2/16,&2<t<3,\\
 5/16,&t\ge3,
 \end{cases}
 \qquad
 \theta(t)=
 \begin{cases}
 0,&t\le1,\\
 (t-1)^2/2,&1<t<2,\\
 t-3/2,&t\ge2.
 \end{cases}$$ For $w\in[0,1]$, put $$v(t)(w)=(\cos(\theta(t)w),\sin(\theta(t)w)),
 \qquad \gamma(t)=a(t)v(t).$$ Thus $\lVert v(t)\rVert=1$, $\gamma(0)=0$, and $\gamma$ follows a straight line for $t\le1$; its tail for $t\ge1$ lies in the ball of radius $5/16$. The next lemma shows that, although the positive tail is bounded, curve points whose parameters differ by a fixed amount remain uniformly separated. This permits a tube of fixed radius along the whole curve.

<a id="stmt-aux-tube"></a>
<div id="aux:tube" class="lemma">

<a id="aux:parameter"></a>
**Lemma 9**. *The curve $\gamma$ is injective, has a bounded Lipschitz derivative, and $$c:=\inf_{t\in\mathbb R}\lVert \gamma'(t)\rVert=\frac18.$$ Its unit tangent $u(t)=\gamma'(t)/\lVert \gamma'(t)\rVert$ is Lipschitz, $\langle u(t),v(t)\rangle\ge0$, and $u(t)=v(t)$ for $t\le1$. There is a radius $0<\rho<1/32$ such that every point in $$U=\{x\in H:\operatorname{dist}(x,\gamma(\mathbb R))<\rho\}$$ has a unique nearest point $\gamma(t(x))$. Writing $e(x)=x-\gamma(t(x))$, we have $e(x)\perp u(t(x))$, and $$\label{aux:parameter}
 |t(x)-t(y)|\le16\lVert x-y\rVert
 \quad\text{if }x,y\in U\text{ and }\lVert x-y\rVert<\rho.$$*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-aux-tube"><summary>Proof of stmt-aux-tube</summary>

*Proof.* Let $J(\xi_1,\xi_2)=(-\xi_2,\xi_1)$. Since $0\le w\le1$, the scalar trigonometric Taylor remainders are uniform in $w$, which justifies differentiation in $H$. We obtain $$\gamma'(t)(w)=a'(t)v(t)(w)+a(t)\theta'(t)wJv(t)(w),
 \qquad
 \lVert \gamma'(t)\rVert^2=a'(t)^2+\frac{a(t)^2\theta'(t)^2}{3}.$$ For $t\le2$ the first term is $1/64$; for $t\ge2$ we have $a(t)\ge1/4$ and $\theta'(t)=1$. Equality in the claimed lower speed bound holds for $t\le1$. The first derivatives match at the junctions $1,2,3$, and their derivatives on the intervening intervals are bounded. On the unbounded negative interval the angular derivatives vanish. Consequently $\gamma'$ is bounded and Lipschitz. The same is true of $v$ and $u$, and $\langle u(t),v(t)\rangle=a'(t)/\lVert \gamma'(t)\rVert\ge0$.

<a id="aux:separation"></a>
We need the following uniform separation property: $$\label{aux:separation}
 \sigma(d):=\inf_{|t-s|\ge d}\lVert \gamma(t)-\gamma(s)\rVert>0
 \qquad(d>0).$$ Indeed, with $\operatorname{sinc}(q)=\sin(q)/q$ for $q\ne0$ and $\operatorname{sinc}(0)=1$, we have $$\langle v(s),v(t)\rangle=\operatorname{sinc}(\theta(t)-\theta(s)).$$ Since $|\operatorname{sinc}(q)|<1$ for $q\ne0$, distinct angular parameters give noncollinear unit vectors. On $(-\infty,1]$ the coefficient $a$ is strictly increasing, whereas $\theta$ is strictly increasing on $(1,\infty)$ and $a$ is positive there. This proves injectivity.

If <a href="#aux:separation" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="aux:separation">[aux:separation]</a> failed, choose $s_n<t_n$ with $t_n-s_n\ge d$ and $\lVert \gamma(t_n)-\gamma(s_n)\rVert\to0$, and pass to subsequences for which both parameters have limits in the extended real line. Finite limits contradict injectivity. If both limits are $-\infty$, the distances equal $(t_n-s_n)/8$ eventually. If only $s_n\to-\infty$, the first radii tend to infinity while the second remain bounded. If $s_n\to s\in\mathbb R$ and $t_n\to+\infty$, the displayed inner product tends to zero, and the squared distances tend to $a(s)^2+(5/16)^2$. Finally, if both parameters tend to $+\infty$, then eventually $$\lVert \gamma(t_n)-\gamma(s_n)\rVert^2
 =2(5/16)^2\bigl(1-\operatorname{sinc}(t_n-s_n)\bigr).$$ This is bounded away from zero because $\sup_{q\ge d}\operatorname{sinc}(q)<1$, by continuity and decay at infinity. These cases establish <a href="#aux:separation" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="aux:separation">[aux:separation]</a>.

<a id="aux:tangent"></a>
Write $K=\mathop{\mathrm{Lip}}(\gamma')$ and $K_u=\mathop{\mathrm{Lip}}(u)$. Choose $d>0$ with $Kd\le c/2$. The estimate $$\lVert \gamma(t)-\gamma(s)-(t-s)\gamma'(s)\rVert
 \le\frac K2|t-s|^2$$ then implies $$\label{aux:tangent}
 |\langle \gamma(t)-\gamma(s),u(s)\rangle|
 \ge\frac{3c}{4}|t-s|
 \qquad(|t-s|\le d).$$ Choose $\rho>0$ so that <a id="aux:radius"></a>$$\label{aux:radius}
 \rho<\frac1{32},\qquad 4\rho<\sigma(d),\qquad \rho K_u<\frac c4.$$

For $x\in U$, take a minimizing sequence $\gamma(t_n)$ for the distance from $x$ to the curve. Its late terms satisfy $\lVert x-\gamma(t_n)\rVert<2\rho$, so their pairwise distances are less than $4\rho$. By <a href="#aux:separation" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="aux:separation">[aux:separation]</a> their parameters differ by less than $d$. Fixing one late parameter therefore confines all later ones to a compact real interval. A convergent subsequence attains the minimum. Differentiating $\lVert x-\gamma(t)\rVert^2$ at any minimizing parameter gives $(x-\gamma(t))\perp u(t)$.

For $x,y\in U$ with $\lVert x-y\rVert<\rho$, choose any minimizing parameters $t,s$ and write $x=\gamma(t)+e_t$, $y=\gamma(s)+e_s$. The curve points are less than $3\rho$ apart, so $|t-s|<d$. Orthogonality and <a href="#aux:tangent" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="aux:tangent">[aux:tangent]</a> give $$\begin{aligned}
 \lVert x-y\rVert
 &\ge |\langle \gamma(t)-\gamma(s),u(s)\rangle|
       -|\langle e_t,u(s)-u(t)\rangle|\\
 &\ge\left(\frac{3c}{4}-\rho K_u\right)|t-s|
 \ge\frac c2|t-s|.
\end{aligned}$$ Taking $y=x$ proves uniqueness; the same inequality proves <a href="#aux:parameter" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="aux:parameter">[aux:parameter]</a>. ◻

</details>

We next arrange that a residual perpendicular to the curve’s tangent is carried to a vector perpendicular to $v(t)$. This will let us change the coefficient along $v(t)$ without cancellation by that residual.

<a id="stmt-aux-rotation"></a>
<div id="aux:rotation" class="lemma">

**Lemma 10**. *There is a family of orthogonal operators $R_t:H\to H$, Lipschitz in operator norm, such that $R_tu(t)=v(t)$ and $R_t$ is the identity for $t\le1$.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-aux-rotation"><summary>Proof of stmt-aux-rotation</summary>

*Proof.* Suppress $t$ and put $\kappa=\langle u,v\rangle\ge0$. Define $$Ah=v\langle u,h\rangle-u\langle v,h\rangle,
 \qquad R=\mathrm{Id}+A+\frac{A^2}{1+\kappa}.$$ If $u,v$ are independent, then $A$ is skew-adjoint, $A^2=-(1-\kappa^2)\mathrm{Id}$ on their span, and $R$ restricts there to $\kappa\mathrm{Id}+A$. This restriction is orthogonal and sends $u$ to $v$, while $R$ is the identity on the orthogonal complement. If $u=v$, then $A=0$ and $R=\mathrm{Id}$. These exhaust the cases because $\kappa\ge0$. The formula, the Lipschitz dependence of $u,v$, and $1+\kappa\ge1$ show that $t\mapsto R_t$ is Lipschitz in operator norm, including where $u=v$. ◻

</details>

<details id="proof-stmt-aux-rotation-2"><summary>Proof of stmt-aux-rotation</summary>

<a id="aux:G"></a>
*Proof of Proposition <a href="#aux:map" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="aux:map">8</a>.* Fix the tube radius from Lemma <a href="#aux:tube" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="aux:tube">9</a> and the rotations from Lemma <a href="#aux:rotation" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="aux:rotation">10</a>. Set $$b(t)=\min\{a(t),-1/8\},\qquad
 q(t)=b(t)-a(t)=-\max\{a(t)+1/8,0\}.$$ Then $|q(t)|\le7/16$ and $\mathop{\mathrm{Lip}}(q)\le1/8$. Let $\beta,\chi:[0,\infty)\to[0,1]$ be the continuous piecewise linear functions specified by $$\beta(r)=
 \begin{cases}
 1,&r\le\rho/4,\\
 2-4r/\rho,&\rho/4<r<\rho/2,\\
 0,&r\ge\rho/2,
 \end{cases}
 \qquad
 \chi(r)=
 \begin{cases}
 1,&r\le\rho/2,\\
 2-2r/\rho,&\rho/2<r<\rho,\\
 0,&r\ge\rho.
 \end{cases}$$ Define $G:B\to H$ to equal the identity outside $U$. For $x\in B\cap U$, write $t=t(x)$, $e=x-\gamma(t)$, $r=\lVert e\rVert$, and set $$\label{aux:G}
 \begin{split}
 G(x)&=\gamma(t)+\beta(r)q(t)v(t)
       +\bigl((1-\chi(r))\mathrm{Id}+\chi(r)R_t\bigr)e\\
 &=x+\beta(r)q(t)v(t)+\chi(r)(R_t-\mathrm{Id})e.
 \end{split}$$

First, $G$ is globally Lipschitz on $B$. For tube points at distance less than $\rho$, <a href="#aux:parameter" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="aux:parameter">[aux:parameter]</a> controls their parameters, and, with $M=\sup_t\lVert \gamma'(t)\rVert$, their residuals satisfy $$\lVert e(x)-e(y)\rVert\le(1+16M)\lVert x-y\rVert.$$ Moreover, $r(x)=\operatorname{dist}(x,\gamma(\mathbb R))$ is $1$-Lipschitz. Every factor in the second line of <a href="#aux:G" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="aux:G">[aux:G]</a> is therefore Lipschitz for such pairs with uniform constants; the factors are uniformly bounded, using $\lVert e\rVert<\rho$, $|q|\le7/16$, $\lVert v\rVert=1$ and $\lVert R_t\rVert=1$. The product estimates give one finite Lipschitz bound for all these pairs. Also, on all of $B$, $$\lVert G(x)\rVert\le1+7/16+2\rho<3/2,$$ so pairs at distance at least $\rho$ are controlled by $3/\rho$. For the remaining pairs, let $x\in U$ and $y\notin U$. Both cutoffs satisfy $\beta(r),\chi(r)\le2(\rho-r)/\rho$, whence $$\lVert G(x)-x\rVert
 \le\left(\frac{7}{8\rho}+4\right)(\rho-r).$$ Since distance to the curve is $1$-Lipschitz and $y\notin U$, $\rho-r\le\lVert x-y\rVert$. Together with $G(y)=y$, this proves the required bound across the tube boundary. Outside the tube $G$ is the identity.

<a id="aux:nonzero"></a>
Next, we claim that $$\label{aux:nonzero}
 \lVert G(x)\rVert\ge\rho/4\qquad(x\in B).$$ Outside $U$, the fact that $0=\gamma(0)$ gives $\lVert G(x)\rVert=\lVert x\rVert\ge\rho$. Inside $U$, if $r\le\rho/2$, then $\chi(r)=1$ and $R_te\perp v(t)$, with $\lVert R_te\rVert=r$. For $r\le\rho/4$, the coefficient along $v(t)$ is $b(t)\le-1/8$; for $\rho/4\le r\le\rho/2$, the perpendicular component has norm at least $\rho/4$. If $\rho/2\le r<\rho$, then $\beta(r)=0$ and the last term in the first line of <a href="#aux:G" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="aux:G">[aux:G]</a> has norm at most $r<\rho$. When $t>1$, we have $a(t)\ge1/8$, so $\lVert G(x)\rVert\ge1/8-\rho>\rho/4$. When $t\le1$, we have $R_t=\mathrm{Id}$ and $e\perp v(t)$, so $\lVert G(x)\rVert\ge r\ge\rho/2$. This proves <a href="#aux:nonzero" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="aux:nonzero">[aux:nonzero]</a>.

Finally, $G(x)=x$ whenever $\lVert x\rVert\ge1/2$. This holds by definition outside $U$. Inside $U$, the reverse triangle inequality gives $$|a(t)|=\lVert \gamma(t)\rVert\ge\lVert x\rVert-r>1/2-\rho>5/16.$$ Since $a(t)\le5/16$ everywhere, this forces $a(t)<-1/8$ and $t\le1$. Thus $q(t)=0$ and $R_t=\mathrm{Id}$, so <a href="#aux:G" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="aux:G">[aux:G]</a> again gives $G(x)=x$.

Define $$f(x)=-\frac{G(x)}{\lVert G(x)\rVert}.$$ Normalization on vectors of norm at least $\rho/4$ is Lipschitz with constant at most $8/\rho$, so $f$ is globally Lipschitz by <a href="#aux:nonzero" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="aux:nonzero">[aux:nonzero]</a>, and its values have norm one. If $\lVert x\rVert<1/2$, then $\lVert f(x)-x\rVert\ge1-\lVert x\rVert>1/2$. Otherwise $G(x)=x$, and $\lVert f(x)-x\rVert=1+\lVert x\rVert\ge3/2$. ◻

</details>
