## 1. Abstract and result contract

> We prove that every inclusion $`N\subset M`$ of von Neumann algebras with separable preduals and a faithful normal conditional expectation contains an expected amenable subalgebra $`P\subset N`$ such that $`P'\cap c(M)=N'\cap c(M)`$, where $`c(M)`$ denotes the continuous core of <i>M</i>. This resolves the relative bicentralizer conjecture in this setting.

- **C1** (theorem, [source](#stmt-thm-main)): Let $N\subset M$ be a unital inclusion of von Neumann algebras with separable preduals, and suppose that there is a faithful normal conditional expectation $E_N:M\to N$. There exist a unital amenable von Neumann subalgebra $P\subset N$ and a faithful normal conditional expectation $F:N\to P$ such that $$P'\cap c(M)=N'\cap c(M).$$ Here amenable means injective.
  - Explanation: Every unital inclusion of von Neumann algebras with separable preduals and a faithful normal conditional expectation admits an expected unital amenable subalgebra with the same relative commutant in the continuous core.
- **C2** (conditional_result, [source](#stmt-cor-masa)): Under the hypotheses of Theorem <a href="#thm:main" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:main">1</a>, there is a maximal abelian subalgebra $A\subset N$ with a faithful normal conditional expectation $F:N\to A$ such that $$A'\cap c(M)=c(A)\vee\bigl(N'\cap c(M)\bigr).$$
  - Explanation: Under the hypotheses of the main theorem, the lower algebra contains an expected maximal abelian subalgebra whose relative commutant in the continuous core is generated by its own core and the relative commutant of the lower algebra.
- **C3** (conditional_result, [source](#stmt-prop-inputs)): In this setting the following hold.* 1. *If a state $\Phi$ on $\mathbf B(\mathcal H)$ satisfies $$\Phi\circ\lambda=\Phi\circ\rho=\varphi,\qquad \Phi(e_N)=1,\qquad \Phi(f(X_M))=f(0)\quad(f\in C_0(\mathbb R)),$$ then, for $a,b\in B$, $$\label{eq:binormal} \Phi(\lambda(a)\rho(b))=\langle a\Omega b,\Omega\rangle.$$* 2. *For each $s\in\mathbb R$ there is a unital completely positive map $\theta_s:\mathbf B(\mathcal H)\to\mathbf B(\mathcal H)$ such that $$\begin{aligned} \theta_s(e_N)&=e_N,& \theta_s\circ\rho&=\rho,\label{eq:shift1}\\ \theta_s\circ\lambda&=\lambda\circ\gamma_s\circ E_B,& \theta_s(f(X_M))&=f(X_M-s)\quad(f\in C_0(\mathbb R)).\label{eq:shift2} \end{aligned}$$ These maps need not be normal.
  - Explanation: States on the bounded operators of the standard Hilbert space with prescribed left and right state marginals, unit value on the lower-algebra projection, and zero modular energy evaluate on products of left and right relative bicentralizer actions via the standard inner product, and completely positive spectral shift maps translate the modular generator while inducing the bicentralizer flow.
- **C4** (theorem, [source](#stmt-thm-absolute)): For every type $\mathrm{III}_1$ factor $N$ with separable predual and every faithful normal state $\varphi$ on $N$, one has $\mathrm B(N,\varphi)=\mathbb C1$.
  - Explanation: Every factor of the stated type with separable predual has scalar bicentralizer for every faithful normal state.
- **C5** (conditional_result, [source](#stmt-lem-products)): The expectations satisfy $$E_N\|_B=\varphi(\cdot)1,\qquad E_B\|_N=\varphi(\cdot)1.$$ Consequently the maps $$R(x\Omega\otimes a\Omega)=xa\Omega,\qquad L(x\Omega\otimes a\Omega)=ax\Omega \qquad(x\in N,\ a\in B)$$ extend to isometries from $H\otimes K$ to $\mathcal H$.
  - Explanation: The conditional expectations between the lower factor and the relative bicentralizer restrict to scalar state evaluations on each other, which makes both orders of multiplication extend to isometries from the Hilbert tensor product into the ambient standard space.
- **C6** (bound, [source](#stmt-prop-energy)): For every $\eta\in K$ there is a function $\epsilon_\eta:(0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)$ with $\epsilon_\eta(\delta)\to0$ as $\delta\downarrow0$ such that $$\label{eq:energy} \\|(C-D_{\mathrm{all}})(\xi\otimes\eta)\\| \le\epsilon_\eta(\delta)$$ for every $n\ge1$, every $d_0,\ldots,d_n\in\mathbb R$, and every unit vector $\xi\in H^{\otimes n}$ whose $S_n$-spectral support lies in an interval of length $\delta$. The same function works for all these choices and for all positions of the interval.
  - Explanation: The norm difference between an iterated product word and the corresponding ideal unitary word on a tensor vector whose total modular energy lies in a narrow interval is bounded by a modulus depending only on the last-factor vector and the interval length, uniformly in tensor length, shifts, and interval position.
- **C7** (construction, [source](#stmt-lem-kernel)): Formula <a href="#eq:Zkernel" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:Zkernel">[eq:Zkernel]</a> defines a unital completely positive map $Z:\mathbf B(\mathcal H)\to\mathbf B(H^{\otimes n})$, possibly nonnormal. It satisfies $$\begin{aligned} Z(\lambda(a))&=Z(\rho(a))=\varphi(a)1\quad(a\in M), &Z(e_N)&=1,\label{eq:Zmarg}\\ Z(f(X_M))&=f(S_n+d)\quad(f\in C_0(\mathbb R)).\label{eq:Zspectral} \end{aligned}$$ For modular-analytic $a\in B$, put $a_+=\sigma_{i/2}(a)$, so that $\Omega a_+=a\Omega$. Then, for $b\in B$ and $\xi,\xi'\in H^{\otimes n}$, $$\label{eq:Zmixed} \langle Z(b^*\rho(a_+))\xi,\xi'\rangle =\langle C(\xi\otimes a\Omega),\xi'\otimes b\Omega\rangle.$$
  - Explanation: The backward recurrence of shifted completely positive maps defines a unital completely positive map from the bounded operators on the standard space to the tensor power that preserves left and right state marginals, shifts the modular generator by the total modular energy plus the total shift, and reproduces matrix coefficients of the iterated operator word.
- **C8** (bound, [source](#stmt-lem-hybrid)): The estimate <a href="#eq:energy" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:energy">[eq:energy]</a> holds with the same modulus if any subset of the $U_j$ is replaced by $D_j$, or if all $U_j$ are replaced by $G_j=(U_j+D_j)/2$. For every $\delta>0$ there is a probability density $p_\delta$ on $\mathbb R$ such that, for every unit $\xi\in H^{\otimes n}$ without a spectral support restriction, $$\label{eq:timeavg} \int_\mathbb R\\|(C-D_{\mathrm{all}})(e^{itS_n}\xi\otimes\eta)\\|^2 p_\delta(t)\,dt\le\epsilon_\eta(\delta)^2.$$ The statement includes the hybrid and averaged words.
  - Explanation: The uniform modular energy estimate remains valid with the same modulus when any subset of step contractions is replaced by ideal unitaries or by their averages, and integrating against a smooth Fourier probability density removes the spectral support restriction on the tensor vector.
- **C9** (theorem, [source](#stmt-thm-ud)): For the operators in <a href="#eq:UD" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:UD">[eq:UD]</a>, one has $U=D$.
  - Explanation: The relative multiplication comparison contraction equals the unitary operator generated by the tensor product of the modular generator and the bicentralizer flow generator.
- **C10** (conditional_result, [source](#stmt-prop-corefixed)): If $N$ is a type $\mathrm{III}_1$ factor, then $$\mathrm B(N\subset c(M),\varphi)^\gamma=N'\cap c(M).$$
  - Explanation: When the lower algebra is a factor of the stated type, the fixed-point algebra of the extended bicentralizer flow on the relative bicentralizer in the continuous core coincides with the relative commutant of the lower algebra in the continuous core.

## 2. Definitions, validity and bounds

- **expected inclusion** ([source](#pm-h-2)): An inclusion $N\subset M$ is *expected* if it admits a faithful normal conditional expectation $E_N:M\to N$.
- **continuous core** ([source](#pm-h-2)): For a faithful normal state $\psi$ on $M$, the continuous core is $c(M)=M\rtimes_{\sigma^\psi}\mathbb R$, with its canonical copy of $M$.
- **amenable von Neumann algebra** ([source](#stmt-thm-main)): Here amenable means injective.
- **maximal abelian subalgebra** ([source](#pm-h-5)): A maximal abelian subalgebra, or MASA, $A\subset N$ satisfies $A'\cap N=A$.
- **standard Hilbert space and modular generator** ([source](#sec:background)): Put $$\mathcal H=L^2(M,\varphi),\qquad \Omega=\varphi^{1/2},\qquad X_M=\log\Delta_\varphi.$$
- **predual module conventions** ([source](#sec:background)): Our predual module conventions are $$(x\varphi)(y)=\varphi(yx),\qquad (\varphi x)(y)=\varphi(xy).$$
- **relative analytic bicentralizer** ([source](#sec:background)): Its relative analytic bicentralizer $B=\mathrm B(N\subset M,\varphi)$ consists of those $a\in M$ for which $[a,x_n]\to0$ strongly whenever $(x_n)$ is a bounded sequence in $N$ with $\\|x_n\varphi-\varphi x_n\\|_{N_*}\to0$.
- **bicentralizer flow logarithmic convention** ([source](#pm-h-9)): Write $\gamma:\mathbb R\curvearrowright B$ for the bicentralizer flow in the following logarithmic convention: an element $v\in N^\omega$ of modular frequency $s$, meaning $\sigma_t(v)=e^{its}v$, satisfies $$\label{eq:frequency} va=\gamma_s(a)v\qquad(a\in B).$$
- **relative multiplication contraction and unitary twist** ([source](#eq:UD)): Define the contraction $U$ and the unitary $D$ on $H\otimes K$ by $$\label{eq:UD} U=L^*R,\qquad D=\exp(iX\otimes Y).$$
- **iterated completely positive kernel** ([source](#eq:Zkernel)): For $x_j,y_j\in N$, define a kernel by $$\label{eq:Zkernel} \left\langle Z(T)\bigotimes_{j=1}^n x_j\Omega, \bigotimes_{j=1}^n y_j\Omega\right\rangle =\langle T_0\Omega,\Omega\rangle,$$ where the operators $T_j\in\mathbf B(\mathcal H)$ are obtained backwards: $$T_n=\theta_{-d_n}(T),\qquad T_{j-1}=\theta_{-d_{j-1}}(y_j^*T_jx_j) \quad(j=n,\ldots,1).$$
- **bandlimited element** ([source](#pm-h-21)): Call $x\in N$ bandlimited if it admits a finite bound $L\ge0$ such that, for every interval $J$, $$x\mathbf 1_J(X_M)\mathcal H \subset\mathbf 1_{J+[-L,L]}(X_M)\mathcal H.$$
- **weak Dixmier property** ([source](#pm-h-28)): For an expected inclusion, that conjecture is equivalent to the *weak Dixmier property* for $N\subset c(M)$: $$\label{eq:WD} \overline{\mathop{\mathrm{conv}}\{uxu^*:u\in\mathcal U(N)\}}^{\,w^*} \cap(N'\cap c(M))\ne\varnothing\qquad(x\in c(M)).$$

## 3. Proof roadmap

- **R1** [claim](#stmt-prop-inputs) / [proof](#pm-h-10): Combine the binormal-state evaluation at zero modular energy with modularly smoothed partial-isometry rows in the Ocneanu ultrapower to construct unital completely positive spectral shift maps. -> Binormal state inner-product identity and spectral shift maps on the bounded operators of the standard Hilbert space.
- **R2** [claim](#stmt-thm-absolute) / [proof](#pm-h-15): Reduce to a self-bicentralizing factor with scalar centralizer, average the modular action along an ultrafilter mean to make left and right multiplications isometric, apply the binormal identity and reflection involution to force a joint spectral lattice relation, and exclude pure point and axial spectra by KMS mass comparison and flow ergodicity. -> Triviality of the absolute bicentralizer for every factor of the stated type with separable predual.
- **R3** [claim](#stmt-lem-products) / [proof](#sec:products): Apply the conditional expectation onto the lower factor to the asymptotic commutation condition, use absolute bicentralizer triviality to show mutual expectations are scalar state evaluations, and polarize inner products. -> Isometric extensions of left and right relative multiplication maps on the Hilbert tensor product.
- **R4** [claim](#stmt-lem-kernel) / [proof](#pm-h-19): Iterate the spectral shift maps and left multiplications backwards to construct a unital completely positive map into the tensor power, and use bandlimited labels with a spectral cutoff radius to justify the continuous functional calculus without normality. -> Unital completely positive kernel map reproducing word coefficients and total modular energy shifts.
- **R5** [claim](#stmt-prop-energy) / [proof](#pm-h-22): Evaluate the iterated kernel state on vectors with narrowly supported total modular energy, apply weak* compactness of the state space on the fixed operator algebra and the binormal identity, and recenter arbitrary intervals by a final shift. -> Approximation bound between iterated operator words and ideal unitary words uniform in tensor length, shifts, and interval position.
- **R6** [claim](#stmt-lem-hybrid) / [proof](#sec:repeat): Disintegrate replaced unitary positions over their modular spectral variables, expand averaged contractions convexly, and apply vector-valued Plancherel integration against a smooth compactly supported window. -> Time-averaged squared-norm error bound for hybrid and averaged words without spectral support restrictions.
- **R7** [claim](#stmt-thm-ud) / [proof](#pm-h-24): Propagate fresh spectrally localized vectors through randomly shifted averaged step operators, combine the parallelogram norm loss and capture probabilities on linearly growing intervals, and contradict finite total telescoping norm loss via divergence of the harmonic capture sum. -> Exact operator identity equating the relative multiplication contraction and the unitary exponential twist.
- **R8** [claim](#stmt-prop-corefixed) / [proof](#pm-h-30): Identify the standard space of the generated subalgebra with the tensor product via left multiplication, untwist the action of the lower factor by conjugating with the unitary twist in the continuous core representation, and observe that flow-fixed elements act only on the complementary tensor factor. -> Equality of the core bicentralizer flow fixed-point algebra and the relative commutant in the continuous core for a lower factor of the stated type.
- **R9** [claim](#stmt-thm-main) / [proof](#pm-h-33): Combine the factor cases with direct-integral disintegration over the center of the lower algebra, average arbitrary core elements into the centralizer core via the compact-convex fixed-point theorem, and invoke the weak Dixmier characterization of expected amenable subalgebras. -> Existence of a unital amenable expected subalgebra preserving the relative commutant in the continuous core.
- **R10** [claim](#stmt-cor-masa) / [proof](#pm-h-5): Apply the maximal abelian subalgebra characterization to the expected amenable subalgebra obtained in the main theorem. -> Existence of an expected maximal abelian subalgebra with the stated relative commutant in the continuous core.

## 4. Engineering Triptych

### Panel 1 - epistemic contract

- **INV-1**: Every unital inclusion $N\subset M$ of von Neumann algebras with separable preduals admitting a faithful normal conditional expectation $E_N:M\to N$ contains a unital amenable von Neumann subalgebra $P\subset N$ with a faithful normal conditional expectation $F:N\to P$ such that $$P'\cap c(M)=N'\cap c(M).$$

## 6. Applicability limits and search

- **L1** (hypothesis): The inclusion of von Neumann algebras is required to be unital and both algebras must have separable preduals; separability supplies standard measurable fields for the direct-integral disintegration over the center. Blocked use: ['Applying the relative commutant preservation result to von Neumann algebras with non-separable preduals without checking whether the central disintegration and weak Dixmier characterization still hold', 'Applying the main inclusion theorem to non-unital inclusions']
- **L2** (hypothesis): The inclusion must be expected, admitting a faithful normal conditional expectation from the ambient algebra onto the subalgebra; passing directly to the continuous core as the upper algebra fails because the original inclusion does not supply an expectation from the continuous core onto the subalgebra. Blocked use: ['Applying the theorem to inclusions of von Neumann algebras that do not admit a faithful normal conditional expectation', 'Applying subalgebra preservation results directly to the inclusion inside the continuous core as if a conditional expectation from the continuous core onto the subalgebra were available']
- **L3** (hypothesis): Obtaining a maximal abelian subalgebra of the bottom algebra that remains maximal abelian in the ambient algebra from the continuous core commutant formula requires the additional core commutant condition that the relative commutant of the bottom algebra in the continuous core is contained in the continuous core of the bottom algebra. Blocked use: ['Concluding that the expected maximal abelian subalgebra produced by the corollary is maximal abelian in the ambient algebra without verifying the additional core commutant inclusion']
- **L4** (formal_scope): The bicentralizer triviality theorem, the mutual scalar expectation identities, and the continuous core bicentralizer fixed-point identity are proved specifically for the regime where the bottom algebra is a type $\mathrm{III}_1$ factor, whereas other factor types and general centers are handled via external reductions and central disintegration. Blocked use: ['Invoking scalar restriction of expectations between the subalgebra and its relative bicentralizer when the bottom algebra is not a type $\\mathrm{III}_1$ factor', 'Using the continuous core bicentralizer fixed-point formula directly without the type $\\mathrm{III}_1$ factor assumption']
- **L5** (nonconstructive): The existence of the expected amenable subalgebra and the intermediate spectral shift maps rely on nonconstructive functional-analytic tools, including free ultrafilters on $\mathbb N$, Ocneanu state ultrapowers, weak limits along ultrafilters, weak* accumulation states, and compact-convex fixed-point averaging. Blocked use: ['Treating the theorem as an explicit finite algorithm that constructs the amenable subalgebra or its conditional expectation', 'Assuming the spectral shift maps or the iterated kernel map are normal (weak* continuous) completely positive maps']
- **L6** (approximation): The uniform energy estimate bounds the norm difference between the word operator and the ideal unitary word by an abstract modulus $\epsilon_\eta(\delta)\to0$ as $\delta\downarrow0$ obtained by a compactness contradiction in the state space of $\mathbf B(\mathcal H)$, without an explicit quantitative rate in $\delta$, and requires the total modular energy $S_n$-spectral support of the unit vector $\xi$ to lie in an interval of length $\delta$. Blocked use: ['Using the uniform energy estimate as a quantitative finite-sample error bound with an explicit convergence rate in $\\delta$', 'Applying the unaveraged word estimate directly to vectors whose total modular energy is not localized to an interval of length $\\delta$']

### Introduction

An inclusion $N\subset M$ is *expected* if it admits a faithful normal conditional expectation $E_N:M\to N$. Such an expectation makes the inclusion compatible with modular theory: a faithful normal state on $N$ extends through $E_N$ to a state on $M$ whose modular action preserves $N$. For a faithful normal state $\psi$ on $M$, the continuous core is $c(M)=M\rtimes_{\sigma^\psi}\mathbb R$, with its canonical copy of $M$.

We ask whether an amenable expected subalgebra of $N$ can impose exactly the same commutation constraints as $N$ on the whole core. Equality of commutants inside $M$ alone does not answer this question: the core also contains the unitaries implementing the modular action. Our main theorem retains these additional constraints.

<a id="stmt-thm-main"></a>
<div id="thm:main" class="theorem">

**Theorem 1**. *Let $N\subset M$ be a unital inclusion of von Neumann algebras with separable preduals, and suppose that there is a faithful normal conditional expectation $E_N:M\to N$. There exist a unital amenable von Neumann subalgebra $P\subset N$ and a faithful normal conditional expectation $F:N\to P$ such that $$P'\cap c(M)=N'\cap c(M).$$ Here amenable means injective.*

</div>

The canonical core isomorphism associated with a change of faithful normal state fixes $M$, so the stated relative-commutant equality is independent of the state used to define the core.

<a id="pm-h-3"></a>
###### History and the relative conjecture.

Popa’s solution of Kadison’s problem for irreducible expected inclusions with separable preduals and type II bottom factor provides a model for finding amenable subalgebras that retain prescribed commutation properties <a id="pm-cite-1"></a>[[Popa1981](#ref-Popa1981)]. For type III factors, modular asymptotic centralization supplies a related approach. The bicentralizer of a faithful normal state consists of those elements that commute asymptotically with every bounded sequence asymptotically centralizing the state in predual norm. Connes introduced the problem of its triviality in the classification of injective factors <a id="pm-cite-2"></a>[[Connes1982](#ref-Connes1982), [Connes1985](#ref-Connes1985)]. Haagerup solved the amenable type $\mathrm{III}_1$ case, completing the uniqueness theorem for the injective type $\mathrm{III}_1$ factor <a id="pm-cite-3"></a>[[Haagerup](#ref-Haagerup)]. For type $\mathrm{III}_1$ factors with separable predual, he characterized triviality by norm-density of faithful normal states whose centralizers have trivial relative commutant <a id="pm-cite-4"></a>[[Haagerup](#ref-Haagerup), Theorem 3.1]. His proof uses Popa’s construction to find a maximal abelian subalgebra of such a centralizer that remains maximal abelian in the ambient factor; the expectations onto the centralizer and onto its abelian subalgebra compose <a id="pm-cite-5"></a>[[Haagerup](#ref-Haagerup), proof of Theorem 3.1].

Masuda introduced a relative bicentralizer in the finite-index setting <a id="pm-cite-6"></a>[[Masuda2005](#ref-Masuda2005), Definition 4.1]. Ando, Haagerup, Houdayer, and Marrakchi constructed the canonical relative bicentralizer flow for expected inclusions with type $\mathrm{III}_1$ bottom factor <a id="pm-cite-7"></a>[[AHHM](#ref-AHHM), Theorem A]. This extends the absolute flow discovered by Haagerup and independently by Marrakchi, as described in that paper. The structural results of Houdayer and Isono <a id="pm-cite-8"></a>[[HI2017](#ref-HI2017), Proposition 3.4 and Theorem 3.5] and Marrakchi’s absolute flow-ergodicity theorem <a id="pm-cite-9"></a>[[M0](#ref-M0), Theorem D] underlie the absolute reduction used here. Marrakchi formulated the relative bicentralizer conjecture and proved that, for separable preduals, it is equivalent to the conclusion of Theorem <a href="#thm:main" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:main">1</a> . Our theorem therefore resolves that conjecture positively, including inclusions with arbitrary centers and types.

Marrakchi’s recent Theorem B gives an expected amenable subalgebra $P\subset N$ preserving $N'\cap M$ for general expected inclusions with separable preduals. Theorem <a href="#thm:main" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:main">1</a> preserves the commutant in the continuous core. Applying the earlier result with upper algebra $c(M)$ would require an expectation from $c(M)$ onto $N$, which the original inclusion does not supply. The binormal-state and zero-shift completely positive map of <a id="pm-cite-10"></a>[[M2](#ref-M2), Lemmas 2.1 and 2.2(1)] are essential inputs to the proof below.

<a id="pm-h-4"></a>
###### The absolute problem and the recent announcement.

Houdayer and Marrakchi have announced that every type $\mathrm{III}_1$ factor admitting a faithful normal state has trivial bicentralizer <a id="pm-cite-11"></a>[[HoudayerMarrakchi2026](#ref-HoudayerMarrakchi2026), Theorem G]; their theorem statement has no separability restriction. Section <a href="#sec:absolute" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:absolute">3</a> gives an alternative proof of its separable-predual instance as part of our relative argument. The multiplication maps and spectral resonance of <a id="pm-cite-12"></a>[[HoudayerMarrakchi2026](#ref-HoudayerMarrakchi2026), Section 8] are closely related to this absolute comparison. Their paper also develops relative bicentralizer theory for tracial group actions; the general expected-inclusion core-commutant conclusion of Theorem <a href="#thm:main" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:main">1</a> is obtained here through the relative tensor and core argument.

The companion <a id="pm-cite-13"></a>[[BoundedRecovery](#ref-BoundedRecovery), Theorem 4.1 and Section 5.1] develops a complementary absolute argument through bounded recovery: for a state with scalar centralizer, a fixed multiplication defect whose modularly averaged squared norm has positive limsup along unit vectors with spectral supports shrinking to one point can be detected by uniformly bounded algebra elements. The binormal-state identity used here also admits the iteration that yields the relative estimate uniformly in tensor length and flow shifts.

<a id="pm-h-5"></a>
###### An abelian consequence.

A maximal abelian subalgebra, or MASA, $A\subset N$ satisfies $A'\cap N=A$. Another part of Marrakchi’s characterization gives such a subalgebra with a precise core commutant. For an expected $A\subset N$, we view $c(A)$ inside $c(N)\subset c(M)$ using the expectation onto $A$ and its composition with $E_N$.

<a id="stmt-cor-masa"></a>
<div id="cor:masa" class="corollary">

**Corollary 2**. *Under the hypotheses of Theorem <a href="#thm:main" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:main">1</a>, there is a maximal abelian subalgebra $A\subset N$ with a faithful normal conditional expectation $F:N\to A$ such that $$A'\cap c(M)=c(A)\vee\bigl(N'\cap c(M)\bigr).$$*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-cor-masa"><summary>Proof of stmt-cor-masa</summary>

*Proof.* Apply to Theorem <a href="#thm:main" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:main">1</a>. ◻

</details>

For an irreducible expected inclusion of factors, Kadison’s problem asks for a MASA in $N$ that is also maximal abelian in $M$. The corollary gives such a MASA with expectation under the additional core condition $N'\cap c(M)\subset c(N)$: its commutant in $M$ then lies in $c(N)\cap M=N$, and hence equals $A$. This is the expected formulation identified in <a id="pm-cite-14"></a>[[M1](#ref-M1), Conjecture B and the discussion after Theorem D].

<a id="pm-h-6"></a>
###### Proof structure.

The analytic work concerns the case where $N$ is a type $\mathrm{III}_1$ factor. Choose a faithful normal state $\varphi$ on $N$ and extend it by $E_N$. The relative bicentralizer $B\subset M$ consists of elements that commute asymptotically with every bounded sequence in $N$ asymptotically centralizing $\varphi$. It carries a state-preserving flow $\gamma$. Section <a href="#sec:background" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:background">2</a> recalls these objects and the binormal-state identity used below. Existing bicentralizer criteria reduce our task to showing that the extension of $\gamma$ to $B\rtimes_{\sigma^\varphi}\mathbb R$ has fixed algebra $N'\cap c(M)$.

The connection with multiplication is the central point. Set $H=L^2(N,\varphi)$, $K=L^2(B,\varphi)$, and $\Omega=\varphi^{1/2}$. Consider maps from $H\otimes K$ to $L^2(M,\varphi)$, initially given for $x\in N$ and $a\in B$ by $$R(x\Omega\otimes a\Omega)=xa\Omega,\qquad
 L(x\Omega\otimes a\Omega)=ax\Omega.$$ We prove that these maps are isometries and that $$R=L\exp(iX\otimes Y),$$ where $X$ generates the modular action on $H$ and $Y$ generates the bicentralizer flow on $K$. After a change of coordinates in the core representation, $N$ acts on $H$ alone, whereas an element fixed by the bicentralizer flow acts independently of the $H$ coordinate. They therefore commute. This is the required fixed-point criterion.

The absolute theorem supplies the isometry assertion. If that theorem failed, the standard reduction would give a nontrivial factor equal to its own bicentralizer and with scalar modular fixed algebra. Its two multiplication maps become isometries after averaging the modular action. A binormal eigenstate identity gives their spectral comparison; reflection then forces a spectral lattice condition incompatible with modular ergodicity. Section <a href="#sec:absolute" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:absolute">3</a> carries out this argument, and Section <a href="#sec:products" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:products">4</a> applies the absolute theorem to obtain the unaveraged relative isometries.

To prove the relative comparison, Section <a href="#sec:energy" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:energy">5</a> packages words of arbitrary tensor length into states on one fixed operator algebra. The binormal-state identity yields an estimate uniform in tensor length, all intervening flow parameters, and the location of the total modular energy. The packaging maps are completely positive but may be nonnormal; a spectral cutoff argument justifies the functional calculus they require. Section <a href="#sec:repeat" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:repeat">6</a> removes the energy restriction by repeatedly testing a hypothetical discrepancy. Averaging the comparison contraction $L^*R$ with its proposed unitary value loses norm wherever they differ. Random shifts capture such a difference with probabilities whose sum diverges, while the same probabilities bound the propagated error. The resulting expected norm loss exceeds the available norm, so the comparison is exact.

Finally, Section <a href="#sec:core" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:core">7</a> proves the core fixed-point identity and derives the weak Dixmier property: every weak\* closed convex hull of an $N$-unitary orbit in $c(M)$ meets $N'\cap c(M)$. Marrakchi’s characterization then supplies the amenable subalgebra. Existing results handle the other factor types; a central decomposition and averaging over the center give the general inclusion.

### Modular notation and bicentralizer inputs

The absolute proof and the relative tensor estimate use states on the bounded operators of the standard Hilbert space whose left and right marginals are prescribed and whose modular energy is concentrated at one point. We first state the resulting bicentralizer identity and the completely positive maps that shift its energy parameter.

We use standard modular theory <a id="pm-cite-16"></a>[[Takesaki2003](#ref-Takesaki2003)] and the modular-invariance criterion for state-preserving expectations <a id="pm-cite-17"></a>[[Takesaki1972](#ref-Takesaki1972)]. We may assume that the algebras are nonzero. Choose a faithful normal state on $N$ and extend it by $E_N$; denote both states by $\varphi$. Put $$\mathcal H=L^2(M,\varphi),\qquad \Omega=\varphi^{1/2},\qquad
 X_M=\log\Delta_\varphi.$$ Scalar products are linear in the first variable. Our predual module conventions are $$(x\varphi)(y)=\varphi(yx),\qquad
 (\varphi x)(y)=\varphi(xy).$$ The left and right standard actions are denoted by $\lambda$ and $\rho$, with $\rho(b)\xi=\xi b$; we usually omit $\lambda$. If $A\subset M$ is invariant under $\sigma^\varphi$, its state-preserving expectation identifies $L^2(A,\varphi)$ with a subspace of $\mathcal H$. Write $e_A$ for that projection.

Fix a free ultrafilter $\omega$ on $\mathbb N$; all state ultrapowers below are Ocneanu ultrapowers <a id="pm-cite-18"></a>[[AndoHaagerup2014](#ref-AndoHaagerup2014)]. For now $N$ is a type $\mathrm{III}_1$ factor. Its relative analytic bicentralizer $B=\mathrm B(N\subset M,\varphi)$ consists of those $a\in M$ for which $[a,x_n]\to0$ strongly whenever $(x_n)$ is a bounded sequence in $N$ with $\|x_n\varphi-\varphi x_n\|_{N_*}\to0$. Equivalently, $B$ is the part of $M$ commuting in the state ultrapower with the centralizer of $N^\omega$. It is modularly invariant and has a $\varphi$-preserving faithful normal expectation $E_B$.

<a id="pm-h-9"></a>
###### The flow convention.

<a id="eq:frequency"></a>
Write $\gamma:\mathbb R\curvearrowright B$ for the bicentralizer flow in the following logarithmic convention: an element $v\in N^\omega$ of modular frequency $s$, meaning $\sigma_t(v)=e^{its}v$, satisfies $$\label{eq:frequency}
 va=\gamma_s(a)v\qquad(a\in B).$$ With the multiplicative parameter of <a id="pm-cite-19"></a>[[AHHM](#ref-AHHM)], this means $\gamma_s=\beta_{e^{-s}}$: modular frequency $s$ corresponds to $v\varphi^\omega=e^{-s}\varphi^\omega v$. The flow preserves $\varphi|_B$ and commutes with its modular action <a id="pm-cite-20"></a>[[AHHM](#ref-AHHM), [M1](#ref-M1)].

<a id="stmt-prop-inputs"></a>
<div id="prop:inputs" class="proposition">

**Proposition 3** (Binormal states and spectral shifts). *In this setting the following hold.*

1.  *If a state $\Phi$ on $\mathbf B(\mathcal H)$ satisfies $$\Phi\circ\lambda=\Phi\circ\rho=\varphi,\qquad
     \Phi(e_N)=1,\qquad
     \Phi(f(X_M))=f(0)\quad(f\in C_0(\mathbb R)),$$ then, for $a,b\in B$, <a id="eq:binormal"></a>$$\label{eq:binormal}
     \Phi(\lambda(a)\rho(b))=\langle a\Omega b,\Omega\rangle.$$*

2.  *For each $s\in\mathbb R$ there is a unital completely positive map $\theta_s:\mathbf B(\mathcal H)\to\mathbf B(\mathcal H)$ such that <a id="eq:shift1"></a><a id="eq:shift2"></a>$$\begin{aligned}
     \theta_s(e_N)&=e_N,& \theta_s\circ\rho&=\rho,\label{eq:shift1}\\
     \theta_s\circ\lambda&=\lambda\circ\gamma_s\circ E_B,&
     \theta_s(f(X_M))&=f(X_M-s)\quad(f\in C_0(\mathbb R)).\label{eq:shift2}
    \end{aligned}$$ These maps need not be normal.*

</div>

Part (i) is <a id="pm-cite-21"></a>[[M2](#ref-M2), Lemma 2.1], based on the binormal-state implementation <a id="pm-cite-22"></a>[[M1](#ref-M1), Theorem 2.9]. For part (ii), we combine the zero-shift map of <a id="pm-cite-23"></a>[[M2](#ref-M2), Lemma 2.2(1)] with the eigenoperator characterization of the flow in <a id="pm-cite-24"></a>[[AHHM](#ref-AHHM), Theorem A(ii)]. The construction below determines the spectral shift in convention <a href="#eq:frequency" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:frequency">[eq:frequency]</a>.

<a id="pm-h-10"></a>
###### The shift construction.

Let $\zeta$ be the map from <a id="pm-cite-25"></a>[[M2](#ref-M2), Lemma 2.2(1)], which has the properties in part (ii) for $s=0$. Choose a finite row of partial isometries $v_i\in N^\omega$ of modular frequency $s$ with $\sum_i v_iv_i^*=1$. Such rows exist by <a id="pm-cite-26"></a>[[AHHM](#ref-AHHM), Lemma 3.4 and the proof of Theorem A(ii)]. Starting with bounded representing sequences $w_{i,n}$, take the modularly smoothed lifts $$v_{i,n}=\int_\mathbb R\pi^{-1/2}e^{-u^2}e^{-isu}\sigma_u(w_{i,n})\,du.$$ Modular convolution commutes with passage to the ultrapower <a id="pm-cite-27"></a>[[AndoHaagerup2014](#ref-AndoHaagerup2014), Lemma 4.14]; since $v_i$ has frequency $s$, these are still lifts of $v_i$. Translation of the convolution kernel shows that their modular orbits are uniformly norm-continuous in $n$. Define $$\theta_s(T)=\underset{n\to\omega}{\mathrm{weak}\!\lim}\,
 \sum_i\lambda(v_{i,n})\zeta(T)\lambda(v_{i,n}^*).$$ The row normalization makes the limit unital. The right algebra and $e_N$ commute with all row entries, so they are fixed. Relation <a href="#eq:frequency" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:frequency">[eq:frequency]</a> gives the left restriction $\gamma_s\circ E_B$.

For the spectral formula, the modular action passes to the ultrapower <a id="pm-cite-28"></a>[[AndoHaagerup2014](#ref-AndoHaagerup2014), Theorem 4.1]. Hence, for each $t$, $\sum_i v_{i,n}\sigma_t(v_{i,n}^*)\to e^{-its}1$ strongly along $\omega$; the equicontinuity just obtained makes convergence uniform on compact $t$-intervals. Now $\zeta(f(X_M))=f(X_M)$ for $f\in C_0(\mathbb R)$. For a function of the form $f(x)=\int e^{itx}h(t)\,dt$, with $h\in L^1(\mathbb R)$, we may therefore integrate the finite-row conjugations to obtain $$\theta_s(f(X_M))
 =\int_\mathbb Re^{-its}\Delta^{it}h(t)\,dt=f(X_M-s).$$ Compact-uniform convergence of the row coefficients and the $L^1$ tail bound justify passing to the limit. Density gives the formula for all $f\in C_0(\mathbb R)$. This calculation fixes the sign and uses no normality of $\zeta$ or $\theta_s$.

<a id="eq:binormal-s"></a>
If the last hypothesis of (i) is instead $\Phi(f(X_M))=f(s)$, apply (i) to $\Phi\circ\theta_s$. The fixed right algebra belongs to the multiplicative domain of $\theta_s$, so $$\label{eq:binormal-s}
 \Phi\big(\lambda(\gamma_s(a))\rho(b)\big)
 =\langle a\Omega b,\Omega\rangle\qquad(a,b\in B).$$ The two marginals remain $\varphi$ because $\gamma_s$ and $E_B$ preserve it.

<a id="sec:absolute"></a>
### The absolute bicentralizer

We first use the binormal identity to settle the absolute problem. This will make the ordinary multiplication maps for a relative bicentralizer isometric in Section <a href="#sec:products" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:products">4</a>. In the absolute argument itself, averaging the modular action provides the two isometries to be compared.

<a id="stmt-thm-absolute"></a>
<div id="thm:absolute" class="theorem">

**Theorem 4**. *For every type $\mathrm{III}_1$ factor $N$ with separable predual and every faithful normal state $\varphi$ on $N$, one has $\mathrm B(N,\varphi)=\mathbb C1$.*

</div>

<a id="eq:self"></a>
Two established facts reduce the proof to a spectral contradiction. If the absolute conjecture fails, the self-bicentralizing reduction of <a id="pm-cite-29"></a>[[HI2017](#ref-HI2017), Proposition 3.4 and Theorem 3.5], recalled in <a id="pm-cite-30"></a>[[AHHM](#ref-AHHM)], gives a nontrivial type $\mathrm{III}_1$ factor with separable predual and a faithful normal state satisfying $$\label{eq:self}
 M=\mathrm B(M,\varphi),\qquad M_\varphi=\mathbb C1.$$ The absolute bicentralizer flow is ergodic <a id="pm-cite-31"></a>[[M0](#ref-M0), Theorem D]. Neither fact assumes fullness.

The averaged products, reflection, and spectral resonance below follow the architecture of <a id="pm-cite-32"></a>[[HoudayerMarrakchi2026](#ref-HoudayerMarrakchi2026), Propositions 8.8–8.10]. Here the averaging uses the modular action, and the binormal identity supplies the comparison between the products.

<details id="proof-stmt-thm-absolute"><summary>Proof of stmt-thm-absolute</summary>

*Proof.* Suppose that the theorem fails, and choose $(M,\varphi)$ as in <a href="#eq:self" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:self">[eq:self]</a>. Put $H=L^2(M,\varphi)$ and $X=\log\Delta_\varphi$. Let $e^{isY}$ implement $\gamma_s$ on $H$, so that $e^{isY}(a\Omega)=\gamma_s(a)\Omega$. Since the flow preserves the state and commutes with its modular action, $X$ and $Y$ strongly commute.

<a id="pm-h-13"></a>
###### Two products after averaging.

The scalar fixed algebra $M_\varphi$ implies that the weak\* averages of $\sigma_t(x)$ over $[-T,T]$ converge to $\varphi(x)1$. Indeed, any cluster point is modularly invariant and has state value $\varphi(x)$. We use this factorization to turn the two orders of multiplication into isometries.

Let $m$ be the symmetric translation-invariant mean obtained from these interval averages as $T\to\infty$ along an ultrafilter. Quotient bounded measurable $H$-valued functions by the null space of $m(\|f(t)\|^2)$, and complete to a Hilbert space $\mathcal H_m$. Define on elementary tensors $$\begin{aligned}
 R(x\Omega\otimes a\Omega)(t)&=\sigma_t(x)a\Omega,\\
 L(x\Omega\otimes a\Omega)(t)&=a\sigma_t(x)\Omega.
\end{aligned}$$ The inner products of the images of $x\Omega\otimes a\Omega$ and $y\Omega\otimes b\Omega$ are, respectively, $$m\big(\varphi(b^*\sigma_t(y^*x)a)\big),\qquad
 m\big(\varphi(y^*\sigma_{-t}(b^*a)x)\big).$$ Both equal $\varphi(y^*x)\varphi(b^*a)$. By linearity and completion, $R,L:H\otimes H\to\mathcal H_m$ are isometries. Translation $(\tau_u f)(t)=f(t+u)$ is unitary on $\mathcal H_m$ and satisfies $$\tau_uR=R(e^{iuX}\otimes1),\qquad
 \tau_uL=L(e^{iuX}\otimes1).$$

<a id="pm-h-14"></a>
###### From eigenstates to the product identity.

<a id="eq:absolute-RL"></a>
We claim that $$\label{eq:absolute-RL}
 R=L\exp(iX\otimes Y).$$ The binormal identity first compares the two sides on vectors whose $X$-spectrum lies in a short interval. Translation covariance then lets us combine these comparisons without accumulating an error over the intervals.

For a unit vector $h\in H$, define the state $$\Phi_h(T)=m\big(\langle Te^{itX}h,e^{itX}h\rangle\big).$$ The modular averages just computed give $\Phi_h\circ\lambda=\Phi_h\circ\rho=\varphi$; here $e_M=1$. Moreover, $\Phi_h(f(X))=\langle f(X)h,h\rangle$. For modular-analytic $a\in M$, put $a_+=\sigma_{i/2}(a)$, so that $\Omega a_+=a\Omega$. The definitions of $R$ and $L$ give $$R(h\otimes a\Omega)(t)=\rho(a_+)e^{itX}h,\qquad
 L(h\otimes a\Omega)(t)=ae^{itX}h.$$ These identities extend from $h=x\Omega$ to every $h\in H$ by continuity.

Suppose the spectral support of a sequence of unit vectors $h$ is contained in intervals shrinking to $s$. Every weak\* accumulation state of $\Phi_h$ then has spectral evaluation at $s$. By <a href="#eq:binormal-s" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:binormal-s">[eq:binormal-s]</a>, $$\begin{aligned}
 \langle R(h\otimes a\Omega),L(h\otimes\gamma_s(a)\Omega)\rangle
 &=\Phi_h\big(\lambda(\gamma_s(a^*))\rho(a_+)\big)\\
 &\longrightarrow\langle a^*\Omega a_+,\Omega\rangle
   =\|a\Omega\|^2.
\end{aligned}$$ Both vectors on the left have squared norm $\|a\Omega\|^2$, so their difference tends to zero.

This convergence is uniform when the centers $s$ range over a fixed compact set and the interval lengths tend to zero. Otherwise choose violating vectors and a convergent subsequence of centers, and take a weak\* accumulation state. The preceding argument gives a contradiction. In this step the varying test $\gamma_s(a^*)$ may be replaced by its limit: the common left marginal $\varphi$ and Cauchy–Schwarz bound the change by $$\|a_+\|\,
 \|\gamma_s(a)\Omega-\gamma_{s_0}(a)\Omega\|,$$ which tends to zero. Thus no operator-norm continuity of $\gamma$ is needed.

Analytic vectors $a\Omega$ are dense in $H$, and all maps being compared are isometric. Hence the same local conclusion holds for a fixed $\eta\in H$, with $\gamma_s(a)\Omega$ replaced by $e^{isY}\eta$. Spectral calculus gives $$\|\exp(iX\otimes Y)(h\otimes\eta)-h\otimes e^{isY}\eta\|
 \le \sup_{|r-s|\le\delta}\|e^{irY}\eta-e^{isY}\eta\|$$ whenever $\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}_X(h)\subset[s-\delta,s+\delta]$ and $\|h\|=1$. Consequently, for $T=R-L\exp(iX\otimes Y)$, a fixed compact interval $I$, and fixed $\eta$, there is a function $\alpha_{I,\eta}(\delta)\to0$ such that $$\|T(h\otimes\eta)\|\le\alpha_{I,\eta}(\delta)\|h\|$$ when $h$ has spectral support in an interval of length at most $\delta$ contained in $I$.

Translation covariance shows that $T^*T$ commutes with the spectral projections of $X\otimes1$. For $\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}_X(h)\subset I$, partition $I$ into finitely many disjoint Borel intervals $I_j$ of length at most $\delta$ and write $h_j=\mathbf 1_{I_j}(X)h$. The vectors $T(h_j\otimes\eta)$ are orthogonal, so $$\|T(h\otimes\eta)\|^2
 =\sum_j\|T(h_j\otimes\eta)\|^2
 \le\alpha_{I,\eta}(\delta)^2\sum_j\|h_j\|^2
 =\alpha_{I,\eta}(\delta)^2\|h\|^2.$$ Letting $\delta\downarrow0$ and then using density proves <a href="#eq:absolute-RL" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:absolute-RL">[eq:absolute-RL]</a>.

<a id="pm-h-15"></a>
###### Reflection forces a lattice.

<a id="eq:latticephase"></a>
Define $(J_0f)(t)=\Delta^{it}f(-t)$. Symmetry of $m$ makes $J_0$ a unitary involution on $\mathcal H_m$. On elementary tensors, $$J_0R(x\Omega\otimes a\Omega)(t)
 =\Delta^{it}\sigma_{-t}(x)a\Omega
 =x\sigma_t(a)\Omega
 =L(a\Omega\otimes x\Omega)(t).$$ Similarly $J_0L=R\Sigma$, where $\Sigma$ flips the input factors. Applying $J_0$ to <a href="#eq:absolute-RL" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:absolute-RL">[eq:absolute-RL]</a> and composing with $\Sigma$ gives $L=R\exp(iY\otimes X)$. Substitute this back into <a href="#eq:absolute-RL" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:absolute-RL">[eq:absolute-RL]</a> and cancel the isometry $R$ to obtain $$\label{eq:latticephase}
 \exp(iY\otimes X)\exp(iX\otimes Y)=1.$$

Let $S\subset\mathbb R^2$ be the support of the joint spectral measure of $(X,Y)$. The tensor-product spectral measure has support $S\times S$, so the continuous phase in <a href="#eq:latticephase" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:latticephase">[eq:latticephase]</a> satisfies <a id="eq:lattice"></a>$$\label{eq:lattice}
 rs'+sr'\in2\pi\mathbb Z
 \quad\text{for all }(r,s),(r',s')\in S.$$ If $S$ contains two linearly independent points, pairing with them confines $S$ to the inverse image of $(2\pi\mathbb Z)^2$ under an invertible linear map. Thus $S$ is countable. If $S$ spans a non-axis line, choose a nonzero $(r_0,s_0)\in S$; then $r_0s_0\ne0$, and any point $t(r_0,s_0)\in S$ satisfies $2tr_0s_0\in2\pi\mathbb Z$. Again $S$ is countable. In both cases the joint spectral measure is supported on countably many points, so $X$ has pure point spectrum. The remaining possibilities put $S$ on a coordinate axis and give $X=0$ or $Y=0$.

<a id="pm-h-16"></a>
###### Excluding the spectral alternatives.

A modular action with scalar fixed algebra has no Hilbert-space eigenvectors other than multiples of $\Omega$; we recall the argument of <a id="pm-cite-33"></a>[[MarrakchiVaes2024](#ref-MarrakchiVaes2024), Lemma 2.1]. Let $P_r$ be the spectral projection of $X$ at $r$. For $x\in M$, the bounded character averages $$x_T=\frac1{2T}\int_{-T}^T e^{-irt}\sigma_t(x)\,dt$$ satisfy $\|x_T\|\le\|x\|$ and $x_T\Omega\to P_r(x\Omega)$. A weak\* cluster point $y\in M$ represents this vector, since $\varphi(z^*x_T)$ converges to $\langle P_r(x\Omega),z\Omega\rangle$ for every $z\in M$. Thus $y\Omega=P_r(x\Omega)$, and the separating property of $\Omega$ gives $\sigma_t(y)=e^{irt}y$. Density of $M\Omega$ shows that any nonzero eigenspace is detected by a nonzero such eigenoperator.

For this $y$, both $y^*y$ and $yy^*$ lie in $M_\varphi=\mathbb C1$. Hence $y$ is a nonzero scalar multiple of a unitary $u$. Since $\sigma_{-i}(u)=e^r u$, the KMS identity gives $$1=\varphi(uu^*)=\varphi(u^*\sigma_{-i}(u))
   =e^r\varphi(u^*u)=e^r.$$ Therefore $r=0$, and then $y$ itself is scalar. It follows that the only eigenvectors of $X$ are multiples of $\Omega$. They cannot span $H$ because $M$ is nontrivial. This excludes pure point spectrum, including $X=0$. Finally, $Y=0$ would make the bicentralizer flow trivial on $M$, contradicting its ergodicity. All alternatives are excluded, proving the theorem. ◻

</details>

Return to an expected inclusion with $N$ a type $\mathrm{III}_1$ factor; $M$ remains arbitrary. Keep $B=\mathrm B(N\subset M,\varphi)$ and put $$H=L^2(N,\varphi),\qquad K=L^2(B,\varphi),\qquad
 X=\log\Delta_{\varphi|_N}.$$ Let $W_s=e^{isY}$ implement $\gamma_s$ on $K$. Since the flow preserves $\varphi|_B$, these unitaries commute with the modular implementation on $K$. The absolute theorem makes multiplication in either order isometric. This will let us compare the two products on the fixed Hilbert space $H\otimes K$.

 =\varphi(b^*)\varphi(x),$$ which characterizes $E_B(x)=\varphi(x)1$. Using $E_B$ for the first product and $E_N$ for the second gives $$\begin{aligned}

\end{aligned}$$ Polarization and completion prove the assertion. ◻

### An estimate uniform in total modular energy

<a id="eq:Cword"></a>
<a id="eq:Dall"></a>
For $n\ge1$, work on $H^{\otimes n}\otimes K$. Let $X_j$ act on the $j$th copy of $H$, and let $U_j,D_j$ act on that copy together with the common last factor $K$. Write $S_n=\sum_{j=1}^nX_j$ for the total modular generator on $H^{\otimes n}$. For arbitrary real numbers $d_0,\ldots,d_n$, set $$\begin{aligned}
 C&=W_{d_n}U_nW_{d_{n-1}}\cdots W_{d_1}U_1W_{d_0},\label{eq:Cword}\\
 D_{\mathrm{all}}&=W_d\exp(iS_n\otimes Y),\qquad
 d=\sum_{j=0}^n d_j.\label{eq:Dall}
\end{aligned}$$ Here every $W$ acts on $K$ alone. Replacing each $U_j$ in $C$ by $D_j$ gives $D_{\mathrm{all}}$, because those unitaries commute with all the shifts.

<a id="stmt-prop-energy"></a>

**Proposition 6** (Uniform energy estimate). *For every $\eta\in K$ there is a function $\epsilon_\eta:(0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)$ with $\epsilon_\eta(\delta)\to0$ as $\delta\downarrow0$ such that $$\label{eq:energy}
 \|(C-D_{\mathrm{all}})(\xi\otimes\eta)\|
 \le\epsilon_\eta(\delta)$$ for every $n\ge1$, every $d_0,\ldots,d_n\in\mathbb R$, and every unit vector $\xi\in H^{\otimes n}$ whose $S_n$-spectral support lies in an interval of length $\delta$. The same function works for all these choices and for all positions of the interval.*

</div>

The estimate must remain uniform even when both the tensor length and the shifts vary. To achieve this, we express a coefficient of $C$ through a state on the single algebra $\mathbf B(\mathcal H)$. The state will have fixed left and right marginals, while its modular distribution records the sum $S_n+d$. Proposition <a href="#prop:inputs" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:inputs">3</a>(i) can then be applied in one fixed state space.

For $x_j,y_j\in N$, define a kernel by $$\label{eq:Zkernel}
 \left\langle Z(T)\bigotimes_{j=1}^n x_j\Omega,
                  \bigotimes_{j=1}^n y_j\Omega\right\rangle
 =\langle T_0\Omega,\Omega\rangle,$$ where the operators $T_j\in\mathbf B(\mathcal H)$ are obtained backwards: $$T_n=\theta_{-d_n}(T),\qquad
 T_{j-1}=\theta_{-d_{j-1}}(y_j^*T_jx_j)
 \quad(j=n,\ldots,1).$$ The operators $x_j,y_j$ here act on $\mathcal H$ by left multiplication.

<div id="lem:kernel" class="lemma">

<a id="eq:Zmixed"></a>
**Lemma 7** (The iterated kernel). *Formula <a href="#eq:Zkernel" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:Zkernel">[eq:Zkernel]</a> defines a unital completely positive map $Z:\mathbf B(\mathcal H)\to\mathbf B(H^{\otimes n})$, possibly nonnormal. It satisfies $$\begin{aligned}
 Z(\lambda(a))&=Z(\rho(a))=\varphi(a)1\quad(a\in M),
 &Z(e_N)&=1,\label{eq:Zmarg}\\
 Z(f(X_M))&=f(S_n+d)\quad(f\in C_0(\mathbb R)).\label{eq:Zspectral}
\end{aligned}$$ For modular-analytic $a\in B$, put $a_+=\sigma_{i/2}(a)$, so that $\Omega a_+=a\Omega$. Then, for $b\in B$ and $\xi,\xi'\in H^{\otimes n}$, $$\label{eq:Zmixed}
 \langle Z(b^*\rho(a_+))\xi,\xi'\rangle
 =\langle C(\xi\otimes a\Omega),\xi'\otimes b\Omega\rangle.$$*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-kernel"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-kernel</summary>

*Proof.*

<a id="pm-h-19"></a>
###### Construction and marginals.

Take finitely many elementary tensors. For $T\ge0$, apply the recurrence simultaneously to their two indices. At each stage the operator matrix is multiplied on the left and right by the adjoint and the original of the diagonal matrix of that position’s labels. Its positivity is preserved, and complete positivity of $\theta_{-d_j}$ preserves it at the next stage. Taking the final $\Omega$ coefficients therefore gives a positive scalar kernel. For $T=1$, the identity $E_B|_N=\varphi(\cdot)1$ reduces this kernel to $$\prod_{j=1}^n\varphi(y_j^*x_j),$$ the Gram kernel of the elementary tensors. Applying positivity to $0\le T\le\|T\|1$ now bounds the quadratic form by $\|T\|$ times the tensor norm squared. Thus the form depends only on the tensor vector, extends to the completion, and defines a positive unital map. The same argument with an additional matrix index proves complete positivity.

For $T=\lambda(a)$, the first map produces $\gamma_{-d_n}(E_B(a))\in B$ with state value $\varphi(a)$. At a subsequent stage, an element $b\in B$ is replaced by $\gamma_{-d_{j-1}}(E_B(y_j^*bx_j))\in B$. Its state value is $$\varphi(y_j^*bx_j)=\varphi(b)\varphi(y_j^*x_j),$$ using $E_N|_B=\varphi(\cdot)1$. Iterating this scalar identity gives the left marginal in <a href="#eq:Zmarg" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:Zmarg">[eq:Zmarg]</a>. For the right marginal, the full right algebra is fixed by each $\theta$, hence belongs to its multiplicative domain. Right multipliers commute with the labels and pass through every stage, while $E_B|_N=\varphi(\cdot)1$ factors the labels as above. For $e_N$, use $[e_N,N]=0$ and the fact that the fixed projection $e_N$ also belongs to every map’s multiplicative domain. Since $e_N\Omega=\Omega$, the recurrence gives the same Gram kernel as for $T=1$. These computations establish all of <a href="#eq:Zmarg" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:Zmarg">[eq:Zmarg]</a>.

<a id="pm-h-20"></a>
###### The modular unitary group.

First extend the spectral formula for each individual $\theta_{-q}$ from $C_0(\mathbb R)$ to the functions $s\mathrel{\pdfliteral direct{/Span << /ActualText <FEFF21A6> >> BDC}\mapsto\pdfliteral direct{EMC}}e^{its}$. Its restriction to $C_0(X_M)$ is a homomorphism, so that algebra belongs to its multiplicative domain. For $f\in C_0(\mathbb R)$, it follows that $$f(X_M+q)\theta_{-q}(e^{itX_M})
 =\theta_{-q}(f(X_M)e^{itX_M})
 =f(X_M+q)e^{it(X_M+q)}.$$ The ranges of these cutoffs span a dense subspace of $\mathcal H$. Consequently $\theta_{-q}(e^{itX_M})=e^{it(X_M+q)}$; these unitaries also belong to the multiplicative domain. No limiting operation has been passed through $\theta_{-q}$.

<a id="eq:Zgroups"></a>
In the kernel recurrence, commute $e^{itX_M}$ to the right across each $x_j$, using $e^{itX_M}x_j=\sigma_t(x_j)e^{itX_M}$. The next map replaces the left factor $y_j^*\sigma_t(x_j)\in N$ by its state value. The resulting coefficient is $$e^{itd}\prod_{j=1}^n\varphi(y_j^*\sigma_t(x_j)),$$ which proves $$\label{eq:Zgroups}
 Z(e^{itX_M})=e^{it(S_n+d)}.$$ A nonnormal map need not preserve weak operator integrals, so this identity alone does not yet justify <a href="#eq:Zspectral" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:Zspectral">[eq:Zspectral]</a>. We next show that the coefficient states needed to recover continuous functions are supported inside a compact spectral interval.

<a id="pm-h-21"></a>
###### Spectral cutoffs and continuous functions.

Call $x\in N$ bandlimited if it admits a finite bound $L\ge0$ such that, for every interval $J$, $$x\mathbf 1_J(X_M)\mathcal H
 \subset\mathbf 1_{J+[-L,L]}(X_M)\mathcal H.$$ Such elements are strongly dense. Indeed, convolve the modular action with an approximate identity having smooth, compactly supported Fourier transforms. If the Fourier support of a smoothing kernel lies in $[-L,L]$, the resulting element has the displayed transfer property: testing $e^{itX_M}xe^{-itX_M}=\sigma_t(x)$ against scalar Fourier kernels shows that spectral pieces separated by more than $L$ have zero matrix coefficient.

Two operations in the kernel preserve a suitable interval on which a positive operator vanishes. Suppose that $A\ge0$ annihilates the spectral subspace associated with an open interval $I$. For $f\in C_c(I)$, $f(X_M)A=0$, so the multiplicative-domain identity gives $$f(X_M+q)\theta_{-q}(A)=0.$$ The cutoff ranges span the spectral subspace for $I-q$; thus $\theta_{-q}(A)$ annihilates that translated interval. If $x$ has transfer in $[-L,L]$, the preceding transfer rule also shows that $x^*Ax$ annihilates every interval $J$ with $J+[-L,L]\subset I$.

Apply these rules to a diagonal kernel with bandlimited labels $x_j$, of transfer bounds $L_j$, starting from $A=\mathbf 1_{\{|X_M|>R\}}$. Translating by each $-d_j$ and shrinking by each $L_j$ leaves an open interval about zero whenever $$R>\sum_{j=0}^n|d_j|+\sum_{j=1}^nL_j.$$ The final operator then annihilates $\Omega$, whose modular energy is zero. Hence its diagonal kernel coefficient vanishes. For a finite sum of bandlimited elementary tensors, choose one $R$ that works for every term; positivity and Cauchy–Schwarz make the off-diagonal coefficients vanish as well. These tensor sums form a dense subspace of $H^{\otimes n}$.

For a unit vector $\xi$ in this subspace, set $\Phi_\xi(T)=\langle Z(T)\xi,\xi\rangle$ and $P_R=\mathbf 1_{[-R,R]}(X_M)$. We have proved $\Phi_\xi(1-P_R)=0$. Cauchy–Schwarz therefore yields $$\Phi_\xi(T)=\Phi_\xi(P_RTP_R).$$ The transfer property applied to $\Omega$ also puts $x_j\Omega$ in the $X$-spectral subspace for $[-L_j,L_j]$. Our choice of $R$ therefore places the spectral distribution of $S_n+d$ on $\xi$ in $[-R,R]$ as well. Both sides of <a href="#eq:Zgroups" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:Zgroups">[eq:Zgroups]</a>, evaluated on $\xi$, are thus determined by functions restricted to this same compact interval. Finite linear combinations of the exponentials form a uniformly dense self-adjoint algebra in $C([-R,R])$. Uniform approximation now proves $$\langle Z(f(X_M))\xi,\xi\rangle
 =\langle f(S_n+d)\xi,\xi\rangle\qquad(f\in C_0(\mathbb R)).$$ Continuity in $\xi$ and polarization prove <a href="#eq:Zspectral" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:Zspectral">[eq:Zspectral]</a> on the whole tensor product. The radius $R$ may depend on the labels, shifts, and tensor length: only the resulting exact identity will enter the uniform estimate.

<a id="pm-h-22"></a>
###### The word coefficient.

First, <a href="#eq:Ucoeff" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:Ucoeff">[eq:Ucoeff]</a> and state preservation of $E_B$ give $$\langle U(x\Omega\otimes\zeta),y\Omega\otimes b\Omega\rangle
 =\langle\zeta,E_B(x^*by)\Omega\rangle
 \qquad(x,y\in N,\ b\in B,\ \zeta\in K).$$ The identity follows first for $\zeta=a\Omega$ and then by density. It shows that moving an output test backwards through $U$ keeps its last factor in $B\Omega$. To compute a word coefficient, take elementary tensors and record the adjoints of the successive test labels as $$c_n=\gamma_{-d_n}(b^*),\qquad
 c_{j-1}=\gamma_{-d_{j-1}}\bigl(E_B(y_j^*c_jx_j)\bigr)
 \quad(j=n,\ldots,1).$$ Each $c_j$ belongs to $B$. Because the right multiplier is fixed and lies in the multiplicative domain of every $\theta$, the recurrence for $T=b^*\rho(a_+)$ gives $T_j=c_j\rho(a_+)$. Its final coefficient is $\varphi(c_0a)$.

The same recurrence computes the coefficient of $C$ backwards from its output vector. Moving the output test across $W_{d_j}$ applies $\gamma_{-d_j}$, and <a href="#eq:Ucoeff" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:Ucoeff">[eq:Ucoeff]</a> moves it across $U_j$ by replacing $c_j$ with $E_B(y_j^*c_jx_j)$. The last coefficient is again $\varphi(c_0a)$. This proves <a href="#eq:Zmixed" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:Zmixed">[eq:Zmixed]</a> for elementary tensors; linearity and continuity give the general case. ◻

</details>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-kernel-2"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-kernel</summary>

*Proof of Proposition <a href="#prop:energy" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:energy">6</a>.* First restrict to data for which the spectral support of $S_n+d$ on $\xi$ lies in $[-\delta,\delta]$. The state $$\Phi(T)=\langle Z(T)\xi,\xi\rangle$$ has both marginals equal to $\varphi$ and satisfies $\Phi(e_N)=1$. By <a href="#eq:Zspectral" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:Zspectral">[eq:Zspectral]</a>, along any sequence of such data with $\delta\to0$, every weak\* accumulation state satisfies $\Phi(f(X_M))=f(0)$ for $f\in C_0(\mathbb R)$. Proposition <a href="#prop:inputs" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:inputs">3</a>(i), applied to the fixed operator $a^*\rho(a_+)$, and <a href="#eq:Zmixed" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:Zmixed">[eq:Zmixed]</a> give $$\langle C(\xi\otimes a\Omega),\xi\otimes a\Omega\rangle
 \longrightarrow\langle a^*\Omega a_+,\Omega\rangle
 =\|a\Omega\|^2$$ for every modular-analytic $a\in B$. Since $C$ is a contraction, $$\|C(\xi\otimes a\Omega)-\xi\otimes a\Omega\|^2
 \le 2\|a\Omega\|^2
 -2\operatorname{Re}\langle C(\xi\otimes a\Omega),
                            \xi\otimes a\Omega\rangle,$$ so this difference tends to zero. Density of analytic vectors $a\Omega$ and contractivity give the same conclusion for each fixed $\eta\in K$. The conclusion is uniform in $n$, the shifts, and $\xi$: otherwise a sequence of violating choices would have an accumulation state in the state space of the same algebra $\mathbf B(\mathcal H)$ and contradict the argument just given.

For the ideal word, the same spectral localization gives $$\|D_{\mathrm{all}}(\xi\otimes\eta)-\xi\otimes\eta\|
 \le\sup_{|s|\le\delta}\|W_s\eta-\eta\|\longrightarrow0.$$ Combining these bounds proves the centered estimate. Finally suppose the $S_n$-support of $\xi$ lies in any interval of length $\delta$. Choose $q$ so that the containing interval for $S_n+d+q$ is centered at zero, and replace the final shift $d_n$ by $d_n+q$. This replaces $C$ and $D_{\mathrm{all}}$ by $W_qC$ and $W_qD_{\mathrm{all}}$. Their difference has the same norm, and the input vector $\eta$ is unchanged. The centered estimate therefore proves <a href="#eq:energy" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:energy">[eq:energy]</a> with all the asserted uniformity. ◻

</details>

<a id="sec:repeat"></a>
### Repeated tensor tests

We now prove that the comparison is exact, using only the uniform estimate and the operator structure in <a href="#eq:Cword" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:Cword">[eq:Cword]</a>–<a href="#eq:Dall" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:Dall">[eq:Dall]</a>. A discrepancy between $U$ and $D$ makes their average $(U+D)/2$ lose squared norm. Yet arbitrarily long words in these averages remain uniformly close, after time averaging, to the ideal unitary words. Random recentering will capture the discrepancy with a nonsummable sequence of probabilities, forcing more total norm loss than is available. The sampling must control the approximation error as well as detect the discrepancy; independent sampling provides the same probability factor in both estimates.

<a id="stmt-lem-hybrid"></a>
<div id="lem:hybrid" class="lemma">

**Lemma 8** (Hybrid words and time averaging). *The estimate <a href="#eq:energy" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:energy">[eq:energy]</a> holds with the same modulus if any subset of the $U_j$ is replaced by $D_j$, or if all $U_j$ are replaced by $G_j=(U_j+D_j)/2$. For every $\delta>0$ there is a probability density $p_\delta$ on $\mathbb R$ such that, for every unit $\xi\in H^{\otimes n}$ without a spectral support restriction, <a id="eq:timeavg"></a>$$\label{eq:timeavg}
 \int_\mathbb R\|(C-D_{\mathrm{all}})(e^{itS_n}\xi\otimes\eta)\|^2
       p_\delta(t)\,dt\le\epsilon_\eta(\delta)^2.$$ The statement includes the hybrid and averaged words.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-lem-hybrid"><summary>Proof of stmt-lem-hybrid</summary>

*Proof.* If no $U_j$ remains, the word equals $D_{\mathrm{all}}$. Otherwise disintegrate the replaced positions over their $X$-spectral variables. On a fiber with values $(x_j)$, each replaced $D_j$ acts as the shift $W_{x_j}$ on $K$, and the sum of the remaining energies lies in the original interval translated by $-\sum x_j$. The interval’s length is unchanged. The fiber vector in the remaining positions need not be a simple tensor: Proposition <a href="#prop:energy" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:energy">6</a> applies to every such vector, with the same last factor $\eta$. If a fiber has spectral multiplicity, expand in an orthonormal basis of that multiplicity space and sum the homogeneous squared bounds. Integrating proves the hybrid assertion. Convex expansion proves the assertion for $G_j$; the coefficients of the expansion sum to one, independently of $n$.

Choose an even real $L^2$-normalized smooth function $g$ supported in an interval of length $\delta$, and put $p_\delta(t)=|\widehat g(t)|^2$, with unitarily normalized Fourier transform. For the bounded operator $T=C-D_{\mathrm{all}}$, vector-valued Plancherel gives $$\int_\mathbb R\|T(e^{itS_n}\xi\otimes\eta)\|^2|\widehat g(t)|^2\,dt
 =\int_\mathbb R\|T(g(S_n-z)\xi\otimes\eta)\|^2\,dz.$$ Here Plancherel is applied to $z\mathrel{\pdfliteral direct{/Span << /ActualText <FEFF21A6> >> BDC}\mapsto\pdfliteral direct{EMC}}g(S_n-z)\xi\otimes\eta$ and then to its image under the bounded operator $T$; no commutation of $T$ with $S_n$ is required. The Fourier sign does not matter with this choice of $g$. Each vector on the right is spectrally localized as required by <a href="#eq:energy" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:energy">[eq:energy]</a>, and $$\int_\mathbb R\|g(S_n-z)\xi\|^2\,dz=\|\xi\|^2=1.$$ Apply the homogeneous form of that estimate and integrate. ◻

</details>

<div id="thm:UD" class="theorem">

**Theorem 9**. *For the operators in <a href="#eq:UD" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:UD">[eq:UD]</a>, one has $U=D$.*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-thm-ud"><summary>Proof of stmt-thm-ud</summary>

<a id="eq:localgap"></a>
*Proof.* Suppose otherwise. Density of compact-spectrum vectors gives unit vectors $h\in H$ and $\eta\in K$ with $\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}_X(h)\subset[-l,l]$ and $\|(U-D)(h\otimes\eta)\|>0$. By strong continuity there are $c>0$ and $0<b\le1$ such that $$\label{eq:localgap}
 \|(U-D)(e^{it'X}h\otimes W_{s'}\eta)\|\ge c
 \qquad(|t'|,|s'|\le b).$$ Use this fixed $\eta$ in Lemma <a href="#lem:hybrid" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:hybrid">8</a>. Choose $\delta$ so that $\epsilon:=\epsilon_\eta(\delta)<c/8$, and choose $A\ge1$ with $\int_{-A}^A p_\delta(t)\,dt\ge3/4$.

<a id="pm-h-24"></a>
###### The repeated experiment.

<a id="eq:idealclose"></a>
Fix a finite terminal length $m$. On one product probability space choose $t$ with density $p_\delta$ and mutually independent parameters $$r_j\sim\mathrm{Unif}[-2A,2A],\qquad
 s_j\sim\mathrm{Unif}[-B_j,B_j],\qquad B_j=b+1+jl
 \quad(1\le j\le m),$$ all independent of $t$. At position $j$ put the fresh input $e^{i(t-r_j)X}h$. Starting with $\eta$ on $K$, propagate using $W_{s_j}G_jW_{-s_j}$. Let $\zeta_k$ denote the result after $k$ positions, and let $\chi_k$ be the ideal result with $D_j$ in place of $G_j$. Since $D_j$ commutes with the shifts on $K$, $$\chi_k=\exp(iS_k\otimes Y)
   \left(\bigotimes_{j=1}^k e^{i(t-r_j)X}h\otimes\eta\right).$$ Condition on every parameter other than $t$. Then $\xi_k=\bigotimes_{j=1}^k e^{-ir_jX}h$ is a fixed unit vector, the input is $e^{itS_k}\xi_k\otimes\eta$, and the successive conjugations $W_{s_j}G_jW_{-s_j}$ form an averaged word with fixed shifts summing to zero. Lemma <a href="#lem:hybrid" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:hybrid">8</a> therefore gives $$\label{eq:idealclose}
 \int\|\zeta_k-\chi_k\|^2p_\delta(t)\,dt\le\epsilon^2.$$ For $k=0$, both vectors are $\eta$.

<a id="pm-h-25"></a>
###### A definite loss at each captured test.

<a id="eq:normloss"></a>
<a id="eq:idealbound"></a>
<a id="eq:errorbound"></a>
At step $j=k+1$, insert the fresh position into $\zeta_k$ and apply $W_{-s_j}$ on $K$; call this input $v$. Let $v_0$ be the analogous input formed from $\chi_k$. The parallelogram identity and contractivity give $$\label{eq:normloss}
 \|v\|^2-\|G_jv\|^2\ge\tfrac14\|(U_j-D_j)v\|^2.$$ Define the random projection $$Q=\mathbf 1_{\{|t-r_j|\le b\}}\,\mathbf 1_{\{|S_k-s_j|\le b\}},$$ where the first factor is a scalar indicator and the second acts on the previous $k$ positions. For $k=0$, interpret $S_0$ as zero. The projection $Q$ commutes with $U_j,D_j$ and the last-position shifts. Put $$a_j=\frac{b}{2A}\frac{b}{B_j}.$$ We claim $$\begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E\|(U_j-D_j)Qv_0\|^2&\ge\tfrac34c^2a_j,\label{eq:idealbound}\\
 \mathbb E\|(U_j-D_j)Q(v-v_0)\|^2&\le4\epsilon^2a_j.\label{eq:errorbound}
\end{aligned}$$ Here $\mathbb E$ integrates all parameters involved, including $t$.

For <a href="#eq:idealbound" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:idealbound">[eq:idealbound]</a>, disintegrate the previous ideal vector over $S_k=e$. Its last factor is $W_e\eta$, and $|e|\le kl$. The interval $[e-b,e+b]$ lies in $[-B_j,B_j]$, so the probability over $s_j$ of capturing it is exactly $b/B_j$, as illustrated in Figure <a href="#fig:capture" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="fig:capture">1</a>. For $|t|\le A$, the probability over $r_j$ of $|t-r_j|\le b$ is $b/(2A)$. On these fibers the current last factor is $W_{e-s_j}\eta$, so <a href="#eq:localgap" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:localgap">[eq:localgap]</a> applies. Integrating, and using the mass at least $3/4$ of $[-A,A]$, proves <a href="#eq:idealbound" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:idealbound">[eq:idealbound]</a>.

###### The contradiction.

<a id="eq:positive-loss"></a>

<a id="sec:core"></a>
### The continuous core and the general inclusion

The multiplication identity now determines the fixed points of the bicentralizer flow in the core. We recall why the core fixed-point equality yields an amenable subalgebra, then prove that equality in coordinates adapted to $N$. The final reduction passes from factors to an arbitrary center and invokes the existing amenable-subalgebra theorem.

<a id="pm-h-28"></a>
###### The core criterion.

<a id="eq:core-bic"></a>
<a id="eq:core-auto"></a>
<a id="eq:WD"></a>
For a type $\mathrm{III}_1$ bottom factor, the bicentralizer in the core and its flow have the following description. Here $\mathrm B(N\subset c(M),\varphi)$ uses the same asymptotic commutation condition on bounded sequences in $N$, with the state $\varphi$ on $N$. In the realization $c(M)=M\rtimes_{\sigma^\varphi}\mathbb R$, with group unitaries $l(t)$, one has $$\label{eq:core-bic}
 \mathrm B(N\subset c(M),\varphi)=B\rtimes_{\sigma^\varphi}\mathbb R,
 \qquad \gamma_s(l(t))=e^{-its}l(t).$$ The algebra identity is <a id="pm-cite-34"></a>[[M1](#ref-M1), Proposition 7.2]; the action extends the flow on $B$ as in <a id="pm-cite-35"></a>[[M1](#ref-M1), Proposition 8.6, proof]. One inclusion in the desired fixed-point equality is automatic: $$\label{eq:core-auto}
 N'\cap c(M)\subset\mathrm B(N\subset c(M),\varphi)^\gamma.$$ This inclusion follows by combining <a id="pm-cite-36"></a>[[M1](#ref-M1), Proposition 8.1 and its proof, Theorem 6.20(5), and Proposition 8.4]. We will prove the reverse inclusion. Equality is equivalent to the relative bicentralizer conjecture by <a id="pm-cite-37"></a>[[M1](#ref-M1), Proposition 8.6]. For an expected inclusion, that conjecture is equivalent to the *weak Dixmier property* for $N\subset c(M)$: $$\label{eq:WD}
 \overline{\mathop{\mathrm{conv}}\{uxu^*:u\in\mathcal U(N)\}}^{\,w^*}
 \cap(N'\cap c(M))\ne\varnothing\qquad(x\in c(M)).$$ With separable preduals, <a href="#eq:WD" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:WD">[eq:WD]</a> is equivalent to the conclusion of Theorem <a href="#thm:main" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:main">1</a>, by <a id="pm-cite-38"></a>[[M1](#ref-M1), Theorem D].

<a id="stmt-prop-corefixed"></a>
<div id="prop:corefixed" class="proposition">

**Proposition 10**. *If $N$ is a type $\mathrm{III}_1$ factor, then $$\mathrm B(N\subset c(M),\varphi)^\gamma=N'\cap c(M).$$*

</div>

<details id="proof-stmt-prop-corefixed"><summary>Proof of stmt-prop-corefixed</summary>

*Proof.*

<a id="pm-h-29"></a>
###### The algebra generated by $N$ and $B$.

By Theorem <a href="#thm:UD" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:UD">9</a>, $L^*R=D$ is unitary. Since $L,R$ are isometries, this implies $R=LD$, so their ranges agree. Put $A=B\vee N$. The common range is $L^2(A,\varphi)$: it contains $\Omega$, is invariant under left $N$ by its $R$ description and under left $B$ by its $L$ description, and is contained in $L^2(A,\varphi)$. Applying adjoints gives invariance under the generated von Neumann algebra. Both $N$ and $B$ are modularly invariant, so the expected algebra $A$ has a canonically embedded core inside $c(M)$.

<a id="pm-h-30"></a>
###### Coordinates adapted to $N$.

Use $L$ to identify $L^2(A,\varphi)$ with $H\otimes K$. In these coordinates left $B$ acts as $1\otimes B$, left $N$ acts as $D(N\otimes1)D^*$, and the modular group is $$e^{itX}\otimes\Delta_{\varphi|_B}^{it}.$$ Represent $c(A)$ faithfully on $H\otimes K\otimes L^2(\mathbb R)$ with constant base algebra and group unitaries $$e^{itX}\otimes\Delta_{\varphi|_B}^{it}\otimes l_{\mathbb R}(t),$$ where $l_\mathbb R$ is the translation representation. This is the usual regular crossed-product representation after conjugating its variable base algebra by the modular implementation.

<a id="eq:corefiber"></a>
Conjugate by $D^*$ on the first two positions. This removes the twist from the action of $N$, which now acts on $H$ alone, and transfers it to the core of $B$. Since $W_s$ commutes with $\Delta_{\varphi|_B}^{it}$, the algebra $B\rtimes\mathbb R$ is represented, at spectral value $X=h$, by $$\label{eq:corefiber}
 a\mathrel{\pdfliteral direct{/Span << /ActualText <FEFF27FC> >> BDC}\longmapsto\pdfliteral direct{EMC}}\gamma_{-h}(a)\otimes1,\qquad
 l(t)\mathrel{\pdfliteral direct{/Span << /ActualText <FEFF27FC> >> BDC}\longmapsto\pdfliteral direct{EMC}}e^{ith}\Delta_{\varphi|_B}^{it}\otimes l_\mathbb R(t).$$ The two transformations in <a href="#eq:corefiber" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:corefiber">[eq:corefiber]</a> are the same flow: on $B$ it is $\gamma_{-h}$, and on $l(t)$ it multiplies by $e^{ith}$. Thus these fibers are a fixed normal representation of $B\rtimes\mathbb R$ composed with the core bicentralizer flow at $-h$, by <a href="#eq:core-bic" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:core-bic">[eq:core-bic]</a>. More explicitly, if $H=\int^\oplus H_h\,d\nu(h)$ and $\pi_0$ is the fixed regular representation on $K\otimes L^2(\mathbb R)$, the representation of an element $z\in B\rtimes\mathbb R$ is $$\int^\oplus I_{H_h}\otimes\pi_0(\gamma_{-h}(z))\,d\nu(h).$$ This normal direct-integral representation agrees with <a href="#eq:corefiber" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:corefiber">[eq:corefiber]</a> on generators, so the formula applies to the entire crossed product.

<a id="pm-h-31"></a>
###### The fixed algebra.

If $z$ is fixed by the core bicentralizer flow, then $\pi_0(\gamma_{-h}(z))=\pi_0(z)$ in every fiber. It therefore acts on $K\otimes L^2(\mathbb R)$ alone, while $N$ acts on $H$ alone. Hence it commutes with $N$, proving $$\mathrm B(N\subset c(M),\varphi)^\gamma\subset N'\cap c(M).$$ The opposite inclusion is <a href="#eq:core-auto" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:core-auto">[eq:core-auto]</a>, so the fixed algebras agree. ◻

</details>

<details id="proof-stmt-prop-corefixed-2"><summary>Proof of stmt-prop-corefixed</summary>

*Proof of Theorem <a href="#thm:main" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:main">1</a>.*

<a id="pm-h-32"></a>
###### Factor bottom algebras.

For a type $\mathrm{III}_1$ bottom factor, Proposition <a href="#prop:corefixed" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:corefixed">10</a> and <a id="pm-cite-39"></a>[[M1](#ref-M1), Proposition 8.6 and Theorem D] give the weak Dixmier property <a href="#eq:WD" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:WD">[eq:WD]</a>. The state on $N$ is a strictly semifinite weight, as required there. For a bottom factor of any other type, the same weak Dixmier property is <a id="pm-cite-40"></a>[[M1](#ref-M1), Proposition 4.11]. These factor cases allow arbitrary upper algebras.

<a id="pm-h-33"></a>
###### Disintegration over the center.

For a general $N$, put $J=\mathcal Z(N)$ and $M_0=J'\cap M$. The passage to $M_0$ makes $J$ central in both algebras, so that we can apply the factor cases fiber by fiber. The modular action of $\varphi=\varphi\circ E_N$ fixes $J$ pointwise. Consequently $M_0$ is modularly invariant, and $E_N$ restricts to a faithful normal expectation $M_0\to N$. Moreover, <a id="eq:center-core"></a>$$\label{eq:center-core}
 J'\cap c(M)=c(M_0)$$ inside the chosen core, by <a id="pm-cite-41"></a>[[M1](#ref-M1), Proposition 4.7].

Disintegrate $N\subset M_0$ over the common central algebra $J$, using the probability measure $\mu$ determined by $\varphi|_J$. The bottom fibers $N_z$ are factors because $J=\mathcal Z(N)$; the upper fibers $(M_0)_z$ need not be factors. The simultaneous disintegration theorem for operator-valued weights gives faithful normal fiber expectations $E_z:(M_0)_z\to N_z$ and compatible faithful state fibers $\varphi_z$ <a id="pm-cite-42"></a>[[IK2007](#ref-IK2007), Propositions 2.1–2.2 and Theorem 2.3]. Indeed, $E_N$ is $J$-bimodular, and its fiber weights satisfy $E_z(1)=1$, so they are conditional expectations. The state identity $\varphi=\varphi\circ E_N$ and the modular actions also disintegrate, as in the proof of that theorem.

To see the resulting core compatibility, write $H_0=L^2(M_0,\varphi)=\int^\oplus H_z\,d\mu(z)$, where $H_z=L^2((M_0)_z,\varphi_z)$. The regular representation of the core on $L^2(\mathbb R,H_0)=\int^\oplus L^2(\mathbb R,H_z)\,d\mu(z)$ is given by $$(\pi(a)\xi)(t)=\sigma_{-t}^\varphi(a)\xi(t),\qquad
 (l(s)\xi)(t)=\xi(t-s).$$ Both formulas act fiberwise. A countable family of algebra generators and the rational translation parameters generate the fiber cores; the common central algebra $J$ is already present in the representation. Thus $c(M_0)=\int^\oplus c((M_0)_z)\,d\mu(z)$ with the corresponding copies of $N_z$. Separability supplies these standard measurable fields. The factor cases and the direct-integral criterion <a id="pm-cite-43"></a>[[M1](#ref-M1), Proposition 4.12] now give the weak Dixmier property for $N\subset c(M_0)$.

<a id="pm-h-34"></a>
###### Averaging into the central commutant.

We have proved the weak Dixmier property inside $c(M_0)$. To apply it to an arbitrary $x\in c(M)$, we first average $x$ into this smaller core. The weak\* compact convex hull of its orbit under $\mathcal U(J)$ is preserved by the commuting affine maps $\mathop{\mathrm{Ad}}(u)$, $u\in\mathcal U(J)$. The compact-convex fixed-point theorem for commuting affine maps gives a common fixed point $y\in J'\cap c(M)=c(M_0)$. One can obtain it by taking rectangular Cesàro averages for each finite family of commuting maps, then using compactness and the finite-intersection property. Since $J\subset N$, this point belongs to the weak\* closed convex $N$-orbit hull of $x$. That hull is $N$-invariant. Applying weak Dixmier averaging inside $c(M_0)$ therefore produces a point of $N'\cap c(M)$ still in the original hull. This proves <a href="#eq:WD" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:WD">[eq:WD]</a> for the original inclusion.

<a id="pm-h-35"></a>
###### The amenable subalgebra.

Finally supplies an amenable subalgebra $P\subset N$ with a faithful normal conditional expectation and $P'\cap c(M)=N'\cap c(M)$. The expectation is faithful and normal by the meaning of “with expectation” in that theorem. Unitality is automatic: if $p=1_P<1_N$, bimodularity would imply $F(1-p)=0$, contradicting faithfulness. This proves the result. ◻
